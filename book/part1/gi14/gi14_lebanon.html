Lebanon

The war with Lebanon after October 2023 began with Hezbollah's fire, not Israel's. On 8 October 2023, the day after the Hamas attack, Hezbollah fired on three Israeli positions in the Shebaa Farms "in solidarity with the victorious Palestinian resistance," and Israel answered with artillery and a drone strike. [cite:hz7_aj_8oct] This was far from the first round. Israel invaded in 1978 and 1982 and held a zone in the south until 2000. The Hezbollah volume and Part Eight of this book cover that history. [scene:gi8_lebanon|Part Eight: Lebanon, 1978–2000] [scene:hz4_zone|The Hezbollah Architecture: the security zone] The earlier tolls are counted there too: by the Lebanese police's count, 19,085 people were killed between 4 June and 31 August 1982, a figure that includes fighters; and in the 2006 war Human Rights Watch documented at least 1,109 Lebanese killed, "the vast majority of whom were civilians," while 121 Israeli soldiers and 44 Israeli civilians died. [cite:hz2b_csm_dec82] [cite:hz5_rw_why] [cite:hz5_wiki_war] [scene:hz5_toll|The Hezbollah Architecture: the toll of 2006]

The support front, 2023–2024

For almost a year the fighting stayed close to the border, and both sides shot at it. ACLED counted at least 13,608 cross-border attacks between 7 October 2023 and 18 October 2024. About 83 per cent were Israel's, and they killed at least 2,318 people in Lebanon; Hezbollah and allied groups carried out 2,370, which killed at least 41 Israelis. [cite:hz7_aj_acled_year] On 27 July 2024 a rocket killed twelve Druze children and teenagers on a football pitch in Majdal Shams, in the Israeli-held Golan. Israel and the United States attributed it to Hezbollah. Human Rights Watch found the damage consistent with a Hezbollah Falaq-type rocket. Hezbollah denied any connection. [cite:hz7_wiki_majdal] [cite:hz7_aj_majdal] About 60,000 residents of northern Israel were displaced by official count. About half had still not gone home by March 2025. [cite:hz7_afp_north_return]

In September 2024 Israel turned the border war into a campaign across Lebanon: the pager detonations, an air campaign that killed Hassan Nasrallah, and a ground invasion on 1 October. [scene:hz7_war|The Hezbollah Architecture: the autumn 2024 war] When the ceasefire of 27 November 2024 took effect, the Lebanese health minister counted at least 4,047 killed and 16,593 wounded since 8 October 2023, including 790 women and 316 children. The ministry did not separate civilians from fighters. [cite:hz7_olj_abiad_4047] Alma, an Israeli research centre, counted 45 Israeli civilians and 83 soldiers killed on the northern front over the same period. [cite:hz7_alma_summary] The World Bank put Lebanon's losses to 20 December 2024 at $14 billion, $6.8 billion of it physical damage, and recovery needs at $11 billion; the economy shrank 7.1 per cent in 2024. [cite:hz9_wb_rdna]

The truce that was not quiet, and the 2026 war

The truce stopped Hezbollah's fire but not Israel's. The UN Human Rights Office counted at least 127 civilians killed by Israeli forces in its first year. The health ministry counted 331 dead of all kinds by 24 November 2025. Israel said its strikes targeted Hezbollah's attempts to rearm. [cite:hz8_ohchr_nov25] [cite:hz8_aa_tabatabai] [scene:hz8_violations|The Hezbollah Architecture: the truce in numbers] On 2 March 2026, after the joint American and Israeli strikes on Iran that killed Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, Hezbollah fired missiles and drones at Israel. It cited his killing and fifteen months of Israeli strikes. That afternoon the Lebanese cabinet banned all of Hezbollah's military activity. [cite:hz8_am_mar2] [cite:hz8_olj_ban] On 7 March Hezbollah told the residents of Kiryat Shmona to "evacuate immediately. Head south." Defence Minister Israel Katz urged northern residents to stay. [cite:gi14_almon_kiryat] Israel's stated aim was to remove Hezbollah's ability to threaten the north. On 16 March its forces entered the south, and Katz said displaced Lebanese would not return "south of the Litani" until northern Israel was safe. [cite:hz8b_afp_ground] [scene:hz8_war2026|The Hezbollah Architecture: the 2026 war]

Lebanon's health ministry counted 3,912 killed by 19 June 2026, when a ceasefire took effect under the American–Iranian interim deal. By 11 October the total was 4,387 killed and 12,424 injured since 2 March. [cite:hz8b_olj_jun19] [cite:hz8b_qna_toll] The ministry's figure from 8 October 2023 onward was 8,954 killed and 30,660 injured, a total that spans both wars and the truce between them. [cite:hz8b_qna_toll] Over the 2026 war the ministry counted at least 100 health and rescue workers killed by mid-April. [cite:hz8_olj_toll] More than 1.2 million people were displaced at the peak, by the government's count. Israel put its losses to Hezbollah at 39 soldiers, a contractor and two civilians, and UNIFIL lost seven peacekeepers during the year. [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] Netanyahu claimed by 31 May that 3,000 Hezbollah fighters had been killed. The figure is Israel's alone; Hezbollah published none. [cite:hz8b_ap_beaufort]

The destruction was concentrated in the south. A rapid survey by the UN Development Programme and Lebanon's National Council for Scientific Research compared satellite images from late April 2026 with October 2025. It found 11,095 buildings completely destroyed, containing 17,891 housing units, and more than 11,500 damaged. It valued direct building damage at $1.38 billion, and it did not cover the weeks of fighting that followed. [cite:gi14_an_undp] In the border village of Yaroun the mayor told NPR that 180 of about 200 houses had been destroyed since March. Israel said it was targeting Hezbollah infrastructure, and in March Katz had said Israel meant to depopulate the border villages to create a "buffer zone." [cite:gi14_npr_yaroun] Lebanon's finance minister put the cost of the 2026 war at about $20 billion. [cite:hz8b_olj_salam]

Destabilisation, and where things stand

The war tested a Lebanese state that had been in economic collapse since 2019. Its economy had lost close to 40 per cent of its output since then. [cite:hz9_wb_rdna] [scene:hz9_economies|The Hezbollah Architecture: the economies] The state now faced two armed forces on its soil that it did not control. Hezbollah went to war in defiance of a cabinet decision. Israel held a strip behind the Yellow Line covering about 6 per cent of the country, kept up strikes and demolitions after the June ceasefire, and barred residents from dozens of villages. [cite:hz8_olj_ban] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] About 40 per cent of the displaced had returned by late June. [cite:hz8b_olj_salam] UNIFIL's mandate ends on 31 December 2026. [cite:hz8_scr_nov25] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] On 20 October 2026 military delegations met in Tampa, with the United States in the middle. Lebanon said it would demand "an end to Israeli attacks in Lebanon"; Israel says the targets it strikes violate the agreement. [cite:gi14_toi_tampa] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] Part Nine follows the Yellow Line. [scene:gi9_lebanon|Part Nine: south Lebanon] [scene:hz8_now|The Hezbollah Architecture: where things stand]

Whether this campaign served expansion is an argument, and the record cuts both ways. Netanyahu said that "as long as it is not disarmed, we will remain in this zone." Katz spoke of depopulating border villages, and the army crossed the Litani to take Beaufort castle, which Israel had held from 1982 to 2000. [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] [cite:gi14_npr_yaroun] [cite:hz8b_ap_beaufort] Against that reading, Israel signed a framework in June 2026 under which it may withdraw to its frontiers once the danger to its citizens ends. It withdrew from the first pilot village in July. It left Lebanon entirely in 2000 and has made no claim to Lebanese territory in any law or government decision.
