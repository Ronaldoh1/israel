Inside American Newsrooms

CNN. CNN requires all reporting on Israel and Palestine, wherever its journalists are based, to be reviewed by its Jerusalem bureau before publication; the network says the policy reflects "many unique and complex local nuances that warrant extra scrutiny," and that it does not share copy with the censor in advance. A November 2023 directive from its standards department, citing Hamas's "inflammatory rhetoric and propaganda," warned: "We should be careful not to give it a platform." [cite:gz_intercept_cnn] In February 2024 The Guardian reported, from six CNN staffers and more than a dozen internal memos, that staff believed the rules led the network to take Israeli government statements at face value; one called the coverage "journalistic malpractice." CNN answered that its reporting "has confronted Israel's response to the attacks, including some of our most detailed and high-profile investigations." [cite:gz_an_cnnstaff]

The New York Times. An internal memo first issued in November 2023, reported by The Intercept in April 2024, told Times journalists to restrict the terms "genocide" and "ethnic cleansing," to avoid "occupied territory," to use "Palestine" only in rare cases, and not to describe areas historically settled by displaced Palestinians as "refugee camps." [cite:gz_nl_nytmemo] A Times spokesman, Charlie Stadtlander, said: "Issuing guidance like this to ensure accuracy, consistency and nuance in how we cover the news is standard practice." [cite:gz_dawn_nyt]

What was published. The Intercept analysed more than 1,100 articles in the New York Times, the Washington Post and the Los Angeles Times from the first six weeks of the war. For every Israeli killed, Israelis were mentioned eight times, a rate sixteen times higher per death than for Palestinians. The words "slaughter," "massacre" and "horrific" were used almost only for Israelis killed by Palestinians, and only two headlines in the whole sample mentioned the children of Gaza. [cite:gz_aa_intercept]

Ownership. In August 2025 Skydance, controlled by David Ellison, son of the Oracle founder Larry Ellison, completed its takeover of Paramount, owner of CBS, after promising the federal government greater "viewpoint diversity" at the network, according to the chairman of the FCC. In October Paramount bought The Free Press and made its founder, Bari Weiss, known for her pro-Israel positions, editor-in-chief of CBS News, reporting directly to David Ellison. The Free Press had published an article titled "The Gaza Famine Myth." [cite:gz_aj_weiss] The library's [scene:ell_s02|account of the Ellison architecture] sets out the family's documented ties. What the record documents here is ownership and appointment; whether owners direct particular coverage is a question of motive that these sources do not settle.

What Americans came to think. Despite all of this, opinion moved. In February 2026, for the first time in Gallup's annual measurement since 2001, Americans' sympathies no longer lay more with the Israelis: 41 percent sympathized more with the Palestinians and 36 percent with the Israelis, a gap Gallup said was not statistically significant; three years earlier the figures had been 31 and 54. [cite:gz_gallup_2026] About two-thirds of Democrats and half of Americans aged 18 to 34 now sympathized more with the Palestinians, while about seven in ten Republicans still sympathized more with Israel. [cite:gz_ap_gallup2026]

The record shows the mechanisms: an access ban, a military censor, newsroom rules, a measured imbalance in language, and changes of ownership. It does not show a single directing hand, and the outlets named here deny bias and point to their own investigations, several of which this volume cites, among them the New York Times's reporting on the "Jericho Wall" plan in Part Six. The gap between what happened in Gaza and what most Americans were shown is documented in the mechanisms themselves.
