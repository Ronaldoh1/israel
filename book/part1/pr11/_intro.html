Part Eleven asks what of all this reached American readers. Gaza's journalists filed their stories; the question is how American newsrooms carried the count of the dead and the deaths of the journalists themselves. This part sets out studies of the coverage from both directions: critics who found Israeli deaths given more weight and Israeli claims repeated in headlines, and critics who found the press relying too heavily on the Gaza health ministry; the internal policies of two major newsrooms; how fifteen outlets reported the killing of Anas al-Sharif; and how American opinion moved.
