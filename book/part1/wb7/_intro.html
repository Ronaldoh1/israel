Part Seven describes the law under which the Palestinians of the West Bank live, and the systems that watch them. Since 1967 two legal systems have operated side by side in the same territory, one for Israeli citizens and one for Palestinians. This part sets out how they differ, as an Israeli civil-rights group documented and as Israel's own Justice Ministry defended; the military courts and their conviction rate; detention without charge; the detention of children; the facial-recognition systems used at checkpoints; and the conclusion of three human rights organizations, which Israel rejects, that the whole amounts to apartheid.
