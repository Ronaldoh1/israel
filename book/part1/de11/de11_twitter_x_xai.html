Twitter, X and xAI

In 2022 Musk bought the company that ran one of the world's main public forums. By 2026 that forum, an artificial-intelligence company trained partly on its posts, and the satellite network described in the previous section all sat under one corporate parent controlled by him. This section sets out how that happened, who put money into it, what changed on the platform, and the record of government pressure on speech, which runs in more than one direction. The surveillance and data questions raised by one owner holding all three are taken up in Book Five.

The purchase

Musk disclosed a stake of about 9 per cent in Twitter on 4 April 2022 and on 14 April offered to buy the whole company for $54.20 a share, about $44 billion. [cite:de11_wiki_acq] [cite:de11_cnbc_coinvest] On 8 July he announced he was walking away, claiming Twitter had breached the agreement; Twitter sued him in the Delaware Court of Chancery on 12 July; and on 27 October 2022 the deal closed. [cite:de11_wiki_acq] The purchase was paid for with Musk's own money, about $13 billion of bank debt loaded onto the company, and equity from co-investors. [cite:de11_wiki_acq]

The co-investors were named in a filing on 5 May 2022. They included the Oracle founder Larry Ellison with $1 billion, the venture firm Sequoia with $800 million, the crypto exchange Binance with $500 million, Qatar Holding, part of Qatar's sovereign wealth fund, with $375 million, and Fidelity with $316 million, for a total of $7.14 billion in new commitments. [cite:de11_cnbc_coinvest] The Saudi investor Prince Alwaleed bin Talal and his Kingdom Holding company rolled over their existing stake of about 35 million shares, worth about $1.89 billion, rather than selling, and described themselves as jointly the second-largest investor after Musk; Twitter's co-founder Jack Dorsey rolled over shares worth just under $1 billion. [cite:de11_ab_gulf] Foreign sovereign and royal money, in other words, was documented in the purchase from the start, as an investment in a company. No document in this book's record shows foreign money flowing to the theorists of Part One; the distinction is drawn in [scene:de36_foreign_money|Part Thirty-Six].

What changed

On the day the deal closed, Musk fired the chief executive, Parag Agrawal, the chief financial officer, the head of legal and policy, Vijaya Gadde, and the general counsel. On 4 November 2022 he laid off roughly half the workforce; staff eventually fell from about 7,500 to about 1,500. [cite:de11_wiki_acq] On 19 November, after an online poll in which 51.8 per cent voted yes, he restored Donald Trump's account, suspended after the attack on the Capitol of 6 January 2021, with the words "Vox Populi, Vox Dei." [cite:de11_globe_trump] In December 2022 he suspended several journalists who had covered his removal of an account that tracked his private jet. [cite:de11_wiki_acq] In July 2023 the service was renamed X. [cite:de11_wiki_acq]

Musk has repeatedly described himself as a "free speech absolutist." [cite:de11_fortune_turkey] The record of his ownership shows that commitment applied unevenly, and he has given his reasons. On the eve of Turkey's presidential election in May 2023, X restricted access to some content in Turkey at the government's demand. Musk's defence was practical: "The choice is have Twitter throttled in its entirety or limit access to some tweets." [cite:de11_fortune_turkey] Wikipedia's founder, Jimmy Wales, replied that his site had fought Turkey's government in court and won. [cite:de11_fortune_turkey] In Brazil the outcome was the reverse at first: Justice Alexandre de Moraes of the Supreme Federal Court ordered X blocked on 30 August 2024 after it failed to comply with orders to block accounts accused of spreading anti-democratic content, to name a legal representative and to pay fines; X complied and paid, and the ban was lifted in October. [cite:de11_jurist_brazil]

Government pressure on speech: the record both ways

Musk's purchase was framed, by him and by supporters, as a rescue of free speech from a platform that had bent to government. The record on that claim cuts in more than one direction. From 2 December 2022 selected writers, among them Matt Taibbi, Bari Weiss and Michael Shellenberger, published internal Twitter documents in releases coordinated with Musk, as the "Twitter Files." [cite:de11_wiki_tfiles] They showed internal deliberation over the October 2020 suppression of a New York Post story about Hunter Biden's laptop, over Trump's suspension, and over contacts with the FBI. They also showed that requests to remove content came from both sides: the Biden campaign asked for review of tweets, and the Trump White House made takedown requests often enough that the company kept a database of them. [cite:de11_wiki_tfiles] Taibbi himself wrote that there was "no evidence - that I've seen - of any government involvement in the laptop story." [cite:de11_wiki_tfiles]

The legal test of federal pressure on platforms reached the Supreme Court in Murthy v. Missouri. The Fifth Circuit had found that federal agencies likely coerced platforms through "intimidating messages and threats of adverse consequences"; on 26 June 2024 the Court ruled 6 to 3, in an opinion by Justice Amy Coney Barrett, that the states and users who sued lacked standing to seek an injunction, without deciding whether coercion had occurred. Justice Samuel Alito, joined by Justices Thomas and Gorsuch, dissented. [cite:de11_scotus_murthy] [cite:de11_wiki_tfiles] The record therefore supports a narrower statement than either side's: governments of both parties asked platforms to act on content; whether any of those requests crossed into unconstitutional coercion was argued and never resolved on the merits; and under Musk's ownership X has both resisted and complied with governments, abroad as at home, for reasons he has stated as practical. The record of press and speech under the administration since 2025 is set out in [scene:us3_speech|the library's volume on the United States].

xAI and Grok

Musk launched xAI, an artificial-intelligence company, in July 2023, to compete with products such as ChatGPT; its chatbot, Grok, was built into X. [cite:de11_wiki_musk] Grok's outputs have twice become news for what they said about race and Jews. On 14 May 2025 it began inserting claims of "white genocide" in South Africa into answers to unrelated questions; xAI blamed an "unauthorized modification" to its instructions by an employee. [cite:de11_wiki_musk_sa] In July 2025 it posted praise of Hitler and claims that people with Jewish surnames were behind "anti-white" activism; xAI said it had "taken action to ban hate speech before Grok posts on X," and the Anti-Defamation League called the output "irresponsible, dangerous and antisemitic, plain and simple." [cite:de11_reuters_grok] These are the company's product failures and the company's explanations. They are not, on the record, statements by Musk, and no document shows him directing the outputs. In the same month, on 14 July 2025, the Defense Department's AI office awarded xAI, alongside Anthropic, Google and OpenAI, an agreement with a ceiling of $200 million to develop AI tools for national-security work. [cite:de11_cdao]

The mergers of 2025 and 2026

On 28 March 2025 Musk announced that "xAI has acquired X in an all-stock transaction," valuing xAI at $80 billion and X at $33 billion, which he calculated as "$45B less $12B debt." [cite:de11_tc_xai_x] The valuation of X was well below the $44 billion he had paid. xAI kept raising money, including from the Gulf: its $20 billion round announced on 8 January 2026 included the Qatar Investment Authority and MGX, an Abu Dhabi technology fund, alongside Nvidia and Cisco. [cite:de11_agbi_xai] On 2 February 2026 SpaceX acquired xAI in what Reuters described as the largest merger on record, valuing SpaceX at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion; Musk called it "the next book in SpaceX and xAI's mission." [cite:de11_reuters_spacex_xai] SpaceX went public in June 2026, briefly making Musk the first person worth a trillion dollars; Forbes estimated his fortune at about $1.1 trillion in October 2026. [cite:de11_wiki_musk]

The result, as of October 2026, is a single company that owns a public forum, the chatbot trained on it, a satellite internet network used by armies, and a satellite network built for an intelligence agency, with Gulf sovereign funds among its shareholders and Musk as its controlling owner. [cite:de11_reuters_spacex_xai] [cite:de11_agbi_xai] [cite:de11_reuters_starshield] What that combination can do with data is a capability question, examined in [scene:de24_data_and_fusion|Part Twenty-Four]; no document shows the data streams being combined.
