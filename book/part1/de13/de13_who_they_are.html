Who They Are

In October 2007 the business magazine Fortune gathered thirteen former employees of PayPal in Tosca, a bar in San Francisco, dressed them in tracksuits and gold chains in a nod to The Sopranos, and photographed them for a story it titled "The PayPal Mafia." [cite:de13_fortune_2007] [cite:de13_fortune_2024] The name stuck. It is a journalist's joke, not a description of any organisation: there is no charter, no membership roll, no common fund and no shared programme. What there is, and what the photograph records, is a group of people who worked together at one payments company between 1998 and 2002 and who, in the magazine's words, "call upon one another when they need money or advice." [cite:de13_fortune_2007] This section sets out who they are, what each went on to build, and how far their politics diverge, because the rest of the book traces money and appointments that run through some of them, and the reader needs to know from the start that the network is not one bloc.

The company they shared

PayPal began as two companies. Confinity was founded in December 1998 by Peter Thiel and Max Levchin, with Luke Nosek and Ken Howery among the co-founders; X.com was Elon Musk's online-banking start-up. The two merged in March 2000, and in September 2000 the board replaced Musk with Thiel as chief executive. [cite:de9_companies] [cite:de13_wiki_howery] [cite:de9_snopes_paypal] The company went public on 15 February 2002 and eBay bought it for $1.5 billion that October. [cite:de9_wiki_thiel] [cite:de13_fortune_2007] Most of its senior people left eBay soon after, "but they stayed in touch." [cite:de13_fortune_2007] The founders' own story of PayPal, and Thiel's and Musk's parts in it, are told in [scene:de9_paypal_palantir_facebook|Thiel's life] and [scene:de11_zip2_xcom_paypal|Musk's life]; they are not repeated here.

One point about the present needs stating plainly, because it is often blurred. PayPal today is not a company of the people in the photograph. eBay owned it from 2002 until July 2015, when it was spun off as an independent company listed on the Nasdaq exchange under its own management. [cite:de13_rtp_spin] Nothing in the record reviewed for this part shows Thiel, Musk or any other alumnus holding a controlling or reported stake in PayPal Holdings since then; they are its founders and early staff, not its owners. The "PayPal network" in this book means the people, not the company.

The photograph, person by person

The caption Fortune printed in 2007 named each person and his job at the time. In the back row stood Jawed Karim, a co-founder of YouTube; Jeremy Stoppelman, chief executive of the review site Yelp; Andrew McCormack, then running a restaurant called Laiola; and Premal Shah, president of the micro-lending charity Kiva. In front of them were Luke Nosek and Ken Howery, managing partners of the venture firm Founders Fund; David Sacks, chief executive of the genealogy site Geni; Peter Thiel, of Clarium Capital and Founders Fund; Keith Rabois, then at the start-up Slide and an early YouTube investor; Reid Hoffman, the founder of LinkedIn; Max Levchin, chief executive of Slide; Roelof Botha, a partner at the venture firm Sequoia Capital; and Russel Simmons, Yelp's co-founder and technology chief. [cite:de13_fortune_2007] Musk was not in the picture: the magazine said he was in Chicago receiving an award. YouTube's other founders, Chad Hurley and Steve Chen, did not attend either. [cite:de13_fortune_2007]

The companies that came out of this group are large by any measure. Hurley, Chen and Karim founded YouTube in 2005 and sold it to Google on 9 October 2006 for $1.65 billion. [cite:de13_wiki_hurley] Hoffman built LinkedIn and later became a founding investor in OpenAI. [cite:de13_fortune_2024] Levchin sold Slide to Google in August 2010 for $182 million and in 2012 co-founded Affirm, a consumer-lending company. [cite:de13_wiki_levchin] Sacks founded the workplace network Yammer, which Microsoft bought in July 2012 for $1.2 billion, and in late 2017 co-founded the venture firm Craft Ventures. [cite:de13_wiki_sacks] Rabois was an executive at LinkedIn and later chief operating officer of the payments company Square, co-founded the home-selling company Opendoor in 2014, and has moved between the venture firms Khosla Ventures and Founders Fund. [cite:de13_wiki_rabois] Thiel co-founded Palantir in 2003 and Founders Fund in 2005, the latter with Howery and Nosek. [cite:de9_companies] Musk went on to Tesla and SpaceX, and in August 2008 Founders Fund invested $20 million in SpaceX, with Nosek joining its board; Musk said the firm had "a track record of investing in companies with the potential to revolutionize industries." [cite:de13_space_ff] Andrew McCormack, the restaurateur in the back row, later came to lead, with James Fitzgerald, the financial-technology fund Valar Ventures, which Thiel co-founded; that fund returns in [scene:de14_epstein_valar|Part Fourteen]. [cite:de14_nyt_valar]

The degree of each tie matters for everything that follows. Nosek and Howery were Thiel's business partners for two decades, a direct tie by employment and ownership. Rabois worked inside Founders Fund for five years, a direct tie by employment that ended in January 2024. [cite:de13_wiki_rabois] Sacks co-wrote The Diversity Myth with Thiel in 1995 and shared PayPal with him, a direct tie by authorship and former employment; his later companies are his own. [cite:de13_wiki_sacks] Hoffman, Levchin, Botha, Stoppelman and the YouTube founders share the PayPal years and little documented since, which makes them, for this book's purposes, one step removed: former colleagues, not partners.

Not one bloc: the political spread

The most common error about the PayPal network is to treat it as a single political actor. The record does not allow that. Reid Hoffman, the second-best-known person in the 2007 photograph, is one of the largest Democratic donors in the country: since 2020 he has given nearly $9 million to the Wisconsin Democratic Party alone, and he gave at least $7 million to Kamala Harris's presidential campaign in 2024. [cite:de13_badger_hoffman] Fortune described him in 2024 as "a supporter of Democrats and various progressive causes" who had clashed with Thiel and Sacks over their support for Donald Trump. [cite:de13_fortune_2024] Levchin was appointed in 2015, under the Obama administration, to the advisory board of the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, an agency many conservatives sought to abolish, and he and Rabois are both listed among the contributors to FWD.us, an immigration-reform lobbying group co-founded by Mark Zuckerberg. [cite:de13_wiki_levchin] [cite:de13_wiki_rabois]

Even the alumni who backed Trump arrived by different routes and at different times. Sacks gave $50,000 to Mitt Romney in 2012 and nearly $70,000 to Hillary Clinton's campaign in 2016; in 2022 he co-hosted a fundraiser with Rabois for the Senate candidates J.D. Vance and Blake Masters and gave more than $1 million to Senate candidates; in June 2024 he hosted a fundraiser for Trump at his home that raised about $12 million, and he spoke at the Republican convention that July. [cite:de13_wiki_sacks] [cite:de13_cbs_klaidman] Rabois said in January 2020 that he would rather vote for Trump than for Bernie Sanders, and in September 2024 hosted Vance at a fundraiser for Trump's campaign. [cite:de13_wiki_rabois] Thiel spoke for Trump at the 2016 convention and gave $1.25 million to his campaign, but in 2024 he turned down a $10 million request from the Trump campaign and said he would not give to Trump's super PAC. [cite:de9_cnn_transition] [cite:de13_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de9_nbc_aspen] Musk spent more than $290 million in support of Trump and other Republicans in 2024; his record is in [scene:de11_money_and_influence|Musk's money ledger]. [cite:de14_cnn_musk290]

Nor do the Trump-backing alumni agree with one another or with the texts this book examines. None of the people named in this section has, in any statement found, described himself as a follower of Curtis Yarvin's or Nick Land's program; Thiel, the one most often linked to Yarvin, said in 2023 that he did not think Yarvin's ideas would "work." [cite:de6_cnn_2025] Whatever links the network to the Dark Enlightenment's texts runs through specific, documented acts by specific people, which the following parts trace one by one, and not through shared membership in a group.

What the 2007 photograph does show is a real mechanism, one Fortune named at the time: a small set of people with early wealth from the same sale who fund one another's companies, hire one another, and, "when they need both" money and advice, "go to Thiel, who seems to be at the center of it all." [cite:de13_fortune_2007] That is a network of capital and trust. Its political expression, from 2022 onwards, has been partial: some of its members put money and time behind Vance, Masters and Trump, others put more money behind their opponents. The [scene:de13_where_they_sit_now|next section] records which of them took public office in 2025 and 2026, and on what terms.
