The Secretary of State

Marco Rubio was born in Miami on 28 May 1971 to Cuban immigrants. He served in the Florida House from 2000, as its speaker from 2006 to 2008, won a seat in the US Senate in 2010 and was re-elected in 2016 and 2022, sitting on the Foreign Relations and Intelligence committees. On 20 January 2025 the Senate confirmed him as Secretary of State by 99 to 0. [cite:wiki_rubio] He opposed the 2015 Iran agreement, supported the move of the embassy to Jerusalem and opposed calls for a ceasefire after 7 October 2023; he describes support for Israel as an American interest. [cite:wiki_rubio]

In the 2016 primaries Trump called him "Little Marco" and Rubio called Trump a "con artist"; he endorsed Trump later that year. [cite:yahoo_rubio_4jobs] As Secretary of State he also became acting administrator of USAID, which his department absorbed, acting archivist of the United States, and, from May 2025, acting national security adviser, the first person to hold that post alongside the State Department since Henry Kissinger. [cite:yahoo_rubio_4jobs]

The money

OpenSecrets credits Rubio with about $1 million over his career from donors and committees it classifies as pro-Israel, among the larger totals in Congress; Federal Election Commission filings show gifts from committees including Friends of Israel PAC and the Republican Jewish Coalition PAC. [cite:pp_rubio_fipac] [cite:pp_rubio_rjc] PolitiFact found that he received a comparable sum, about $1.1 million, from the oil and gas industry over 2009–2018. [cite:politifact_rubio_oil] The money is documented; that it shaped his positions is not, and his views on Israel predate most of it.

A different standard

Under an executive order of 6 February 2025 the United States sanctioned the prosecutor of the International Criminal Court, Karim Khan. Rubio extended sanctions to four judges in June 2025 and to two more judges and both deputy prosecutors in August, and on 9 July 2025 sanctioned the UN special rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territory, Francesca Albanese, for "efforts to prompt" the court to act against American and Israeli officials. [cite:verfassungsblog_icc] [cite:cnn_albanese] Albanese was removed from the list and then restored in May 2026; her family has sued on First Amendment grounds, and the International Commission of Jurists says the sanctions violate UN immunities. [cite:aj_albanese_relist] [cite:aj_albanese_suit] [cite:icj_albanese] Rubio's case is that neither the United States nor Israel is party to the court, so its actions against them are "lawfare". [cite:fdd_rubio_lawfare]

In the other direction, on 1 March 2025 Rubio signed an emergency declaration expediting about $4 billion in military assistance to Israel, including 2,000-pound bombs and armoured bulldozers, without congressional review; the Arms Control Association noted that he cited no specific emergency. [cite:khaleej_rubio_4b] [cite:aca_rubio_bypass]

The children's visas

In August 2025 an Ohio charity, HEAL Palestine, brought eleven injured children from Gaza to the United States for treatment on visitor visas. The activist Laura Loomer called them "Islamic invaders" and tagged Rubio; she told the New York Times she had spoken to him. On 16 August the State Department announced that "all visitor visas for individuals from Gaza are being stopped" pending review, and Rubio defended it: "First of all, it's not just kids."
