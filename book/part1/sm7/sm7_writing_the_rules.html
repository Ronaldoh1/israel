Writing the Rules, at Home and Abroad

The best-known slogan of the road was not Zionist in origin. "A land without a people for a people without a land" was used by Christian clergy from before 1833 to 1853, and the claim that it was a Zionist coinage is a myth by the record. [cite:ts2_wiki_landwithout] [cite:ts2_muir_meq] Its effect was the same either way: a country with a counted population was described as empty.

The law came later. The Ribicoff Amendment of 1976 and the anti-boycott law of 1977, renewed in 2018, forbid American companies to comply with a foreign government's boycott, chiefly the Arab League's, and were written with business support. [cite:ow11_carter_1977] [cite:ow11_bis_oac] The state anti-boycott laws passed from 2015 onward are a different instrument: they bind state contractors and funds and penalise Americans' own boycott choices, chiefly those of the boycott, divestment and sanctions movement, and they remain contested in court. [cite:pm_aclu_waldrip] Executive Order 13899 of 2019 placed the IHRA definition of antisemitism, with its examples about Israel, inside civil-rights enforcement; the definition's own drafter, Kenneth Stern, has warned against its use this way. [cite:ow11_eo13899] [cite:ow11_npr_stern]

The same machine abroad

The road ran through London before it ran through Washington: the Balfour letter was written there in 1917. [cite:w_balfour_decl] Today the advocacy is built country by country. In Britain about 180 members of the 2019–24 Parliament received pro-Israel funding worth more than £1 million. [cite:pm_declassified_quarter] In Canada the Centre for Israel and Jewish Affairs lobbied or sponsored trips for 58 percent of the MPs running in 2025. [cite:pm_maple_cija] In Europe ELNET has received at least $11 million from more than a hundred American foundations and charities since 2022 and says it has taken about 300 European policymakers to Israel. [cite:pm_intercept_elnet] An Israel Allies caucus network reaches 50 countries. [cite:pm_ih_iaf50]

Follow the Money tested a common explanation of this work: that it keeps arms and aid flowing and protects Israel's place at the United Nations. The first half fits the evidence in the United States and Germany, Israel's largest arms suppliers; the second fits only when restated as a contest for votes and observer seats.
