The Rulers Within

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

In the Apocryphon of John, the flawed creator Yaldabaoth looks at what he has made and declares: "I am a jealous God, and there is no other God beside me". The text pauses to note that by saying this he "indicated to the angels who attended him that there exists another God", for if there were no other, of whom would he be jealous? [cite:vt24_apjohn] He and his rulers, the archons, then shape the first human, who comes alive only because of a power taken from above, and the rest of the myth is the story of that hidden power waking up. [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch] Origen describes a diagram used by one Gnostic group, with formulas the soul was to speak to the archons at their gates as it rose after death. [cite:vt24_origen_cc6]

The Gnostic authors meant these as real beings in a real cosmos. The inner reading, which is much later, takes them as a portrait of the self. Yaldabaoth is the part of a person that believes it is the whole: the voice that says there is nothing above it, and that betrays by its anxiety that something is. The archons are the forces that shape a life from outside the person's knowing: fear, appetite, habit, the opinion of others. The power hidden in Adam is what the traditions call the spark, the part of the self that does not come from those forces. The gates the soul passes after death become states it passes through now, in this life, by recognising each ruler for what it is.

The Gospel of Thomas gives the condition for the waking in two sayings. The first ties the kingdom to self-knowledge: it "is inside of you, and it is outside of you", and it is found "When you come to know yourselves". [cite:gn_ut_thomas] And: "If you bring forth what is within you, what you bring forth will save you." [cite:gn_ut_thomas] In the inner reading these are instructions for passing the rulers. Each archon holds power only while it is mistaken for the self; seen clearly, it loses its hold.

The closing reading of The Canon looked at a modern extension of this picture: Robert Monroe's claim, in his 1985 book Far Journeys, that other beings harvest an energy humans produce, which he called "loosh". [cite:cn_wiki_monroe] In the inner reading the rulers need not be outside beings at all. They feed on the same fear and craving, but they are fed from within, and they starve when attention is withdrawn from them.

The record sets the limits. The psychological reading of the Gnostic myth is modern. Its best-known early statement is Carl Jung's Seven Sermons to the Dead, which he wrote and privately published in 1916 and attributed to the second-century teacher Basilides. [cite:vt24_wiki_septem] The ancient authors described a cosmos with rulers in it, and Irenaeus and Origen argued against them on those terms. [cite:vt24_apjohn] [cite:vt24_origen_cc6] Reading the archons as inner forces is a reader's translation, not what the texts meant to their first readers.
