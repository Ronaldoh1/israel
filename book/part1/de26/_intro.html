This part closes the survey of the toolkit with three kinds of power that do not depend on reading anyone's files. The first is sight: face recognition at the border and on an officer's phone, drones and sensor towers, and a classified constellation of spy satellites built by one company. The second is the infrastructure underneath everything else: the few firms whose clouds host the state's data, the code platforms and app stores that decide what software exists and who can get it, and the AI models now installed inside federal agencies, including the one dispute in which a vendor's limits on use were tested in court. The third is money: the reports banks file on their customers, the closing of accounts in both political directions, and the freeze-and-burn switch that federal law now requires in every regulated stablecoin. A last section asks what has actually stopped any of these tools, and closes with the book's worst-case reading tested against the part as a whole.
