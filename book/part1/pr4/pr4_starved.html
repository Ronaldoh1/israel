The Starved

By 16 October 2026 Gaza's health ministry had recorded 466 deaths from starvation and malnutrition since 2024, 157 of them children. It counted more than 158,000 cases of acute malnutrition among children under five between 2024 and August 2026, about 18 percent of them severe, and projected that 74,200 children would need treatment through April 2027. [cite:pr_memo_starv2026] An Israeli military official, in January 2026, rejected the ministry's malnutrition deaths, saying they included people with serious underlying illness. [cite:pr_toi_idf70k]

After the Ceasefire

In December 2025 the IPC found that no part of Gaza was any longer in famine, after the October ceasefire and the reopening of crossings to aid and commercial goods. It still found at least 1.6 million people, 77 percent of the population, in high acute food insecurity, and projected that more than 100,000 children and 37,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women would suffer acute malnutrition by April 2026. "Gaza's children are no longer facing deadly famine, but they remain in grave danger," said UNICEF's Lucia Elmi. [cite:pr_un_ipc_dec2025] Israel's coordinator of activities in the territories said 600 to 800 aid trucks had entered a day since late October, nearly 30,000 of them carrying food, and called the IPC's December report "distorted, biased, and unfounded." [cite:pr_fdd_ipc_dec2025] By October 2026, aid trucks were entering at about a third of the rate the ceasefire agreement set. [cite:gz_aj_year2026]
