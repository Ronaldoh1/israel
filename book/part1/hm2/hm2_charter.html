The Charter of 1988

On 18 August 1988 Hamas published its covenant. It describes the land of Palestine as an Islamic trust that cannot be given up, and rejects negotiated settlements: "There is no solution for the Palestinian question except through Jihad" (Article 13). It quotes a religious saying: "The Day of Judgement will not come about until Moslems fight the Jews" (Article 7). It blames Jews for the world's revolutions and wars, claiming they "were behind World War I, when they were able to destroy the Islamic Caliphate" (Article 22), and it cites as evidence of Zionist plans the "Protocols of the Elders of Zion" (Article 32), an antisemitic forgery first published in Russia in 1903. [cite:hm_avalon_charter] [cite:hm_ushmm_protocols]

The charter is not a document about the occupation of 1967 alone. It claims all of Palestine, and it speaks of Jews, not only of Israel or Zionism. These passages are why scholars such as Bruce Hoffman read Hamas as driven by ideology rather than by grievance. [cite:hm_atl_hoffman] [scene:hm3_question|Would Hamas exist?]

The Document of 2017

On 1 May 2017, in Doha, Hamas's political leader Khaled Mashaal presented a new "Document of General Principles and Policies." It said Hamas's conflict "is with the Zionist project, not with the Jews because of their religion"; accepted, as a "formula of national consensus," a Palestinian state on the lines of 4 June 1967, with Jerusalem as its capital and the return of refugees, without recognizing Israel; kept armed resistance as its "strategic choice"; and dropped its formal tie to the Muslim Brotherhood. The Palestinian scholar Khaled Hroub called it a significant change, while noting that the 1988 charter's antisemitic overtones had never before been fully renounced. [cite:hm_jps_hroub2017] [cite:hm_cnn_2017doc] A spokesman for Prime Minister Netanyahu answered that Hamas "is attempting to fool the world but it will not succeed." [cite:hm_cnn_2017doc]

Six years later, on 7 October 2023, Hamas led the attack described in Part Seven. Which of its two documents better describes it has been argued ever since.
