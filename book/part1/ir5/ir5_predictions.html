Thirty-Four Years of Dates

Part Eleven of the Hezbollah volume set five of Netanyahu's warnings beside the intelligence of their day. [scene:hz11_pattern|The Hezbollah Architecture: the thesis and the test] This section gives the fuller list, from 1992 to 2026, each in his own words where a text exists, with the date, the venue and what kind of claim it was. A warning that Iran "could" have a bomb if nothing changed is not the same claim as a statement that it "will" have one by a given year, and the test in the next section depends on keeping the two apart. Where only later retellings exist, or where versions differ, that is said.

1992 to 2002: Years, a Deadline and Iraq

The first dated estimate is reported from 1992, when Netanyahu was a member of the Knesset: Iran was three to five years from being able to produce a nuclear weapon, and the threat had to be "uprooted by an international front headed by the U.S." The remark is repeated in retrospectives by The Intercept, The Week and Al Jazeera; The Week traces it to a Christian Science Monitor timeline, and none of them cites a Knesset record, which this book did not find. [cite:hz11b_intercept_wolf] [cite:hz11b_week_1992] [cite:hz11b_aje_rhetoric] His 1995 book Fighting Terrorism gave a similar estimate, but its wording is disputed. Mondoweiss quotes page 121 as saying that "the best estimates at this time place Iran between three and five years away from possessing the prerequisites required"; The Week renders it as "five to seven years at most." [cite:hz11b_mw_book] [cite:hz11b_week_1992] On the first reading the claim concerned the prerequisites for a bomb, not a bomb.

On 10 July 1996, now prime minister, Netanyahu told a joint meeting of Congress that Iran was "the most dangerous" of the region's regimes and that its or Iraq's acquiring nuclear weapons "could presage catastrophic consequences." The sentence later quoted as a prediction, "the deadline for attaining this goal is getting extremely close," referred to the goal he had just set out: preventing the nuclear arming of such states, under American leadership. [cite:hz11b_jvl_1996] It set no date for an Iranian bomb. Versions in circulation that have him telling Congress Iran would have weapons "within a decade," or in "8–10 years," do not appear in the text. On 12 September 2002, out of office, he told a House committee that Saddam Hussein "is seeking and is working and is advancing toward the development of nuclear weapons" and that removing him "will have enormous positive reverberations on the region"; on Iran he said, "I don't know that Iran has nuclear weapons." [cite:hz11b_gpo_2002] That testimony was about Iraq. Two years later the Iraq Survey Group reported that Saddam had ended Iraq's nuclear programme after the 1991 Gulf War. [cite:hz11b_cnn_duelfer]

2009 to 2015: One or Two Years, a Red Line, a Bad Deal

In February 2009, weeks before he returned to office, Netanyahu told a visiting congressional delegation that Iran was "probably one or two years away," according to a US embassy cable (09TELAVIV457) later published by WikiLeaks. [cite:hz11b_intercept_wolf] [cite:hz11b_week_1992] A second cable from later that year, as The Intercept reported it, records him telling another delegation that "Iran has the capability now to make one bomb," a statement about material on hand rather than a date. [cite:hz11b_intercept_wolf] In 2012, by The Intercept's account of a Ynet report, he said in closed talks that Iran was "a few months away" from nuclear capability. [cite:hz11b_intercept_wolf] On NBC's Meet the Press on 16 September 2012 he said Iran was "six month away from being about 90 percent of having the enriched uranium for an atom bomb." [cite:ir5_npr_mtp2012]

On 27 September 2012 he made the argument at the UN General Assembly with a drawing of a bomb. Iran had completed the first stage of enrichment, he said, and was "70% of the way there"; "by next spring, at most by next summer, at current enrichment rates," it would finish medium enrichment and move to the final stage, and "from there it's only a few months, possibly a few weeks," before it had enough enriched uranium for a first bomb. "A red line should be drawn right here," he said, marking the line before the final stage. [cite:ir5_li_un2012] Ynet's report of the speech as delivered gives the conditional the same way, "by next summer, at current enrichment rates," and his argument: "Red lines don't lead to war, red lines prevent war." [cite:ir5_ynet_un2012] The UN's own summary of the speech records the second stage as complete "by next summer at the latest." [cite:hz11b_un_2012] The claim was explicitly conditional on the rate of enrichment, and its subject was a quantity of uranium, not a finished weapon.

On 3 March 2015 he addressed Congress again, at the invitation of Speaker John Boehner and against the objections of the Obama administration, to oppose the nuclear agreement then being negotiated. "It doesn't block Iran's path to the bomb; it paves Iran's path to the bomb," he said, and warned that the deal would "all but guarantee" that Iran obtained nuclear weapons. More than four dozen Democrats had said beforehand that they would not attend, and Vice President Biden was abroad; President Obama said he had read the text and found nothing new in it. [cite:ir5_wt_2015] [cite:ir5_ap_2015] This was a warning about the deal's design and its expiry dates, not a forecast with a date. Part Seven sets out the deal and its fate. [scene:ir7_deal|The deal]

2018 to 2026: An Archive, Two Wars and No Date

On 30 April 2018 Netanyahu presented in Tel Aviv what he said were about 55,000 pages and 183 discs from Iran's secret "Project Amad," taken by Israeli intelligence from a warehouse in Tehran, and said they proved Iran had lied about seeking weapons. The documents appeared to date from the early 2000s; Iran denied seeking nuclear weapons, and its foreign minister, Mohammad Javad Zarif, called Netanyahu "the boy who can't stop crying wolf." [cite:hz11b_cbs_archive] The presentation was a claim about the past, not a timeline. Accounts that place it at the UN are mistaken.

On 13 June 2025, as Israel's air force struck Iran in the operation it called Rising Lion, Netanyahu said in a recorded statement that Iran had produced "enough highly enriched uranium for nine atom bombs" and had taken "steps to weaponize" it. "If not stopped, Iran could produce a nuclear weapon in a very short time," he said. "It could be a year. It could be within a few months, less than a year." [cite:ir5_dcnf_jun2025] [cite:ir5_ani_jun2025] The day before, the IAEA's Board of Governors had found Iran in breach of its safeguards obligations for the first time in almost two decades. [cite:ir5_aj_iaea2025] On 24 June he declared victory: "For decades, I have promised you that Iran would not have nuclear weapons." [cite:ir5_pmo_jun2025]

For the second war no comparable date has been found. Meeting President Trump at Mar-a-Lago on 29 December 2025, Netanyahu raised a possible second round of strikes and reviewed Iran's nuclear status, but Israel's warnings by then centred on its missiles; Axios reported no timeline for a bomb. [cite:ir5_axios_dec2025] His statement announcing the joint US–Israeli strikes of 28 February 2026 spoke of removing "the existential threat" and said Iran "must never be allowed to arm itself with nuclear weapons," but gave no estimate of when it might. [cite:ir5_jvl_feb2026] A circulating version in which he said "months, or even weeks" before the 2026 war could not be traced to any dated source and is not used here. In June 2026 he looked back: "Had we not acted in Operation Rising Lion and then in Operation Roaring Lion, Iran would have had atomic bombs. And let me tell you something, they would have used them." [cite:ir5_ap_jun2026]
