Foreign Money

Money from abroad is where speculation about the network runs furthest ahead of the record. This section applies the book's money standard to every country alike: each flow is named with its source, date, amount and type, whether it bought equity in a company, a stake in a fund, or a passive share through an index. It separates two questions that are often merged. The first is whether foreign and sovereign money has gone into the companies founded or run by people in this network. It has, in large amounts, and the record is public. The second is whether foreign money has gone into the ideology itself: the writers, their publishers, their platforms. On the record, it has not. Israel's documented ties are examined on the same footing in [scene:de37_palantir_and_israel|Part Forty].

X, xAI and SpaceX

The largest documented foreign stakes are in Elon Musk's companies. When Musk bought Twitter in October 2022, his filing of 5 May 2022 listed equity commitments including $375 million from Qatar Holding, part of the Qatar Investment Authority, and $500 million from the cryptocurrency exchange Binance, and Prince Alwaleed bin Talal pledged to roll over 34,948,975 Twitter shares. [cite:de11_cnbc_coinvest] Kingdom Holding and the prince's office said they had rolled over shares worth $1.89 billion and described themselves jointly as the second-largest investor. [cite:de11_ab_gulf] Musk's artificial-intelligence company xAI raised $20 billion in a round announced on 8 January 2026 whose investors included the Qatar Investment Authority and MGX of Abu Dhabi, alongside Nvidia and Cisco. [cite:de11_agbi_xai] The Saudi company Humain put $3 billion into that round and said it had become a "significant minority shareholder," roughly 0.24 per cent of the combined company once SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026. [cite:de36_bl_humain] [cite:de11_reuters_spacex_xai] Kingdom Holding said on 5 February 2026 that its and Prince Alwaleed's investments in X, xAI, SpaceX and Starlink were "now $9.2bn." [cite:de36_meed_kh] [scene:de11_twitter_x_xai|Part Eleven] covers the companies themselves.

Founders Fund, Palantir and Anduril

Peter Thiel's venture firm has at least one sovereign investor on the record. In April 2023 Sanabil Investments, the venture arm of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, began publishing a list of about 50 venture and growth funds in which it had invested, and Founders Fund was among them; Sanabil gave no amount or date, and Founders Fund declined to comment. [cite:de36_gn_sanabil] An investor in a venture fund, known as a limited partner, provides capital but does not normally choose the fund's investments.

Palantir's largest documented sovereign holder is not in the Gulf. Norway's sovereign wealth fund, managed by Norges Bank Investment Management, raised its Palantir holding about twelvefold during the second half of 2025, from $427.5 million to $5.15 billion, according to its regulatory filings. The fund said its equity holdings largely follow the FTSE Global All Cap Index under a mandate from Norway's finance ministry, which means its stake grows as Palantir's value grows. [cite:de36_aicio_nbim] A passive index holding is not patronage. Anduril's June 2025 round of $2.5 billion, at a valuation of $30.5 billion, named Founders Fund, which put in $1 billion, along with General Catalyst, Andreessen Horowitz, Valor, BlackRock and Lightspeed; no sovereign fund was named. [cite:de36_cb_anduril]

The theorists: nothing documented

The second question has a short answer. No document shows foreign or sovereign money paying Curtis Yarvin, Nick Land, their publishers or their platforms. Yarvin's newsletter, Gray Mirror, is a Substack publication that describes itself as having "tens of thousands of subscribers," his books are sold by the American publisher Passage, and his software company Tlon's disclosed seed money came from two American venture firms, Founders Fund and Andreessen Horowitz. [cite:de5_gm_about] [cite:de5_passage] [cite:de5_wiki_urbit] [scene:de14_foundations_and_opacity|Part Fourteen] maps what is and is not public about the money around him. Land's publishers are commercial and academic houses in Britain, Australia and the United States, and his only documented Chinese publisher issued travel guides in 2010. [cite:de36_wiki_land] No foreign donor to Brazil's Partido Missão, the party at whose convention Yarvin spoke, is documented. [cite:de36_publica] Próspera, the Honduran charter city, lists American investors through Pronomos Capital, whose backers include Thiel and Marc Andreessen; no foreign state is among its named funders, and Próspera is itself a foreign claimant against Honduras in international arbitration. What the money means, and what it does not

The foreign stakes are real, large and lawful, and they create interests that deserve attention on their own terms. Gulf states that own part of SpaceX, which builds rockets and satellite networks for the American government, have a financial stake in that company's contracts; [scene:de21_conflicts_and_contracts|Part Twenty-One] and [scene:de26_faces_drones_satellites|Part Twenty-Eight] describe the government's reliance on it. That is a question about foreign investment in defence-relevant companies, and it applies equally to any foreign investor from any country. It is not evidence that a foreign state directs the network's politics, and no document shows that. Nor do the stakes show any foreign state funding the Dark Enlightenment as an idea. The documented pattern is the ordinary one of global capital seeking returns in successful American companies, with the ideology's writers supported, as far as the record shows, by their readers and their publishers.
