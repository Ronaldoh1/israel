The German Scientists in Egypt and the Fall of Isser Harel, 1962–1963

In the same summer that Washington was weighing wheat for Cairo, Israel's government learned that Egypt was building rockets with the help of German engineers, some of whom had worked on the V1 and V2. What followed over the next nine months, a covert campaign against the scientists, a scandal in Switzerland, a crisis with Bonn and the resignation of the head of the Mossad, Isser Harel, is known in the literature as Operation Damocles. It is an episode in which much rests on secondary accounts, and in which several of the most dramatic events are told differently by different writers. This section sets out what each source says, and which details remain without a date or a firm attribution.

The rockets, July–August 1962. In July 1962 Egypt test-fired rockets and paraded two new types through the streets of Cairo. Nasser announced four successful tests. Israel learned that he had recruited German scientists who had worked on the V1 and V2. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles] In August 1962, according to accounts compiled from the secondary literature, the Mossad obtained a document written by Wolfgang Pilz about Egypt's Factory 333; it cited 900 rockets under construction. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] The figure is the document's, as these accounts report it. Neither the document itself nor an assessment of whether the number was a plan, a boast or a production record is in the sources reached for this book.

For Israel's leaders the difficulty lay in the combination. The programme was Nasser's; the men building it were veterans of the V1 and V2; and they were German citizens, so that any action against them touched Israel's relations with West Germany, which, as the record below shows, Ben-Gurion and Peres believed the campaign would rupture. How Israel answered would depend on which of those facts it put first.

Krug, September 1962. The first event in the accounts reached here is a disappearance. In September 1962 Heinz Krug, head of a Munich company that supplied the Egyptian programme, vanished. Who killed him is disputed between the writers who have told the story. Raviv and Melman name Otto Skorzeny as the killer; Ronen Bergman attributes the killing to a Mossad squad. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles] The two versions are incompatible, and neither rests on a document quoted in the sources reached for this book. The exact day of the disappearance is not given.

The parcel bombs, November 1962. On 27 November 1962 a parcel bomb exploded in Pilz's office in Cairo, injuring his secretary. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] The Jewish Virtual Library, drawing on Bergman, adds that a second parcel killed five Egyptian workers. [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles] The second detail appears in one compilation and is not dated separately there; it is recorded here as that source's account. The accounts place the parcels within the campaign against the scientists; no Israeli document ordering them is quoted in the sources reached here.

Kleinwächter and Switzerland, February–March 1963. In February 1963 an ambush on Hans Kleinwächter failed. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles] Then came Switzerland. There, Joseph Ben-Gal and Otto Joklik were arrested for threatening the daughter of Paul Goerke. The arrests caused a public scandal. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles] The day of the ambush and the day of the arrests are not given in the sources reached here, and neither is the outcome of the case against the two men.

Harel against Ben-Gurion and Peres, March 1963. Isser Harel had headed the Shin Bet from 1948 and the Mossad from 1952. [cite:rs_cw5_enc_harel] The end of his tenure came from inside Israel's own government. In 2013 the Israel State Archives released 105 documents from the period. As reported by Israel Hayom, they show that the "fear campaign" was exposed in March 1963, and that Harel "had to resign" after clashing with Ben-Gurion and with Shimon Peres, who believed the campaign would rupture Israel's relations with West Germany. [cite:rs_cw5_israelhayom_2013] An encyclopedia entry on Harel states simply that he was "forced to resign in 1963." [cite:rs_cw5_enc_harel] The exact day of his resignation is not given in the sources reached for this book.

The mechanism of the crisis is visible in that account. Harel ran the services that carried out the campaign; Ben-Gurion, on the archives' account and on Begin's charge below, was the one who ended his tenure, and Peres stood with Ben-Gurion. On one reading of that account, the issue between them was not whether the rockets were a danger but what the campaign was costing: Ben-Gurion and Peres weighed the threat from Cairo against the relationship with Bonn. Harel, on that reading, put the first fact first; his superiors put the third.

The Knesset and Bonn, April 1963. The resignation became a political fight at home. In the Knesset debate reported by the Jewish Telegraphic Agency on 12 April 1963, Menachem Begin said that Ben-Gurion had "forced" the security chief's resignation. Ben-Gurion answered that "Our grave concern over the designs of the Egyptian dictator to destroy Israel" should not throw Israel off balance. [cite:rs_cw5_djn_19630412] The same report announced that an Israeli ministerial body would study what course to take on the German scientists working in Egypt's arms race. [cite:rs_cw5_djn_19630412] Ben-Gurion's sentence is a direct statement of his position: he did not deny the danger, and called it grave, but said it should not throw Israel off balance.

Bonn's answer was carefully limited. The Adenauer government said it had done all it could about the scientists. Carlo Schmid, Vice-President of the Bundestag, called their work unconstitutional. [cite:rs_cw5_djn_19630412] Israel formally asked Germany to end their involvement, and Germany denied that its citizens were developing non-conventional weapons in Cairo. [cite:rs_cw5_djn_19630412] [cite:rs_cw5_israelhayom_2013] On the German side, then, the government did not deny that the scientists were there, and a senior parliamentarian called their work unconstitutional, while the government denied the most serious charge and said it had done all it could. Israel's request was formal; whatever followed was not, on these sources, a German law or order.

How it ended. By the end of 1963, according to the accounts reached here, pressure and diplomacy had driven the scientists out of Egypt. Bergman writes that West Germany offered them jobs, and that "Nearly all the scientists accepted." [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles] The outcome Harel had pursued by threat was reached, on this account, by an offer of employment from Bonn. His successor, Meir Amit, later judged that Harel had overestimated the danger. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_damocles] [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles] [cite:rs_cw5_enc_harel] That judgment is a successor's verdict, given after the fact, and the sources do not record when Amit gave it.

The two readings. The episode can be read in two ways, and the evidence supports elements of each. On Harel's side, the case is that Egypt was openly testing rockets, that a document from inside the programme spoke of 900 under construction, and that the engineers were veterans of the V1 and V2; a state threatened by such weapons acted to stop the men building them, and the scientists did in the end leave. On the other side, the case drawn from Ben-Gurion and Peres, as the archives report it, is that the campaign would rupture the relationship with West Germany; Amit later added that Harel had overestimated the danger; and on Bergman's account the scientists left because Bonn offered them work. The sources reached here do not allow this book to measure the rocket programme's real capacity, and so cannot decide which side judged the danger rightly.

What the record leaves open. Much of this story rests on Bergman and on compilations that draw on him and on Raviv, Melman, Black and Morris, and on Time magazine's reporting of 1963; the operations themselves are not documented here by an Israeli order. Krug's killer is disputed. The day of Krug's disappearance, the day of the Kleinwächter ambush, the day of the Swiss arrests, the verdict in the Ben-Gal and Joklik case and the day of Harel's resignation are all missing from the sources reached. The second parcel and its five dead come from one compilation. Whether this crisis weighed on Ben-Gurion's own decision to leave office in June 1963 is a separate question: the sources reached for this book do not make that link, and it is taken up, as a question, in [scene:cw5_bengurion_resigns|the resignation].
