The 2026 War

The ceasefire did not collapse over Lebanon. It collapsed over Iran. On 28 February 2026 the United States and Israel opened a joint air campaign against Iran; Israel described its strikes as pre-emptive, and President Donald Trump announced American combat operations. Within hours Trump wrote that Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, Iran's supreme leader, was dead, and Iranian state television confirmed it early the next day. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] Khamenei was killed in those opening strikes of 28 February, which were joint American and Israeli operations; his son Mojtaba was named his successor on 8 March. [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] The wider war, its causes and its costs belong to [scene:ow7_iran|the account of American forces in that war] and to [scene:hz12_wars|Part Twelve]; this section follows the Lebanese front.

How it started

On 2 March Hezbollah launched missiles and drones at Israel, its first fire since the strikes on Iran began and its first acknowledged attack on Israel since December 2024. Its statement tied the attack to Khamenei's killing and to fifteen months of Israeli strikes in Lebanon, saying Israel's continued attacks and assassinations gave it "the right to defend ourselves and respond at the appropriate time and place." The Israeli army began striking targets across Lebanon, including Beirut's southern suburbs, and said it "will not enable the organization to constitute a threat to the State of Israel." On 28 February Lebanon's presidency had said the American ambassador assured it Israel would not escalate against Lebanon so long as there were no hostile acts from the Lebanese side. [cite:hz8_am_mar2]

The Lebanese government's response was without precedent. On the afternoon of 2 March the cabinet banned all "military and security actions" by Hezbollah and ordered the army to "immediately and firmly begin implementing" its disarmament plan north of the Litani, using "all necessary means." Salam announced the "absolute rejection, without ambiguity or possible interpretation, of any military or security action" from Lebanese territory outside state institutions. Hezbollah's two ministers objected to the text but did not resign; "We expressed our opposition to the text, and that's all," said one of them, Mohammad Haidar. [cite:hz8_olj_ban] Hezbollah thus went to war against Israel in defiance of a decision of the government it sat in, and Israel went to war against Hezbollah in a country whose government had just outlawed Hezbollah's military activity.

Israel's campaign

The detailed chronology below is drawn from the account compiled on Wikipedia from news reports; the main dates are consistent with the named-outlet reporting cited elsewhere in this section, but individual figures in it have not all been checked against a primary source. Israel ordered residents south of the Litani to move north on 4 March and struck buildings it said housed Hezbollah headquarters in Beirut. On 16 March it began what it called a "targeted ground operation" in the south; a second division followed the next day. In late March it destroyed bridges over the Litani, and Defence Minister Katz said the army would take control of southern Lebanon up to the river. Lebanon declared Iran's ambassador persona non grata on 24 March. On 6 April Israel ordered the evacuation of Beirut's southern suburbs, and on 8 April, the day a two-week ceasefire with Iran began that Israel said did not cover Lebanon, it struck more than 100 targets across the country in an attack the chronology says killed at least 357 people. [cite:hz8_wiki_lebwar] [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] By 24 March, AFP reported, Israeli strikes had killed at least 1,039 people in Lebanon and displaced more than a million. [cite:hz8_am_mar24]

Israel's stated aim throughout was to remove Hezbollah's ability to threaten northern Israel; Katz said displaced Lebanese would not return south until that threat ended. Hezbollah's stated aim was to answer the killing of Khamenei and the strikes of the truce years. The Israeli army claimed to have killed more than 150 Hezbollah fighters by 16 April, a claim Hezbollah did not answer with figures of its own. [cite:hz8_wiki_lebwar] Israeli military and civilian losses on this front were reported incident by incident; this book did not find an authoritative total.

The toll, by date

Lebanon's health ministry counts all deaths from Israeli attacks, without separating civilians from fighters, and calls its figures provisional until rubble is cleared and bodies identified. For 2 March to midnight on 16 April it reported 2,294 killed and 7,544 injured, including 100 health and rescue workers killed. [cite:hz8_olj_toll] The government's disaster-management unit raised the toll to 2,454 dead and 7,658 wounded on 21 April. [cite:hz8_aawsat_2454] By 2 May the ministry's count was 2,659 killed and 8,183 injured, with 41 dead in the previous 24 hours and more than one million people registered as displaced. [cite:hz8_aj_may2] The Wikipedia chronology records a ministry figure of more than 3,000 dead by 18 May and gives an undated total of more than 4,000. [cite:hz8_wiki_lebwar] Totals above 4,000 that are sometimes given for the period before the April truce do not match the ministry's own dated counts.

A truce with shooting

Trump announced a ten-day truce between Israel and Lebanon on 16 April, which took effect that night and was extended after Lebanese and Israeli ambassadors met in Washington. Israel kept its forces in place and on 18 April declared a "yellow line" across 55 villages whose residents it barred from returning. Qassem rejected the direct talks; Aoun said Lebanon would not sign a "humiliating agreement." [cite:hz8_wiki_lebwar] The truce did not stop the strikes. Lebanon's government counted 3,491 Israeli air strikes, 407 controlled demolitions and six operations that razed villages between 17 April and 7 June, while Hezbollah kept firing at northern Israel and Israel answered with strikes on Beirut's suburbs. Trump said that in the Middle East ceasefires involve "shooting in a more moderate manner." [cite:hz8_an_jun8]

According to the Wikipedia chronology, Israeli forces crossed the Litani in late May and took Beaufort Castle on 31 May; a further US-brokered truce took effect on 19 June after an American–Iranian memorandum that covered Lebanon; on 26 June Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced a framework that included Hezbollah's full disarmament, which Hezbollah rejected; and in mid-July Israel began handing sections of its buffer zone to the Lebanese army. [cite:hz8_wiki_lebwar] These later steps rest on that single secondary compilation and are reported here as such. The same compilation and its companion on the Iran war give different dates for the US–Iran memorandum (15 and 17 June) and record that the US–Iran ceasefire itself broke down on 8 July. [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar]

Where things stand

As of October 2026 this book could not confirm the state of the Lebanese front against a named outlet later than June: the most recent dated reporting it verified, from 8 June, described Israeli troops "deep inside southern Lebanon," more than a million people displaced, US-mediated talks under way and rejected by Hezbollah, and exchanges of fire continuing. [cite:hz8_an_jun8] The secondary chronology runs to late July. [cite:hz8_wiki_lebwar] UNIFIL's mandate, which the Security Council extended for a final time in August 2025, runs to 31 December 2026, after which the force is to withdraw within a year. [cite:hz8_scr_nov25] What the dated record does settle is that the ceasefire of November 2024 did not survive into the spring of 2026: it ended in a war that killed more people in Lebanon in six weeks than the whole truce had in fifteen months.
