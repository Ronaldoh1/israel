The Toll and How It Was Counted

The 2006 war has no single agreed death toll, and the differences come mostly from who counted and what they counted. The figures below give each source and its method. One misreading should be set aside first. Human Rights Watch's count is sometimes quoted, including in earlier versions of this library's own material, as showing "400+" civilian deaths. It does not. Human Rights Watch identified at least 1,109 Lebanese killed by Israeli attacks, "the vast majority of whom were civilians." [cite:hz5_rw_why] [cite:hz5_hrw_why9]

Lebanese dead. Human Rights Watch spent five months in Lebanon and Israel. It investigated 561 deaths in depth through site visits and separate witness interviews, and gathered information on 548 more from graveyards and hospital records, for a total of 1,109 named deaths; it said the list was incomplete because some strikes went unreported. [cite:hz5_hrw_why10] To separate civilians from fighters it checked cemeteries, where Hezbollah buried its dead as "martyrs" with distinct markings, and the movement's own "martyr" posters. [cite:hz5_hrw_whymethod] In the 94 attacks it examined most closely, 510 civilians and 51 Hezbollah fighters were killed, and at least 300 of the 510 civilians were women or children. [cite:hz5_hrw_pr] [cite:hz5_rw_why] The Lebanese government put the national toll at 1,123 and its Higher Relief Council at 1,191; about 4,400 people were wounded. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] [cite:hz5_rw_why]

Hezbollah's dead. Here the sources are far apart and each has a stake in its number. Hezbollah said 250 of its fighters were killed. Israel's Foreign Ministry put the figure at "over 600." A UN official estimated about 500, not all of them front-line fighters. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] None of these is an independent count, and the range should be read as 250 to 600.

Israeli dead. Israel lost 121 soldiers. Hezbollah's rockets and mortars killed 44 Israeli civilians; different tallies give 43, 44 or 46. At least 19 of the civilians killed were Arab citizens of Israel. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Human Rights Watch's own count was 43 Israeli civilians and 12 soldiers killed by rockets. [cite:hz5_rw_why] The total of about 165 Israeli dead follows from the official breakdown of 121 soldiers and 44 civilians; the totals of 158 and 159 that appear in some summaries do not match it.

On these figures, Lebanese deaths outnumbered Israeli deaths by roughly seven to one, and, taking Human Rights Watch's estimate of about 900 Lebanese civilian dead, civilian deaths by about twenty to one. [cite:hz5_hrw_pr] The ratio describes the outcome; why it came out that way is what the investigators argued about.

What the investigators found about Israel. Human Rights Watch concluded that the main cause of the civilian toll was Israel's targeting: Israel "often, even though not deliberately attacking civilians, did not distinguish between military objectives and civilians." It warned civilians to leave the south by leaflet, radio, phone message and loudspeaker, then acted as though everyone still there was a combatant, treating the area "effectively as a free-fire zone." Many who stayed were old, poor or unable to leave. [cite:hz5_hrw_why9] [cite:hz5_hrw_whymethod] Its overall judgement was that Israel "conducted the war with reckless indifference to the fate of Lebanese civilians," and that responsibility for the death toll "lies squarely with Israeli policies." [cite:hz5_rw_why] Israel's position was that civilian deaths were the result of Hezbollah fighting from among civilians and using them as "shields," and that it attacked only military targets. Human Rights Watch replied that this claim "rings hollow" because Israeli officials had publicly named Hezbollah's political, social and welfare institutions as targets. [cite:hz5_hrw_whymethod]

What the investigators found about Hezbollah. The same report found that Hezbollah broke the laws of war on some occasions: it fired rockets from populated areas, kept weapons in at least one apartment building in the Dahieh, and in at least one case sheltered armed fighters in a basement with civilians. But it found "a handful of instances but nothing to suggest a widespread practice" of using civilians as shields; most rockets and fighters were placed outside villages. [cite:hz5_hrw_why9] [cite:hz5_rw_why] Hezbollah's own rocket fire was a separate violation. Its rockets were unguided and most were fired at towns, and Human Rights Watch said it was "directly targeting civilians." [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Nasrallah's stated position was that Hezbollah struck Israeli cities in response to Israel's bombing of Lebanese civilians. [cite:hz5_wiki_war]

Qana, 30 July 2006. Ten years after the shelling of the UN compound there, Qana was hit again. [scene:hz4_qana|Qana, 1996] An Israeli strike brought down a house where 63 members of two extended families had taken shelter. First reports said at least 54 were dead. Human Rights Watch's later investigation confirmed 27, including 16 children, and traced the earlier figure to a misreading of the shelter's register rather than deliberate inflation. [cite:hz5_hrw_why10] Israel's explanations changed: one spokesperson said the bombs had missed a Hezbollah target about 300 metres away; another said the house had been used by Hezbollah; a senior air force officer said the building was struck on the assumption that it sheltered rocket crews and that "had we known there were that many civilians inside, we certainly would not have attacked." The army later produced no evidence of rocket launches from the house. [cite:hz5_hrw_why10] After the strike Israel announced a pause in its air attacks. [cite:hz5_wiki_war]

Cluster munitions. In the war's final days Israel fired cluster munitions across south Lebanon. By the estimate most often cited, about 4.6 million submunitions were fired into dozens of towns and villages, about 90 percent of them in the last 72 hours, after the Security Council had already voted for a ceasefire. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Human Rights Watch found the use "indiscriminate and disproportionate," and the UN's humanitarian chief, Jan Egeland, called it immoral. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Because 30 to 40 percent of the bomblets failed to explode, the war left up to one million live submunitions in fields, orchards and homes. By 20 June 2007 they had killed 24 civilians and injured 183 after the ceasefire. [cite:hz5_hrw_whymethod] The army's position was that it "mostly fired cluster munitions at military objectives in open areas"; Human Rights Watch's field research found many strikes at or near towns with no evident military target. [cite:hz5_hrw_whymethod] Israel's own Winograd Commission concluded that the army's "current manner of employing" cluster munitions "does not conform to international law." [cite:hz5_wiki_war] The issue was not new for Washington: in 1979 the US secretary of state said Israel "may have violated" its agreement with the United States by using American cluster bombs in south Lebanon.
