Where the Shield Fails

An argument that turns policy into fidelity works only where the listener accepts the premise. The record shows several places where the premise was rejected, where a speaker withdrew from it, or where it met an interest it could not override. None of these cases shows that the shield has stopped working. Together they show that it is contested from inside Christianity, from inside the American right, and by governments that weigh other interests. This section takes the cases in four groups and keeps each attributed and dated.

Christians who reject the reading

The first failure is theological, and it is old. The covenantal reading of Genesis 12:3, which holds that the promise to Abraham is fulfilled in Christ and extended to all who share Abraham's faith, is the reading of the Catholic Church and of many Protestant churches; the two readings are set out in [scene:gx_s05|One verse, two readings] and the churches' positions in [scene:ts11_churches|Part Eleven]. Within American evangelicalism there is dissent as well. In July 2007, 34 evangelical leaders signed a letter in The New York Times supporting a two-state solution. After Hagee's webcast of January 2011, Richard Mouw, president of Fuller Theological Seminary, argued that God's promise to bless Ishmael in Genesis 17:20 is one Christians have "an obligation today to honor" as well, and that Israel is done no favours if its leaders are not encouraged "to practice justice and righteousness"; Joel Hunter, a board member of the National Association of Evangelicals and the World Evangelical Alliance, said Hagee's view reflected "a limited portion of Christians who are millennial dispensationalists in their theology." [cite:gi11_cp_hagee2011] [cite:ts10a_cp_hagee2011]

The Cruz exchange of June 2025 drew the same answer from several traditions within a week. Joe Heschmeyer of Catholic Answers wrote on 25 June that the promises "apply to whoever shares the faith of Abraham, including the faithful Gentiles," citing Galatians 3, and called the reading that ties them to the modern state "disastrous theology, since it undermines the New Covenant itself." [cite:ts10_ca_heschmeyer] The Baptist editor Brian Kaylor pointed out that the verse "does not actually mention Israel." [cite:ts10_ww_kaylor] Cruz himself set a limit on the argument in the same interview: "Not everyone I represent is a Christian, so I don't use my faith as the reason we should support Israel." [cite:ts10_cp_cruz] The senator who made the most-quoted use of the verse in 2025 did not rest his public case on it.

An ambassador's maximal reading

The second failure came when the reading was stated at its widest by an official. In an interview with Tucker Carlson aired on 20 February 2026, the US ambassador to Israel, Mike Huckabee, an evangelical and a long-standing supporter of the settlement movement, was asked whether Israel had a right to the land promised in Genesis from the Nile to the Euphrates. He answered: "It would be fine if they took it all," and then, when Carlson listed the countries involved, "Not sure we'd go that far." [cite:gi11_abc_huckabee] Saudi Arabia called the remarks "extremist rhetoric"; Egypt, the Arab League, Jordan, Kuwait, Oman and the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation also condemned them. [cite:gi11_abc_huckabee] [cite:gi11a_abc_huckabee] Huckabee then called his answer "somewhat of a hyperbolic statement" and said Carlson's version "edited out my full response"; the State Department did not publicly address it, and neither government adopted the statement as policy. [cite:gi11_aje_huckabee] [cite:gi11a_aje_huckabee] Once a theological reading was spoken by an ambassador, it was measured against the interests of the states it named, and its author withdrew it within two days. The episode and the ambassador's other duties are examined in [scene:ts12_officials|Part Twelve].

A president and his exporters

The third failure is the clearest case of the shield meeting a material interest. Brazil's president Jair Bolsonaro, who spoke of his Christian devotion to the Holy Land, suggested in January 2019 that Brazil would follow the United States in moving its embassy to Jerusalem; on his visit to Israel in March 2019 Netanyahu said Bolsonaro had told him the move was a matter of "when, not if". Vice-President Hamilton Mourão called the move "a bad idea" because of exports to Arab countries, including an estimated $5 billion in halal food. What Brazil announced on 31 March 2019 was an office to promote trade and investment, and when it opened on 15 December 2019 the embassy stayed in Tel Aviv. [cite:ts7_ynet_brazil] [cite:ts10a_ynet_brazil] [cite:ts7_pti_brazil] The case is set out in full in [scene:ts7_abroad|Part Seven].

A split on the American right

The fourth failure is political and recent. Charlie Kirk, the founder of Turning Point USA, had long presented himself as a defender of Israel and an opponent of antisemitism. In a letter to Netanyahu dated 2 May 2025, published after his death by the New York Post and reported by JTA, he wrote that he was "defending Israel in public more than your own government," that Israel "is losing the information war," and that "This should be a 5 alarm fire" because support was slipping even among conservatives. He wrote "from a place of deep love for Israel and the Jewish people," and complained that he was called "a paid apologist for Israel" when he defended it and antisemitic when he did not defend it enough. [cite:ts10_jta_kirk] Kirk was shot dead while speaking at Utah Valley University in Orem on 10 September 2025; Netanyahu later appeared to cite the letter in a pre-emptive denial of any Israeli involvement in his death. [cite:ts10_jta_kirk]

At Turning Point's AmericaFest in Phoenix, held from 18 to 21 December 2025, the dispute was argued from the stage. Tucker Carlson said: "God is not on any country's side," and, on Gaza: "If a man commits a crime, do we kill his kids? I don't care if it's in Minneapolis or Gaza City. No, we don't." Al Jazeera reported that he criticised Christian ministers he said had justified killing innocent people in Gaza. Ben Shapiro answered that Carlson's hosting of Nick Fuentes was "an act of moral imbecility" and that Candace Owens had been "vomiting all sorts of hideous and conspiratorial nonsense into the public square for years"; Steve Bannon called Shapiro and his allies the "Israel First" crowd. [cite:ts10_aje_americafest] Carlson's line is a direct denial of step three of the mechanism. That it was spoken at Turning Point's own conference, and answered there, shows the premise being argued over within the movement that had carried it.

What the failures show and do not show

The cases do not show that the shield has collapsed. The Brazilian decision turned on exports, not on theology; the Huckabee episode left US policy where it was; and Carlson and Owens are commentators, not church leaders, whose positions their opponents on the right have answered in strong terms. What the cases do show is that each step of the mechanism has been refused by someone with standing to refuse it: the reading of the verse by theologians, the transfer to policy by a senator who used it, the maximal map by the official who stated it, and the claim that God takes sides by a commentator speaking to the movement's young. The surveys point the same way. In LifeWay's 2017 poll, support for Israel among Americans with evangelical beliefs was 58 per cent at ages 18 to 34 and 77 per cent at 65 and over. [cite:gi11_ct_lifeway] The later polling, and the churches and Palestinian Christians who have argued against the system from within the faith, are the subject of [scene:ts11_generations|Part Eleven].
