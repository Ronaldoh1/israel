The Toll and the World's Response

Every count of the dead in the 2026 war comes from a party with a stake in it or from monitors working without access to the sites. This section sets the counts side by side, says how each was made, and then sets out how the United Nations, the Gulf states, the great powers and the International Criminal Court responded in the war's first weeks. It covers the forty days from 28 February to the ceasefire of 8 April; later deaths are covered in the next section and, for American forces, in Part Fourteen. [scene:ir14_bill|Dragged in: the American bill]

The counts, and how they were made

In Iran, two kinds of count exist and they do not measure the same thing. Iran's government gives totals without separating civilians from soldiers: the head of the state Foundation of Martyrs, Ahmad Mousavi, put the dead at 3,468 on 18 April, after a figure of 3,375 from the Legal Medicine Organisation on 12 April. HRANA, counting incident by incident from field reports, medical sources and open sources and using the lowest figure where a report says "more than", counted at least 3,636 dead by 7 April: 1,701 civilians, including at least 254 children, 1,221 military personnel and 714 people it could not classify. AFP could not verify either. [cite:ir11_afp_tolls] [cite:ir11_hrana_day22] The two totals are close; the division between civilians and combatants is where they cannot be compared, because only one of them makes it.

The strike on civilians in Iran documented in most detail hit the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in Minab on 28 February. Iran's judiciary first put the toll at 108 and blamed Israel. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] Amnesty International later found that American Tomahawk missiles had hit the school, killing more than 150 people, 120 of them children, and that the US military "did not take all feasible precautions" to avoid civilian harm. The White House said on 10 March that the Pentagon was investigating and would publish a full report; Trump said the strike was not "on purpose." [cite:ir11_amnesty_minab] Reuters had reported on 5 March that an initial internal inquiry found American forces likely responsible, apparently because targeters used out-of-date intelligence; the head of Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, told Congress in May that the school sat on an active Iranian cruise-missile base, and Trump said on 24 June, "I don't think it was us." Iranian officials put the dead at more than 175 children and teachers. On 13 July, answering a demand from more than two dozen senators, the Pentagon said the investigation was ongoing, and the public record reviewed for this book shows no findings released as of October 2026. [cite:ir11a_almon_minab]

In Israel the deadliest Iranian strike reported came on 1 March, when an Iranian ballistic missile hit a shelter in the Ramat Lehi neighbourhood of Beit Shemesh, killing nine civilians, four of them teenagers. Amnesty International said the strike "must be investigated as a war crime." [cite:ir11_amnesty_beitshemesh] Israeli reporting put the dead from Iranian strikes at 24 over the forty days, a figure that may include four Palestinians killed in the West Bank, with more than 7,000 injured; later counts rose to between 26 and 28. These figures are compiled from Israeli outlets and are given here as a range. [cite:ir11_wiki_strikesisrael] Amnesty counted at least 23 people killed in the other Gulf states by 27 March. [cite:ir11_amnesty_beitshemesh] Iran's government said it was striking Israeli and American assets, and its ambassador to the United Nations accused the United States and Israel of possibly committing a "war crime" by attacking civilians in Iran. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] [cite:ir11a_almon_feb28]

The war's other fronts and later phases added their own dead, counted separately. In Lebanon the health ministry counted 4,387 people killed between 2 March and 11 October 2026; the Hezbollah volume sets out that front and its figures. [cite:hz8b_qna_toll] [scene:hz8_war2026|The 2026 war in Lebanon] The Pentagon's casualty database listed 21 American service members dead and 865 wounded in operations against Iran by 9 October, a count that rose through the summer and that The Washington Post and The Intercept reported as incomplete. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026] [cite:hz12_intercept_cas] No consolidated count of Iranian dead after the July collapse of the ceasefire was found in the sources reviewed for this book.

The international response

The Secretary-General, António Guterres, warned on the first day that military action "carries the risk of igniting a chain of events that no one can control." Russia called the strikes a "dangerous adventure"; the European Union called the situation "perilous"; Oman's foreign minister, the mediator, said he was "dismayed"; and the Gulf states jointly condemned Iran's "cowardly" attacks on them. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] [cite:ir11a_almon_feb28] Britain did not join the strikes. [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026]

The Security Council acted against Iran's attacks, not against the strikes that preceded them. On 11 March it adopted Resolution 2817, put forward by Bahrain for the Gulf Cooperation Council and Jordan, by 13 votes to none, with Russia and China abstaining. It demanded that Iran immediately stop attacking the Gulf states. Iran's ambassador, Amir-Saeid Iravani, called it "a deeply regrettable day" that left "a lasting stain" on the Council's record; the American ambassador, Mike Waltz, said, "This is exactly what the United Nations should be about." A Russian draft calling for an end to hostilities without assigning blame failed, with four votes in favour (Russia, China, Pakistan and Somalia), the United States and Latvia against, and nine abstentions. [cite:ir11_week_2817] [cite:ir11a_week_2817] Iran is not a party to the Rome Statute, so the International Criminal Court has no jurisdiction over acts on its territory unless Iran lodges a declaration accepting it or the Security Council refers the situation; the advocacy group DAWN urged it to do so, and the public record reviewed for this book does not show that Iran did, or any ICC action on the war. [cite:ir11_dawn_icc]
