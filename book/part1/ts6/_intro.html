Part Six follows the restorationist idea out of the prophecy conferences and into the offices of presidents and prime ministers, between 1891 and 1953. It begins with William E. Blackstone, a Chicago businessman turned evangelist, and the petition he carried to President Benjamin Harrison in March 1891, then follows the second petition he prepared at Louis Brandeis's request in 1916. It turns next to Britain: what can be shown about the Christian beliefs of Arthur Balfour and David Lloyd George, how much weight those beliefs carried beside the war aims their own cabinet recorded, and the career of Orde Wingate, a British officer whose Bible-reading Zionism shaped the Jewish night squads he led in 1938. Last, it examines the American Palestine Committee and the Christian Council on Palestine of the 1940s, which the record shows were organised from the Zionist movement's political office and staffed largely by liberal Protestant clergy rather than end-times teachers, and Harry Truman's remark of 1953, "I am Cyrus." Throughout, the part separates what each person said and did from what historians argue about their motives.
