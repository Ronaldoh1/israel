Part Fourteen established that the two systems burden families in different places. Israeli real pay was flat for about fifteen years after 2000 and its OECD average wage fell from 76 to 66 per cent of America's between 2000 and 2024, while American median household income reached a record $87,460 in 2025 with gains twice as large at the ninetieth percentile as at the median. [cite:us14_globes_taubwages] [cite:us14_oecd_avwage] [cite:us14_census_story2025] Homes grew less affordable in both: 155 average monthly salaries for the average Israeli home in 2022, up from 129 in 2010, and an American price-to-income ratio of 5.0 in 2024, up from 3.2 in the 1990s, with half of American renters cost-burdened. [cite:us14_globes_salaries2023] [cite:us14_jchs_pti] [cite:us14_nlihc_jchs2026] Israel's food and cars are among the OECD's dearest and its phones among the cheapest, the result of a 2012 competition reform; its preschool is free from age three and its college tuition set nationally, while American families carry higher household debt and, for health, premiums of $26,993 a year for a family plan.
