A Basic Law and a Coalition, 2018–2022

Platforms and party resolutions bind no one outside the party. Two later texts carried more weight: a Basic Law, part of the set of laws that serve as Israel's constitution, and the written guidelines and coalition agreements of a government. Both name the Land of Israel and Jewish settlement. Neither defines borders. That gap is itself a finding of this section. [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw] [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines]

The Nation-State Law, 19 July 2018

Early on 19 July 2018 the Knesset passed "Basic Law: Israel as the Nation-State of the Jewish People" by 62 votes to 55, with two abstentions. [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw] Its first clause states that "the land of Israel is the historical homeland of the Jewish people, in which the State of Israel was established", and that "the right to exercise national self-determination in the State of Israel is unique to the Jewish people." It names "Jerusalem, complete and united" as the capital, makes Hebrew the state language and gives Arabic "a special status". [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw] Clause 7, headed "Jewish settlement", reads: "The state views the development of Jewish settlement as a national value", and commits the state to encourage and promote its establishment and consolidation. [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw] [cite:gi5_adalah_nsl]

Prime Minister Netanyahu called the vote "a defining moment in the annals of Zionism and the history of the state of Israel." Arab members of the Knesset tore up copies of the bill and called it the "death of democracy"; one called it an "apartheid" law. [cite:gi5_voa_nsl] Its supporters argued that it wrote down the state's existing Jewish character. [cite:gi5_aje_hcj2021]

The settlement clause does not say where it applies. Clause 1 uses "the land of Israel" for the historical homeland and "the State of Israel" for the state, and clause 7 speaks of "Jewish settlement" without a territorial definition. [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw] The Arab rights group Adalah read clause 7 together with clause 1 to mean that the law covers the whole Land of Israel, including the West Bank and East Jerusalem. On that reading it gives constitutional backing to settlement there. Adalah also argued that inside the Green Line the clause could support Jewish-only towns in the Galilee and the Negev, and noted that the law contains no guarantee of equality. [cite:gi5_adalah_nsl] That is the reading of the law's leading opponent. It is not a finding of any court, and the law does not mention Judea, Samaria or the occupied territories by name. [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw]

When the law was challenged, the state took a narrower position. In Hassoun v. The Knesset (HCJ 5555/18), decided on 8 July 2021, an eleven-justice panel of the Supreme Court upheld the law by ten votes to one. [cite:gi5_aje_hcj2021] The majority, according to the summary of the judgment translated by Adalah, held that the value of Jewish settlement can be realised alongside equality, and that clause 7 is not meant to legalise discrimination or to exclude non-Jews from state land. The state's lawyers had told the court the same. The justices said equality remains a basic principle of Israeli law, and most of them said it would have been better to write it into the law. [cite:gi5_adalah_hcj] Justice George Karra, the court's only Palestinian Arab justice, dissented. He held that the clause was meant to create a constitutional norm that would in practice negate the Supreme Court's earlier Qa'adan ruling and weaken equality in the allocation of land and housing. [cite:gi5_adalah_hcj] [cite:gi5_aje_hcj2021] The ruling, as summarised, did not decide whether clause 7 reaches beyond the Green Line. [cite:gi5_adalah_hcj] The Justice Minister, Gideon Sa'ar, welcomed it as anchoring "the essence and character of Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people". [cite:gi5_aje_hcj2021]

The record therefore supports three statements and no more. Since 2018, Jewish settlement has been a "national value" in a Basic Law. The law leaves its territorial reach undefined; its leading opponent, Adalah, reads it as reaching the West Bank, and no court has decided the point. [cite:gi5_adalah_nsl] [cite:gi5_adalah_hcj] And the Supreme Court has held that the clause cannot justify discrimination in land allocation, but has not ruled on where it applies. A claim that the Nation-State Law "annexed" the West Bank, or that it made Israel "the state of a religion", goes beyond its text, which speaks of "the nation-state of the Jewish people". [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw]

The 37th government's guidelines, 28 December 2022

After the election of November 2022 Netanyahu formed a coalition that included Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism and Itamar Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit, whose leader received an expanded national security ministry. [cite:hm_jta_coalition2022] On 28 December 2022 the new government published its basic guidelines. The first sentence reads: "The Jewish people has an exclusive and inalienable right to all parts of the Land of Israel." The guidelines continue: "The government will promote and develop the settlement of all parts of the Land of Israel – in the Galilee, the Negev, the Golan Heights and Judea and Samaria." [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines] [cite:gi1_jns_guidelines] They also commit the government to "preserve the Jewish character of the state" and to respect "the practices and traditions of members of all religions in the country". [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines]

This is the guidelines' exact wording, and it differs from a common paraphrase. Some accounts have it as an "exclusive right on all the land between the Mediterranean Sea and the Jordan River". The text says "all parts of the Land of Israel" and then lists four regions. Like the 2018 law, it does not define the Land of Israel. The list places Judea and Samaria, the West Bank, beside the Galilee and the Negev inside Israel and the Golan, which Israel annexed in 1981. [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines] [cite:gi5_wiki_golanlaw]

The coalition agreements went further than the guidelines. The agreement between Likud and Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism, signed on 1 December 2022, states, as reported, that "the prime minister will lead the formulation and advancement of policies within the framework of applying sovereignty in Judea and Samaria, while choosing the timing and weighing all of the State of Israel's national and international interests." [cite:gi5_cgtn_sov] [cite:gi5_wiki_37gov] The clause commits the prime minister to a policy of sovereignty, leaves the timing to him, and names international interests as a reason to wait. The same agreement gave Religious Zionism a ministerial post inside the Defense Ministry with responsibility for the Civil Administration and the liaison offices in the West Bank. The minister was to act "with the coordination and agreement of the prime minister", and the party also took over the World Zionist Organization's Settlement Division. Smotrich said the government would "regulate and develop the settlement enterprise." [cite:gi5_ih_rzdeal] Defence Minister Yoav Gallant signed the transfer of those powers to Smotrich on 23 February 2023. [cite:gi5_wiki_37gov] What the government did with them is the subject of Part Six and of the West Bank volume. [scene:gi6_plans|Part Six: the annexation track] [scene:wb9_annex|The West Bank, Documented: since October 2023]

By the end of 2022 the land claim of the 1977 platform had moved from a party platform into a government's guidelines. It sat in the coalition agreement as a sovereignty policy whose timing was left to the prime minister, and a Basic Law named Jewish settlement a national value without saying where. These are documented facts. That the government intended formal annexation of the whole West Bank does not follow from them. The texts commit to settlement "in all parts" and to sovereignty at a chosen time; the formal steps that followed, the Knesset votes and the pressures that stopped or slowed them, are set out in Part Six.
