The Chymical Wedding

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The Chymical Wedding of Christian Rosenkreutz, printed in Strasbourg in 1616, tells of an old man invited to a royal wedding at a castle. The story is divided into seven days, which the Wikipedia summary relates to the seven stages of the alchemical Great Work, and the guests pass through "processions of tests, purifications, death, resurrection, and ascension". [cite:od_wiki_chymical] Its goal, in alchemical language, is the sacred marriage. Its author later called the whole Rosicrucian affair a jest. [cite:vt30_wiki_rosic]

The inner reading takes the wedding as the shelf has taken every such image: as a picture of a change in the one who reads it. The castle is the self; the guests who fail the tests are the parts of the self that came for the wrong reasons; the death and resurrection are the old self dissolved and the new one raised. The Hermetic book on this shelf named the same work in the alchemists' motto, dissolve and bind, and the Gnostic Gospel of Philip spoke of a bridal chamber in which two are made one. [cite:gn_pu_solve] [cite:gn_ecw_gphil]

The reading is not embarrassed by Andreae's word ludibrium. A jest can be a play in the theatrical sense, a performance meant to work on its audience, and a story that tells its readers they are invited to their own transformation does its work whether or not a brotherhood stands behind it. [cite:vt30_wiki_rosic] What the reading notices is that no institution in the story administers the change. The invitation comes directly, and the tests are passed or failed by the guest.

The record limits the reading. The Chymical Wedding is a Lutheran alchemical romance of the early seventeenth century; its author's own account of it is ambiguous, and the parallels with Gnostic and Hermetic texts are a reader's comparison, not a documented line of descent.
