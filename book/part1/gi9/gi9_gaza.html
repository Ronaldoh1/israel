Gaza: The Yellow Line, 53 Per Cent and Rising

Israel withdrew its army and removed its settlers from the Gaza Strip in 2005, a decision told in the Gaza volume and in Part Twelve. [scene:gz3_withdrawal|The withdrawal] [scene:gi12_withdrawals|The withdrawals] Even before the war of 2023 it enforced a restricted zone inside the Strip along the fence; the UN's humanitarian office, OCHA, estimated in 2010 that the restricted area covered 17 per cent of Gaza's land and 35 per cent of its farmland. [cite:gi9_aj_maps] This section follows the line Israel's army has held since the ceasefire of October 2025, the share of the Strip behind it as each source has measured it, and what Israel, the Palestinians and the UN have said it is. The war itself and the plans for Gaza's future are in the Gaza volume. [scene:gz7_war|The war] [scene:gz11_visions|The plans for Gaza]

The line in the plan

The ceasefire rested on the 20-point plan the White House released on 29 September 2025. Its third point said that "Israeli forces will withdraw to the agreed upon line to prepare for a hostage release," with "battle lines" to "remain frozen until conditions are met for the complete staged withdrawal." Its sixteenth point said: "Israel will not occupy or annex Gaza." As an international stabilisation force took control, the army would "progressively hand over the Gaza territory it occupies," "save for a security perimeter presence that will remain until Gaza is properly secure from any resurgent terror threat." The plan did not define either the line or the perimeter. [cite:gi9_pbs_plan] Netanyahu had described the aim in similar terms two months earlier: "We want to have a security perimeter. We don't want to govern it." [cite:gz_mm_netanyahu_aug25]

The line, marked on the ground by yellow-painted concrete blocks and later by earth berms, came to be called the Yellow Line. When the ceasefire took effect on 10 October 2025 it left about 53 per cent of the Strip on the Israeli side, mostly farmland and the eastern border areas. [cite:gi9_aj_maps] [cite:gi9_wiki_yellow] Netanyahu himself later put the figure at 50 per cent. [cite:gi9_olj_net70]

How the share grew

The line moved. BBC Verify found blocks in the Tuffah district of Gaza City moved about 295 metres deeper into the Strip between 27 November and 25 December 2025. [cite:gi9_wiki_yellow] The research group Forensic Architecture estimated that Israel controlled 58 per cent of Gaza by December; Al Jazeera, reading Israeli military maps in January 2026, gave roughly the same figure. [cite:gi9_aj_maps] [cite:gi9_aj_jan26] On 7 December 2025 the chief of staff, Lieutenant General Eyal Zamir, told reservists in Gaza: "We have operational control over extensive parts of the Gaza Strip, and we will remain on those defense lines. The Yellow Line is a new border line, serving as a forward defensive line for our communities." [cite:gi9_olj_zamir]

In mid-March 2026 the army sent aid agencies maps of a second boundary, an "orange line," marking a restricted area beyond the Yellow Line that covers about 11 per cent of Gaza and inside which humanitarian movement must be coordinated with the army. Reuters reported the maps in April; Jad Isaac of the Applied Research Institute–Jerusalem put Israeli control at no less than 64 per cent. The army's coordinating body, COGAT, said the boundaries are "determined and updated in accordance with the operational situational assessment" and that the zone exists to allow coordinated aid, not to affect civilians; the military called the area beside the Yellow Line "a sensitive and dangerous operational environment." At least three Palestinians working with UN agencies had been killed by Israeli fire between the lines. [cite:gi9_reu_orange]

In May Netanyahu stated both the share and the aim. At a conference in the West Bank on 28 May, in remarks broadcast by Channel 12, he said: "Right now, we have Hamas by the throat. We control 60% of the territory of the Strip," and "my directive is to go to 70." [cite:gi9_olj_net70] (Anadolu dates a first such acknowledgement to 15 May.) [cite:gi9_aa_hamas] On 29 June the UN's deputy special coordinator, Ramiz Alakbarov, told the Security Council: "Israel has said that it currently controls approximately 70% of the Gaza Strip." [cite:gi9_unsco_jun26] In July the Associated Press, from satellite imagery, reported berms more than 23 kilometres long separating the Israeli-held part of the Strip from the rest, and the army confirmed it was building the barrier. [cite:gi9_wiki_yellow] By October OCHA reported that access restrictions covered 65 per cent of Gaza, and Human Rights Watch put the area behind the Yellow Line at about 64 per cent, noting that Israeli officials called it a "new border." [cite:gi9_aj_maps] [cite:gz_hrw_anniv] On 11 October Israeli forces moved the blocks several metres west in Shujaiya, in eastern Gaza City. [cite:gi9_aj_shujayea]

The figures measure different things and should not be read as one series. The 53, 58 and 64 per cent figures estimate land behind the lines; Netanyahu's and Israel's 60 and 70 per cent are the government's own statements of control; OCHA's 65 per cent counts land where Palestinian movement is restricted. What they share is a direction. By Al Jazeera's arithmetic, about 2.1 million people were left on roughly 35 per cent of the Strip, some 128 square kilometres. [cite:gi9_aj_maps]

What each side says

Israel's stated position has two parts that sit in tension. The military describes the line as a defensive one: a "forward defensive line for our communities," in Zamir's words, and a restricted area whose limits follow the operational situation, in COGAT's. [cite:gi9_olj_zamir] [cite:gi9_reu_orange] The political leadership has tied it to Hamas: Netanyahu's "by the throat," and, in July 2026, an Israeli diplomatic source's statement that there would be "no Israeli withdrawal from the Yellow Line" before Hamas disarmed. [cite:gi9_olj_net70] [cite:hm_jp_noWithdraw] Against both stands the plan Israel accepted, which says Israel "will not occupy or annex Gaza." [cite:gi9_pbs_plan] Hamas's spokesman Hazem Qassem said in June 2026 that moving the line was "a blatant violation of the ceasefire agreement" meant to "blow up the negotiation track." [cite:gi9_aa_hamas] The UN's account, given to the Security Council, is that Israel's expanding control was reducing the space left to civilians. [cite:gi9_unsco_jun26] Armed groups opposed to Hamas operate from the Israeli side of the line, a story told in the Hezbollah volume. [cite:hz11_acled_gaza] [scene:hz11_proxies|Armed by Israel]

Three Zones: What the Record Shows

Set side by side, the three zones share a vocabulary and a sequence. Reuters noted that Israel describes the territory it holds in Gaza, Syria and Lebanon alike as buffer zones against militant attacks. [cite:gi9_reu_orange] In each, the first statement was temporary or conditional, and the later ones were not: "deployed temporarily" in Syria became "for an indefinite period"; five posts "at the moment" in Lebanon became a zone held until Hezbollah disarms; a line "frozen" pending withdrawal in Gaza became, to the chief of staff, "a new border line." [cite:hz10s_cnn_480] [cite:hz10s_ih_hermon] [cite:hz8_national_five] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] [cite:gi9_pbs_plan] [cite:gi9_olj_zamir] In each, the line has been fortified, by berms in Gaza and Sufa 53 in Syria, or emptied of residents, as in south Lebanon. [cite:gi9_wiki_yellow] [cite:hz10s_eb_sufa] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] The Carnegie Middle East Center's term for the result is "a ring of buffer zones," an order "defined by reinforced boundaries and expanded buffer zones encircling Israel." [cite:gi9_carnegie_ring]

What the record does not show, as of October 2026, is the step that would make any of the three a finding about "Greater Israel" in this volume's sense: a government decision, law or coalition clause claiming the land. No such step was found for south Lebanon, the Syrian buffer zone or Gaza, and Israel's foreign minister has denied territorial ambitions in Lebanon. [cite:gi9_nahar_saar] The case for reading the belt as expansion rests on the pattern above, on the absence of any departure date, and on statements by named officials: Zamir's "new border line," and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich's description of Gaza in September 2025 as a real-estate "bonanza," with the remark, "We have to see how we are dividing up the land in percentages." [cite:gi9_olj_zamir] [cite:gz_toi_bonanza] [scene:gz11_visions|The plans for Gaza] The case against rests on the stated security purpose, on each zone's link to a named armed enemy, and on the precedents of 1957, 2000 and 2005, when Israel left ground it had held as a buffer. [scene:gi12_withdrawals|The withdrawals] Which reading the record will bear depends on what happens after the election of 27 October 2026 and the end of UNIFIL on 31 December; both are open.
