Every system of government has to say who chooses the person at the top, who can remove that person, and what everyone else is allowed to do about it. A democratic republic answers with elections. The Dark Enlightenment's texts abolish that answer and offer another: shareholders who elect a board, a board that appoints a chief executive, and residents who may complain to customer service or leave. This part examines that substitute. The first section sets out what Moldbug, Land and Thiel actually wrote and said about choosing and removing a ruler, including what Curtis Yarvin did not answer when the question was put to him directly in January 2025. The second looks at how real systems have chosen and removed their rulers, from the East India Company and a Roman throne sold at auction to Gulf succession councils, China's party state and a modern corporate board, and at what went wrong in each. The third asks what citizens become under the design: customers rather than voters, with exit open mainly to those who hold passports and money. It closes by testing the book's worst-case reading against the material.
