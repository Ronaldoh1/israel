BDS is a boycott campaign with stated political demands; whether those demands are acceptable is the real dispute. It was launched on 9 July 2005 by about 170 Palestinian unions, refugee networks and civil-society groups, modeled on the anti-apartheid boycott, with three demands: end the occupation of Arab lands, full equality for Israel's Arab-Palestinian citizens, and a right of return for refugees under Resolution 194 [cite:bds_call]. The movement says it opposes all racism, including antisemitism.

Critics' strongest point is not the tactic but the goal: they argue a full right of return for refugees and descendants would end Israel's Jewish majority, and that "Arab lands" can be read as all of Israel [cite:wm_bds] [cite:fdd_bds]. By early 2024, 38 US states had taken measures against BDS, mostly barring state contracts with companies that boycott Israel [cite:britannica_bds]. Courts are split: four federal courts found such laws likely unconstitutional, while in February 2023 the Supreme Court declined to hear an appeal of a ruling upholding Arkansas's law [cite:pl_antiboycott].
