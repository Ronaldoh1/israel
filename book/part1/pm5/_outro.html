Part Five followed the money into the primaries. Before 2010 the record is thin: four defeats between 1982 and 2002 in which pro-Israel donors backed the challenger, without figures that can be checked. [cite:pm_wiki_aipac] [cite:pm_jweekly_mckinney] From 2022 it is exact. The super PAC spent nearly $24.3 million in its first cycle's Democratic primaries, winning most and losing in Pennsylvania and Michigan's 13th; [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2022] in 2024 it spent almost $9.9 million against Jamaal Bowman and more than $5.2 million against Cori Bush, and both lost; [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] in 2026 it helped defeat Thomas Massie alongside the president, and lost in Michigan, California and Illinois despite record sums. What is settled is the spending and the results. What is contested is the effect: whether the money decided races that had other causes, and whether, as critics argue, its main work is done in the races it never has to enter, by members who choose not to risk one. What the record leaves open is the full 2026 cycle, which was still running when this was written. Part Six turns to those members: the ones who refuse the money, and the ones who left office under attack.
