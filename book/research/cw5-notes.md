## cw5_fulbright_inquiry_1963
- **Agree:** the inquiry rested on S. Res. 362 (87th Congress) and S. Res. 26 (88th Congress). S. Res. 26 ran from 1 March 1963 to 31 January 1964 with a $48,600 cap. The hearings ran to 13 parts, of which Part 9 is the Jewish Agency.
- **Disagree:** whether the Jewish Agency was one case among many or the core finding. The catalogue shows a broad inquiry; Smith and Barda read Part 9 in opposite ways.
- **Not verified:** the committee's final report and its recommendations, and the 1966 FARA amendments that the American Council for Judaism (1997) attributes to the inquiry. The ACJ account is the only source for that link, and the FARA statute history page was blocked.

## cw5_jewish_agency_testimony_1963
- **Agree:**
  - closed sessions in June 1963, with Hammer and Boukstein (JTA)
  - Hamlin's testimony and Kenen's affidavit (Barda)
  - the Agency was already a FARA registrant (JTA; Attorney General's report)
- **Disagree:**
  - the amount of money: $5.1 million, the figure in the order and in the simulation's s11a, against $1.5 million (Barda, unsourced)
  - what the hearing showed: "no wrong doing" (Neumann; Barda) against a conduit (Smith; ACJ)
- **Not verified:**
  - $5,100,001.02, which the simulation cites to JTA; not re-fetched this pass
  - the dates of the public Part 9 sessions (s11a says "May and August 1963"; no fetched source gives the days). Barda gives 2 August 1963 for the opening of the investigation, from Kenen's affidavit.
  - ACJ's claims that the White House intervened, that witnesses' conditions were refused, and that Woods reported the Agency paid convention expenses for Johnson's entourage in 1960
- **Weight note:** Neumann was the Agency's own chairman, so his "vindicated" reading is a party's account. Barda's article argues a thesis. The ACJ article is unsigned advocacy. Smith is an advocate for registration. No load-bearing claim should rest on any one of them alone.

## cw5_doj_order_azc_1962_1963
- **Agree:** an order dated 21 November 1962. It was not enforced to completion. Enforcement stalled after November 1963.
- **Disagree:** whether the AZC ever registered (a 2016 Wikipedia snapshot says yes; every other source says no), and why enforcement stopped (it faded, per Smith and Arikat; it became moot, per Barda's argument).
- **Not verified:**
  - the order's text and signatory
  - the Rifkind, Lenvin and Wall Street Journal details (Smith only)
  - the October 1963 FBI offer (Smith only; the scene cites the same lineage)
  - the 120-day delay (Arikat, citing unnamed correspondence)
  - Volume II's statement that the Justice Department "later withdrew its demand", and its date
  - Smith's statement that the order stayed secret until 2008

## cw5_azcpa_aipac_lineage
- **Agree:** Kenen's committee existed well before 1962. The Near East Report dates from 1955–57. The AZC continued after 1963 under later names (Powerbase).
- **Disagree:**
  - the committee's founding: 1951 (Wikipedia), 1953 (Kenen in 1963) or March 1954 (Barda, from AZC records)
  - the AIPAC name: 1958 (Barda) or 1959 (Wikipedia, citing Rossinow)
  - whether AIPAC was restructured or incorporated in early 1963 (s11a, fis_s06; Smith's "split off six weeks after")
- **Writers' rule:** say which step each date marks. Do not write that AIPAC was "founded in 1963".
