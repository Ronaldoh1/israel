Syria: The Corridor, the Campaign and the Buffer Zone

Syria was never an armed group in Iran's network. It was a state ally, and for four decades it was the route by which Iranian weapons and equipment reached Hezbollah. When the civil war began in 2011, Iran spent billions of dollars to keep Bashar al-Assad in power, deployed thousands of military advisers and troops, and mobilised tens of thousands of foreign fighters, many from Afghanistan and Pakistan. [cite:hz10s_usip_iran] Hezbollah sent thousands of its own fighters, a story told in Part Six ([scene:hz6_syria|The War for Assad]). Iran's stated position throughout was that it had entered Syria "at the official invitation of the Syrian government" and would keep its advisers there as long as Damascus wished; its foreign ministry repeated this on 2 December 2024, six days before Assad fell. [cite:hz10s_rferl_advisers]

The campaign between the wars

Israel answered that presence with a long air campaign that, for years, it would neither confirm nor deny. The scale became public in January 2019, when two officials described it in the same weekend. At a cabinet meeting on 13 January, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said "the IDF has attacked hundreds of times Iranian and Hezbollah targets." [cite:hz10s_jc_netanyahu] The outgoing chief of staff, Gadi Eisenkot, told the Sunday Times that Israel had begun striking Iranian infrastructure in Syria in January 2017, that about 2,000 bombs had been dropped on Iranian targets in 2018 alone, and that "we carried out thousands of attacks without taking responsibility and without asking for credit." [cite:hz10s_jc_netanyahu] [cite:hz10s_toi_eisenkot]

The figures differ because they count different things. The Times of Israel noted that accounts of the number of raids in his interviews ranged from 200 to 400. [cite:hz10s_toi_eisenkot] "Hundreds of strikes" and "thousands of bombs" are both supported by Israeli officials' own statements; "thousands of strikes" depends on how a single attack is counted. Israel's stated aim was to stop Iran entrenching in Syria: Eisenkot said Israeli intelligence had identified an Iranian plan to build a force of about 100,000 Shia fighters there by the end of 2018. That figure is an Israeli intelligence claim, not independently confirmed. [cite:hz10s_jc_netanyahu] Iran's position was that its forces were in Syria as guests of its government. [cite:hz10s_rferl_advisers]

Israel's own Syrian partners, 2013–2018

In the same interview Eisenkot made a second admission. He said Israel had given light weapons to rebel groups along the Golan frontier, "for self-defense." It was the first official acknowledgement; until then Israel had said only that it gave humanitarian aid. [cite:hz10s_toi_eisenkot]

The humanitarian programme was real and was announced. The IDF said that more than 4,900 Syrians wounded in the war, 1,300 of them children, had been treated in Israel since 2013, and about 7,000 people at a border clinic. It named the work Operation Good Neighbor in 2016, calling it "reaching out to needy civilians on the borders of the country," and closed it in September 2018 after Assad's forces retook southern Syria. [cite:hz10s_jta_goodneighbor]

In June 2017 the Wall Street Journal, citing about half a dozen Syrian fighters, reported that Israel had for years given rebels on the Golan cash, food, fuel and medical supplies, that the money paid salaries and bought ammunition and weapons, and that Israel had set up a unit to oversee it. The main partner was named as Fursan al-Joulan, the "Knights of the Golan," whose spokesman said, "We wouldn't have survived without Israel's assistance." The IDF neither confirmed nor denied the report. [cite:hz10s_jn_wsj] In September 2018 Foreign Policy, drawing on interviews with more than two dozen commanders and fighters, reported that Israel had armed and funded at least 12 groups in southern Syria from 2013 until July 2018, supplying assault rifles, machine guns, mortars and vehicles, money to buy arms, and about $75 a month per fighter, through the aid crossings. The Israeli embassy in Washington declined to comment. [cite:hz10s_fp_rebels] When the government offensive came in summer 2018, Israel did not intervene; one Fursan al-Joulan fighter told the magazine, "This is a lesson we will not forget about Israel." [cite:hz10s_fp_rebels]

Both accounts give the same purpose: to keep Iran-backed fighters and Islamic State militants away from the Israeli frontier. [cite:hz10s_fp_rebels] [cite:hz10s_jn_wsj] That is a documented case of Israel arming non-state groups across a border, the pattern Part Eleven examines ([scene:hz11_proxies|the proxy frame]).

After Assad, December 2024 to October 2026

Assad fell on 8 December 2024. The same day Netanyahu declared that the 1974 disengagement agreement with Syria "has collapsed, the Syrian soldiers have abandoned their positions," and said he had ordered the army to seize the UN-patrolled buffer zone "and the commanding positions nearby." [cite:hz10s_newarab_collapse] Israeli forces took the Syrian side of Mount Hermon. Over the next two days, the military said, it carried out about 480 strikes across Syria, hitting most of the country's strategic weapons stockpiles, and its navy struck two naval facilities where 15 vessels were docked. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar said the aim was to keep weapons from falling "into the hands of extremists." [cite:hz10s_cnn_480] Israel told the Security Council it had "deployed temporarily in few points." [cite:hz10s_cnn_480] UNDOF, the UN force on the line, said Israel was in "violation of the 1974 disengagement agreement," and Secretary-General António Guterres said he was "deeply concerned by the recent and extensive violations of Syria's sovereignty," insisted the agreement "remains in force," and called for withdrawal. [cite:hz10s_almon_katz] [cite:hz10s_un_guterres]

"Temporary" soon changed. On 28 January 2025 Defence Minister Israel Katz said on the summit of Hermon that the army would stay there and in the security zone "for an indefinite period," adding, "We will not return to the reality of October 7." [cite:hz10s_ih_hermon] On 16 July 2025, during fighting between government forces and Druze militias in Sweida that a UK-based monitor said had killed about 260 people, Israel bombed the defence ministry in Damascus and an area near the presidential palace. Netanyahu said Israel was "acting to save our Druze brothers" and was committed to keeping southwestern Syria demilitarised. Syria's foreign ministry condemned the strikes "in the harshest terms"; its defence ministry blamed Druze militias for breaking a ceasefire. [cite:hz10s_npr_sweida]

In Paris on 6 January 2026 the two sides agreed to a US-supervised communication cell for de-escalation and intelligence sharing; the US statement spoke of "lasting security and stability arrangements," but nothing committed Israel to stop striking. [cite:hz10s_almon_paris] By late June, Katz was quoted as saying, "we will not withdraw from the security zone in Syria." [cite:hz10s_jpost_slim] Syria's foreign minister met an Israeli delegation in Jordan on 23 August 2026; Damascus said the priority was an Israeli return to the lines of 8 December 2024 and that the Golan's final status was not yet on the table. [cite:hz10s_eb_amman] On 23 September Syria's president, Ahmed al-Sharaa, said the talks had reached "almost 90%" agreement before Israel pulled back or added conditions; this is his account, and the report carried no Israeli response. [cite:hz10s_jpost_sharaa90] On 4 October Israel resumed work on Sufa 53, a fortified line of military road, five-metre berms and trenches inside the buffer zone in Quneitra, begun in 2022. Israel says it is meant to stop attacks by Iran-backed groups; Syria called it a "flagrant breach" of the 1974 agreement, and Jordan and Qatar condemned it. [cite:hz10s_eb_sufa] [cite:hz10s_aa_sufa] As of October 2026 there is no signed agreement, Israeli forces remain in the zone and on Hermon, and the US-mediated channel remains open.

Assad's fall cut Iran's land route to Hezbollah. Iran's president said on 8 December that it was for "the Syrian people" to decide their future; Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei blamed "a joint American-Zionist plot." [cite:hz10s_usip_iran] Iran's ally was gone; Israel's strikes and its forces in the south remained. Part Eleven tests whether that is a response to a threat or a standing posture.

Palestinian Islamic Jihad

Palestinian Islamic Jihad was founded in Gaza in 1981 by Fathi Shaqaqi and Abd al-Aziz Awda, former members of the Muslim Brotherhood who thought it too moderate; it rejects negotiation and seeks Israel's destruction by force. [cite:hz10s_aph_pij] The US designated it a Foreign Terrorist Organization on 8 October 1997 and says it "receives financial assistance and training primarily from Iran"; its record includes suicide bombings against Israeli civilians and rocket fire on southern Israel. [cite:hz10s_state_pij] Its relationship with Iran and its fighters' share of the dead in Gaza are covered in the Hamas volume ([scene:hm6_allies|Patrons, Not Masters]; [scene:hm11_scale|The Two Forces]).
