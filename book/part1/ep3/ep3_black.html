$170 Million from Leon Black, 2012–2017

The largest documented flow of money to Epstein came after his conviction, from one man, mostly without a contract.

Leon Black co-founded the private-equity firm Apollo Global Management. In 2021 Apollo's board commissioned a review by the law firm Dechert, which found that Black had paid Epstein $158 million between 2012 and 2017 for what were described as tax and estate-planning services. Black stepped down as chief executive. [cite:wyden_apollo_letter_2022] [cite:qz_black_testimony_2026] Investigators for the Senate Finance Committee, under its senior Democrat Ron Wyden, later found $12 million the review had missed, bringing the total to about $170 million. They reported that $100 million of it, $70 million in 2014 and $30 million in 2015, was paid without any written agreement for services, at rates about thirty times those Black paid his other elite advisers, and that the bank handling the payments filed no report on them until more than eight months after Epstein's arrest in 2019. [cite:wyden_doj_treasury_2025] [cite:wyden_house_oversight_2026] [cite:senate_finance_black_2026]

In 2023 Black paid $62.5 million to settle claims by the US Virgin Islands over how Epstein used that money to fund his operations in the territory. [cite:yahoo_black_170m] Testifying to the House Oversight Committee in 2026, Black said Epstein had misled him about the tax treatment of the fees. Wyden says Black has refused to substantiate the services with invoices or documents. Black has not been charged with any crime. [cite:qz_black_testimony_2026] [cite:wyden_house_oversight_2026] Allegations against him by individual women, and his answers to them, are recorded at their own level of evidence in his entry in Part Five.

Why it matters to the architecture

Three features of Black's payments recur throughout this book. They came after the 2008 conviction, when Epstein was a registered sex offender and anyone could read why. They were very large and largely undocumented, which is the condition under which money can buy almost anything. And the bank that moved them said nothing until after Epstein was arrested again. Each feature is a choice made by someone with the power to make it otherwise, and none of those choices was illegal on its face. That combination, discretion that is lawful at every step and unaccountable in sum, is what this book means by architecture.
