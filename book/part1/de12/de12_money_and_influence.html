Money and Political Influence

This section is a ledger. It lists the documented political money that has flowed to Vance as a candidate, the documented financial interests he holds as Vice President, and the documented acts of influence he has exercised in office, each with its date, amount or range, and source. It applies one rule throughout: timing is not causation. Where a donation precedes an outcome, the ledger records the sequence and says no more than the sequence shows; where a connection between Vance's office and a donor's interest is suggested but not documented, it says so.

Money in: the candidate

Almost all the documented money behind Vance's political rise came in one cycle, 2021–22, and most of it through one vehicle. Protect Ohio Values received $10 million from Thiel in March 2021, before Vance had declared, and $3.5 million more in the week after Trump's endorsement in April 2022, reaching about $15 million in all by April 2022 according to reporting from Federal Election Commission filings. [cite:de12_foxbiz_pov] [cite:de12_pw_pov] [cite:de15_cnbc_fundraiser] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] Ballotpedia, citing Forbes, gives $10 million, and NBC, from the filings in July 2022, "over $14 million"; the differences reflect the counting date and are recorded rather than reconciled. [cite:de12_ballotpedia_thiel] [cite:de15_nbc_mtp] At least $200,000 more came through Per Aspera Policy, a non-profit Thiel controlled; the Mercer, Uihlein and Lindner families gave to the same PAC; and David Sacks, recruited by Thiel, gave $1 million to a pro-Vance super PAC. [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de12_forbesau] In the general election Thiel gave nothing, and the Senate Leadership Fund booked $28 million in Ohio advertising. [cite:de15_cnbc_results] [cite:de12_vindy_q3]

No comparable flow is documented in 2024. Vance said in August 2024 that he was "talking to Peter and persuading him" to give to the Trump campaign; Thiel had refused Trump's own request for $10 million that April, and no gift to the campaign or its super PACs has been reported. [cite:de12_dbeast_ft] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de12_ballotpedia_thiel] Vance's own political giving as a private citizen before 2021 has not been publicly reported, and it is recorded here as unknown rather than as nothing.

Money held: the Vice President's disclosures

As Vice President, Vance files an annual public financial disclosure with the Office of Government Ethics on Form 278e, which reports assets, income and debts in statutory value ranges rather than exact sums. The ranges cannot be added into a single figure, and are given here as filed. His report covering 2024, filed in 2025, lists an interest in Narya Capital Fund I worth $500,001 to $1 million; a small interest in Narya Fund II; a share of Fund I's carried interest, the portion of profits paid to the fund's managers, at 56.4 per cent of the first $7 million; a receivable of $500,001 to $1 million; and a promissory note from Narya Capital Management valued at $1,000,001 to $5 million, which the form says had earlier been mischaracterised as equity, paying interest of $100,001 to $1 million. [cite:de12_oge_2025] It also lists an interest of $100,001 to $250,000 in the Rise of the Rest seed fund from his Revolution years, royalties from Hillbilly Elegy of $50,001 to $100,000, and a Charles Schwab line of credit of $1,000,001 to $5 million as a liability. [cite:de12_oge_2025]

His next annual report, made public on 1 July 2026 after a 45-day extension, again listed his Narya fund interests, carried interest and fee rights, now reporting the Narya note at $500,001 to $1 million with interest of $100,001 to $1 million; it showed book royalties of $1,000,001 to $5 million, holdings of $1 million to $5 million each in three exchange-traded funds that track broad stock indexes, and the same line of credit. [cite:de12_ians_2026] The two filings describe the note in different ranges; the second is the more recent and is the one in force as of October 2026.

These disclosures establish the one continuing financial link between Vance and Thiel that the public record shows. Thiel was named in January 2020 as an investor in Narya's debut fund; Vance still holds an interest in that fund, a share of its profits and a note owed to him by its management company. [cite:de12_techcrunch_narya] [cite:de12_oge_2025] Whether Thiel remains an investor in the fund is not shown in any public filing. The connection, then, is a shared financial interest at the level of a fund, documented as of 2020 on Thiel's side and as of 2026 on Vance's. It is not a transaction between the two men, and no transaction between them since January 2025 has been reported.

Influence exercised: the office

The documented acts of influence in Vance's record are those of the office. He has cast eight tie-breaking votes, among them the confirmation of the Secretary of Defense, the passage of the 2025 reconciliation bill and the defeat of resolutions on the tariff emergency and on war with Venezuela. [cite:de12_wiki_ties] He urged the rehiring of a Department of Government Efficiency staffer after racist posts, and the President agreed. [cite:de12_ap_elez] He set out a deregulatory position on artificial intelligence in Paris in February 2025, and since March 2025 he has chaired the Republican National Committee's finance operation, which makes him the party's chief fundraiser as well as a recipient of past fundraising. [cite:de12_wex_paris] [cite:de12_rnc_finance]

The deregulatory position on artificial intelligence overlaps with the commercial interests of Thiel's companies, as it overlaps with those of almost the entire American technology industry; the overlap is real, it is not specific to Thiel, and no document shows Thiel or any of his companies asking Vance to take it. One indirect exposure is documented. The news site Sludge reported in July 2025 that the Rise of the Rest seed fund, in which Vance holds an interest of up to $250,000 and a small share of the managers' profits, lists among its portfolio companies the defence firm Anduril, which had received more than $220 million in federal contract actions since January 2025; Vance holds no direct stake, and vice presidents are exempt from the rules that require many senior officials to sell such holdings. [cite:de12_sludge_rotr] [cite:de12_oge_2025] That is a fund-level exposure through Steve Case's firm, not a Thiel vehicle. Contracts awarded to Palantir and Anduril since 2025 were made by agencies under the President, not by the Vice President; they are listed in [scene:de27_money_and_war|Part Twenty-Nine] and [scene:de14_network_map|Part Fourteen], and none has been documented as involving Vance.

Appointments are a second channel of influence, and here too the record is the administration's rather than Vance's. People connected to Thiel and the PayPal network took posts in 2025, and their positions are set out in [scene:de13_where_they_sit_now|Part Thirteen]; no public document shows Vance recommending or securing any of those appointments, and none is attributed to him here.

The ledger supports three findings and no more. First, the money that launched Vance's political career came overwhelmingly from Thiel and arrived before the candidacy. Second, Vance and Thiel still share a documented financial interest at the level of a fund Vance founded, through disclosures that are public, filed in ranges and current to 2026. Third, nothing in the record connects any act of Vance's office to the interest of Thiel or any Thiel company. The sequence from donation to office is documented; the return path from office to donor is not.
