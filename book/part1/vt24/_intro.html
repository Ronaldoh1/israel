Part Twenty-Four turns from the councils to the Christians they excluded. The Sophia platform calls them the Evangelical Gnostics; the record knows them as Valentinians, Sethians, Basilideans, Carpocratians and the readers of the Gospel of Thomas, teachers of the second and third centuries who held that salvation comes from knowing one's own origin in God. The first section sets out who they were and how we know, mostly through their enemies. The second sets out what they taught, and answers three of the platform's questions directly: whether they taught the kingdom within, the ascent of the soul and alchemy. The third describes how they lived and answers the other three: whether they asked for a tithe, imposed commandments, or demanded Sunday and holy-day attendance, measured against the proto-orthodox church of the same centuries rather than the church of the Middle Ages. The fourth follows the bishops and the one pagan philosopher who wrote against them, and the emperor who turned the argument into law.
