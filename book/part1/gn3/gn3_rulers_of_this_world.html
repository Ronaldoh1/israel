The Rulers of This World: Yaldabaoth and the Archons

The most influential Gnostic myth survives in four copies, three from the Nag Hammadi jar and one from a separate codex bought in Cairo in 1896. It is called the Apocryphon, or Secret Book, of John, and it was written before about 180, since Irenaeus summarised a version of it in that decade. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] [cite:vt25_iren_129] It presents itself as a revelation given by the risen Christ to the apostle John, and it retells the opening chapters of Genesis from the point of view of the powers that, in its telling, rule the world.

At the top of everything is the unknown God, the Monad, "a monarchy with nothing above it". [cite:vt25_ecw_apjohn] From the Monad comes a first thought, a female power named Barbelo, and from her a series of divine beings, the aeons. The last of the aeons is Sophia, Wisdom. Sophia acts on her own, without the consent of the highest God or of a partner, and brings forth an imperfect being whom she names Yaltabaoth, or Yaldabaoth. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] [cite:vt24_apjohn]

Yaldabaoth does not know where he came from. Holding power taken from his mother, he creates rulers, the archons, to share his dominion, and makes the material world. Then he makes his claim: "I am a jealous God, and there is no other God beside me". The text pauses to comment that by saying this he "indicated to the angels who attended him that there exists another God", for if there were no other, of whom would he be jealous? [cite:vt24_apjohn] [cite:vt25_ecw_apjohn] Irenaeus recognised the scheme and answered it by quoting the same verses of Exodus and Isaiah back against it. [cite:vt25_iren_129] The ruler of this world, in the myth, is the God of Genesis as an ignorant and boastful lesser power, who takes credit for a creation built with stolen light.

The archons make Adam, and he is brought to life by the power stolen from above. The first human therefore carries more of the higher light than his makers do, and the rest of the story turns on their efforts to hold him: Eve is made, and the rulers send a "counterfeit spirit" to lead humanity astray. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] Salvation comes from above. Christ appears as the remembrance of the divine Forethought, Pronoia, who enters the prison and calls the sleeper to wake. Those who awaken are sealed "in the light of the water with five seals", a baptism unlike the church's. [cite:vt24_apjohn] [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn]

The same myth appears in other forms. The Hypostasis of the Archons, also from the jar, tells how the archons formed Adam, how a spiritual woman escaped them and left Eve in her place, how the serpent was an instructor rather than a deceiver, and how one of the rulers, Sabaoth, repented and was raised above the others. [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch] A related treatise, On the Origin of the World, shares much of this material and may come from Alexandria in the late third century. [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch] [cite:vt25_wiki_origin]

The rulers were not only characters in a story. They were obstacles a soul would meet. Origen, writing against the critic Celsus in about 248, describes a diagram used by a group called the Ophites, which he says he had seen with his own eyes: ten circles, and formulas for the soul to address to the archons at their gates, among them Ialdabaoth, Iao, Sabaoth and Astaphaeus. [cite:vt24_origen_cc6] Irenaeus reports that some groups anointed the dying so that they would be "incapable of being seized or seen by the principalities and powers". [cite:vt24_ah1_21] The Gospel of Mary, in a passage found outside the jar, describes the soul's ascent past hostile powers who try to hold it back. [scene:vt26_gospel_of_mary|The Gospel of Mary] In these texts, the way home passed through checkpoints, and knowing the right words was the way through.

The Apocryphon ends with an instruction that sets it apart from almost everything that later happened to it. "Cursed be everyone who will exchange these things for a gift or for food or for drink or for clothing", the risen Christ says. [cite:vt24_apjohn] The knowledge of the rulers and the way past them was not to be sold. Part Five follows what happened to the book that contained that curse after a farmer found it in 1945.
