Part Five turns from the man to the book. The Scofield Reference Bible, published by Oxford University Press in 1909 and revised in 1917, put a full dispensational commentary on the same page as the King James text, and it stayed in print through a heavily revised "New Scofield" of 1967 and later editions. This part sets out how the edition was made and sold, and corrects two figures repeated in earlier accounts: the 1917 revision did not, on any source found, refer to the Balfour Declaration, and the citable sales figure is about two million copies by 1945, not "tens of millions". It then quotes what the 1917 notes actually say about Israel, the land and the nations, and what they do not say. The last section asks who paid for the work. The documented answer is a handful of Brethren-linked businessmen and an oil executive; the widely circulated story that a Jewish Wall Street lawyer, or the Rothschilds, paid for it is traced to its sources and shown to fail.
