The Election of 2026

Israelis voted on 27 October 2026, with turnout of almost 78 percent. Official results, published on 4 November, gave Netanyahu's bloc 53 seats, a bloc led by the former army chief Gadi Eisenkot 48, and the two Arab-led parties a record 19. [cite:ny_wiki_election2026] [cite:dv5_pbs_mandate]

Likud remained the largest party, and Ben-Gvir's party grew to seven seats. But the coalition that had governed since 2022 lost its majority. On 8 November President Herzog gave Eisenkot the mandate, on the recommendation of parties holding 67 seats, including Ra'am and the Joint List; the new Knesset was sworn in on 10 November, and the deadline to form a government, with the extension, runs to about 20 December. [cite:ny_wiki_election2026] [cite:dv5_pbs_mandate] Netanyahu and his ministers remain in office as a caretaker government until a new one is sworn in. [cite:dv5_pbs_mandate]

The arithmetic is difficult for both sides. Eisenkot's bloc cannot reach 61 without the support of an Arab party or a party from the other bloc; Ra'am said it would back an Eisenkot government without a formal agreement, and the Joint List offered support from outside. United Torah Judaism recommended no one and is courted by both. Bennett has urged a unity partnership with the Likud with Eisenkot serving first. The Likud has appealed against the results in up to 120 polling stations. [cite:ny_wiki_election2026] [cite:ny_toi_vindicated] If Eisenkot fails, Netanyahu could be given a turn; if he fails too, Israel votes again. [cite:dv5_pbs_mandate]

Status as of 25 November 2026: no new government; Eisenkot holds the mandate; Netanyahu is caretaker prime minister.
