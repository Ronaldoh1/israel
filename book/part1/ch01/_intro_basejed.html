The previous part asked who decides what children learn about this history in public schools. This part asks the same question of the schools, camps and trips that American Jews built for their own children. It matters to the whole volume, because the difference between Judaism and Zionism is learned, or lost, in exactly these places.

It begins with the schools: nearly 300,000 pupils in day schools, most of them Orthodox, and a Hebrew-school system that lost almost half its pupils between 2006 and 2020. [cite:jed_fwd_census2019] [cite:jed_jw_hebrewschool2023] It follows the Israeli emissaries sent each summer to American camps, 2,050 of them in 2025, [cite:jed_jns_shlichim2025] and the published route of the free trip at eighteen. [cite:jed_br_itinerary] It ends with what former pupils say was missing and how the views of younger American Jews now differ from their elders'. [cite:jed_ji_jfna2026]

The institutions described here were built in good faith, to hold a community and its children together. Their leaders say openly that they teach attachment first and complexity later. This part sets that design beside the record, and beside the testimony of the people it taught.
