Barak, Carbyne and Valar: The Documented Business

Two of Epstein's documented business ties lead toward the subjects of this book: one to Ehud Barak, a former Israeli prime minister and defence minister, and through him to an Israeli emergency-response company that now handles 911 calls in American cities; the other to Peter Thiel, Palantir's co-founder, through Thiel's venture firm. Both are documented by records, by the participants' own statements and by named reporting. Neither, as the previous section showed, is evidence of an intelligence operation. This section sets out what each contained.

Barak and Epstein. Barak has said the two first met in 2003, and no earlier meeting has been published. [cite:ow12_wiki_barak] Reporting based on Epstein's records found that they exchanged private emails and met about 36 times between 2013 and 2017, by The Wall Street Journal's count from Epstein's calendars, [cite:ow12_ynet_wsj] well after Epstein's 2008 conviction; Barak stayed at Epstein's New York apartments several times, and he has said he flew on Epstein's private planes twice, with his wife and security guards present. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] [cite:ow12_wiki_barak] Barak has denied any wrongdoing. [cite:ow12_wiki_barak] Defending the relationship on Israel's Channel 12 in July 2019, he said Epstein "had served his sentence." [cite:ow10_toi_barak]

The Carbyne investment. In 2015 Barak formed an Israeli limited partnership, Sum (E.B.) 2015, to invest in Reporty Homeland Security, a start-up that renamed itself Carbyne in 2018. Barak's partnership invested in the company's 2015 seed round, $1.5 million by the Times of Israel's account, and he became its chairman; [cite:ow12_toi_carbyne] the partnership held all of its series A preferred stock. [cite:ow10_ctech_barak] Haaretz reported in July 2019 that "a large part of the investment money was supplied by Epstein"; Israel's public broadcaster, Kan, reported that Epstein had put $1 million into the partnership in 2016 through Southern Trust, a company registered in the British Virgin Islands. [cite:ow10_toi_barak] [cite:ow10_ctech_barak] Barak's response, on Facebook, was that "a firm linked to Epstein has been a passive investor" in the partnership and that he had told his lawyers to explore removing it. [cite:ow10_toi_barak] Peter Thiel's Founders Fund was also among Carbyne's backers, joining in 2018; the reporting does not connect Founders Fund to the Barak partnership or to Epstein's money. [cite:ow10_ctech_barak] [cite:ow10_wiki_carbyne] Barak left Carbyne's board in 2020. The company moved its headquarters to New York in 2019, came to serve emergency centres in 23 states, and on 18 February 2026 became wholly owned by the American company Axon. [cite:ow10_wiki_carbyne] Its place among Israeli-founded firms in America is set out in [scene:ow10_firms|Part Ten].

Ivory Coast. The fullest picture of the Barak–Epstein business comes from emails released by the House Oversight Committee and from Barak's own correspondence, published by a hacker group calling itself Handala, which Canada's government has linked to Iranian intelligence, and partly corroborated [cite:ow12_aj_handala] by The Sunday Times. They show Epstein helping Barak promote an Israeli state-surveillance package to Ivory Coast between 2012 and 2014, for which Barak commissioned technical plans from former Israeli intelligence officers for monitoring the country's telephone and internet traffic, and Epstein promoting Israeli surveillance technology to the government of Mongolia. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] Barak was then a private businessman. The emails document a commercial venture that drew on Israeli security expertise; they do not document direction by the Israeli government, and the part of them that came from Barak's account was obtained by hacking, which is reason to weigh it with care and to rely on what official releases corroborate.

Thiel and Valar. Epstein invested $40 million in funds managed by Valar Ventures, a firm co-founded by Peter Thiel, in 2015 and 2016. Thiel had about six scheduled meetings with Epstein at his townhouse between 2014 and 2016; there is no record of Thiel visiting Epstein's homes or flying on his jet. Valar declined Epstein's 2016 pitch to invest in Reporty. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] The record does not show any connection between this investment and Palantir's government work.

Why the business ties matter, and why they do not prove more. These records are the documented intersection between Epstein and the Israeli security world. Through Barak, Epstein's money entered a company built by Israeli founders that came to process emergency calls for American cities, and through Barak's correspondence he appears as a facilitator of Israeli surveillance sales abroad. [cite:ow10_ctech_barak] [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] Through Valar, his money sat in funds run by Palantir's co-founder. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] Each tie is with a named person or firm, by a named instrument, on a known date. None is a finding that a state used Epstein, and none supports any claim about people beyond those named. The questions that remain open, what the withheld or still-redacted federal files contain and whether Epstein had any relationship with any intelligence service, are questions about documents not yet seen, and they are listed as open in Part Fourteen.
