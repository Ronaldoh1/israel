Who Are "the Palestinians"? One People, Several Legal Situations

When this book refers to "Palestinians," the word may mean any of several groups whose rights and daily lives differ sharply. A common assumption holds that Palestinians all live in one place under one government. The record shows something quite different: a single people dispersed across several territories and several legal systems, with the law that applies to any individual determined largely by where that person was born and now lives.

The Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics estimated about 14.8 million Palestinians worldwide in 2024.[cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees] Their circumstances fall into several distinct categories.

The first group is the Palestinian Arab citizens of Israel, about a fifth of Israel's population. They vote in Israeli elections and sit in the Knesset. Their status has been shaped by the 2018 Nation-State Law and other measures, which later chapters examine in their own right.[cite:jta_nsl_upheld]

The second group lives in East Jerusalem, the part of the city Israel annexed after 1967. Roughly 360,000 Palestinians live there, most of them holding Israeli residency rather than Israeli citizenship.[cite:minorityrights_palestine] They live under Israeli rule without, for the most part, the full standing of Israeli citizens.

The third group lives in the West Bank: about 3.4 million Palestinians as of mid-2025. They are governed by the Palestinian Authority in Areas A and B and remain under Israeli military rule overall. Israeli settlers living in the same territory are subject to Israeli civil law. Two populations in one territory thus live under two different legal systems.[cite:minorityrights_palestine] [cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees]

The fourth group lives in Gaza, about 2.1 million people as of mid-2025. About two-thirds of them are refugees or the descendants of refugees.[cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees]

Beyond these four territories lies the largest single category. About 5.9 million Palestinians are registered with UNRWA, the United Nations agency for Palestinian refugees. About 40 percent of them are in Jordan, with others in Lebanon, Syria, the West Bank and Gaza.[cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees] [cite:minorityrights_palestine] Altogether, about 6.6 million Palestinians live in Arab countries.

These categories overlap with the map described in the preceding section, and they explain why statements about "the Palestinians" so often talk past one another. A claim that Palestinians vote in national elections is true of one group and false of others. A claim that Palestinians live under military rule is true of one group and not of another. A refugee in Jordan, a resident of Gaza, a West Bank villager, an East Jerusalem resident and a Palestinian Arab citizen of Israel share a national identity, but they do not share a legal status.

The differences carry weight beyond description. Much of the legal debate over Israel's conduct, including the question of whether its policies amount to apartheid, turns on these differences. That question is taken up in the next section. For now it is enough to hold the four situations, and the refugee population beyond them, clearly in view.
