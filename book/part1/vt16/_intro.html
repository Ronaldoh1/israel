Part Sixteen lays out the documents for the first half of the volume's central question, from Theodor Herzl's audiences in Rome in January 1904 to the end of the Second World War. It sets the Holy See's interests in Ottoman and Mandate Palestine, the shrines, the Latin Patriarchate and the Custody, beside each document in turn: Herzl's diary of his meetings with Cardinal Merry del Val and Pius X; Nahum Sokolow's audience with Benedict XV in May 1917, known from Sokolow's own report; the Holy See's reaction to the Balfour Declaration, Benedict XV's allocutions of 1919 and 1921 and Cardinal Gasparri's notes to the League of Nations against the draft Mandate; the Latin Patriarch Barlassina and the 1920s and 1930s; the Secretariat of State's notes of 1943, including Archbishop Cicognani's letter to the United States government of 22 June 1943; and the note Pius XII is claimed to have written in 1944 in favour of a homeland. Each document is given with its date, author, content, where it is held or published, and how strong it is, and the part ends by setting the refusal record against the evidence offered for a front.
