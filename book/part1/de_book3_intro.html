This is the third book of the Dark Enlightenment Series. It examines the Department of Government Efficiency, created by executive order on the first day of the second Trump administration and wound down on 4 July 2026. It follows the campaign money that preceded the office, what DOGE was in law, who ran it, what it reached inside federal payment, personnel, tax and benefit systems, what the courts said, what was cut and who paid, the promised dividend, the conflicts of interest and what outlasted it.

It tests one reading of the series' worst case: that cutting government was the occasion for a different aim, an executive without a veto. Each part sorts its material into what the record documents, what it makes probable and what it does not support, and says where the record runs against the hypothesis. The government's own auditors, inspectors general and courts are the main sources, beside the participants' own words.
