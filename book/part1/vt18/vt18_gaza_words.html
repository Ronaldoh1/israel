Gaza, 2023–2026: What the Popes and Their Officials Said

From October 2023 the Holy See's relationship with Israel was tested by a war in which the Catholic parish of Gaza City sheltered hundreds of people, two of its parishioners were killed in December 2023 and three more in July 2025, and two popes and the Secretary of State spoke about the war in public again and again, in terms that Israel's government protested several times. This section sets out what was said, by whom and when, with the Israeli government's responses beside it. It does not decide whether the words were right; it records them precisely, because several of them are misquoted in circulation, including in this series' own earlier book. As of October 2026, the Gaza health ministry counted 74,382 people killed since October 2023, 1,493 of them after the ceasefire of 10 October 2025. [cite:gz_aj_year2026]

Francis: calls to a parish, "terrorism", "cruelty"

The most personal act was a telephone habit. Through the war Francis kept in constant contact with Fr Gabriel Romanelli and the Holy Family parish, where about 500 people were sheltering; his last call was on 19 April 2025, Holy Saturday, two days before he died. Romanelli said the pope "told us he was praying for us, blessed us, and thanked us for our prayers". [cite:vt18_cwr_lastcall2025]

His first public words to cause a diplomatic dispute came on 22 November 2023. That morning he met relatives of Israeli hostages and, separately, Palestinians with family in Gaza; at the general audience he said: "This isn't war anymore, this is terrorism." [cite:vt18_ncr_terror2023] Palestinian participants said he had also used the word "genocide" in their meeting; the Vatican spokesman, Matteo Bruni, said he had not. [cite:vt18_jta_clarify2023] Jewish bodies objected that the remark put the two sides on one level. Noemi Di Segni, president of the Union of Italian Jewish Communities, said she would have preferred a "clear condemnation of Oct. 7"; the council of the Assembly of Italian Rabbis said the pope was "publicly accusing both sides of terrorism"; the American Jewish Committee wrote that Hamas's killing and kidnapping of civilians was terrorism and "Israel's self-defense is not". Cardinal Matteo Zuppi answered that the pope's words did "not mean putting everyone on the same level". [cite:vt18_jta_clarify2023]

On 16 December 2023 the Latin Patriarchate said an Israeli army sniper had killed two parishioners, Nahida and her daughter Samar, inside the Holy Family compound, with no warning given. At the Angelus the next day Francis said that some called such acts terrorism and war: "Yes, it is war, it is terrorism." The Israeli army said no report of a hit on the church or of civilian casualties had been raised with it, that its operational findings supported this, and that it does not target civilians. [cite:vt18_almon_dec2023]

On 17 November 2024 an excerpt was released from a book of interviews with Francis by Hernán Reyes Alcaide, Hope Never Disappoints, published on 19 November. In it Francis said that "according to some experts" what was happening in Gaza "has the characteristics of a genocide", and that it should be carefully investigated to determine whether it fits the technical definition used by jurists and international bodies. [cite:vt18_ncreg_genocide2024] He did not say it was genocide; he said the question should be investigated. Israel's ambassador to the Holy See, Yaron Sideman, replied that Israel was defending itself after 7 October and that "any attempt to call it by any other name is singling out the Jewish state". [cite:vt18_ncreg_genocide2024] (The date of the remark is November 2024, not December, as an earlier simulation scene had it.)

On 21 December 2024, speaking to members of the Holy See's government, Francis said: "Yesterday children were bombed. This is cruelty, this is not war." He added that the Latin Patriarch had not been let into Gaza as promised. An Israeli foreign ministry spokesman called the remarks "particularly disappointing" and said: "Enough with the double standards and the singling out of the Jewish state and its people"; the Israeli army said the strike had targeted militants and that the reported casualty figures did not match its information. [cite:vt18_almon_cruelty2024]

Parolin: "carnage", and what "inhuman and indefensible" referred to

Cardinal Pietro Parolin, the Secretary of State, spoke for the Holy See's diplomacy rather than for the pope personally. On 13 February 2024 he said Israel's response could not be called proportionate "with 30,000 dead" and spoke of "carnage". Israel's embassy to the Holy See called the remarks "regrettable" and said he had not considered "the relevant facts"; its Italian version used "deplorevole", and the embassy said the next day that the English word was the original. [cite:vt18_ap_parolin2024]

Parolin's interview with Vatican Media on 6 October 2025, on the eve of the second anniversary of the Hamas attack, is the statement most often misquoted. He called "the terrorist attack carried out by Hamas and other militias" against Israelis and migrants "inhuman and indefensible", and "a shameful and, I repeat, inhuman massacre". He then said the war that followed had brought "disastrous and inhuman consequences", that it was "unacceptable and unjustifiable to reduce human beings to mere 'collateral damage'", and that the states able to influence events "have so far failed to act to stop the ongoing massacre". He said those attacked have a right to defend themselves but "even legitimate defence must respect the principle of proportionality", called on Hamas to release the hostages, and said: "Antisemitism is a cancer that must be fought and eradicated." [cite:vt18_vn_parolin2025] [cite:vt18_cns_parolin2025] Book 7 of this series attributed "inhuman and indefensible" to the situation in Gaza and the interview to the Times of Israel; both are wrong. The phrase described the 7 October attack, and the interview was given to Vatican Media.

Leo XIV: the church strike and after

Leo XIV was elected on 8 May 2025. [cite:vt2_or_leo14] On 17 July 2025 an Israeli strike hit the Holy Family compound, killing Najwa Abu Daoud, 69, Foumia Issa Latif Ayyad, 84, and Saad Issa Kostandi Salameh, 60, and wounding about ten people, including Fr Romanelli. Pizzaballa said "they hit the church directly". Israel's foreign ministry expressed "deep sorrow" and said Israel "never targets churches or religious sites"; Netanyahu said "stray ammunition accidentally struck" the church and on 18 July telephoned the pope, the first public contact between them, in which Leo urged "a ceasefire and an end to the war". [cite:vt18_ncr_netcall2025] [cite:ts11_ncr_holyfamily] At the Angelus at Castel Gandolfo on 20 July Leo called for "an immediate halt to the barbarity of the war" and reminded states of "the prohibition against collective punishment, indiscriminate use of force" and "forced displacement of the population". [cite:vt18_vn_angelus2025]

Leo has been more guarded than Francis on the word genocide. In excerpts of an interview with the journalist Elise Ann Allen released on 18 September 2025 he said: "Officially, the Holy See does not believe that we can make any declaration at this time about that", noting "a very technical definition". [cite:vt18_ansa_genocide2025] His later statements turned increasingly to the West Bank: on 9 January 2026 he told diplomats that violence against Palestinian civilians there had increased, and at the Angelus of 16 August 2026 he appealed for an end to "repeated acts of violence against the Palestinian population of the West Bank" and for progress "toward a two-state solution". [cite:vt18_ncr_westbank2026] [cite:vt18_vn_westbank2026] As of October 2026 no further statement of his on genocide was found.

Pizzaballa, the Patriarch on the ground

Cardinal Pierbattista Pizzaballa, the Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem, is the Church's senior figure in the territory, not an official of the Holy See. On 16 October 2023, asked whether he would offer himself in exchange for child hostages held by Hamas, he said: "Anything, if that can lead to freedom and bring those children home, no problem." [cite:vt18_cwr_pizza2023] On 26 August 2025 the Latin and Greek Orthodox Patriarchates said their clergy and nuns would stay in the Gaza City compounds, where hundreds of civilians were sheltering, because fleeing south "would be nothing less than a death sentence"; the same statement asked for "the return of the missing people and the Israeli hostages". [cite:vt18_ncreg_patriarchs2025] On 15 August 2026 he visited Taybeh, a Christian village in the West Bank whose parish priest, Fr Bashar Fawadleh, called settler violence there a "deliberate strategy of harassment"; Israel said its army's presence was meant to protect the residents. [cite:vt18_ncr_westbank2026]
