The Brotherhood, 1928–1967

The Society of the Muslim Brothers was founded in 1928 by Hassan al-Banna, a schoolteacher in Ismailiyah, Egypt. Its aim was to free Muslim lands from foreign rule and to build an Islamic state, and its method was gradual: reform the individual first, then the family, then society. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007] Twenty years before the State of Israel existed, then, the religious movement that Hamas would later call its parent was already organized. Hamas's own 1988 charter describes it as "one of the wings of Moslem Brotherhood in Palestine". [cite:hm_avalon_charter]

Branches opened in Gaza in the 1940s, and the Brotherhood inaugurated a central office in Jerusalem on 6 May 1946. In the war of 1948 it sent several hundred volunteers from Egypt and other countries to fight in Palestine. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007] The war left Gaza a narrow strip under Egyptian military administration, its population swollen by refugees from the towns and villages to the north; Gaza, Documented sets out how that happened. [scene:gz1_refugees|The refugees of 1948] Under Gamal Abdel Nasser, who crushed the Brotherhood in Egypt in the mid-1950s and again in the mid-1960s, its members in Gaza were arrested too, and many left the strip. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007]

Yassin and the Mujama, 1967–1987

Ahmed Yassin, a paraplegic cleric in Gaza, was detained by Egypt in 1965 for Brotherhood activity. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007] After Israel occupied Gaza in 1967, he rebuilt the movement there in the early 1970s, starting with religious education. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007] Its vehicle was the Mujama al-Islami, the Islamic Centre, which built mosques, schools and clinics and, after 1979, an Islamic university. [cite:hm_wsj_spawn]

What the Brotherhood did not do in these years matters for everything that follows. Through the 1970s and most of the 1980s it largely stayed out of armed resistance. Its position was that an Islamic society had to be built before any liberation could come, and it refused to join the secular, nationalist armed groups of the PLO. When Fatah offered in 1965 to absorb the Brotherhood, the Brotherhood asked Fatah to commit itself to Islamic ideology, and Fatah refused. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007] Its rivals in Gaza were those secular nationalists, and the two camps clashed. [cite:hm_wsj_spawn]

There were signs that this would change. In 1984 Israel found a cache of weapons linked to Yassin and sentenced him to prison; he said the arms were meant for use against rival Palestinians, not Israel, and he was freed about a year later in a prisoner exchange. [cite:hm_wsj_spawn] Azzam Tamimi, a historian sympathetic to Hamas, writes that the decision to turn the movement into an armed one had been prepared for about a decade before it was taken in December 1987. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007]
