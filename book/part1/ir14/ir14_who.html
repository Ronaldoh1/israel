Who Pushed, Who Decided

Ron's thesis is that the United States was dragged into war with Iran. The phrase joins three questions that the record answers differently: who argued for war, who ordered it, and what Congress and the public did about it. The Hezbollah volume sets out the decisions of June 2025 and February 2026, the war-powers votes and the polls of 2026 in detail. [scene:hz12_wars|Two wars with Iran] This section adds the longer record of advocacy, the American voices on both sides, and an assessment of who began each American action.

Israeli advocacy, dated

Israeli leaders asked the United States to act against Iran's nuclear programme for at least eighteen years before 2026, and were often refused. In 2008 Prime Minister Ehud Olmert sought American approval to bomb Iran, and President Bush declined; by Benjamin Netanyahu's own account in his memoir, he asked President Obama in 2013 to strike Iran's nuclear sites, and Obama declined. [cite:hz12_cato_hoffman] In 2011, according to the former Mossad director Tamir Pardo, Netanyahu ordered him to begin a countdown to an Israeli strike; Netanyahu's office did not comment at the time. [cite:ir6_bg_pardo2018] At the UN in September 2012 he drew his red line on a cartoon bomb, and on 3 March 2015, invited by Speaker John Boehner without consulting the White House, he told Congress that the nuclear deal "paves Iran's path to the bomb." [cite:ir5_li_un2012] [cite:ir7_aje_invite] [cite:ir7_fox_speech] On 30 April 2018 he presented documents taken from an Iranian nuclear archive; eight days later President Trump withdrew from the deal. [cite:ir7_pmo_archive] [cite:ir7_toi_trump] Parts Five and Seven set these out. [scene:ir5_predictions|The predictions] [scene:ir7_withdrawal|The withdrawal]

Israel's own security establishment did not speak with one voice. Gadi Eisenkot, the army chief in 2018, said in 2022 that "no one spoke with the security establishment" about the American withdrawal, that it had been handled by Netanyahu, the Mossad director Yossi Cohen and the ambassador Ron Dermer, with the Mossad pressing for it, and called it "a strategic mistake"; other former chiefs said the same. [cite:ir7_jpost_eisenkot] [cite:ir7_toi_eisenkot] [cite:ir7_nl_zilber] Part Six follows these dissenters. [scene:ir6_dissent|Israel's dissenters]

The 2026 war is where the advocacy question is sharpest. Reuters reported, from three anonymous sources, that Netanyahu called Trump less than 48 hours before the strikes and argued there might never be a better chance to kill Khamenei; Trump had already approved an operation but not its timing, and Reuters could not say how much the call weighed. [cite:hz12b_reuters_call] Netanyahu, in a video message as the strikes began, said the alliance with Trump let him do what he had "yearned to do for 40 years". [cite:ir14_aje_rubio] Asked later about claims that he had dragged the United States in, he called them "fake news". [cite:hz12b_mediaite_bibi]

American officials gave two accounts. On 2 March Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters, "We knew that there was going to be an Israeli action," and "we knew that that would precipitate an attack against American forces," so the United States struck first; he also said "there absolutely was an imminent threat". [cite:ir14_aje_rubio] The next day Trump said, "if anything, I might have forced Israel's hand," explaining that he believed Iran "were going to attack first", and Under Secretary of Defense Elbridge Colby said, "The president made our decision." [cite:ir14_sn_forced] Rubio later said "this had to happen no matter what", as quoted by Aaron Goren of the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, a think tank that has long urged pressure on Iran, who argued that critics had misread Rubio, that American forces had been massing for months, and that Israel had called off strikes in June 2025 when Trump told it to. [cite:ir14_fdd_goren]

AIPAC

The American Israel Public Affairs Committee has campaigned on Iran openly. In 2015 it backed a new group, Citizens for a Nuclear Free Iran, which ran a national advertising campaign against the nuclear deal; by press reports AIPAC committed $20 million to $40 million, and the group raised nearly $30 million. A Senate resolution of disapproval failed on 10 September 2015, 58–42, short of the 60 votes needed, and J Street ran a multimillion-dollar campaign for the deal. [cite:ir7_ap_cnfi] [cite:ir7_972_clifton] [cite:ir7_npr_vote] [cite:ir7_toi_jstreet] On 28 February 2026 AIPAC welcomed the strikes, saying "Iran has American blood on its hands" and that it "will never acquire a nuclear weapon". [cite:ir14_aipac_feb2026] By early August its PAC and super PAC had spent $104.3 million in the 2026 election cycle, by Sludge's count of federal filings, including about $8 million by pro-Israel PACs against Thomas Massie, the Republican who led the House effort to end the war. [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] AIPAC says it is funded by private American donors and receives no money from Israel. [cite:pm_wiki_aipac] The spending is documented; that it caused any member's vote on Iran is a separate claim that the filings alone do not show. [scene:pm5_massie|The Massie race]

American voices for and against

The loudest American advocates were American. Senator Lindsey Graham told Axios a week before the war that those counselling restraint "seem to ignore the consequences of letting evil go unchecked". [cite:ir14_axios_graham] On 15 February 2026 the Fox News host Mark Levin told viewers, "Now is the time," and "I cannot think of any reason not to take this regime out"; after the June 2025 strikes he had said that "there were a handful of us who encouraged this night", as quoted by Media Matters. [cite:ir14_mm_levin] Vice-President JD Vance had called the June strike "a very precise, a very surgical strike tailored to an American interest". [cite:ir14_wp_maga]

The opposition crossed party lines. In June 2025 Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene wrote, "I don't want to fight or fund nuclear-armed Israel's wars", and Trump called Massie "not Maga". [cite:ir14_wp_maga] On 17 March 2026 Joe Kent, director of the National Counterterrorism Center, resigned, writing that "Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation" and that the war began "due to pressure from Israel and its powerful American lobby". [cite:ir14_fox_kent] Trump called him "very weak on security"; Senator Mitch McConnell said the letter was tainted by "virulent anti-Semitism"; J Street's Ilan Goldenberg said the claim of an Israeli plot played on "the worst antisemitic tropes" and that responsibility lay with Trump; and the Democratic senator Mark Warner said he shared Kent's opposition to the war. [cite:ir14_npr_kent] [cite:ir14_fox_kent] Tulsi Gabbard, who as Director of National Intelligence had testified in March 2025 that Iran was not building a weapon, declined in March 2026 to say whether the intelligence community saw an imminent threat. [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard] [cite:ir6_abc_gabbard2026] She resigned in May 2026, citing her husband's illness, not the war, which she had defended. [cite:ir14_aje_gabbard] Senator Tim Kaine asked, "Have we learned nothing from 25 years of war in the Middle East?", and Senator Angus King said he was disturbed that the country was going to war "based upon a decision by … a staunch ally". [cite:ir14_sn_forced]

Congress and the public

After Soleimani's killing the Senate passed Kaine's war-powers resolution 55–45 on 13 February 2020, with eight Republicans, and the House followed; Trump vetoed it as "insulting", and on 7 May an override failed, 49–44. [cite:ir14_rc_override] [cite:ir14_nbc_override] On 5 March 2026 the House rejected the Massie–Khanna resolution 212–219, with four Democrats voting no and two Republicans yes. [cite:ir14_sac_mar5] The later votes, including the resolution adopted in June that never reached the President, are in the Hezbollah volume. [scene:hz12_wars|Congress]

The public opposed both wars from the start. CNN's poll of 22–23 June 2025 found 56 per cent disapproving of the strikes and 65 per cent saying Congress should have to approve further action. [cite:ir14_cnn_poll2025] In March 2026 Quinnipiac found 53 per cent opposed to the war and Pew 59 per cent calling it the wrong decision; by June Gallup put approval at about 34 to 35 per cent. [cite:hz12_qu_mar2026] [cite:hz12b_pew_mar2026] [cite:hz12b_fox_gallup]

Dragged in? An assessment

The documents settle who decided. Every American operation in this ledger was ordered by an American president: Reagan's escort of Kuwait's tankers and his strikes of 1988, Bush's invasion of Iraq, Trump's killing of Soleimani, Biden's defence of Israel in 2024 and Trump's two wars. [cite:ir14_wiki_earnestwill] [cite:hz10_fox_pentagon2020] [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] No document shows an American president compelled to act. The record also shows presidents saying no to Israeli requests, in 2008 and 2013, and Israel standing down when Trump told it to in June 2025. [cite:hz12_cato_hoffman] [cite:ir14_fdd_goren]

The documents also show that Israeli action and advocacy shaped the two most recent decisions. In June 2025 Israel struck first and the United States joined eight days later; in 2026 the American Secretary of State tied the timing of the strikes to a planned Israeli action, and Reuters tied it in part to Netanyahu's call. [cite:hz12_cow_2025] [cite:ir14_aje_rubio] [cite:hz12b_reuters_call] Whether the wars would have happened without Israel is a counterfactual, and it is argued both ways. One side points to Rubio's words, to Netanyahu's thirty-year campaign and to Kent's letter; the other to Trump's own statements, his "very close" claim of June 2025, months of American build-up, and Iran's own record of hostility to the United States since 1979. [cite:ir14_fox_kent] [cite:ir14_sn_forced] [cite:ir14_fdd_goren] [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard] The finding this book can support is narrower than the thesis: the United States was not forced into war with Iran, but in 2025 and 2026 it went to war on a timetable set partly by Israel, against the judgement of most of its public, without Congress's authorisation, and on a threat whose imminence the Director of National Intelligence declined to confirm to Congress and the head of counterterrorism publicly denied.
