Part Seventeen established that the Holy See's refusal to recognise Israel from 1948 to 1993 was stated in public, pursued at a cost to Israel's position and grounded in reasons the Holy See named: an international Jerusalem, sought in the encyclicals of 1948 and 1949 and replaced in 1967 by a call for an internationally guaranteed statute, the refugees, to whom its one material commitment of those years went, and the legal security of Catholic communities. [cite:vt17_va_imc1948] [cite:dv1_rnc_1949] [cite:vt17_cnewa_pmp] [cite:vt17_mfa_relations] The one private record available for 1948–49 shows the Holy See declining to turn that aim into a Catholic political project, and the first study of the Pius XII files on Palestine has yet to be weighed. [cite:vt17_ewtn_irishfiles] [cite:vt17_berghahn_rioli] Its teaching on the Jewish people changed on a separate track: Nostra Aetate rejected the deicide charge in 1965, and the 1985 Notes placed the state under "the common principles of international law." [cite:ts11_va_nostra4] [cite:ts1_crrj_notes1985] Recognition came after Madrid and after the Israel–PLO recognition of September 1993, in a treaty that secured the Church's institutions and, in Article 11, kept the Holy See "a stranger to all merely temporal conflicts" over "disputed territories and unsettled borders."
