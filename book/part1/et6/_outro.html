The British Museum asked in October 1867 for an archaeologist to go with the Maqdala expedition, and its curator, with a £1,000 budget, bought from the loot that left the fortress on fifteen elephants and two hundred mules. [cite:et_cup_maqdala_blog] [cite:et_cup_maqdala] The auction raised £5,000, about £4 a soldier; by 1877 the Museum held 350 Ethiopian manuscripts, of at least 500 taken. [cite:et_cup_maqdala] Gladstone regretted the removal in 1871, but the objects stayed: eleven tabots remain in the British Museum, out of public view, and some objects have returned only by private purchase.
