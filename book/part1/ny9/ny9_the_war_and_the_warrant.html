The War, the Warrant and the Questions, 2023–2026

The war that followed the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023 is told in Gaza, Documented. Here it matters for what it did to Netanyahu's standing in law and at home.

The warrant

On 21 November 2024 the Pre-Trial Chamber of the International Criminal Court issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant, finding reasonable grounds to believe they bore responsibility for the war crime of starvation as a method of warfare and for crimes against humanity, including murder and persecution. [cite:gz_almon_icc] [cite:icc_warrant_issued_nov2024] Israel rejected the court's jurisdiction and appealed; the appeal was rejected. [cite:gz_toi_icc2025] The United States, which is not a member, imposed sanctions on the court's officials, and Netanyahu has travelled to Washington and to the United Nations in New York without arrest. [cite:bgv_icc_us_sanctions] [cite:nhh_mem_unga]

The inquiry that was not set up

Three years after 7 October, no commission of inquiry had been established. Netanyahu said that a commission appointed by the government alone "would have no public trust" and proposed one whose members would be named half by the coalition and half by the opposition, rather than the independent state commission his critics demanded; former army chief Herzi Halevi, former military intelligence chief Aharon Haliva and former Shin Bet director Ronen Bar supported a state commission. A bill for the government's model passed a first reading in July 2026 with 59 votes, the opposition boycotting, and lapsed when the Knesset dissolved. [cite:ny_ynet_inquiry]

The aides

Two investigations reached the prime minister's office. In the first, prosecutors announced in May 2026 that his close adviser Jonatan Urich would be indicted for transmitting classified information with intent to harm state security, over a classified document leaked to the German tabloid Bild in 2024; a former spokesman, Eli Feldstein, had already been charged. [cite:ny_arabnews_urich] In the second, known as Qatargate, aides are suspected of working for Qatar while employed in the prime minister's office; according to police transcripts reported in October 2026, Urich admitted receiving "hundreds of thousands of shekels" from Qatar and continuing the work after 7 October. Netanyahu testified to the police and has not been named a suspect. [cite:ny_toi_urich_qatar]

The film

In September 2026 NAZA, a documentary on the war's civilian toll by Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, screened at the Venice Film Festival. On 16 September Netanyahu said his government would introduce a bill allowing citizenship to be revoked from people who "defame" Israeli soldiers abroad, and another raising damages for defamation; the culture minister had already threatened to strip the two directors of their citizenship, protesters gathered outside Szor's parents' home, and the Committee to Protect Journalists called it "a grave threat to press freedom". [cite:ny_cpj_naza] The bills had not become law.
