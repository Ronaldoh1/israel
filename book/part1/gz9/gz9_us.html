Washington, and the Mediators

At the Security Council. Between October 2023 and November 2024 the UN Security Council voted fourteen times on drafts about Gaza. Four resolutions passed, the last of them, in June 2024, an American draft. The United States vetoed six others, on 18 and 25 October and 8 December 2023, 20 February, 18 April (on Palestinian membership of the UN) and 20 November 2024; Russia and China vetoed two American drafts. [cite:gz_aj_unsc] The United States again cast the only vote against ceasefire drafts on 4 June 2025, when its acting ambassador, Dorothy Shea, said the text did not condemn Hamas or call on it to disarm and "draws false equivalence" between Israel and Hamas, [cite:gz_axios_veto2025] and in September 2025, which JURIST counted as its sixth veto of a Gaza ceasefire draft; its deputy envoy, Morgan Ortagus, said the text did not condemn Hamas or recognize Israel's right to defend itself, and "seeks to return to a failed system." [cite:gz_jurist_veto2025]

The weapons. The United States spent $21.7 billion on military aid to Israel in the first two years of the war, as Part Eight records. [cite:gz_cow_aid] [scene:gz8_bill|The bill]

The "Riviera." On 4 February 2025, with Netanyahu beside him, President Donald Trump said that "the U.S. will take over the Gaza Strip," that "we'll own it," and that it could become "the Riviera of the Middle East," and he proposed that its people be moved to other countries. Hamas called the remarks irresponsible; the Palestinian president, Mahmoud Abbas, rejected them; and Saudi Arabia restated its rejection of any displacement of Palestinians. [cite:gz_whyy_riviera]

The plan. At the end of September 2025 the White House published a twenty-point plan that became the basis of the October ceasefire, mediated by the United States with Qatar, Egypt and Turkey. [cite:gz_wiki_timeline2025] It placed Gaza under a "Board of Peace" chaired by Trump and including the former British prime minister Tony Blair, with a temporary, apolitical Palestinian committee to run services. Hamas's tunnels and weapons were to be destroyed under independent monitoring, and members who gave up their weapons pardoned. Israel's troops were to withdraw in stages as an International Stabilisation Force took over. "No resident will be forced to leave," it said, and once the Palestinian Authority had reformed, "conditions may finally exist for Palestinian self-determination and statehood." Netanyahu endorsed the plan. [cite:gz_gn_20points] On 3 October Hamas announced its "approval of releasing all occupation prisoners — both living and remains" and its readiness "to hand over the administration of the Gaza Strip to a Palestinian body of independents (technocrats)," while seeking to negotiate the details; it did not say whether it would disarm. [cite:gz_almon_hamas_plan]

A year later, as Part Seven records, the first stage's exchange of hostages and prisoners had been completed, though aid entered at about a third of the agreed rate, and the rest had not: no stabilisation force had deployed, and Israel held nearly two-thirds of the strip.
