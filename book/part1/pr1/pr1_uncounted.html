The Uncounted

Under the rubble. In April 2026, six months into the ceasefire, about 10,000 people were missing in Gaza, believed buried under collapsed buildings. Civil defence crews had recovered 759 bodies; heavy machinery had not been let in, and the UN estimated more than 61 million tonnes of rubble. [cite:pr_aj_rubble2026] The missing are not in the ministry's count of the dead. [cite:pr_toi_idf70k]

The surveys. Two independent studies, published in The Lancet's journals, found the ministry's count too low, not too high. The first, using a statistical method that compares overlapping lists, estimated 64,260 deaths from traumatic injury by the end of June 2024, against the ministry's 37,877 at the time, an undercount of 41 percent. [cite:gz_aj_lancet] The second, led by Spagat and published in February 2026, was the first independent household survey of mortality in Gaza: interviewers visited 2,000 households from 30 December 2024. It estimated about 75,200 violent deaths in the war's first fifteen months, when the ministry had counted about 49,000, and about 16,300 more deaths from disease, untreated illness and other indirect causes. By January 2025, it found, three to four percent of Gaza's people had been killed by violence. [cite:pr_almon_survey2026] [cite:pr_ctv_survey2026]

The survey's authors said their findings contradict claims that the ministry inflates its figures; if anything, they wrote, its numbers are conservative. [cite:pr_almon_survey2026]

Civilians and Fighters

The ministry's count does not say how many of the dead were fighters, and here the record holds two readings. In August 2025 the Guardian, with the Israeli outlets +972 Magazine and Local Call, reported that a classified Israeli military database listed 8,900 named Hamas and Islamic Jihad fighters as dead or probably dead by May 2025, when the ministry counted at least 53,000 dead, implying that about 83 percent of the dead were civilians. The army said the figures were "incorrect." [cite:pr_aj_83pct] Critics of the report answered that the database lists only fighters the army has confirmed by name, that unidentified fighters were counted as civilians, and that the army's own estimate of 22,000 combatants killed, set against the ministry's total, gives about two civilians for each fighter. [cite:pr_alg_83critique]

Both readings put civilians at a majority of the dead. The ministry's count, the army's estimate and the household survey differ in size, but none of them is small. The rest of this book follows the people inside these numbers.
