Counterweights: What Has Actually Stopped the Tools

The toolkit parts describe what exists. This section takes a narrower subject: what has actually stopped, narrowed or turned back one of these tools. Six kinds appear: encryption, courts, state laws, companies and their employees, journalists, and juries; each has a documented win and a documented limit. A counterweight counts here only where a document shows it changed what a tool did.

Encryption

End-to-end encryption means a message is scrambled on the sender's phone and unscrambled only on the recipient's, so the company carrying it holds no readable copy. The practical effect shows in what such a company can hand over. When a federal grand jury in the Central District of California subpoenaed Signal, the encrypted messaging service, for a user's identity, contacts, activity and correspondence, Signal produced two timestamps: when the account was created and when it last connected. "Signal still knows nothing about you," the company said. [cite:de26_sw_signal] Governments have pushed back on the design itself. Britain's government ordered Apple, under its Investigatory Powers Act, to provide access to its encrypted iCloud backups; Apple stopped offering its Advanced Data Protection to new users in the United Kingdom rather than build a back door, and on 19 August 2025 the US Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, said Britain had agreed to drop the demand. [cite:de26_ai_appleuk] Encryption's limit is the device. Spyware reads messages after they are unscrambled, and a seized phone can be copied, as [scene:de25_spyware_and_phones|Part Twenty-Six] sets out.

Courts

Two Supreme Court rulings have carried the Fourth Amendment's warrant requirement into location data. In Carpenter v. United States (22 June 2018), decided 5–4 in an opinion by Chief Justice Roberts, the Court held that police generally need a warrant to obtain historical records of a phone's location from a carrier: "We decline to grant the state unrestricted access to a wireless carrier's database of physical location information." [cite:de26_jurist_carpenter] In Chatrie v. United States (29 June 2026), decided six to three, Justice Kagan, writing for five, held that obtaining a person's Google Location History is a search, rejecting the argument that sharing data with a company forfeits privacy in it; Justice Gorsuch concurred in the judgment and Justices Alito, Thomas and Barrett dissented. The Court did not declare such "geofence" warrants unconstitutional and sent the case back to decide whether the warrant was valid. [cite:de26_crs_chatrie] Both rulings govern data the government compels from a company. Neither reaches data the government buys, the gap examined in [scene:de28_brokers_and_doctrine|Part Thirty].

Lower courts have checked particular uses. A federal judge in Chicago found in April 2026 that the Justice and Homeland Security departments had likely coerced Apple and Facebook into removing tools that tracked immigration agents, and enjoined further pressure ([scene:de26_code_and_cloud|the second section of this part]). [cite:de26_reason_rosado] A federal court in San Francisco set aside one of two designations excluding an AI company that had refused domestic-surveillance uses, while an appeals court upheld the other ([scene:de26_ai_layer|the third section]). [cite:de26_taft_dccir] Rulings on government datasets shared with ICE are in [scene:de30_silos_and_subpoenas|Part Thirty-Two].

State laws

Where Congress has not legislated, a few states have. Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act of 2008 forbids a private entity to collect a person's face or fingerprint template without a written release, and lets the person sue for $1,000 per negligent violation and $5,000 per intentional or reckless one. [cite:de26_justia_bipa] After the Illinois Supreme Court's 2019 Rosenbach ruling set off a wave of class actions, Facebook settled a class action over its photo-tagging feature for $650 million. [cite:de26_br_bipa] Under a consent order of 11 May 2022 in a suit brought by the ACLU, Clearview AI is permanently barred from making its faceprint database available to most private companies nationwide, and for five years could not sell to any Illinois entity, including police. [cite:de26_aclu_clearview] The law's limit is written into it: "A private entity does not include a State or local government agency," and it does not apply to contractors of a state agency; federal agencies are outside it entirely. [cite:de26_justia_bipa]

Other states have followed different paths. Washington's My Health My Data Act, in force since March 2024, covers health data that falls outside federal medical privacy law and lets individuals sue; the first suit, filed on 10 February 2025, accused Amazon of collecting location and advertising identifiers through code embedded in other companies' apps. [cite:de26_wh_mhmda] California's Delete Request and Opt-out Platform let residents send one deletion request to more than 500 registered data brokers from 1 January 2026, with brokers required to process requests every 45 days from 1 August 2026. [cite:de26_cppa_drop] Montana in May 2025 became the first state to forbid its own agencies to buy communications, precise location or other sensitive data without a warrant or investigative subpoena. [cite:de26_reason_mt282] And Illinois's licence-plate law stopped a federal use directly: after the Secretary of State's audit found that CBP had accessed Illinois plate data in "a clear violation of the state law," the camera company Flock paused its pilots with CBP and Homeland Security Investigations on 25 August 2025. [cite:de26_ap_flock]

Companies and their employees

In June 2018, after thousands of Google employees petitioned and dozens quit, Google said it would not renew its contract for Project Maven, the Pentagon's drone-imagery AI programme. [cite:de26_eng_maven] The programme continued with other contractors; Palantir's Maven Smart System contract was raised to nearly $1.3 billion in May 2025. [cite:ow9_ds_maven] In June 2020 Amazon put a one-year moratorium on police use of its face-recognition product, and IBM left the business. [cite:de26_idt_rekognition] Microsoft's September 2025 decision to disable specified Israeli defence subscriptions followed press reporting on how the service was used. [cite:ow4_tc_msft] GitHub's employees, by contrast, did not stop the company renewing its ICE licence in 2019. [cite:de26_bhr_github]

Journalists and juries

Much of this part became public through reporting and records requests, not agency disclosure: Mobile Fortify's no-consent rule through a privacy assessment obtained under the Freedom of Information Act, CBP's drone flights through flight logs, Starshield through Reuters' sources, and the wrongful arrests through the Washington Post's count. [cite:de26_reason_fortify] [cite:de26_404_drones] [cite:de26_toi_starshield] [cite:de26_fortune_wapo] Juries have acted on the spyware trade, as in WhatsApp's verdict against NSO Group, set out in [scene:de25_spyware_and_phones|Part Twenty-Six].

If it were connected

Every join on [scene:de28_the_ecosystem_map|the ecosystem map] has to pass one of these counterweights or go around it. Documented: counterweights have stopped particular joins between layers. Illinois's audit cut a federal agency off from the state's plate data; a judge first limited Medicaid sharing with ICE to five fields and later paused it; and the courts blocked the IRS address exchange after 42,695 unlawful disclosures. [cite:de26_ap_flock] [cite:de30_bl_medicaid] [cite:us10_cpa_irs_sep] Not supported: that any counterweight here reaches a join made inside an agency between records it already holds or has bought; none of the six acts at that step, which is governed by internal policy, privacy assessments and, afterwards, inspectors general and congressional letters, so the joins graded possible on the map would meet no court, state law or encryption on the way in. What would act at the join itself is a court ruling on purchased data, or a state law that limits what local agencies pass to federal ones; no such ruling exists, and Montana's law limits only what the state's own agencies buy. [cite:de26_reason_mt282]

The worst-case reading, tested

The hypothesis is the Sophia platform's, not a finding: immigration enforcement as the occasion, a sovereign executive without a veto as the destination. On this part's material, the occasion is documented. ICE holds a face-recognition app that gives the person scanned no way to refuse and keeps citizens' photographs for 15 years; CBP photographs every non-citizen at the border and lent its drones to ICE at least 50 times; Clearview's scraped gallery is under ICE contract; and the money rails now carry a statutory freeze-and-burn switch for stablecoins and a warrantless reporting system DHS searched about 290,000 times in a year. [cite:de26_reason_fortify] [cite:de26_bu_entryexit] [cite:de26_404_drones] [cite:de26_bu_clearviewice] [cite:de26_crec_genius] [cite:de26_aba_fincen] It is also documented that the one AI vendor that demanded written limits on domestic surveillance was excluded from federal work, and that officials pressed Apple to remove an app that tracked immigration agents, pressure a federal judge found likely coercive. [cite:de26_d1_anthropic] [cite:de26_reason_rosado]

Probable: that concentration makes the few firms that host the state's data and distribute the public's software into points of leverage, since the record shows that lever pulled in both directions, by a company against a foreign ministry and by a government against an app. That rests on two cases, not on a documented policy. [cite:ow4_tc_msft] [cite:de26_audacy_iceblock]

Not supported: that these tools have been joined into one domestic tracking system, that any of them has been turned on a named critic for speech, or that the theorists' texts call for this machinery; Land's programme calls for hard money and the end of central banking, the reverse of a freeze switch. [cite:de4_land_de] And the record runs against the worst case at several points. The administration that wrote the stablecoin switch also barred a central-bank digital currency and ordered an end to reputation-based debanking; most of the tools here began or grew under both parties; and courts, a state audit and company decisions narrowed particular uses. [cite:de26_fr_14178] [cite:de26_fr_14331] [cite:de26_ap_flock] [cite:de26_crs_chatrie] The counterweights have one shared gap: none of them reaches the federal purchase of commercial data, which is the subject of [scene:de28_brokers_and_doctrine|Part Thirty], or the integration that Palantir's software performs, which is the subject of [scene:de27_origins_and_control|Part Twenty-Nine].
