Human Rights Were Born the Same Week

In the second week of December 1948, the UN General Assembly wrote the founding texts of modern human rights. In those same days, the Palestinian refugees were being counted. It is sometimes assumed that the new human-rights system examined the displacement and passed judgment on it. The chronology tells a different story. The parts of the system that might have judged what happened did not yet exist.

Before 1948 there was no worldwide system of human-rights treaties. The League of Nations' Permanent Mandates Commission reviewed Britain's administration of Palestine, but there was no treaty of individual rights and no court to enforce one. The International Committee of the Red Cross worked under the laws of war, the Geneva Conventions of 1929. Its delegate Jacques de Reynier entered Deir Yassin on 11 April 1948 and reported what he found. That was a record, not a judgment.

Then, in a single week of December 1948, the General Assembly adopted three instruments. On 9 December it adopted the Genocide Convention. [cite:w_genocideconv] On 10 December it adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, by 48 votes to 0 with 8 abstentions. Article 13(2) of the Declaration reads:

Everyone has the right to leave any country, including his own, and to return to his country. [cite:un_udhr]

On 11 December came Resolution 194, on the return of refugees and compensation for those not returning. The modern rights order was written in the very days the Palestinian refugees were being counted. The UN Conciliation Commission later put their number at about 711,000.

The wording of Article 13(2) is not in dispute. Its application is. Palestinians cite it alongside Resolution 194. Israel has argued that neither creates a binding right of return for descendants. [cite:un_udhr]

What the international system did offer, it offered as relief. The General Assembly created UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestine refugees, in December 1949, and it began work in May 1950. UNRWA provides schools, health care and relief to registered Palestine refugees. Its mandate is relief and works. It was not given the task of resettlement, of legal protection, or of securing return. [cite:unrwa_who]

The next instrument set Palestinians apart. The 1951 Refugee Convention, in Article 1D, excluded people already receiving protection or help from another UN agency. Because Palestinian refugees had UNRWA, they fell outside the protection system that covers other refugees, and outside the mandate of the UNHCR, the UN refugee agency, to find lasting solutions. [cite:w_refconv] The very fact that they were receiving relief put them in a category of their own.

And there was no court. The International Criminal Court did not exist in 1948. Its founding treaty, the Rome Statute, was adopted in 1998 and came into force in July 2002, with no reach backward in time. Palestine joined in 2015, which is why the Court's jurisdiction begins in that era and not earlier. [cite:w_romestatute]

The sum is plain. The displacement of 1948 was documented by the Red Cross, counted by a UN commission, addressed in a General Assembly resolution, and relieved by an agency created for it. It was debated. It was never judged, because the institutions that might have judged it were either not yet built or not given the task.
