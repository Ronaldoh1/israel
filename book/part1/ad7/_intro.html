Part Seven follows the asset Americans are told to buy before any other: education. It begins with how public schools are paid for, largely by a tax on the value of local property, and with the 1973 Supreme Court decision that left that design in place, then sets the main measures of the resulting funding gaps beside the evidence that state aid narrows them and that money spent on schools shows up in children's adult earnings. It then measures what a year of college costs in real dollars, before and after grants, how far the federal Pell Grant has fallen behind those costs, what happened to borrowers at for-profit colleges, and how degree requirements spread to jobs that did not need them, and it weighs all of this against the college wage premium. It ends with the debt: how large it is, how the law made student loans almost impossible to discharge in bankruptcy, step by step from 1976 to 2005, how defaulted loans are collected, and where that stands as of October 2026.
