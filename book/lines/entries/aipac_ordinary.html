AIPAC is a legal, American-funded lobby, but its scale and history are not ordinary. Founded in 1953 and operating as a 501(c)(4), it does not take money from foreign governments; its funding comes from American donors [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order]. What sets it apart is the money around it: pro-Israel PAC spending rose from about $25M in 2020 to about $43M in 2022 and over $100M in 2024, much of it through the United Democracy Project, the super PAC AIPAC launched in December 2021 [cite:opensecrets_udp] [cite:opensecrets_pro_israel_pacs]. In 2024 that super PAC spent about $14.5M against Rep. Jamaal Bowman and about $8.5M against Rep. Cori Bush in their primaries, and both lost [cite:wiki_bowman_primary_2024] [cite:factcheck_udp_2024].

The history is unusual too. On November 21, 1962, Robert Kennedy's Justice Department ordered AIPAC's parent body, the American Zionist Council, to register as a foreign agent after it received $5,100,001.02 from the Jewish Agency for Israel between 1955 and 1962 [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order] [cite:jta_fulbright_jewish_agency_hearing]. All of this is documented lobbying in public filings, not a hidden operation.
