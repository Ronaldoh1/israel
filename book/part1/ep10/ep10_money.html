The Money and the Places

The fortune did not disappear with him. It became an estate, administered by the two men who had run his finances for years, his lawyer Darren Indyke and his accountant Richard Kahn. Victims sued them in 2024, alleging that they had helped finance the trafficking by structuring bank accounts and cash withdrawals and setting up companies with no real business. In February 2026 they agreed to settle for up to $35 million, denying any wrongdoing; their lawyer said they did so to achieve finality. [cite:ep10_findlaw_estate2026] Separately, about $121 million to $125 million has been paid to victims from a compensation fund. [cite:ep10_findlaw_estate2026]

The places have changed hands. Both Virgin Islands properties were sold in May 2023 to the financier Stephen Deckoff for $60 million, with plans for a resort; under its settlement with the estate, the Virgin Islands government receives half the proceeds of Little St. James for a trust supporting victims of abuse and trafficking. [cite:ep10_nhpr_islands2023] Zorro Ranch was bought at auction in 2023 by a company tied to the family of Don Huffines, a former Texas state senator; the price was not disclosed. [cite:ep10_trd_ranch2026]

Nothing in the released record shows the estate's money, or any of these properties, being used for anything resembling Epstein's operation since his death. On money, the honest verdict is narrower than on people: the wealth survived under the same management, and has been paying victims; no document shows it funding anything else.
