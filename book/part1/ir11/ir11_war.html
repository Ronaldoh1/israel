The War of 28 February 2026

Eight months after the Twelve-Day War, the United States and Israel went to war with Iran together. This section covers the run-up, the first strikes and their stated aims, the killing of the Supreme Leader and the succession, and Iran's response across the region; the tolls and the world's response follow in the next section. [scene:ir11_toll|The toll and the world's response] The account of June 2025 is in Part Ten. [scene:ir10_strikes|The Twelve Days]

The run-up: protests, threats and talks

Protests began in Iran in the last days of December 2025, when shopkeepers took to the streets over the collapse of the rial, which had fallen to about 1.4 million to the dollar, and the cost of living. Within a week they had become the largest anti-government demonstrations since the protests of 2022. On 2 January President Donald Trump warned that if Iran "violently kills peaceful protesters" the United States "will come to their rescue," adding, "We are locked and loaded and ready to go." Iranian officials answered at once. Ali Larijani, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, said Israel and the United States were stoking the protests, without offering evidence, and the parliament speaker, Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf, said American bases and forces would be "legitimate targets." [cite:ir11_ap_lockedloaded] On 11 January Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told his cabinet that "Israel is closely monitoring developments in Iran" and that he hoped Iranians would "soon be freed from the yoke of tyranny." [cite:ir11_olj_bibijan]

The crackdown that followed killed thousands. On 21 January the state Martyrs Foundation gave the first official toll, 3,117 dead, and the authorities described the unrest as "terrorist" riots fuelled by the United States. The Human Rights Activists News Agency (HRANA), a US-based group that verifies deaths through a network inside Iran, counted 4,519 killed at that point, 4,251 of them protesters and 197 security personnel, with 9,049 more deaths under review; Amnesty International documented security forces firing rifles and shotguns loaded with metal pellets at people's heads and torsos. [cite:ir11_aj_toll3117] Two days later HRANA's count passed 5,000. AP could not verify either figure because Iran had cut internet access for more than two weeks. [cite:ir11_gn_hrana5000] The crackdown is set out as part of Iran's own record in Part Nine. [scene:ir9_home|Iran's record at home]

Diplomacy ran alongside the threats. On 6 February American and Iranian delegations held indirect talks in Muscat: Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi for Iran, the envoy Steve Witkoff with Jared Kushner for the United States, with Oman's foreign minister, Badr Albusaidi, carrying messages between them. Iran wanted to discuss only its nuclear programme; Washington wanted missiles and Iran's support for armed groups on the table too. By then the United States had moved an aircraft carrier group into the region, and Iran had said it would strike American bases if attacked. [cite:ir11_gn_muscat] A round in Geneva on 26 February ended, in Albusaidi's words, with "significant progress", and technical talks were set for Vienna the following week. [cite:ir11_omanfm_geneva] Trump said that day, "My preference is to solve this problem through diplomacy," while Iranian state television said Iran would keep enriching uranium. [cite:ir11_fox_geneva] On 27 February Trump said he was "not happy with the fact that they're not willing to give us what we have to have" and that he did not want Iran enriching any uranium at all; Albusaidi said a deal was within reach and that Iran was willing to give up its enriched uranium and accept comprehensive inspections. The same day the American embassy in Jerusalem told non-essential staff they could leave, and a second carrier was on its way. [cite:ir11_ap_nothappy]

28 February: the strikes and their stated aims

The strikes began on 28 February, two days after the Geneva round and before the Vienna talks could meet. The Pentagon called its operation Epic Fury and Israel called its own Roaring Lion. [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] Reuters reported, from three people briefed on the call, that Netanyahu had telephoned Trump less than 48 hours earlier to argue that there might never be a better chance to kill Khamenei; Reuters said its reporting did not suggest that Netanyahu forced Trump to go to war, and Trump has said the decision was his alone. That reporting, and the question of who drew whom in, is weighed in Parts Twelve and Fourteen. [cite:hz12b_reuters_call] [cite:ir11a_reu_call] [scene:ir14_bill|Dragged in: the American bill]

Each government stated its aims. Trump said the purpose was to eliminate "imminent threats from the Iranian regime," claimed Iran was rebuilding its nuclear programme, and told Iranians, "When we are finished, take over your government." Netanyahu said the aim was to "remove the existential threat posed by the terrorist regime in Iran." [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] Israel described its strikes as pre-emptive and said 200 of its aircraft had hit more than 500 targets on the first day. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] The House of Commons Library, the British Parliament's research service, summarized the stated aims as regime change and the destruction of Iran's nuclear and ballistic-missile programmes, with the United States claiming self-defence. [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026] Against the nuclear claim stood the International Atomic Energy Agency, which said it had no evidence that Iran had resumed enrichment, though it also said it could not confirm Iran's claim to have stopped, because it had not been allowed back into the bombed sites. [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] [cite:ir11_ap_nothappy] How this fits Netanyahu's long record of warnings is tested in Part Five. [scene:ir5_test|The fair test]

Khamenei was killed in the first strikes, at his compound in Tehran, where, according to Reuters's sources, intelligence indicated he would meet his key lieutenants. [cite:ir11a_reu_call] Iranian state television confirmed his death early on 1 March, after Trump had announced it. Iranian media reported that his daughter, son-in-law and granddaughter were also killed, and the judiciary said the commander of the Revolutionary Guards, Mohammad Pakpour, and the senior adviser Ali Shamkhani, who had written days before that Araghchi had "sufficient support and authority" to reach a final agreement, were among the dead. [cite:ir11a_almon_feb28] [cite:ir11a_fox_shamkhani]

The succession

Under Article 111 of Iran's constitution, a temporary council took over the Supreme Leader's functions on 1 March: President Masoud Pezeshkian, the chief justice Gholam-Hossein Mohseni-Ejei, and Ayatollah Alireza Arafi of the Guardian Council. Pezeshkian called avenging Khamenei a "legitimate duty and right." [cite:ir11_aj_council] On 8 March the Assembly of Experts named Khamenei's son, Mojtaba Khamenei, as Supreme Leader. Reuters described him as "a mid-ranking cleric with close ties to the powerful Revolutionary Guards" and noted that the Islamic Republic's ruling ideology "frowns on the principle of hereditary succession." [cite:ir11_reu_mojtaba] The war aimed in part, by the American and Israeli governments' own statements, at a change of government; in its first weeks it produced instead a succession from father to son.

Iran's response

Iran struck back on the first day. Its missiles and drones hit Israel, where the emergency service Magen David Adom said a woman was killed in the Tel Aviv area, and the Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted the US Fifth Fleet in Bahrain and other American bases in the Gulf. The United Arab Emirates said 137 missiles and 209 drones were fired at it on 28 February alone, killing two people in Abu Dhabi; explosions were reported in Dubai, Riyadh, Doha and Manama. The Revolutionary Guards moved to close the Strait of Hormuz. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] [cite:ir11a_almon_feb28] Shipping through the strait, which before the war carried about 130 vessels a day, almost stopped. [cite:ir11_aj_blockade] [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026] Hezbollah opened fire on Israel on 2 March, and Israel's war in Lebanon is told in the Hezbollah volume; the Houthis joined on 28 March. [cite:hz8_am_mar2] [cite:hz10_na_houthis2026] [scene:hz8_war2026|The 2026 war in Lebanon] American service members were killed from the first week, among them six soldiers killed by an Iranian drone at a port in Kuwait. [cite:hz12_ktar_deaths]

By 22 March the Israeli military said Iran had fired more than 400 ballistic missiles at Israel and that about 92 per cent had been intercepted. [cite:ir11_olj_400] The monitoring group Airwars estimated about 650 ballistic missiles and thousands of drones fired at Israel, and about 6,400 missiles and drones at the Gulf states, over the forty days to the April ceasefire; on the American and Israeli side, the two governments declared more than 17,000 targets struck in Iran. [cite:ir11_airwars]
