Part Nine described the Holy See's money as its own reports show it today. Part Ten tells how it came to be reported that way. It begins with the IOR's crisis years under Archbishop Paul Marcinkus: the collapse of Michele Sindona's banks in 1974, the failure of Banco Ambrosiano in 1982, the death of its chairman Roberto Calvi under Blackfriars Bridge in London, the settlement of 1984 by which the IOR paid the Ambrosiano's creditors while denying liability, and the ruling of 1987 that the Lateran Treaty kept IOR officials out of Italian courts. The second section follows the Secretariat of State's investment in a London building through the trial that ended on 16 December 2023 with the conviction of Cardinal Giovanni Angelo Becciu and eight others, and the appeal ruling of 17 March 2026 that ordered the hearings to be held again, with the case's status as of October 2026. The third section traces the reforms, from the first anti-money-laundering law of 2010 to the rule of 2022 that every Holy See body keep its money at the IOR, relaxed by Leo XIV in 2025, each of them written after a scandal had exposed a gap, and sets the outside evaluators' ratings beside them.
