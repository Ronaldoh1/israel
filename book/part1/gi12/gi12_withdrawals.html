The Withdrawals and the Treaties

A territorial programme pursued by every Israeli government would leave a record of land taken and kept. The record contains that, and it also contains the opposite: eight withdrawals from captured territory, in seven different years between 1957 and 2005, three peace agreements, and two offers of large withdrawals that were never concluded. This section sets them out in order, and then gives the critics' reply, which is that almost every withdrawal came under pressure and several were meant to secure what was kept. Both are part of the record. The fullest single account, the return of Sinai, is in Part Eight. [scene:gi8_sinai|Part Eight: Sinai and Egypt]

From Sinai 1957 to the Suez Canal 1975

The first withdrawal followed the Suez war. Israel took Gaza and all of Sinai in November 1956, and on 8 November Ben-Gurion wrote to Eisenhower: "We have never planned to annex the Sinai desert." [cite:rs_cw3_jvl_ike_bg] The government nonetheless tried for four months to keep Gaza, and left only after a UN resolution, an American aide-mémoire and Eisenhower's public demand. Israel left Gaza on 8 March 1957 and Sinai the same month. [cite:rs_cw3_jvl_1124] [cite:rs_cw3_jvl_bg_mar57] [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza] Part Three sets out the episode and its two readings. [scene:gi3_sinai56|Part Three: the Sinai test]

The 1973 war produced three more. In January 1974 Israel pulled its forces back from the western bank of the Suez Canal. [cite:gi12_ied_sinai2] The Israel–Syria agreement signed in Geneva on 31 May 1974 put all territory east of "Line A" under Syrian administration, let Syrian civilians return, and placed a UN force of about 1,200 between the armies; Israeli forces stopped at a separate line around Kuneitra. [cite:gi12_jvl_syrdis] The town of Quneitra returned to Syrian civil control when the agreement took effect on 6 June. It came back largely demolished. A report commissioned by the UN concluded that Israeli forces had destroyed it deliberately before leaving, and the General Assembly condemned the destruction on 29 November 1974 by 93 votes to 8. Israel's position was that most of the damage came from the 1967 and 1973 wars and the shelling between them. [cite:gi12_wiki_quneitra] In the second Sinai agreement, signed on 4 September 1975, Israel gave up the Mitla and Gidi passes and the Abu Rudeis oil fields. [cite:gi12_ied_sinai2] A US–Israel memorandum signed with it guaranteed Israel's oil supply for five years. [cite:ow1_jvl_moa1975]

Sinai 1982, Lebanon 2000, Gaza 2005

The Egypt–Israel treaty of 26 March 1979 required Israel to "withdraw all its armed forces and civilians from the Sinai." [cite:gi8_avalon_egtreaty] A Likud government did so. The handover ended on 25 April 1982, and the town of Yamit was evacuated and levelled. [cite:gi1_csm_sinai1982] When an arbitration panel ruled in 1988 that the 250-acre strip at Taba was Egyptian, Israel left it on 15 March 1989. [cite:gi8_enc_taba]

In southern Lebanon Ehud Barak had pledged to bring the soldiers home by July 2000, after a campaign led by the Four Mothers movement, which began when 73 soldiers died in a helicopter accident in 1997. [cite:hz4_csm_mothers] He ordered the withdrawal on 23 May 2000 and the last soldier left the next day. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] [cite:hz4_merip_2000] On 16 June 2000 the UN Secretary-General confirmed that Israel had complied with Security Council Resolution 425. Lebanon, backed by Syria, still claimed the Shebaa Farms, which the UN placed in the Syrian Golan. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] The Hezbollah volume tells the story. [scene:hz4_withdrawal|The Hezbollah Architecture: the withdrawal of 2000]

In Gaza, the withdrawal was carried out by Ariel Sharon, whom the analyst Marc Lynch describes as having held, with the Likud right, that a Palestinian state already existed in Jordan; he removed every settlement from Gaza. [cite:gi8_merip_nojordan] Likud members voted against his plan in a party referendum on 2 May 2004; the cabinet approved an amended version 14 to 7, and the Knesset passed the implementation law 59 to 40 on 16 February 2005. [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] All 21 Gaza settlements were evacuated between 15 and 22 August 2005, with about 8,000 settlers removed, and the last soldier left on 12 September. [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] [cite:gz_wiki_gushkatif] Four settlements in the northern West Bank, Homesh, Ganim, Kadim and Sa-Nur, were evacuated at the same time. They were the only West Bank settlements cleared. [cite:gi12_toi_repeal2023] Gaza, Documented covers the disengagement in full. [scene:gz3_withdrawal|Gaza, Documented: the withdrawal]

The treaties, and two offers not concluded

The Egyptian and Jordanian treaties have both held. The Jordanian treaty of 26 October 1994 committed both states to "respect each other's sovereignty, territorial integrity and political independence" and fixed the border by the Mandate line. [cite:gi8_incore_jotreaty] On 20 March 2023, after Smotrich spoke at a podium bearing a map that included Jordan, Israel's Foreign Ministry stated that Israel "recognizes the territorial integrity of the Hashemite Kingdom." [cite:gi8_ynet_mfajordan] In the Oslo process Israel recognised the PLO "as the representative of the Palestinian people" on 9 September 1993, and the PLO recognised Israel's "right ... to exist in peace and security." [cite:gi12_jvl_letters1993] The interim agreement of 1995 handed parts of the West Bank to the Palestinian Authority, though Israel kept full control of Area C, 72 to 74 percent of the territory under Oslo II in 1995 and about 63 percent by a 2013 count. [cite:wb_wiki_oslo2areas]

Oslo's limits were stated by the prime minister who signed it. Presenting the 1995 agreement to the Knesset on 5 October, Yitzhak Rabin described a Palestinian "entity which is less than a state," said Israel's borders would be "beyond the lines which existed before the Six-Day War," set its security border in the Jordan Valley "in the broadest meaning of that term," and promised "not to uproot a single settlement" in the interim period. [cite:gi12_jvl_rabin1995] Later offers went further. In talks with Syria in 1994–2000 Rabin and then Barak discussed withdrawal from most or all of the Golan; the talks failed in March 2000 over a strip by Lake Tiberias. [cite:gi8_enc_syrtalks] In 2008 Ehud Olmert, by his own account, offered to keep 6.3 percent of the West Bank in exchange for Israeli land equal to 5.8 percent, and to place Jerusalem's Old City under international control. Mahmoud Abbas did not accept, saying Olmert had shown him a map but not given it to him. [cite:gi12_toi_olmert2008]

The critics' reply

Critics of the withdrawal record make three arguments. The first is that the withdrawals were forced. In 1957 the Eisenhower administration weighed cutting reparations, bond sales and aid; the historian Uri Bar-Joseph calls the pressure "brutal." [cite:rs_cw3_frus17_d120] [cite:rs_cw3_barjoseph_hdiplo] In 1975 Kissinger and Ford suspended arms deliveries to win the second Sinai agreement. [cite:gi12_ied_sinai2] Lebanon in 2000 followed fifteen years of attrition by Hezbollah, whose leader declared the withdrawal incomplete until the Shebaa Farms were "liberated." [cite:hz4_merip_2000]

The second argument is that land was given up to keep other land. Begin's autonomy plan of December 1977 gave residents of the West Bank and Gaza self-rule, entitled Israelis "to acquire land and settle" there, and left sovereignty "open." [cite:gi5_ecf_selfrule] In the decade in which Sinai was returned, the settler population of the West Bank, excluding East Jerusalem, grew from 4,400 in 1977 to 60,300 in 1987, by Peace Now's count. [cite:wb_peacenow_pop] In Sinai itself, Sharon said "we have reached the red line of our concessions." [cite:gi1_csm_sinai1982] Gaza in 2005 is the case most cited. In an interview published by Haaretz on 6 October 2004, Sharon's adviser Dov Weisglass said that disengagement "supplies the amount of formaldehyde that is necessary so there will not be a political process with the Palestinians." He said a Palestinian state had been "removed indefinitely from our agenda," and that 190,000 of 240,000 settlers "will not be moved." [cite:gi12_un_weisglass] [cite:gi12_da_weisglass] Sharon's office said he remained committed to the road map. Weisglass told Israel Radio that Haaretz had cut part of one of his remarks, and that under existing conditions "there is not and will not be peace negotiations." [cite:gi12_ap_weisglass] [cite:gi12a_ap_weisglass] Six months earlier, President Bush had written to Sharon that, given "already existing major Israeli populations centers," a "full and complete return to the armistice lines of 1949" was unrealistic. [cite:gi12_ucsb_bush2004]

The third argument is that some withdrawals have been reversed. On 21 March 2023 the Knesset repealed, by 31 votes to 18, the clauses barring Israelis from the four northern West Bank sites. Labor's Gilad Kariv called it a "pre-annexation bill." [cite:gi12_toi_repeal2023] As of October 2026 Israel again holds ground it had left: positions in southern Lebanon, the Syrian buffer zone around Quneitra and Mount Hermon, and, by Human Rights Watch's estimate, about 64 percent of Gaza behind the Yellow Line, which Israeli officials have called a "new border" (Part Nine sets out the competing measures). [cite:hz8_un_unifil_year] [cite:hz10s_ih_hermon] [cite:hz10s_eb_sufa] [cite:gz_hrw_anniv] Israel's stated reason in each case is security, not title. Part Nine examines those claims. [scene:gi9_lebanon|Part Nine: the buffer belt]

Read together, the record does not fit either strong claim. The withdrawals show that a single expansion plan was not carried out by every government: one Likud government gave up all of Sinai, and another took the settlers out of Gaza. The critics' evidence shows that most withdrawals came under pressure or as part of an exchange, while east Jerusalem and much of the West Bank, the land successive governments called central, were kept. That pattern is documented. Whether it amounts to a strategy is an argument, and Part Eighteen weighs it. [scene:gi13_record|Part Eighteen: settled, contested and open]
