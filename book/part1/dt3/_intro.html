Part Three looks at the two filters that work before a general election: the party primary, which decides whose names reach the November ballot, and the district map, which decides which voters each candidate faces. It sets out how the plurality rule and sore loser laws keep the field to two parties, how few eligible voters take part in primaries, how many House seats are settled before November, and how the Supreme Court's 2019 decision in Rucho v. Common Cause left map-drawing to the parties that hold state legislatures, which both parties used in the mid-decade redrawing of 2025 and 2026.
