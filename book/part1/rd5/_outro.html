By the end of 1948, the state that had been only a proposal in November 1947 was a fact. Resolution 181 had passed 33 to 13, with 10 abstentions, after a three-day postponement in which three small states changed their votes. Ben-Gurion had declared independence on May 14, 1948. Eleven minutes later Truman recognized the new state de facto, on his own authority and against the advice of his senior officials. Three days after that, the Soviet Union extended the first de jure, or full legal, recognition.

The new state had an army. It was created by order on 26 May 1948 and consolidated by force, at the Altalena and after the killing of Count Bernadotte. In its first war it was armed not by the United States, which kept its embargo, but by Czechoslovakia with Soviet consent. Part of the bill was paid by a reported $50 million raised in American cities. After the first truce, the war turned in its favor.

The war had also displaced between 700,000 and 750,000 Palestinians and emptied about 500 villages. Resolution 194 affirmed the question of their return in December 1948. It was left unresolved.
