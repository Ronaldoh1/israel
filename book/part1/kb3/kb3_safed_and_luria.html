Safed: the Lion and His Scribe

Isaac Luria, known as the Ari, "the Lion", was born in Jerusalem in 1534. He came to Safed "early in 1570", studied briefly with the kabbalist Moses Cordovero and began teaching his own system; he died there on 5 August 1572, after "a scant two years" of teaching. [cite:kb_brit_luria] He wrote little. His doctrines survive through his pupil Hayyim Vital, who recorded them in a large collection after his death and worked to establish and legitimise it. [cite:kb_brit_luria]

Luria's system is a story of creation in three movements. In the first, tzimtzum, God withdraws into Himself to make room for a world. In the second, shevirat ha-kelim, the "breaking of the vessels", divine light is poured into vessels, most of which shatter, so that sparks of light fall and disharmony and evil enter the world. In the third, tikkun, restoration, the divine realm is repaired and the scattered sparks are returned to their source. [cite:kb_brit_luria] Human beings take part in that repair, through prayer, mystical intentions and secret combinations of words: "Man plays an important role in this process." [cite:kb_brit_luria]

The system put a great deal of power in the hands of whoever could perform the repair. If the world is restored by correct intentions and secret combinations of words, then those who know the intentions and the words hold the key to redemption. [cite:kb_brit_luria] The teaching was also controlled at its source. Luria's doctrines reached the world through Vital's writings, and the age-forty restriction was, as the historian Moshe Idel notes, "influential also among pupils of R. Isaac Luria". [cite:kb_idel_age40] A master who wrote almost nothing, a disciple who recorded and legitimised his teaching, and a rule restricting who could study it: the gate of the orchard had been rebuilt around a new system.

The record also shows how far the system travelled. Lurianic Kabbalah "played a central role" in the movement around Shabbetai Tzevi in the seventeenth century, and it influenced the popular Hasidic movement about a century later. [cite:kb_brit_luria] The same teaching about the scattered sparks and their repair would carry a false messiah to the height of his fame and, a century later, carry a popular revival into every village.
