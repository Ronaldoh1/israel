Before there was a flag, there was ground. Rome crushed the Bar Kokhba revolt and renamed its province there Syria Palaestina. The land then passed through Byzantine, Muslim, Crusader and Mamluk hands and, from 1516, into Ottoman rule. Through all of it, the land held a continuous if smaller Jewish presence alongside a majority population of Arabic-speaking Muslims and Christians. The modern conflict did not begin on a blank map. It began with two documented histories laid on the same territory.

This opening part clears that ground before the story of the state begins. It starts with today's map, because most confusion starts with geography: Israel within its 1949 armistice lines, the West Bank and the divisions drawn in the Oslo era, Gaza, and East Jerusalem. It explains who is meant by "the Palestinians," what the legal words "occupation," "apartheid" and "genocide" mean, who has applied them and who disputes them. Then it turns to the older record: how the story is usually taught, what the name "Israel" first meant in Genesis, a thousand years of Jewish life across the Muslim-majority world, and the departures of 1948 to 1972.

One question runs through every section. It is simple to ask and harder to answer: what does the record actually show, as distinct from the version most readers were handed?
