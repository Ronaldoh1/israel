Part Eight examines the three Catholic bodies most often named as powers behind the pope: the Society of Jesus, Opus Dei and the Sovereign Order of Malta. Each is a real institution with a documented history, a known size and a legal relationship to the Holy See that can be read in papal bulls, decrees and treaties. The part sets out how the Jesuits were founded, abolished by one pope and restored by another, and what they run today; how Opus Dei rose to a unique status in Church law under John Paul II and was reduced under Francis, and what its critics and the organisation itself say about its treatment of members; and how a pilgrims' hospital in Jerusalem became a sovereign body without territory that exchanges ambassadors with more than a hundred states and still answers to the pope as a religious order. For each, the claims that circulate about hidden control are graded against those documents.
