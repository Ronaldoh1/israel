Part Seven covers 1471 to 1592, when an argument about money split the Western canon in two. It follows the indulgence trade from Sixtus IV's decree of 1476, which let the living buy relief for the dead, to the St Peter's campaign of 1517, financed by a Fugger loan to Albert of Brandenburg and split between him and the Medici pope Leo X. It then shows how the quarrel reached the Bible through the Second Book of Maccabees, and how the Farnese pope Paul III convened the council that made the Catholic list a dogma in 1546.
