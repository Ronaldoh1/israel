Part Four covers 366 to 419, the generation in which the lists of scripture were closed. It begins with the bishop of Rome whose faith the law of 380 made the test of orthodoxy: Damasus, installed after a battle that left 137 dead in a single day, in an office a pagan historian described as enriched by the gifts of wealthy women. Damasus commissioned the Latin Bible. The part then follows Athanasius's Easter letter of 367, the debate over the Nag Hammadi jar, and the African councils that drew up the Western list and asked Rome to confirm it.
