By the late summer of 1953 the region had been sorted, unevenly and incompletely, into camps. The sorting rested on a few layers, each built by different hands, and none of them was finished.

The legal layer changed least. The armistices of 1949 remained the only agreed lines, and in 1951 the Security Council twice ruled on what they meant without turning them into anything more. Resolution 95 called the armistice regime "of a permanent character" and held that neither party could reasonably claim to be "actively a belligerent," which disposed of Egypt's case for searching Israel-bound ships without declaring the war over; China, India and the Soviet Union abstained, and Egypt reserved its rights. [cite:rs_cw1_frus1951_res95] [cite:rs_cw1_spv558] On the Syrian front, Resolution 93 found that Israel's air action of 5 April 1951 violated the cease-fire and endorsed the call for the drainage company to cease operations in the zone pending agreement, and the UN Chief of Staff found that neither Israel nor Syria held sovereignty in the demilitarized zone. [cite:rs_cw1_unispal_res93] [cite:rs_cw1_un_yearbook_1951] The Tripartite Declaration added a Western pledge to act against any violation of "frontiers or armistice lines," but an American official told Lebanon's minister that its wording "should not be given a legalistic interpretation." [cite:rs_cw1_avalon_tripartite] [cite:rs_cw1_frus1950_malik] In Iran the International Court of Justice ruled on 22 July 1952 that it had no jurisdiction, and so did not rule on the merits. [cite:rs_cw1_icj_aioc] What was settled, legally, was who could not be a belligerent and who did not own the zones; what the lines were to become was not.

The administrative layer was built inside Israel, and quietly. By 1952 a coordinating institute created by the prime minister's secret directive of 13 December 1949 reported to him, needed his prior authorization for special operations, and was run, according to GlobalSecurity.org, together with the internal security service by one man, Isser Harel. [cite:rs_cw1_jvl_mossad_directive] [cite:rs_cw1_globalsecurity_mossad] The sources name no outside body with a role in reviewing that work, and, according to the one source that records it, the internal service had no statute defining its responsibilities until 2002. [cite:rs_cw1_jvl_isa] In July 1953 the Foreign Ministry moved to Jerusalem over two American objections, and Washington regretted the move and kept its embassy in Tel Aviv. [cite:rs_cw1_jvl_fm_jerusalem_1953]

The institutional and financial layer was where the alignment showed most. Israel asked in March 1951 for a $150 million grant; the Administration offered $23.5 million, matched by the same sum for the Arab states and $50 million for the refugees; Congress pushed Israel's figure up; and in June 1952, as the JTA reported, a conference set it at $70,228,000 plus $2,772,000 for fiscal 1953, cutting Israel's share by under 8% while cutting overall aid by about 20%. [cite:rs_cw1_frus1951_eban_request] [cite:rs_cw1_frus1951_feinberg_memo] [cite:rs_cw1_jta_conference_1952] What Israel finally received for fiscal 1952 remains disputed, $65 million in one account and about $68.8 million authorized in another. [cite:rs_cw1_jta_kenen_obit] [cite:rs_cw1_kenen_silver_letter] All of it was economic; American military aid began only in the 1960s. [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] The lobby that fought for it had a man in Washington from 1951 and a separate committee by 1953–54, under a name the sources date differently. [cite:rs_cw1_jvl_aipac] [cite:cjh_kenen_papers] A tie with the CIA had begun, by one account after Ben-Gurion's 1951 visit, though its date and Angleton's early role are disputed; the CIA's own historian dates Angleton's headship of the Counterintelligence Staff to 1954. [cite:rs_cw1_tablet_spies] [cite:rs_cw1_merip_mcconnell] [cite:rs_cw1_robarge_angleton]

On the Palestinian Arab side the record is of positions held and not met. At Paris in the autumn of 1951 the Arab delegations said "there could be no limitations on the return of the refugees" and asked for individual compensation; Israel rejected return on grounds of security and stability, offered a global sum, and tied any settlement to the Jewish accounts blocked in Iraq. The Commission found that neither side would "recede," naming Israel's refusal on paragraph 11 of Resolution 194 and the Arab governments' on paragraph 5. [cite:rs_cw1_unccp_a1985] The Assembly then funded reintegration "without prejudice" to return, and Palestinians objected, by one historian's account, to the largest scheme yet proposed. [cite:rs_cw1_ga_res513] [cite:rs_cw1_newlines_sinai] Of 785 civilians evacuated from the Huleh zone, 632 were interviewed and about 260 chose to return. [cite:rs_cw1_un_yearbook_1951] Arafat's students' league and Habash's clinic were the beginnings of a politics of their own. [cite:rs_cw1_palquest_arafat] [cite:rs_cw1_palquest_habash]

The regional layer was set by two refusals and one overthrow. Egypt abrogated its treaty with Britain and refused the Middle East Command, and, in Shlaim's account, with the command faded Israel's hope of military integration with the West. [cite:rs_cw1_frus1951_mec_note] [cite:rs_cw1_shlaim_east_west] Moscow, after the Prague trial and the Doctors' Plot, broke relations on 11 February 1953 and restored them in July after Israel said it would join no alliance against the Soviet Union. [cite:rs_cw1_jc_soviet_break] [cite:rs_cw1_shlaim_east_west] In Iran an elected government was removed on 19 August 1953 by an operation the CIA and British intelligence planned and the CIA funded; no documented Israeli role in it exists, and the first Iranian–Israeli credit line had been signed in June, under Mossadeq. [cite:rs_cw1_nsarchive_2013] [cite:rs_cw1_iranica_israel]

The American layer changed tone. Truman's government had resisted the size of Israel's aid but kept the Tripartite pledge; Dulles, as reported, spoke of Arab "deep resentment" over Israel's creation and of a policy that must be "impartial," while standing by the declaration. [cite:rs_cw1_djn_dulles_1953] At home the Knights of Columbus had put "under God" before every member of Congress, a campaign with no documented tie to Israel. [cite:rs_cw1_crec_2002]

What was settled, then, was Israel's side. What remained contested was why it chose it. On one reading, Israel's own needs, arms, aid, American Jewry and German reparations, drew it west; on the other, the Israeli establishment's, Moscow's own logic pushed it there, and the Prague trial and the Doctors' Plot, whose antisemitism the sources name plainly, are its evidence. [cite:rs_cw1_shlaim_east_west] [cite:rs_cw1_cia_doctors_plot_1953] Egypt's case that the war continued was argued in law and rejected by a vote, not by agreement. What the record leaves open is how the new order would be tested: no source here shows the third paragraph of the Tripartite Declaration invoked, the zones had no owner, and the record gives Dulles's "impartial" no worked-out meaning yet. The months that followed would test it, as told in [scene:cw2_banat_yaacov|the 1953 aid suspension].
