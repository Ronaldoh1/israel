The Laws on the Books

Three bodies of law are meant to govern what American weapons may be used for and who may receive them. Section 620I of the Foreign Assistance Act bars assistance to any country that the President is told is prohibiting or restricting, directly or indirectly, the delivery of American humanitarian aid. [cite:aa_pp_620i] The Leahy laws bar assistance to foreign security units credibly implicated in gross violations of human rights, such as torture or extrajudicial killing. [cite:ow8_crs_leahy2025] And National Security Memorandum 20, issued on 8 February 2024, required countries using American arms in war to give written assurances that they would comply with international humanitarian law and would not arbitrarily deny humanitarian aid. [cite:ow8_aca_nsm20]

April and May 2024

In April 2024 two parts of the American government reached the same conclusion. The US Agency for International Development told Secretary of State Antony Blinken that the looming famine in Gaza resulted from Israel's "arbitrary denial, restriction, and impediments of U.S. humanitarian assistance", and the State Department's refugee bureau concluded that Israel was blocking aid and that Section 620I should be triggered, which would have frozen about $830 million in weapons funding. [cite:aa_pp_620i] On 10 May 2024 Blinken told Congress: "We do not currently assess that the Israeli government is prohibiting or otherwise restricting" American-backed aid. [cite:aa_pp_620i] The report under NSM-20, delivered the same day, found it "reasonable to assess" that American arms had been used in instances inconsistent with international humanitarian law, made no specific findings, and accepted Israel's assurances as credible. [cite:ow8_js_nsm20report]

The thirty days

In October 2024 Blinken and Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin gave Israel until 12 November to improve the humanitarian situation in Gaza. When the deadline passed, eight aid groups reported that conditions were at their worst of the war and that Israel had not met the demands; the State Department said, "At this time we haven't made an assessment that Israel is in violation of U.S. law," and the aid continued. [cite:aa_axios_deadline]

Repeal and remediation

The Leahy process for Israel ran through a special forum, and a former head of the State Department's vetting office has said that no Israeli unit has been found ineligible; the One-Way Mirror follows it in detail. [cite:ow8_js_blaha_ilvf] The new administration revoked NSM-20 on 21 February 2025. [cite:ow8_aca_rescind]

The State Department's position was that pressure on Israel worked better than suspension: a spokesperson said that Israel "subsequently took steps to facilitate increased humanitarian access and aid flow into Gaza". [cite:aa_pp_620i] Critics answer that a law never applied is not a condition at all. The record shows the findings, the decisions and the dates; it does not settle which view was right.
