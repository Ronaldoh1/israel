Part Two goes back to the beginning of the shelf. It follows how the current acquired its names, from Curtis Yarvin's "formalism" in 2007 through "neoreactionary" in 2008 to Nick Land's "Dark Enlightenment" in 2012, and settles who coined which term. It then reads the core texts in order: the "formalist manifesto" that opened Yarvin's blog, the "Open Letter" he addressed to progressive readers, the "Patchwork" series that set out his design for states run as corporations, the 2012 talk that turned it toward the American government, and Land's ten-part serial. It ends with the first journalists and critics who read them.
