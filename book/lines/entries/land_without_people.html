Every Ottoman and British census shows a populated country. Britain's 1922 census counted 757,182 people, 83,794 of them Jews, about 11%. [cite:jvl_census_1931] At the end of 1946, the UN's investigating committee estimated about 1,846,000 people: some 1,203,000 Arabs (65%) and 608,000 Jews (33%). [cite:rebelion_unscop_figures]

The slogan was not Zionist in origin. It first appeared in print in 1843, in a book by Alexander Keith, a Scottish clergyman who had visited Palestine in 1839 and knew it was inhabited. The first Zionist use came in 1901, from Israel Zangwill. [cite:meq_land_without_people] Historians debate whether it meant "empty" or "without a nation." The census figures also refute a slogan from the other side: the claim that Palestinian Arabs privately owned 94% of the land counts large areas of state land as Arab-owned. The 1945 Village Statistics survey separates private ownership from state land. [cite:wiki_village_statistics]
