Bill Clinton

Born 1946, Hope, Arkansas. At the time of contact: president of the United States, then former president and head of the Clinton Foundation. Today: former president; not charged or sued over Epstein.

Before Epstein

William Jefferson Clinton was born William Jefferson Blythe III on 19 August 1946 in Hope, Arkansas, three months after his father died in a car crash. He graduated from Georgetown, went to Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar in 1968 and took a law degree at Yale in 1973. He was elected Arkansas attorney general in 1976 and governor in 1978; he lost re-election, won the office back four years later and held it until 1992, when he defeated George H. W. Bush and Ross Perot for the presidency. He served two terms, from 1993 to 2001. In 1998 the House impeached him over his conduct with a White House intern, and the Senate acquitted him. [cite:ep5_wh_bill]

His court record, like everyone's in this dictionary, is reported at the level it reached. In April 1999 a federal judge, Susan Webber Wright, found him in civil contempt for his "willful failure" to obey her orders to testify truthfully in Paula Jones's sexual-harassment suit, and fined him $90,000. On his last full day in office, in January 2001, he settled with the independent counsel: a five-year suspension of his Arkansas law licence, a $25,000 fine, and a written statement that he was "certain my responses to questions about Ms. Lewinsky were false". [cite:ep5_cnn_clinton2001] None of this involves Epstein.

The record with Epstein

The earliest documented contact is from his presidency. On 29 September 1993 the White House photographer Ralph Alswang photographed him greeting Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell at a reception for donors to the White House Restoration Project, an event Hillary Clinton also attended; the photographs were released under a freedom-of-information request and are held in the Clinton Digital Library. Reporting based on visitor logs says Epstein went to the White House at least seventeen times during Clinton's terms; the purpose of those visits is not established. [cite:ep5_snopes_1993] Epstein gave $1,000 to Clinton's 1992 campaign. [cite:ep5_gma_donations2019]

The substantial record begins after he left office. In 2002 and 2003 he flew on Epstein's plane for trips that his spokesman said were connected with the Clinton Foundation, to Europe, Asia and Africa; the flight logs include Thailand, Portugal, Ghana, Russia and China. [cite:ep4_ap_clinton_statement2019] [cite:ep5_spokesman_clinton2026] How many flights those trips amounted to is disputed: his office spoke of four trips, while counts of individual flights from published logs have run from more than a dozen to at least twenty-six, and the committee's chairman put the number at twenty-seven. [cite:ep4_politifact_clinton2020] [cite:ep5_spokesman_clinton2026] Photographs released from the estate in December 2025 show him with Epstein and Maxwell, most of them undated. [cite:ep4_rnz_clinton]

What he said, and what the files show

He gave his own account in a deposition to the House Oversight Committee on 27 February 2026, released on video the following week. He said he met Epstein in 2001 or 2002, introduced by Larry Summers; that the relationship was "cordial" rather than a friendship; that Epstein offered the plane for the foundation's AIDS work in exchange for conversations about economics and politics; that a photograph of him in a hot tub was taken on a foundation trip to Brunei, with a person he did not know, and involved no sexual activity; and that he cut ties before Epstein's 2008 conviction. "I saw nothing, and I did nothing wrong," he said. [cite:ep5_npr_depositions2026]

That account and the 1993 photographs are not quite the same story, though they do not have to conflict. A president shaking hands with donors at a reception is not a relationship, and Clinton dates the relationship from the introduction by Summers. But the reported White House visits, the 1993 handshake and the 1992 donation show that Epstein had access to Clinton's circle years before 2001, and the deposition, as reported, does not account for them.

One account conflicts with his directly. In court papers later unsealed, Virginia Giuffre said she once saw Clinton on Epstein's island; he says he never went there, and no flight log places him there. She did not accuse him of wrongdoing. [cite:ep4_politifact_clinton2020] No accuser has alleged misconduct by him in any proceeding this book has examined.

What the news got wrong

The most repeated false claim is that Clinton visited the island many times; the president said "28 times". His own chief of staff later said there was "no evidence" of the visits. [cite:ep4_rnz_clinton] The opposite error, that Clinton's ties were limited to "four trips", understates the record: the 1993 contact and the White House visits come first, and the flight count is disputed.

What remains unestablished: what the seventeen White House visits were for and whom Epstein saw; how many flights the four trips involved; and what conversations Epstein had in exchange for the plane.
