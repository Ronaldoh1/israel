The Carried Flame

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

Wherever the record shows a burning, it also shows something carried. In 1560 the devotees of Shri Mangesh carried the linga by night across the border, out of Portuguese reach. [cite:vt34_goagov_mangueshi] Some royal manuscripts of the Nasrid kings of Granada, long assumed lost in the fire of 1499, survive because the last sultan took the palace library with him into exile. [cite:vt34_cuni_nasrid] Syriac books banned at Diamper in 1599 survive in Kerala and have been catalogued. [cite:vt34_hmml_diamper] In Mexico a friar and Nahua scholars together recorded the gods, the calendar and the medicine of the Mexica in the twelve books of the Florentine Codex. [cite:vt34_whe_florentine]

The inner reading takes these as one picture. A tradition can lose its buildings and survive in a household, a border village, a carried object, a remembered calendar. What a tribunal can seize is the outer form; what survives is what people carry. Earlier books on this shelf read the temple as something within; the record of the inquisitions shows communities that, under pressure, kept their sacred things by carrying them.

The record limits the reading. The survivals are fragments, the losses were vast and mostly unrecoverable, and those who carried the linga by night were saving a sacred object, not illustrating a theory of inwardness. [cite:vt34_goagov_mangueshi] The reading honours what they did; it does not claim to know what they thought.
