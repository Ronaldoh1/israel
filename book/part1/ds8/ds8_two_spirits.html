The Two Spirits

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

At the centre of the Community Rule is a short treatise on human nature. God, it says, "has created man to govern the world, and has appointed for him two spirits", "the spirits of truth and falsehood." [cite:ds_vermes_1qs] "All the children of righteousness are ruled by the Prince of Light and walk in the ways of light", while "all the children of falsehood are ruled by the Angel of Darkness and walk in the way of darkness." [cite:ds_vermes_1qs] The War Scroll turns the same division into a war at the end of time, in which the children of light defeat the children of darkness. [cite:ds_brit_dss]

Read on its surface, this is a teaching that divides humanity into two camps, the community and everyone else, and the community's name for itself, the Sons of Light, carried that division. [cite:ds_vq_nature] But the treatise does not stop there. In its next column it moves the battle inside the person: "the spirit of truth and falsehood struggle in the hearts of men". [cite:ds_vermes_1qs]

The inner reading takes that line as the key to the whole teaching. The two spirits are not two kinds of people but two movements in each person, and the war between the children of light and the children of darkness is fought in every heart before it is fought anywhere else. The same turn appears across this shelf: the Gnostic rulers read as rulers within, the Watchers' descent read as a fall of attention, the kingdom that is inside the reader. In each case a division drawn between groups becomes, on the inner reading, a division within the self, and the work moves from defeating an enemy to seeing clearly what in oneself serves the light.

The reading also carries a warning. A community that calls itself the children of light and everyone else the children of darkness has a ready-made reason to treat outsiders as enemies; the War Scroll imagines exactly that. [cite:ds_brit_dss] Read inwardly, the same text undoes the warning's danger, because no one who sees both spirits at work in their own heart can easily place all the darkness in someone else.

The record limits the reading. The authors of the Community Rule believed in a real Prince of Light and a real Angel of Darkness, and in a real final war; the treatise is part of a strict sectarian rule, not a manual of introspection. Its own line about the struggle in the heart is the anchor for the inner reading, but the community did not draw from it the universal conclusion drawn here.
