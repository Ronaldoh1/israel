This volume follows the money. It asks how much American organizations that support Israel have spent to shape American government, through which channels, at which levels, against whom and with what result; and how similar organizations work in other democracies. It is built from public filings: Federal Election Commission reports, lobbying disclosures, foreign-agent registrations, congressional travel records, nonprofit tax returns, and the reporting of named journalists who have read them.

It defines its subject by organizations, not by people. Pro-Israel money here means money moving through committees, lobbies, foundations and registered agents whose stated purpose is to support Israel; it never means money given by any religious or ethnic group, and the book treats no donor's identity as evidence of anything. It keeps money and motive apart: what members received and what was spent against them is documented; why they voted as they did is a separate question, and the book says so wherever it arises. And it reports competing figures side by side, with their dates and definitions, rather than choosing the largest.

Ten parts follow. The first fixes the definitions and the channels. The second describes AIPAC, the organization at the center of the American network. The third counts the money cycle by cycle; the fourth names the largest recipients in each party; the fifth follows the campaigns to unseat members of Congress; the sixth turns to members who refuse the money and to those who left office under attack. The seventh follows the trips, the eighth the state and local level, and the ninth the lobby abroad. The last part sets the whole architecture out on one page, with what it costs.
