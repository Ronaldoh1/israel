Part Seventeen follows the Holy See's position on Zionism and Israel from the end of the Second World War to recognition in 1993. The first section covers the years of partition and war, when Pius XII asked in three encyclicals for an internationalised Jerusalem, founded a relief mission for Palestinian refugees and declined to recognise the new state, and reports what the private record, including the Pius XII files opened in 2020, has so far shown. The second follows two tracks that ran side by side for three decades: the Holy See's teaching on the Jewish people, transformed by Nostra Aetate in 1965 and the documents that followed, and its diplomacy toward the State of Israel, which named the state for the first time only in 1984 and declared in 1985 that it was to be judged by international law, not theology. The third reads the Fundamental Agreement of 30 December 1993 article by article and tests whether the timing of each change tracked Israeli pressure, Arab and Palestinian sensitivities, or the Holy See's own stated interests.
