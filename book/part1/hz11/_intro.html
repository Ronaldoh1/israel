Part Eleven tests the thesis that runs beneath this volume: that Hezbollah, the Houthis and the other groups aligned with Iran have served as the standing reason for wars that never end, for military aid that never stops and for drawing the United States in. It sets the warnings about Iran's bomb beside what intelligence agencies, Israel's included, assessed at the time; follows the threat into the laws that fund Israel's military; and asks whether each war in Lebanon ended the threat it was fought against. It then sets out the armed groups Israel itself paid or armed, in south Lebanon, southern Syria and Gaza, and what those cases do and do not show. Last, it names who gains from continued conflict, in money and in politics, and gives the case against the thesis in full: the groups' own attacks, Iran's own aims, and the ceasefires Israel accepted and the restraint Washington applied. Facts, patterns and arguments about motive are kept apart throughout.
