Abroad: Latin America and Africa

The system's fastest growth after 1970 was outside the United States, in the Protestant and Pentecostal churches of Latin America and Africa. That growth can be counted in censuses and surveys. Whether it carried a political attachment to Israel, and whether that attachment moved governments, is harder to show, and this section keeps the two apart. It sets out the church figures first, then the three Latin American governments whose decisions on an embassy in Jerusalem have been tied to their evangelical voters, then the thinner African record.

The Protestant shift in Latin America

In a survey of more than 30,000 people in 18 countries and Puerto Rico, conducted between October 2013 and February 2014, the Pew Research Center found that 19 per cent of Latin American adults identified as Protestant and 69 per cent as Catholic. The shares varied widely: Protestants were 41 per cent in Guatemala and in Honduras, 26 per cent in Brazil and 7 per cent in Paraguay. [cite:ts7_pew_latam2014] About two in three Protestants in the region identified as Pentecostal or attended a Pentecostal church. In Guatemala and Honduras most Protestants had been raised Protestant (only 23 and 26 per cent were former Catholics), while in Paraguay 68 per cent were converts. [cite:ts7_ct_pew2014] Pew reported that the region's Protestants were more likely than its Catholics to believe that Jesus would return in their lifetime, but it did not ask about Israel. [cite:ts7_pew_latam2014]

Brazil's census shows the trend continuing. In the 2022 count, released in June 2025, evangelicals were 26.9 per cent of the population, 47.4 million people, up from 21.6 per cent in 2010; Catholics fell from 65.1 to 56.7 per cent. [cite:ts7_reuters_brcensus] Williams's history notes that US-based broadcast ministries carried Israel-themed worship well beyond North America, and that one American preacher of the movement, Jonathan Cahn, held meetings in Honduras, Cuba, Haiti, India and Nigeria. [cite:ts7_williams_pent] What none of these sources gives is a measure of how many Latin American Protestants hold dispensational or Christian Zionist beliefs about the modern state. The church figures are a measure of the potential audience, not of the belief.

Guatemala, 2018

Guatemala opened its embassy in Jerusalem on 16 May 2018, two days after the United States. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and President Jimmy Morales, whom NPR described as "an evangelical", cut the ribbon. Morales called it "a step forward in favour of union and friendship." Guatemala's tie to Israel was older than its churches' growth: its UN ambassador, Jorge García Granados, had been "one of the architects" of the 1947 partition plan and lobbied Latin American governments to vote for it. [cite:ts7_npr_guatemala] Analysts cited by the University of Pittsburgh's Panoramas tied the 2018 move to three things together: the president's evangelical base, Guatemala's dependence on American aid and goodwill, and the history of Israeli arms, training and surveillance supplied to Guatemala's army in the 1980s after the United States cut military aid in 1977. [cite:pm_pitt_guatemala] Faith is one documented factor among several, and the Follow the Money volume treats the others. [scene:pm9_test|Follow the Money: what partner governments gain]

Paraguay, 2018–2024

Paraguay is the case that most complicates a religious explanation. Under President Horacio Cartes it moved its embassy to Jerusalem in May 2018, following Guatemala. [cite:ts7_npr_guatemala] Months later his successor, Mario Abdo Benítez, closed it, and Israel retaliated by temporarily closing its own embassy in Asunción. President Santiago Peña reopened the Jerusalem embassy on 12 December 2024, saying that "Israel is going to win, and the countries (that) are standing next to Israel, we are going to win." [cite:ts7_abc_paraguay] Paraguay was 89 per cent Catholic and 7 per cent Protestant in Pew's survey. [cite:ts7_pew_latam2014] A country with one of the smallest Protestant shares in the region moved its embassy twice, under two presidents, and reversed it once in between; the sequence follows changes of government, not changes in church membership. The Associated Press, reporting the reopening, noted that only the United States, Guatemala, Honduras, Kosovo and Papua New Guinea then had embassies in Jerusalem, and that Guatemala and Honduras had "similarly strong evangelical churches". [cite:ts7_abc_paraguay]

Brazil, 2019: the pledge and the meat exports

Brazil, with the largest evangelical population in the region, shows the system meeting a material interest. President Jair Bolsonaro suggested in January 2019 that Brazil would follow the United States in moving its embassy. When he visited Israel at the end of March 2019 he spoke of his Christian devotion to the Holy Land, and Netanyahu said Bolsonaro had called the move a matter of "when, not if". What Brazil announced on 31 March was an office in Jerusalem "to promote trade, investment, technology and innovation", attached to the embassy in Tel Aviv. Vice-President Hamilton Mourão had called an embassy move "a bad idea" because it would hurt exports to Arab countries, including an estimated $5 billion in halal food sales. [cite:ts7_ynet_brazil] The office opened on 15 December 2019 in the presence of the president's son, the lawmaker Eduardo Bolsonaro, who said his father was committed to moving the embassy; fears for meat exports to Arab markets were again reported. [cite:ts7_pti_brazil] Brazil's embassy did not move. In this case the documented commitment of a Christian president gave way to a documented export interest.

Africa: pilgrims and preachers

The African record in the sources examined is thinner and of a different kind. Nigeria has a government body, the Nigerian Christian Pilgrim Commission, which sends pilgrims to Israel as well as to Rome, Greece and Egypt. Christianity Today reported in December 2012 that about 30,000 Nigerians had travelled in the last three months of that year, up from 2,000 a decade earlier, and that the commission set a minimum age of 30 for Israel to stop pilgrims staying on illegally. [cite:ts7_ct_nigeria] State-organised pilgrimage is a measure of religious attachment to the Holy Land; it is not evidence of a political doctrine about the modern state, and Nigerian church leaders quoted in the same report disagreed about its spiritual value. [cite:ts7_ct_nigeria] The pentecostal broadcast and conference circuit that Williams traces reached Nigeria too. [cite:ts7_williams_pent] The parliamentary caucus network that the Israel Allies Foundation built on "faith-based diplomacy", which reached 50 countries by December 2020, is treated with Israel's own outreach in Part Nine. [cite:pm_ih_iaf50] [scene:ts9_israel|Part Nine: what Israel gained]

Abroad as at home, the counted facts are church growth and dated decisions. Protestants were 41 per cent of adults in Guatemala and Honduras in 2013–14, and evangelicals more than a quarter of Brazilians in 2022, and three of the five countries with embassies in Jerusalem at the end of 2024 were in Latin America. The link between the two is a correlation that analysts explain by aid, arms, alliances with Washington and presidential faith together. Paraguay moved with few Protestants; Brazil, with many, did not move. The sources examined contain no survey that measures Christian Zionist belief in these countries, and that gap is recorded here as open.
