Part Eight traced the protection that is documented. It began fifty years earlier, with a president firing a prosecutor and a Congress that built an independent check, then let it lapse. In Palm Beach a line prosecutor wrote a 60-count indictment and was overruled; a defence team worked every rule available to it; and an agreement immunised an unnamed class of co-conspirators and was kept from the victims. A judge later found the law had been broken, and an appeals court found the law did not reach what was done. Fourteen ordinary rules, most of them sound on their own, put nearly every decision in the hands of the executive branch and kept courts and victims out of it. The tools built for an enterprise case existed and went unused. The finding is not a staged show of justice. It is a structure that protects whoever is close to the people holding its keys.

Part Nine follows the fight over the files themselves: what was kept, what was released, what was redacted, and who decided.
