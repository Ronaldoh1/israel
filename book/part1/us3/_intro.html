Part Three compares how the two systems let people choose and criticise their governments. It begins with elections: Israel's nationwide party lists and rising threshold beside America's state-run contests and redrawn districts, turnout in each country over time, and the rules on who may stand, tested again weeks before Israel's vote on 27 October 2026 and the American midterms on 3 November. It sets the main democracy ratings, Freedom House, V-Dem, Reporters Without Borders and the Israel Democracy Institute's own survey, against each other over the years they cover, and states what each leaves out. It then follows the money: Israel's publicly funded parties and America's private campaigns, super PACs and lobbying, including the pro-Israel spending that features in American arguments about the two countries. Finally it covers the press, boycott and campus speech, protest, and the laws each state uses on civil-society groups and their foreign funding.
