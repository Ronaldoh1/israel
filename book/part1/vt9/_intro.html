Part Eight closed with the orders and the Order of Malta. This part follows the money. It separates the three bodies usually lumped together as "the Vatican's money": the Institute for the Works of Religion (IOR), a bank for Church clients founded by Pius XII in 1942; the Administration of the Patrimony of the Apostolic See (APSA), created in 1967 out of the office that managed Mussolini's 1929 payment and now the owner of the Holy See's buildings, securities and gold; and the Governorate, which runs Vatican City and its museums. It then reads Peter's Pence, the pope's annual collection, for what each year's report says the money paid for, and ends by sorting the Holy See's wealth into what is on published books, what is left off them, and what was never the Holy See's to count, tracing two famous totals to their sources. Every figure comes from the body's own report for a stated year, or from named reporting on it.
