Repression at Home, 1988–2026

The same institutions that armed allies abroad have used lethal force against Iran's own citizens. The pattern is documented in five episodes: the prison killings of 1988, the crackdown after the 2009 election, the November 2019 fuel protests, the 2022 protests after the death of Mahsa Amini and the crackdown of January 2026. Each produced death tolls that differ widely by source, because the authorities have rarely published figures and have cut the internet while killing was under way. The figures below are given with their source and, where known, how they were counted. The government's own account is given beside each.

1988: the prison killings

In July 1988, weeks before the war with Iraq ended on 20 August, Khomeini issued a written order that imprisoned members of the opposition People's Mojahedin who remained loyal to it be executed as "waging war on God"; a second order reportedly covered leftist prisoners. [cite:ir9_usip_raisi] [cite:ir2_brit_war] Three-member panels that prisoners came to call "death commissions" sat in at least 32 cities and held summary proceedings with no appeal. Amnesty International put the number killed between late July and early September 1988 at no fewer than 5,000, most of them Mojahedin supporters and hundreds from leftist and Kurdish groups; the authorities have never told families how or why their relatives died or returned the bodies. [cite:ir9_amnesty_1988] Other estimates run from 2,800–3,800, the figure given at the time by Khomeini's designated heir Hossein-Ali Montazeri, to the 4,481 names published by Iranian activists. Montazeri protested in writing on 15 August 1988 and told the Tehran panel it was the biggest crime in the Islamic Republic's history; he was forced out as heir the next year. [cite:ir9_usip_raisi]

One member of the Tehran panel was Ebrahim Raisi, then the 27-year-old deputy prosecutor of Tehran and later president of Iran. In 2018 he said he had not headed the court; at his first press conference as president-elect, on 21 June 2021, he said that a prosecutor who "defended the security of the people" should "be praised." [cite:ir9_usip_raisi] [cite:ir9_amnesty_1988] The government has never acknowledged the scale of the killings; officials have described the victims as a "few terrorists" and those responsible as deserving honour. [cite:ir9_amnesty_1988] In July 2024 the UN Special Rapporteur on Iran, Javaid Rehman, concluded that the killings of 1981–82 and 1988 amounted to crimes against humanity, and to genocide against people targeted for their beliefs, and called for an international accountability mechanism. Tehran rejects the findings. [cite:ir9_jurist_rehman]

2009, 2019, 2022

After the presidential election of 12 June 2009, which returned Ahmadinejad and which the defeated candidates said was rigged, mass protests were met by the security forces. A senior Revolutionary Guards commander put the death toll at 36, including three detainees who died in Kahrizak prison, which was then closed; the opposition counted 72. [cite:ir9_ht_2009] The killing of Neda Agha-Soltan on a Tehran street on 20 June 2009 was filmed and seen around the world; officials blamed foreign media and intelligence services, and state television later claimed she had staged her death. [cite:ir9_wiki_neda]

In November 2019 petrol rationing and a price rise set off protests that turned into calls for the clerical leadership to go, and the authorities shut down the internet for days. Reuters reported on 23 December 2019, citing three interior ministry officials, that about 1,500 people were killed in less than two weeks, and that Khamenei had told officials on 17 November: "Do whatever it takes to end it." Amnesty International confirmed at least 304 deaths; Iran Human Rights at least 324. The Supreme National Security Council called the Reuters report "false propaganda," and officials acknowledged only that "some rioters were killed." [cite:ir9_rferl_2019] [cite:ir9_farda_2019] A member of parliament said security officials had told a parliamentary committee the toll was 170; no official figure was published. [cite:ir9_farda_2019]

On 13 September 2022 the morality police arrested Mahsa Amini in Tehran for "inappropriate attire"; she died in custody three days later. [cite:ir9_aj_coroner] The protests under the slogan "Woman, Life, Freedom" spread to most of the country. A UN fact-finding mission reported in March 2024 that her death was "unlawful and caused by physical violence" for which the state was responsible, that credible figures put the number of protesters killed at 551, including 49 women and 68 children, that 104 people were killed in a single day in Zahedan on 30 September 2022, and that the crackdown included crimes against humanity. [cite:ir9_un_ffm2024] [cite:ir9_aj_ffm2024] At least nine young men were executed in connection with the protests between December 2022 and January 2024 after proceedings the mission found arbitrary. [cite:ir9_aj_ffm2024] The government's coroner said on 7 October 2022 that Amini died of cerebral hypoxia linked to "underlying diseases," not blows; her family disputed this. Khamenei blamed the United States and Israel for the unrest, the government said 54 security personnel were killed, and Iran refused to cooperate with the UN mission. [cite:ir9_aj_coroner] [cite:ir9_un_ffm2024]

January 2026

Protests that began in late December 2025 over the collapse of the currency and living costs turned into calls for the end of clerical rule. The authorities deployed the police, the Basij and the IRGC and imposed severe internet and phone restrictions. On 16 January 2026, in a speech on state television, Khamenei acknowledged that "thousands" had been killed and blamed the United States. [cite:ir9_npr_jan2026] The government's only published toll, on 21 January, was 3,117. Iran Human Rights counted more than 3,400 by mid-January; the US-based Human Rights Activists News Agency (HRANA), which verifies each death through a network inside the country, reached at least 7,002 by 12 February, with many more cases unconfirmed. AP said it could not verify any figure because of the communications blackout. [cite:ir9_gn_jan2026] [cite:ir9_npr_jan2026] Higher estimates circulated in some media; they are not used here because their method could not be checked. The government said it would not carry out mass executions of detainees, and at the UN it accused the US and Israel of interfering in its internal affairs. [cite:ir9_npr_jan2026] By April 2026, Iran Human Rights reported, seven people linked to the January protests had been hanged and at least 26 more sentenced to death. [cite:ir9_almon_ihr2025] Six weeks after the crackdown, on 28 February 2026, the United States and Israel went to war with Iran, the subject of Part Eleven. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] [scene:ir11_war|The 2026 war]

The gallows and the minorities

Executions rose steeply after 2022. The UN recorded 834 in 2023. [cite:ir9_un_ffm2024] For 2024 Amnesty counted at least 972, 64 per cent of all known executions worldwide, and Iran Human Rights 975. [cite:ir9_iranintl_amnesty2024] [cite:ir9_almon_ihr2025] For 2025 Iran Human Rights and its French partner ECPM counted at least 1,639, the most since 1989, almost half for drug offences, with 48 women among them; Amnesty, using its own sources, counted 2,159, about four-fifths of all executions it recorded worldwide outside China. [cite:ir9_almon_ihr2025] [cite:ir9_newarab_amnesty2025] Official sources announce only a small share: in 2024, 95 of the executions Iran Human Rights recorded. The same organisation found ethnic minorities overrepresented; nine of the 31 people executed on security charges that year were Kurdish political prisoners. [cite:ir9_usip_minorities2024]

The Bahá'í faith is not among the religions the constitution recognises, which are Zoroastrianism, Judaism and Christianity. [cite:ir3_const_en] More than 200 Bahá'ís were killed or executed after 1979; a 1991 government memorandum cited by Bahá'í organisations set out a policy of preventing the community's development, and Bahá'ís have since been barred from universities and much public employment. UN experts said in July 2026 that about 57 Bahá'ís were in detention, some subjected to mock executions to extract confessions broadcast on state television. [cite:ir9_jurist_bahai2026] The government's position is that the faith is a banned sect: on 1 August 2022 the intelligence ministry announced arrests of what it called "the Bahai spy party," linked to "the Zionist centre," a reference to the faith's world centre in Haifa. [cite:ir9_almon_bahai2022] The treatment of Iran's recognised Jewish minority is examined in Part Three. [scene:ir3_facts|The Jews of Iran: the other fact]
