This book is about two kinds of people. The first are the dead of Gaza and the West Bank since October 2023: by the count of Gaza's health ministry, 74,382 people in Gaza alone by October 2026, with thousands more under the rubble and, by independent estimates, tens of thousands more who were never counted. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] [cite:pr_almon_survey2026] The second are the people who tried to count them and to tell the world: the journalists, most of them Palestinians from Gaza itself, who reported the war from inside it, because foreign reporters were not allowed in. By the count of the International Federation of Journalists, at least 241 journalists and media workers were killed in Gaza. [cite:gz_wiki_journalists] [cite:gz_wlrn_ban]

The book sets out the record of both. It begins with how the dead are counted and why the counts differ; then the children, the women and the unborn, and the hunger. It turns to the journalists: how many were killed and by whose count, who they were, what Israel said about each, and what investigations found; the gate that kept foreign reporters out; the press of the West Bank; the organizations that keep reporting with no power, no money and no protection; and what of all this reached American readers. Every figure carries its source and date. Where Israel disputes a count or says a journalist was a combatant, its position and the evidence it gave are set beside the record. The numbers in this book are the record the journalists were trying to deliver.
