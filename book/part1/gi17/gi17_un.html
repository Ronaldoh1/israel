The United Nations

This volume found documented programmes of territorial expansion for the West Bank and the Golan Heights, and the UN's record on those two territories is older than the present war. [scene:gi13_record|Part Eighteen: the record] The Security Council declared Israel's Golan law "null and void and without international legal effect" in December 1981, unanimously and with American support, and in December 2016 it found that the settlements have "no legal validity." [cite:gi8_wiki_res497] [cite:wb_wiki_2334] [scene:gi8_golan|Part Eight: the Golan] [scene:wb10_rulings|The West Bank, Documented: the rulings] What follows concerns the period since 7 October 2023, when Gaza dominated the UN's business. Most of these votes were about the war, not about borders; they are set out here because they are the bulk of what the world's main forum said and did, and because they show where its power to act stopped.

The Security Council and the veto

Between October 2023 and November 2024 the Security Council held fourteen votes on Gaza. Four resolutions passed, on 15 November 2023, 22 December 2023, 25 March 2024 and 10 June 2024; the last was an American draft. The United States vetoed drafts on 18 October 2023, 8 December 2023, 20 February 2024 and 20 November 2024, and on 18 April 2024 it vetoed a draft that would have admitted Palestine as a full UN member. Russia and China vetoed American drafts on 25 October 2023 and 22 March 2024. [cite:gz_aj_unsc] In June 2025 the United States vetoed a draft, backed by the other fourteen members, demanding an "immediate, unconditional and permanent" ceasefire and unrestricted aid. Washington cited Hamas's "failure to disarm" and said the text did not address Israel's security or the release of hostages. [cite:gi17_air_veto2025] On 18 September 2025 it was again the only member to vote against a ceasefire demand, 14 to 1. Its deputy envoy, Morgan Ortagus, said the draft did not condemn Hamas or recognise Israel's right to self-defence and "seeks to return to a failed system." AFP and JURIST each counted it as the sixth American veto of a Gaza ceasefire draft. [cite:ow11_afp_veto2025] [cite:gz_jurist_veto2025]

Counts differ with method. Al Jazeera's tally of November 2024 also lists 25 October 2023 among the American vetoes, the same date on which Russia and China vetoed an American draft; AFP's "sixth" leaves it out. [cite:gz_aj_unsc] [cite:ow11_afp_veto2025] The long series is larger: AFP counted 46 American vetoes on the Palestinian territories as of September 2025, and Al Jazeera, in 2021, at least 53 vetoes of resolutions critical of Israel since the first in 1972. Part Eleven of The One-Way Mirror explains the different counting rules. [cite:ow11_afp_veto2025] [cite:ow11_aj_vetoes2021] [scene:ow11_exceptions|The One-Way Mirror: the veto record]

After the October 2025 ceasefire the Council changed course. On 17 November 2025 it adopted Resolution 2803, an American text endorsing the Gaza plan Israel and Hamas had agreed in October. It welcomed a "Board of Peace" for reconstruction and authorised an International Stabilization Force. The vote was 13 to none, with China and Russia abstaining; Russia's ambassador said the text gave "complete control over the Gaza strip to the Board of Peace." Netanyahu welcomed it, saying "we believe that President Trump's plan will lead to peace and prosperity." The Palestinian Authority welcomed it; Hamas denounced it. [cite:gi17_wiki_res2803] No further American veto on Gaza appeared in the reporting checked for this part through October 2026. Part Nine of Gaza, Documented sets the vetoes beside the American plan. [scene:gz9_us|Gaza, Documented: the United States]

The General Assembly

The General Assembly, where no state has a veto and resolutions do not bind, voted by growing margins. On 18 September 2024, acting on the World Court's advisory opinion described in the next section, it demanded by 124 votes to 14, with 43 abstentions, that Israel end its presence in the occupied territory within twelve months. The United States called the text "inflammatory"; Israel's ambassador, Danny Danon, said it backed the Palestinian Authority's "diplomatic terrorism." [cite:wb_ahram_unga2024] In June 2025 the Assembly demanded an "immediate, unconditional and permanent ceasefire," the release of all hostages and the resumption of aid, by 149 to 12 with 19 abstentions; Israel and the United States voted against. [cite:gi17_un_unga2025] On 12 September 2025 it endorsed the "New York Declaration," a French and Saudi text on a two-state settlement, by 142 to 10 with 12 abstentions. The declaration condemned "the attacks committed by Hamas against civilians on the 7th of October" and demanded that Hamas free all hostages, end its rule in Gaza and hand its weapons to the Palestinian Authority. [cite:gi17_ewn_nydecl] Israel's foreign ministry said Israel "utterly rejects" it and called the Assembly "a political circus detached from reality"; the day before, Netanyahu had said: "We are going to fulfill our promise that there will be no Palestinian state." [cite:gi17_mm_nydecl] [cite:gi17_ewn_nydecl] The twelve-month deadline passed in September 2025 with record settlement approvals and the E1 plan advancing. [scene:gi6_votes|Part Six: the votes]

UNRWA

The UN's agency for Palestine refugees, created by the Assembly in 1949, became a target in its own right. [cite:gz_un_res302] In January 2024 Israel alleged that twelve UNRWA employees had taken part in the attack of 7 October; the United States paused its funding on 26 January 2024, other donors paused and some later resumed, and American law then barred UNRWA funding until March 2025. [cite:gz_crs_unrwa] The UN's internal oversight office examined nineteen staff members named by Israel and reported in August 2024 that nine may have been involved, that the evidence was insufficient for nine and that there was none for one; it said it could not independently authenticate most of the information Israel supplied. [cite:hm_un_oios] On 28 October 2024 the Knesset voted 92 to 10 for two laws that barred UNRWA from operating in Israel and Israeli officials from contact with it. [cite:gz_jp_knesset_unrwa] Israel's position was that the agency had been penetrated by Hamas; the World Court, in October 2025, found that Israel had not substantiated its allegations against UNRWA's staff. [cite:gz_ji_ao2025] [scene:gz8_aid|Gaza, Documented: aid]

The UN's own findings

On 16 September 2025 the Commission of Inquiry appointed by the UN Human Rights Council, chaired by the South African judge Navi Pillay, concluded that "Israel has committed genocide against Palestinians in the Gaza Strip," that four of the five acts defined in the 1948 Convention had been committed, and that President Isaac Herzog, Netanyahu and Gallant "have incited the commission of genocide." [cite:gz_un_coi2025] Israel's foreign ministry said it "categorically rejects this distorted and false report," which it said "relies entirely on Hamas falsehoods," and called for the commission to be abolished. [cite:gz_ctv_coi2025] The finding is that of investigators appointed by a UN body, not of a court; the genocide question before the World Court is undecided. [scene:gz7_courts|Gaza, Documented: the courts]

Lebanon: the end of UNIFIL

The UN peacekeeping force in south Lebanon is ending. On 28 August 2025 the Security Council unanimously adopted Resolution 2790, extending UNIFIL's mandate a final time to 31 December 2026, to be followed by a one-year "orderly and safe drawdown and withdrawal." The United States said Lebanese forces should take on more responsibility; France warned that premature withdrawal could weaken the Lebanese state. [cite:gi9_un_2790] [cite:hz8_scr_nov25] Part Nine describes the zone Israel held in south Lebanon as of October 2026, with Israel's stated security reasons beside it. [scene:gi9_lebanon|Part Nine: south Lebanon]

The pattern across these bodies is consistent. The Assembly and the UN's own investigators reached far-reaching positions by large majorities; the Council, the only body whose decisions bind, acted only when the United States agreed, and the one force the UN had on Israel's borders is being withdrawn. Israel rejected each critical finding as biased, and the United States opposed most of them, on the stated ground that they failed to condemn Hamas or to recognise Israel's right to defend itself.
