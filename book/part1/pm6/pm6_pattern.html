Two Kinds of Pressure, and What Is Not Documented

A question often asked is whether Marjorie Taylor Greene was the only member to leave government under attack from pro-Israel voices. The record used here answers it in two parts. On resignations, the answer is yes: in the period covered, Greene is the only member of Congress whose departure from office followed a public campaign against her by the pro-Israel network, and even in her case the president's opposition came first in her own account. On defeats, the answer is no: several members lost their seats after the largest campaigns the network has run. This section sets the cases side by side and states what they do and do not show.

Two kinds of pressure. The cases show two different instruments. The first is money: super PAC spending in a primary, the subject of Part Five, which removed Bowman and Bush in 2024 and contributed to Massie's defeat in 2026. The second is reputation: public statements by the network's organizations and allies that a member is "anti-Israel" or antisemitic, which in Omar's case were followed by formal action in the House and in Greene's preceded her resignation. The two instruments are often used together, and the second has a feature the first does not. An accusation of antisemitism can be made against any critic, and it can also be true. The record shows both: statements by Omar and by Greene that critics described as invoking antisemitic tropes, which in Omar's case she apologized for; and members such as Andy Levin, who is Jewish, and Thomas Massie, against whom no such accusation appears in the record used here, opposed for their positions. A reader assessing any single accusation has to look at the words it cites.

Convergence, not a single hand. In the two Republican cases, the pro-Israel network's opposition converged with the president's. Massie and Greene had both crossed Trump on the Epstein files and on the strikes on Iran, and both had opposed military aid to Israel. [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways] [cite:pm_reuters_trump_mtg] The record shows the two forces acting against the same members in the same months. It does not show that one directed the other. Greene herself implied that pro-Israel interests stood behind the president's turn against her, posting the figure for pro-Israel spending that had benefited him. [cite:pm_dcnf_mtg_aipac] That is her reading. The documents show the spending and the timing; they do not show coordination, and this volume does not supply it.

What is not documented. Three things should be stated plainly. The record used here contains no other member who resigned under comparable attack; if there are such cases, they are not in the sources this volume has reached, and later research may find them. The record does not show what members who were never targeted decided because they saw what happened to those who were; that effect, which critics regard as the network's most important, leaves no filing. And the record does not show that any member who took the money voted differently because of it. What it does show is a consistent pattern in who was opposed: members of both parties, of different ideologies, whose common feature was a break with the network's positions on Israel.
