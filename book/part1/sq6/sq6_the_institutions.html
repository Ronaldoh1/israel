The Institutions

"Jerusalem is Israel's eternal, undivided capital"

In July 1980 the Knesset passed a Basic Law declaring united Jerusalem, including East Jerusalem taken in 1967, the capital of Israel. On 20 August 1980 the Security Council adopted Resolution 478 by 14 votes to none, with the United States abstaining; it censured the law, declared measures changing the city's status "null and void" and called on states to withdraw their embassies. [cite:unsc478_text] [cite:wiki_unsc478] [cite:un_photo_478] After Costa Rica and El Salvador left in 2006, no embassy remained. The United States opened its embassy in Jerusalem on 14 May 2018; most countries keep theirs in Tel Aviv, and under the Oslo Accords the city's final status was left to negotiation. [cite:wiki_unsc478] Verdict: true in Israeli law; not recognised in international law or by most states.

"The UN is biased against Israel"

The case for: Item 7 of the UN Human Rights Council, on the occupied Palestinian and Arab territories, is the only country-specific item on its permanent agenda, and Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon criticised the decision to single Israel out. [cite:unwatch_item7] UN Watch, a pro-Israel monitor, counts 17 General Assembly resolutions on Israel in 2024 against 7 on all other countries combined. [cite:unwatch_unga2024] The case against: the United States has vetoed Security Council resolutions critical of Israel 45 times as of December 2023, slightly over half of all its vetoes since 1945, so the one UN body that can bind states has been largely blocked. [cite:ccga_vetoes] Many Assembly resolutions concern an occupation that began in 1967 and a conflict the UN has handled since 1947. Verdict: disputed; both sets of numbers are real.

"The only democracy in the Middle East"

In the V-Dem Institute's 2024 report Israel remained the only electoral democracy in the Middle East and North Africa, a region where 98 percent of people live under autocracy; Israel holds competitive elections, has a free press, and its Arab citizens vote and sit in the Knesset. [cite:toi_vdem] [cite:prio_democracy] The same report moved Israel down from "liberal" to "electoral" democracy for the first time in about fifty years, citing attacks on the judiciary and a decline in freedom from torture; Freedom House's score fell from 80 in 2017 to 73. The Israel Democracy Institute called the change "troubling, but not catastrophic". [cite:globes_vdem] [cite:idi_vdem] Both indexes measure Israel without the occupied territories, where Palestinians live under Israeli military control and cannot vote in Israel's elections. [cite:prio_democracy] Verdict: partly true; true inside Israel's borders, incomplete for the territory it controls.

"BDS is just a boycott" or "BDS is antisemitic"

On 9 July 2005 about 170 Palestinian unions and civil groups called for boycotts, divestment and sanctions until Israel ends its occupation of "Arab lands", grants full equality to its Arab citizens and respects refugees' right of return under Resolution 194. The movement says it opposes all racism, including antisemitism. [cite:bds_call] [cite:britannica_bds] Its critics argue that a full right of return would end Israel's Jewish majority and that "Arab lands" can be read to mean all of Israel. [cite:fdd_bds] [cite:wm_bds] From South Carolina in 2015 onward, 38 US states had adopted anti-BDS measures by early 2024, mostly barring state contracts with companies that boycott Israel; federal courts in four states found such laws likely unconstitutional before they were amended, and in February 2023 the Supreme Court declined to hear a challenge to Arkansas's law. [cite:pl_antiboycott] Verdict: both slogans are too simple; the demands and the dispute over them are documented.
