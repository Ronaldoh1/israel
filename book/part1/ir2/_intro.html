Part Two covers the decade in which Israel's ally became its declared enemy and, at the same time, one of its arms customers. It begins with the revolution of February 1979: how Israel's mission in Tehran was handed to the PLO, what Ayatollah Khomeini and the new constitution said about Israel and about exporting the revolution, and the declaration of Quds Day. It then follows Iraq's invasion of Iran in September 1980 and the Israeli tyres, spare parts and weapons that reached Iran during the war, with the competing estimates of their value, the contradictory statements of Ariel Sharon and other Israeli officials, the reasons Israeli officials gave, and the denials of Iran's leaders. It ends with the 1985 TOW and HAWK shipments that opened the Iran-Contra affair, itemised from the record of the American investigation, and with the question, still disputed, of whether Israel drew Washington in or the reverse.
