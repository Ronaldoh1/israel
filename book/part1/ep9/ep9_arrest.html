The Arrest, the Safe and the Death

Epstein was arrested on 6 July 2019 on federal sex-trafficking charges brought in the Southern District of New York, a different district from the one that had signed the 2008 agreement, which is why the agreement did not bar the case. Bail was denied. [cite:ep9_npr_brown2021] Searching his Manhattan house that weekend, agents found what prosecutors described as hundreds, perhaps thousands, of sexually suggestive photographs, some appearing to show underage girls. In a locked safe they found compact discs with handwritten labels such as "Young [name] + [name]" and "Misc nudes 1". [cite:mediaite_detention_memo] [cite:thewrap_epstein_indictment] At Maxwell's trial, the FBI agent who led the search testified that agents cut the safe open with a saw and that the warrant did not cover the hard drives and discs, so she left them on top of the safe. [cite:insider_fbi_safe_testimony] What happened to them next is part of the chain of custody that Part Eleven examines.

On 10 August 2019 Epstein was found dead in his cell at the Metropolitan Correctional Center. The New York City medical examiner ruled that he had hanged himself. [cite:ep9_nhpr_ig2023] The Justice Department's Inspector General, reporting in June 2023, found negligence and misconduct: staff missed more than 75 required checks overnight, he had been left without a cellmate despite an earlier apparent suicide attempt, cameras malfunctioned, and the two officers on duty were charged with falsifying records, a prosecution later deferred. The Inspector General found no evidence contradicting the FBI's conclusion that no crime caused the death. [cite:ep9_nhpr_ig2023] A pathologist hired by Epstein's brother has disputed the ruling; Part Eleven sets out that dispute in full, with the official finding first.

Whatever the cause, the consequence is documented. A trial would have produced sworn testimony under cross-examination and, possibly, cooperation naming others. His death ended that route. Everything since, the Maxwell prosecution, the bank cases and the releases, has had to rebuild in pieces what a trial would have put on the record in one place.

The one conviction

Ghislaine Maxwell was arrested in July 2020, convicted in December 2021 on five of six counts including sex trafficking of a minor, and sentenced to twenty years. [cite:ep8_abc_cocon_memo] The trial rested on four accusers describing conduct between roughly 1994 and 2004, a deliberately focused case. [cite:ep2_voa_maxwell2021] Her appeals included the argument that the 2008 agreement's open-ended immunity for "potential co-conspirators" should have protected her, more than a decade later and in another district. The courts rejected it, and in October 2025 the Supreme Court declined to hear her appeal. [cite:ep9_scotusblog_maxwell2025] [cite:ep8_abc_maxwell2026] She remains the only person convicted.
