The Qatari Money

From 2018 Israel let Qatar send cash into Hamas-run Gaza. In November 2018 the first $15 million arrived through Israel, the first of six monthly payments totalling $90 million, mainly for the salaries of officials of the Hamas government. Netanyahu called it "the right step" to restore quiet and prevent a humanitarian crisis; Naftali Bennett, then a minister, compared it to "protection money", and the opposition leader Tzipi Livni called it "submission to Hamas". [cite:gz_ds_qatar2018] Before May 2021 about $30 million in Qatari cash was carried into Gaza each month in suitcases through an Israeli-controlled crossing. After the war of May 2021 Israel stopped the cash, and a UN mechanism to pay families directly, with Israel approving the list, replaced it. [cite:gz_aj_qatar2021] The historian Adam Raz's account is that almost $30 million in cash entered every month from summer 2018 until October 2023; it does not match the 2021 record of the cash being stopped and replaced, and he gives no source for the figure. [cite:hm_972_raz]

Qatar's account is that the money was humanitarian and Israel controlled it. Majed al-Ansari, adviser to Qatar's prime minister, told Israel's public broadcaster in April 2024 that it moved under "complete Israeli oversight, complete Israeli vetting", "done for humanitarian reasons". [cite:hm_toi_ansari]

What Israeli Leaders Said

The strongest claim about the purpose comes from a remark Netanyahu is reported to have made to a Likud faction meeting in March 2019, and its wording matters. It was first reported by the Jerusalem Post's Lahav Harkov, from a Knesset member paraphrasing him: whoever is against a Palestinian state should support letting money into Gaza, because keeping the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank and Hamas in Gaza separate helps prevent a Palestinian state. Harkov has since written that the remark concerned money and aid into Gaza and has been "rewritten and twisted" online. [cite:gz_harkov_2019] Politico later reported it as: "Anyone who wants to prevent the establishment of a Palestinian state needs to support strengthening Hamas." [cite:gz_week_qatar] It is a paraphrase from a closed meeting, not a recording.

Other statements are on the record. Bezalel Smotrich, later finance minister, said in a 2015 interview: "The Palestinian Authority is a burden, and Hamas is an asset", reasoning that the Authority hurt Israel in international forums while Hamas never would. [cite:wb_wiki_smotrich] In December 2023 The New York Times, from interviews with more than two dozen officials, reported that Netanyahu "not only tolerated" the payments but "had encouraged them". [cite:gz_week_qatar] The Times of Israel's political correspondent wrote the day after October 7 that "most of the time, Israeli policy was to treat the Palestinian Authority as a burden and Hamas as an asset." [cite:hm_toi_schneider]

Netanyahu denies it. "It's a big lie that I wanted to build [up] Hamas. Ridiculous," he told Politico in November 2023; the money, he said, was to prevent a humanitarian collapse in Gaza. [cite:hm_ynet_biglie] Asked by Time in August 2024 about the 2019 remark, he said: "That's a false statement. I never said that," and that the policy was shared by governments before and after his: "It wasn't bankrolling Hamas." Time stood by the reporting and cited a remark by the former CIA analyst Chip Usher to The New York Times: "Anything that Hamas didn't have to use out of its own budget freed up money for other things." [cite:hm_toi_timefact] In September 2026 he told i24NEWS that the money had been for humanitarian aid, sent on the advice of the Mossad and the Shin Bet, and was "worth 2 percent – 2 percent of all the money that entered the Strip." [cite:hm_aj_i24_2026]

What the Shin Bet Found

In March 2025 the Shin Bet published a summary of its own inquiry into October 7. Among the factors that let Hamas build its forces it listed the "atmosphere of quiet" Israel had sought and "the flow of Qatari funds and their transfer to the military wing and its force build-up", alongside money from Iran. [cite:hm_fdd_shinbet] Israel's Channel 12 reported that an unpublished annex, titled "The Path to October 7", said the policy had continued despite repeated Shin Bet warnings to the prime minister; the annex has not been published. [cite:hm_toi_annex]
