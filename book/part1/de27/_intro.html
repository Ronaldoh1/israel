This part turns from the tools to the company that joins them. Palantir Technologies, co-founded in 2003 by Peter Thiel and run since 2004 by Alex Karp, sells software that lets governments and companies bring separate records together, match those that belong to the same person and search the result. Its early history with American intelligence is told in the library's earlier volume; this part asks who controls the company, what its software does and does not do, what its makers say it is for, how much money it now takes from the American state and from wars, and why several European governments and one constitutional court have refused it or set conditions on it. Throughout, it holds to one rule: what the software can do is documented by how it is built and used, and what anyone intends to do with it only by their own words or acts.
