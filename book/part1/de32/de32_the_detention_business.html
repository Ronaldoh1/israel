The Detention Business

When ICE arrests someone, the person is usually held in a building ICE does not run. About 90 percent of people in ICE detention in early 2025 were in facilities owned or operated by private contractors. [cite:de32_trac_private] That arrangement was not created in 2025; it held under both parties. What changed was the volume of money flowing through it, and the fact that the same company can now hold a person in a cell, track them on release and, in some cases, find them in the first place. This section follows the money from Congress to the two largest contractors, the GEO Group and CoreCivic, and to the people held.

The structure, under both parties

On 26 January 2021 President Biden signed Executive Order 14006, directing the Justice Department not to renew its contracts with private prisons. It covered the Bureau of Prisons and the US Marshals; it did not cover ICE, and neither the Obama nor the Biden administration directed the Department of Homeland Security to move away from private immigration detention. In 2023 ICE supplied 43 percent of GEO's revenue and 30 percent of CoreCivic's. [cite:de32_brennan_14006] On 20 January 2025 President Trump revoked the order in a single line, section 2(q) of Executive Order 14148, among dozens of rescissions. [cite:de26_ucsb_14148]

Contracts also guarantee payment for empty space. CoreCivic's annual report says some federal contracts "guarantee the federal partner access to a minimum bed capacity in exchange for a fixed monthly payment." [cite:de32_cxw_10k] The Government Accountability Office reported in February 2021 that such guaranteed-minimum contracts rose from 29 in fiscal 2017 to 43 in fiscal 2019, and that in May 2020 ICE was paying for an average of 12,027 empty beds a day, $20.5 million for the month. Those years span the first Trump administration and the start of the pandemic. [cite:de32_pln_gao]

New beds and new revenue

GEO's filings show the expansion. In February 2025 it announced a 15-year fixed-price ICE contract for Delaney Hall, a 1,000-bed site in Newark, worth more than $60 million a year and about $1 billion over the term. [cite:de32_geo_delaney] By the end of 2025 it had added about 6,000 ICE beds, taking its capacity from about 20,000 to about 26,000, and called 2025 "the most successful year for new business wins in our Company's history." It also won a two-year ICE contract for "skip tracing," the work of locating people. [cite:de32_geo_fy25] In July and August 2026 it opened two more sites under five-year contracts, Big Horn in Colorado (1,188 beds, about $85 million a year) and Rivers in North Carolina (1,320 beds, about $80 million a year), with ICE reimbursing start-up costs. [cite:de32_geo_q226]

CoreCivic reopened or acquired sites at Dilley and Farmville, California City, West Tennessee and Diamondback in Oklahoma. [cite:de32_cxw_fy25] Its ICE revenue rose from $564.8 million in 2024, 29 percent of the total, to $770.7 million in 2025, 35 percent; it lists 16 facilities where ICE is the main customer. [cite:de32_cxw_10k] Its net income rose 69 percent to $116.5 million, and it spent $218.4 million buying back its own shares. [cite:de32_cxw_fy25] GEO reported 2025 revenue of $2.63 billion and net income of $254.3 million, but that profit includes a $232.4 million gain from selling a prison to the state of Oklahoma, so it is not a measure of what detention earned. [cite:de32_geo_fy25] In the second quarter of 2026 GEO's revenue rose 15 percent to $732.1 million, and it raised its forecast for the year to $2.95–3.05 billion. [cite:de32_geo_q226]

On 6 November 2024, the day after the election, GEO's shares rose 42 percent and CoreCivic's 29 percent in a single session. [cite:de32_fortune_stocks] GEO's executive chairman, George Zoley, spoke of "an expected sea change" and said "The GEO Group was built for this unique moment." [cite:de32_fb_geo]

The political money

Each company gave $500,000 to the Trump-Vance inaugural committee; CoreCivic's gift is dated 19 December 2024 in its lobbying disclosure. CoreCivic's chief executive gave $300,000 to the Trump joint fundraising committee, and a GEO subsidiary gave $1 million to the main pro-Trump super PAC, MAGA Inc., in 2024. [cite:de32_sludge_gifts] [cite:de32_fb_geo] In the 2026 cycle another GEO subsidiary gave $4.4 million to MAGA Inc. and $1.3 million to the Congressional Leadership Fund, the House Republican super PAC; the companies' political action committees gave the maximum to the Republican chairman of the House Homeland Security Committee, and Scripps found the giving "overwhelmingly" Republican. CoreCivic said its political activity informs policymakers about its services. [cite:de32_scripps_pac] The Attorney General, Pam Bondi, had been a registered lobbyist for GEO before taking office; that is her record, not the company's conduct. [cite:de32_fb_geo]

The gifts came about a month before the revocation of the 2021 order, and the contracts followed. That is a documented sequence, not a documented cause: no record shows a contract or a policy given in exchange for a donation. CoreCivic states that it does "not, under longstanding policy, lobby for or against policies or legislation" that determine the basis or length of anyone's detention; giving to a party and bidding for contracts falls outside that statement rather than contradicting it. [cite:de32_cxw_10k]

The jailer runs the tracker

People released from detention are often placed in ICE's Intensive Supervision Appearance Program (ISAP), run by GEO's subsidiary BI Incorporated, which won a new two-year contract on 30 September 2025. [cite:de32_geo_fy25] On its August 2026 earnings call GEO reported about 184,000 people in the programme and nearly 54,000 on GPS ankle monitors, up from about 17,000 a year earlier, as use of its smartphone check-in app, SmartLINK, fell. An ankle monitor earns GEO more than $2 per person per day, against about $0.96 for the app. [cite:de32_lt_isap] Records obtained under the Freedom of Information Act show that BI collects facial images and voice prints, that ICE's contract gives the agency "unlimited rights to use, dispose of, or disclose" the data, and that location data shared by BI contributed to 40 arrests in Virginia in 2018. [cite:de32_cs_smartlink]

Work for a dollar a day

Detainees who join the "voluntary work program," cooking, cleaning and doing laundry, are paid "at least $1 per day," a rate set when Congress funded detainee pay under a 1950 law; in 1982 the immigration service asked for $4 and Congress did not act. [cite:de32_pogo_dollar] In 2021 a federal jury in Washington State found that GEO owed more than 10,000 people held at its Tacoma centre $17.3 million in back pay under the state minimum wage, and a judge added $5.9 million for unjust enrichment; the Ninth Circuit affirmed on 16 January 2025, calling GEO "a private for-profit employer." [cite:de32_wa_nwauzor] GEO petitioned the Supreme Court (No. 25-828); the Court asked for the government's views on 18 May 2026, and the Solicitor General filed on 15 September; according to the detainees' supplemental brief, the government urged review and argued the ruling "threatens to upend" ICE's reliance on private contractors. The petition was set for the Court's conference of 6 November 2026. [cite:de32_scotus_25828] [cite:de32_nwauzor_supp] In a separate Colorado case, where detainees allege they were made to clean without pay on pain of up to 72 hours in solitary confinement, the Supreme Court ruled against GEO on 25 February 2026 that it could not take an early appeal on a claim of contractor immunity, sending the case back toward trial. [cite:de32_lii_menocal]

The state-run camps and the inspectors

Florida opened a detention camp in the Everglades, called Alligator Alcatraz, on 1 July 2025, run by its emergency-management division through emergency-standby vendors; it cost $390.2 million by October 2025. [cite:de32_fp_alligator] Inspecting it in January 2026, the DHS Inspector General found less than half the required living space, cages of about 18 square feet used to hold people for up to two hours, once as punishment, and legal phones that did not work; staff did not know which standards applied, and ICE said Florida ran the site under a 287(g) agreement. [cite:de32_aic_oig2622] On 21 April 2026 the Eleventh Circuit lifted an order to wind it down, holding 2–1 that "until Homeland Security officials decide to fund the facility, no final agency action occurs." [cite:de32_wflx_11th] At GEO's Adelanto centre in California, four people died between August 2025 and August 2026, the most at any facility, while the share of ICE facilities given "superior" grades by ICE's own inspectors tripled. [cite:de32_laist_pogo]

If it were connected

Detention joined to monitoring in one company: documented, by GEO's own filings. Monitoring data joined to arrests: documented, in 2018. GEO joined to locating people for ICE: documented, by the skip-tracing contract. [cite:de32_geo_fy25] [cite:de32_cs_smartlink] BI's location data joined to the Palantir platforms ICE uses for targeting: possible; the contract's "unlimited rights" clause would allow it, and no document shows it. The question of compelled labour on a wider scale belongs to [scene:de35_the_consequence_ladder|Part Thirty-Seven]; on the record here, the dollar-a-day rate rests on a law and an appropriation that long predate 2025, not on any new order. [cite:de32_pogo_dollar]
