The Money and the Wars: Revenue, Contracts and Ukraine

Palantir's argument about itself is that it serves the West; its accounts show how much of its money comes from the American state, and how fast that has grown. This section gives the figures, then the contracts behind them, listed one by one rather than added together, because ceilings, obligations and payments are different things and different awards run over different years. Figures are as of the latest reported quarter at 28 October 2026, the second quarter of 2026; third-quarter results were due in early November.

The revenue

When Palantir filed to list its shares in 2020, it reported 2019 revenue of $746 million, nearly half from government agencies, and a loss of $580 million. [cite:ow9_ap_s1] For 2025 it reported revenue of $4.475 billion, of which United States government revenue was $1.855 billion, up 55 percent on the year. [cite:de10_pltr_fy25] In the second quarter of 2026, reported on 3 August 2026, revenue was $1.935 billion, up 93 percent on a year earlier. United States government revenue was $809 million, up 90 percent, and United States commercial revenue $764 million, up 149 percent; net income attributable to shareholders was about $1.06 billion for the quarter. The company raised its forecast for the year to between $8.150 and $8.158 billion. [cite:de27_8k_q2_2026] No single customer accounted for more than a tenth of revenue. [cite:de27_10q_q2_2026]

Two things follow. The American government is Palantir's largest source of money, at about 42 percent of revenue in the latest quarter, and its spending with the company nearly doubled in a year. And the commercial business, selling the same platforms and the AI layer to companies, has grown even faster, so that the company is less dependent on government as a share of its income even as government pays it more. [cite:de27_8k_q2_2026] The Brennan Center, counting the Department of Homeland Security's commitments alone, found them rising from $20.4 million in 2024 to $162.2 million in 2026 by late July. [cite:de27_brennan_tools]

The defence contracts

Palantir did not enter the Army as a favourite. In 2016 it sued the Army for refusing to consider commercial software for its intelligence-analysis system, won, and in 2019 won a contract worth up to $876 million; that story is told in [scene:de10_philosophy_vs_practice|Part Ten]. [cite:de10_defnews] The scale changed after 2024. The Pentagon's Maven Smart System, which fuses sensor and intelligence data into a picture commanders can act on, was awarded at $480 million in May 2024 and raised to nearly $1.3 billion through 2029 in May 2025. [cite:ow9_ds_maven] NATO completed its own purchase of the system on 25 March 2025, at an undisclosed price, for intelligence fusion, targeting and planning. [cite:de27_dp_nato] In August 2025 the Army folded 75 contracts into a single enterprise agreement worth up to $10 billion over ten years. [cite:ow9_bd_army] Under that agreement, on 16 March 2026, the Army ordered $3.68 million of work to "maintain and sustain" its Personnel Data Environment, a system holding soldiers' personnel data, through 15 November 2026; the order sustains a system that already existed. [cite:de27_govly_pde] In August 2026 Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg pledged up to $243.9 million for Palantir software through March 2027 and told departments to identify options for more through December 2028; the analytics firm GovTribe counted $3.2 billion of obligations to Palantir since 2024, about half without competition, and the department said it had issued similar memos for other companies. [cite:de27_wt_feinberg]

A widely repeated claim puts Palantir in the Golden Dome missile-defence programme. When the Space Force announced prototype agreements for its space-based interceptors on 24 April 2026, the twelve companies named included SpaceX and Anduril; Palantir was not among them. [cite:de27_bd_sbi]

The civilian and enforcement contracts

The company's civilian federal work has widened in the same period. On 22 September 2025 the Treasury announced that Palantir would build a "common API layer," a shared set of connections for developer platforms, workflow automation and analytics, as part of modernising the Internal Revenue Service; no value was stated. [cite:de27_treasury_api] At Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the work that began in 2014 under the Obama administration grew into the $29.9 million ImmigrationOS award of April 2025 and a Department of Homeland Security purchase agreement worth up to $1 billion in February 2026. [cite:ow9_reg_immos] [cite:ow9_sa_dhsbpa] Under that agreement, on 5 May 2026, ICE ordered $86.3 million of work, fully obligated, for the "modernization of enforcement removal operations and operational capability" through May 2027; its statement of work is not public, so the data it covers is not known. [cite:de27_hg_ero] The ELITE tool, reported in January 2026, is Palantir's. [cite:ow9_fortune_elite] [cite:de27_brennan_tools] The full ICE list, with the other vendors, is in [scene:de28_ice_contracts|Part Twenty-Eight]. Abroad, England's National Health Service awarded Palantir a £330 million, seven-year data-platform contract in 2023, now under review, as [scene:de27_europe_says_no|the next section] describes. [cite:de27_reuters_nhs]

Ukraine

Palantir's largest documented war use outside American command is in Ukraine. On 1 June 2022 Karp crossed into Ukraine with five colleagues and met President Volodymyr Zelensky in Kyiv the next day, offering to open an office and deploy the company's software. By early 2024, TIME reported, more than half a dozen Ukrainian agencies used it: the military to analyse satellite imagery, drone footage, open-source data and ground reports, and the prosecutor general to link alleged war crimes to evidence such as satellite images and social-media posts, while the economy ministry used its models to decide where clearing landmines would return the most farmland to use. Karp said the software was "responsible for most of the targeting in Ukraine." Palantir provided the tools free of charge; partner countries contributed tens of millions of dollars to offset the cost. [cite:de27_time_ukraine] Palantir's work for Israel's Ministry of Defense since January 2024 is set out in [scene:ow9_israel|The One-Way Mirror] and in [scene:de37_palantir_and_israel|Part Thirty-Seven]; the documented record there is a contract whose contents are not public, and the company denies building Israel's AI targeting systems.

What the figures do not show is a causal line from politics to contracts. Thiel chairs the company, J.D. Vance once worked for and was funded by Thiel, and Palantir's federal business has grown fast since 2025; those facts form a sequence, examined as a candidate loop in [scene:de14_loops|Part Fourteen] and [scene:de21_conflicts_and_contracts|Part Twenty-One]. No document shows the Vice President or any political appointee directing an award to Palantir, and the ICE business, the Army lawsuit and the Maven award all began before the 2025 administration.
