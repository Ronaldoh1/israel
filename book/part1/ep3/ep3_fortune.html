The gap

That list contains two confirmed clients and half a billion dollars. No audited list of other clients has ever been produced. There is no registration with securities regulators consistent with a man managing billions for others, and no accounting for the years between 1981 and 1987. [cite:legalclarity] The fortune is real; its full source is not documented. It is one of the central questions the withheld files could answer.

The book has to state the plainest explanation as strongly as the dramatic ones, because it fits the documents. A man with a rare skill in structuring the taxes of the very rich, and with the trust of two exceptionally rich clients, could earn enormous fees for advice nobody else was allowed to see, and the record of Black's payments, discussed next, shows exactly that kind of arrangement. Add the money Wexner says was misappropriated, the offshore tax breaks and the investment returns, and the estate does not need a secret sponsor to add up. That explanation leaves open why these men paid so much, and why they paid so little attention. It does not require an intelligence agency, and the book does not supply one.
