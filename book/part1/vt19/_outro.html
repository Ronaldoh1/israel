Part Nineteen has scored the front hypothesis against the whole record and found that none of the five tests favours it. The private record published so far, from Herzl's diary of 1904 to the Holy See's letters of 1943, matches the public refusal or is harder than it; no transfer of money or favours from the Holy See to the Zionist movement or to Israel was found; the Holy See's shifts followed its own stated interests and the Palestinian track; its treaty with Israel conceded nothing on Jerusalem or the territories; and its opposition cost Israel recognition for forty-five years and drew complaints from its leaders, though it rarely changed outcomes.
