What Followed, Tested on the Map

The network map carries these chains in red: 52 later events, bodies and documents, joined by 82 ties, each taken from the later volumes with its source. A tie that says one thing led to another counts as a cause. A few causes are treated as necessary, because the later thing cannot exist without them by definition: the parties to a war, the territory of an occupation, the state an aid programme pays. Remove a cause and a later event that loses every documented cause is counted as lost; one that keeps another cause is weakened. This is a reading of the record's own chains, not a chance.

Removing the declaration takes away almost everything that came after it: the refugee question, the occupation and its wars, the peace plans, the aid and the vetoes, the war in Gaza and the cases in court. What survives does so because the record gives it a root of its own: the Iranian revolution, the Islamist movement, the Shia movement in Lebanon. That is the case's seventh link and its limit together: the wars and the accords follow from the founding, but not everything in the region does.
