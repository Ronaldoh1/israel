At the end of 1917 the project announced at Basel twenty years earlier had institutions but no sovereignty, land but no title to the country, and, in its last weeks, a British promise. Each layer of that architecture was laid by different hands, and each was contested.

The legal layer was still Ottoman. Until British forces occupied Palestine in December 1917 and the Mutasarrifate of Jerusalem was dissolved, the country's law was the empire's. [cite:rs_rd1_jerusalemstory] Under it the movement had won none of what it set out to win in 1897. The Basel Program asked for a home "secured under public law"; Herzl's search for a charter met the Sultan's refusal, the Kaiser's fading interest, Cromer's veto of El-Arish and Pius X's "we could never sanction it." [cite:rs_rd1_basel_wiki] [cite:w_herzl] [cite:wiki_kaiser_levant_1898] [cite:unu_water_elarish] [cite:jcpa_vatican_israel_recognition] What it had instead was private law. Land was registered under the Land Code of 1858 and could be bought from whoever held the title, often an absentee owner, while the tenants who worked miri land held no registered title and had no claim when it was sold. [cite:w_ottoman_land_code] [cite:badil_land_ownership_palestine] The Ottoman restrictions of 1881–83 and 1892 slowed purchase without stopping it: they were evaded through Ottoman Jewish buyers, consular protection and American naturalization, and Jewish-held land reached about 418,000 dunams, around 2 percent of the territory, in 1918. [cite:israelfactcheck_ottoman_land_laws] [cite:state_gov_1892_naturalization] The one commitment in public law came at the very end: a letter of November 1917 in which Britain backed "the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people," issued before Ottoman rule in Palestine had ended. [cite:balfour_letter]

The administrative layer belonged to others. The Zionist Organization held no governing authority in Palestine. [cite:palquest_wzo] It built an administration for settlement alone: the Palestine Office in Jaffa from 1908, coordinating purchase with the Jewish National Fund, aiding immigration and placing settlers, and the Hashomer guards, who made the hiring of Jewish workers a condition of their service. [cite:wiki_palestine_office] [cite:rs_rd1_jvl_defense] Real administrative power lay with Ottoman officials, who could resist but not always prevail. Shukri al-Asali opposed the al-Fula sale of 1910 as governor of Nazareth, and permission came through Hankin's friends in Constantinople. [cite:rs_rd1_ruppin_emek] [cite:rs_rd1_asali_wiki] In wartime that power fell on Palestine's people directly: the expulsion of up to 6,000 Russian-citizen Jews from Jaffa in December 1914, the Ottomanization order of 1915, and the deportation of 6 April 1917, after which Jews were barred from returning until the summer of 1918. [cite:rs_rd1_jaffa_wiki] [cite:rs_rd1_yishuv_wwi]

The institutional and financial layer was the movement's own, and its shape lasted. A Congress elected under the 1899 constitution sat above a seven-member executive and a 37-member council. [cite:palquest_wzo] [cite:ismi_israel_yearbook] Its money came from many small holders and was controlled by a few. The Jewish Colonial Trust raised about £250,000 against a target of £2,000,000, while its first 100 founders' shares carried votes equal to all the rest combined. [cite:jct_wiki] [cite:jewishencyclopedia_colonial_trust] Its Anglo-Palestine Company lent in Jaffa from 1903 and financed Tel Aviv's first neighborhood; the Jewish National Fund held what it bought permanently, leased and never sold; and the Palestine Land Development Company had handled about 50,000 dunams by the war. [cite:apc_wiki] [cite:jnf_wiki] [cite:encyclopedia_otto_warburg] Shareholders and blue-box households paid; settlers on leased land gained; the cost fell on tenants without title, most of it, as later volumes show, after 1917. [cite:wiki_sursock_purchases] Jewish opinion did not stand behind this structure as one. The Bund, Reform rabbis, Agudat Israel and the Antizionistisches Komitee opposed it, and in May 1917 the Board of Deputies and the Anglo-Jewish Association declared against it in The Times. [cite:wiki_bund] [cite:rs_rd1_grill_currents] [cite:jpost_why_rothschild]

The Palestinian Arab layer was a public voice without an institution of its own. Yusuf Diya al-Khalidi set out its case in 1899: the land was Ottoman and inhabited, and should be left alone. [cite:rs_rd1_khalidi_wiki] From 1905 it had a voice in the press: Najib Nassar wrote against Zionism in Cairo and Beirut, then founded al-Karmil in Haifa in 1908, and Filastin appeared in Jaffa from 1911, sent to every village of the Jaffa district, and in 1911 the Jerusalem deputies raised Zionism in the Ottoman parliament, opposing immigration rather than Palestine's own Jews. [cite:rs_rd1_palquest_nassar] [cite:wiki_falastin] [cite:rs_rd1_ruhi_wiki] Nassar's call for a non-Zionist society in Nablus in 1913 suggests that no such body yet existed. [cite:rs_rd1_palquest_nassar] Its strongest case was consent: no documented negotiation sought the agreement of the Arab leadership before organized purchase began. The Zionist case at its strongest was legality and benefit: purchases lawful under Ottoman law, sellers who in Ruppin's account wanted cash, and Herzl's argument that settlement would increase the wealth of the people already there. [cite:rs_rd1_ruppin_emek] [cite:rs_rd1_khalidi_intercept]

The regional layer was an empire in which Arab reformers sought change before the war and Arab nationalists met repression during it. The Arab Congress in Paris in 1913 sought reform within the empire and said nothing about Zionism. [cite:rs_rd1_arabcongress_wiki] Then came famine in Greater Syria and the hanging of Arab nationalists in Damascus, al-Asali among them. [cite:rs_rd1_aub_famine] [cite:rs_rd1_asali_wiki] Britain's other wartime commitments in the region belong to [scene:s06|the next stage].

The American layer was built before the promise it later served. The Federation of American Zionists dated from 1898; Hadassah from 1912; Brandeis took up leadership in 1914 and worked the channel to President Wilson; and in 1918 the bodies merged into a Zionist Organization of America of 147,551 members. [cite:w_zf_gb] [cite:hadassah_wiki] [cite:brandeis_zionism_wiki] [cite:zoa_wiki] A separate Christian constituency had its text in the Scofield Reference Bible of 1909. [cite:wiki_scofield_bible] America was above all where Jewish emigrants went: about 2 million of the more than 2.5 million who left Eastern Europe between 1881 and 1914. [cite:rs_rd1_ettinger] The claim that the Federal Reserve of 1913 was tied to Zionist finance finds no support. [cite:federalreservehistory_fed_act]

What was settled by 1917 was the choice of territory, made by Congress vote in 1905, and the machinery to buy and hold land in it. What was contested was nearly everything else: whether lawful purchase from absentee owners could stand in for consent, whether Herzl's 1895 diary entry foreshadowed transfer, whether the Second Aliyah's institutions grew from ideals or from the contest over land and work, and whether services to Britain shaped the Declaration. The record leaves open even the baseline: the Jewish population of 1914 ranges from 38,754 to 94,000 depending on who was counted, and how far it fell in the war is disputed. [cite:rs_rd1_mccarthy_owen] [cite:rs_rd1_demog_wiki] The documents show that the structure existed before 1917. They do not show what it would become.
