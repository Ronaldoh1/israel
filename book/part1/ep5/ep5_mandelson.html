Peter Mandelson

At the time of contact: Labour MP and minister, then European trade commissioner, then Business Secretary in Gordon Brown's cabinet. Later: co-founder of the lobbying firm Global Counsel and, in 2025, British ambassador to the United States. Today: no office; under police investigation, not charged.

Before Epstein

Peter Mandelson was one of the architects of "New Labour", the party's strategist and communications chief before he became a minister, and the most controversial figure in Tony Blair's government. Twice before he met Epstein's world, he left office over his dealings with rich men. In 1998 he resigned as Trade and Industry Secretary after it emerged that he had taken an undeclared loan of £373,000 from a fellow Labour figure, Geoffrey Robinson, to buy a house in London; "I should have been open about it," he said. In 2001 he resigned as Northern Ireland Secretary over his contact with the Home Office on behalf of the billionaire Srichand Hinduja, who was seeking British citizenship and whose family had given £1 million to the Millennium Dome that Mandelson oversaw; there was no suggestion he benefited personally. [cite:ep5_irishtimes_mandelson2025]

He returned as European trade commissioner from 2004 to 2008. In 2005 he and his friend Nathaniel Rothschild travelled to Siberia and met the Russian billionaire Oleg Deripaska. In 2008 Gordon Brown made him a peer and brought him back into the cabinet as Business Secretary. After Labour lost power in 2010 he co-founded Global Counsel, a consultancy whose clients over the years included Palantir, which hired it in 2018. [cite:ep5_irishtimes_mandelson2025] [cite:bylinetimes_mandelson_russia_palantir] In 2025 Keir Starmer sent him to Washington as ambassador.

The record with Epstein

The friendship is documented from 2002 to 2011. A 2019 internal JPMorgan report, filed in court in New York, said Epstein appeared to maintain "a particularly close relationship" with him, and called him by a nickname, "Petie". [cite:bylinetimes_mandelson_russia_palantir] In Epstein's fiftieth-birthday album, assembled in 2003, Mandelson wrote that Epstein "remains my best pal!" [cite:ep5_irishtimes_mandelson2025]

The released files add money and government business. Epstein's bank records, as reported by the Associated Press, show three payments totalling $75,000 in 2003 and 2004 to accounts linked to Mandelson or to his partner, Reinaldo Avila da Silva; Mandelson says he does not remember receiving the money and will check whether the documents are authentic. In 2009 Epstein sent da Silva £10,000 for an osteopathy course; Mandelson called accepting it "a lapse in our collective judgment". [cite:ep5_ap_mandelson_leaks]

The rest concerns his time as a minister, after Epstein's conviction. In 2009, as Business Secretary, Mandelson apparently told Epstein he would lobby colleagues to cut the tax on bankers' bonuses. He sent Epstein an internal government paper on raising money after the financial crisis, including by selling state assets, with the note "Interesting note that's gone to the PM". In 2010 he messaged Epstein that "sources tell me 500 b euro bailout" was nearly complete, on the day European governments announced the €500 billion deal. [cite:ep5_ap_mandelson_leaks] Those messages are the subject of the police investigation.

Consequences

He was removed as ambassador in September 2025 after the birthday-album entry and other revelations about the friendship. [cite:ep5_irishtimes_mandelson2025] On 1 February 2026, after the Justice Department's release, he resigned from the Labour Party, saying he did not want to cause "further embarrassment", and the Prime Minister ordered an urgent civil-service review of his contacts with Epstein while in government. [cite:ep5_ap_mandelson_leaks] On 23 February the Metropolitan Police arrested him on suspicion of misconduct in public office; on 6 March he was released from bail and remains under investigation. He has not been charged. [cite:ep5_cnn_mandelson_arrest] [cite:ep5_itv_mandelson_bail] [cite:ep4_aj_arrests2026]

No survivor has accused Peter Mandelson of abuse. The investigation concerns his conduct in public office.

Two scandals, not one

Much coverage has merged two separate matters: his firm's work for Palantir, and the Epstein friendship that cost him the embassy. They are different matters with different documents. The Palantir relationship is a question about lobbying and conflicts of interest; the Epstein relationship is a question about a minister's handling of government information and money from a convicted sex offender. Nothing in the record connects Epstein to the Palantir contract, and the book keeps them apart.

What remains unestablished: whether the information he passed moved any market or benefited Epstein; what the 2003–04 payments were for, and whether the bank records are authentic, as he has questioned; what the civil-service review found; and whether he will be charged.
