Part Twelve established that Israel's research effort rose from about the American level in 1996, 2.59 against 2.45 per cent of GDP, to almost twice it, while its tech sector grew to about 17 per cent of output and 57 per cent of exports but stopped adding jobs after 2023. [cite:us12_im_rd_isr] [cite:us12_wiki_rd] [cite:us12_iia_2025] Venture money in both countries fell from its 2021 peak and recovered in 2025, in America on the back of a few very large AI deals. [cite:us12_jn_ivc2025] [cite:us12_nvca_q4_2025] American firms are among Israel's largest employers and exporters, and Israel's government paid Intel the largest grant it has given any company, $3.2 billion, while Intel's Israeli workforce fell from a 2021 peak of about 12,000 to about 9,350 in mid-2025. [cite:us12_globes_intel_grant] [cite:us12_calcalist_intel2025] Israel's announced defence exports rose to a record $19.2 billion in 2025, its share of world arms exports from 3.1 to 4.4 per cent, while America's share rose to 42 per cent; 68 per cent of Israel's arms imports came from the United States, much of it paid for with American grants spent with American firms.
