The Thiel Years

Between 2015 and 2021 Vance's working life ran through venture capital, the business of investing other people's money in young companies in exchange for a share of them, and at every stage Peter Thiel was nearby: first as his employer, then as an investor in his fund, then as the man who introduced him to Donald Trump. This section sets out each tie by its type, its date and its source, because the relationship is often described in a single word ("protégé", "creation", "puppet") that the record does not support in any of its versions. What the record does show is a sequence of five distinct, documented connections, each of a different kind, and a sixth that is widely repeated but rests on one source.

Mithril, 2015 or 2016 to 2017

Vance moved to San Francisco to work at Mithril Capital, a venture firm Thiel co-founded and named after a fictional metal in J.R.R. Tolkien's novels. [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] The dates and title differ by source. Politico, as reported by Forbes, said he joined as a partner in 2015; Fortune, citing the Wall Street Journal, said he began in 2016 and left in the spring of 2017; Wikipedia's account gives "principal" from 2016 to 2017. [cite:de12_forbesau] [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] [cite:de12_wiki_vance] No primary record of his start date has been published, and both versions are recorded here.

What he did there is also contested. Two former Mithril employees told the Journal that they rarely or never saw him in the office, that he spent much of his time promoting Hillbilly Elegy, and that neither could recall a significant deal he had worked on. [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] Crystal McKellar, a former managing director of the firm, wrote publicly in July 2024 that he had been "a great VC," quick to learn new industries and with "great instincts about people." [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] [cite:de12_axios_vc] Neither account is a document; both are recorded. The employment itself is documented, and it is the first financial tie between the two men: an employer and an employee.

It was in these years, according to Ava Kofman's 2025 profile of Curtis Yarvin, that Vance first met Yarvin, in 2015, through Thiel. [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] The meeting is reported by one writer, not documented by a record, and no account of what was discussed has been published. It is the earliest reported contact between Vance and the author whose texts are at the centre of this book, and it is a social introduction, not a working or financial relationship.

Revolution and Ohio, 2017–2019

In the spring of 2017 Vance left Mithril for Revolution, the Washington investment firm founded by Steve Case, the co-founder of AOL, whose "Rise of the Rest" programme toured and invested in regions that venture capital usually ignored. [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] [cite:de12_axios_vc] One Journal source credited him with being "lead partner or co-lead on 14 deals" there, including start-ups in defence and artificial intelligence. [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] His one board seat was at AppHarvest, a Kentucky indoor-farming company in which he invested; he stepped down from the board to run for the Senate, and the company filed for bankruptcy in 2023, after he had left. [cite:de12_axios_vc] [cite:de12_fortune_mithril]

He also moved back to Ohio and founded a charity. Our Ohio Renewal, set up as a 501(c)(4) non-profit not long after the memoir appeared in 2016, was to address jobs, mental health and the opioid crisis across the industrial Midwest, with a stated goal of raising $500,000 a year; a tax-deductible foundation followed in 2017. [cite:de12_ap_ohiorenewal] The Associated Press found that it raised $221,000 in 2018, $80,000 of it Vance's own money, and less than $50,000 a year after that, before closing in 2021; its only notable work was paying for an addiction specialist, Dr Sally Satel, to practise for a year in Ironton, Ohio. [cite:de12_ap_ohiorenewal] The gap between the promise and the result is graded in [scene:de12_claims_vs_record|the last section of this part].

In August 2019 he was baptised and confirmed in the Catholic Church, a conversion he described in the same 2020 essay in which he credited Thiel's faith with unsettling his assumptions. [cite:de12_wiki_vance] [cite:de12_forbesau]

Narya, 2019–2020

In 2019 Vance co-founded Narya Capital in Cincinnati with Colin Greenspon, a former managing director at Mithril and former partner at the Rise of the Rest seed fund. [cite:de12_axios_vc] [cite:de12_techcrunch_narya] On 9 January 2020 TechCrunch reported, from a federal securities filing first flagged by Axios, that the fund had $93 million in commitments toward a target of $125 million, and named four of its investors: Thiel, the venture capitalist Marc Andreessen, the former Google chief executive Eric Schmidt and the software entrepreneur Scott Dorsey. [cite:de12_techcrunch_narya] That makes Thiel a limited partner, an outside investor, in a fund Vance ran: the second financial tie, of a different type from the first. Vance's own financial disclosures as Vice President show that he still holds interests in Narya's first fund and a note owed to him by its management company; [scene:de12_money_and_influence|the fifth section] sets those out by the ranges he filed.

The turn to politics, 2021

In February 2021, according to the New York Times, Thiel brought Vance to Mar-a-Lago for his first meeting with Trump, the man Vance had called "reprehensible" five years earlier. [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de12_axios_trump2016] It was the third kind of tie, an introduction, and the most consequential, because Trump's endorsement fourteen months later would transform the Senate primary. The following month Thiel put $10 million into the super PAC that would carry Vance's campaign; that money, and the race, are the subject of [scene:de12_senate_and_the_ticket|the next section] and of [scene:de15_vance_and_masters|Part Fifteen].

In September 2021, two months after announcing his run, Vance gave the interview that ties him, in his own words, to this book's texts. On the podcast Jack Murphy Live he said: "There's this guy, Curtis Yarvin, who's written about some of these things," and went on, "I'm sympathetic to that project. But another option is that we should just seize the administrative state for our own purposes." [cite:de5_wbur_vance] [cite:de5_vox_2022] In the same appearance he said a future President Trump should "fire every single mid-level bureaucrat, every civil servant in the administrative state" and "replace them with our people," and that when the courts objected the President should answer, as Andrew Jackson is popularly said to have done, "The chief justice has made his ruling. Now let him enforce it." [cite:de5_nextgov_vance] [scene:de5_the_public_years|Part Five] places the remark in Yarvin's life; [scene:de6_the_program|Part Six] sets out the program he was naming, including the proposal Yarvin called RAGE, "Retire All Government Employees."

The remark is the one occasion on which Vance cited Yarvin by name in public, and two things about it need to be held together. It is documented, in his own voice, and it endorses the central practical idea of Yarvin's program, replacing the civil service wholesale with loyalists, together with a stated willingness to ignore the courts. It is also a candidate's remark on a podcast, made in 2021, before he held any office; it is not a policy document, and it expressly offers the "seize the administrative state" route as an alternative to the project he was "sympathetic to," not as that project. Yarvin, for his part, later described their acquaintance as slight: he said he had interacted with Vance once after the 2024 election, at a party, and a Vance adviser told CNN in 2025 that the two had met "like once." [cite:de5_politico_ward] [cite:de6_cnn_2025] What happened to these words when their speaker reached office is the subject of [scene:de12_one_heartbeat_away|the fourth section].

Read as a whole, these six years support a narrow conclusion and rule out two broad ones. The narrow conclusion is that Thiel repeatedly backed, employed, invested alongside and introduced a man whose outlook he had already helped to shape, and that each tie was of a different, documented type. The broad readings it rules out, on the present record, are that Thiel "created" Vance, since the first tie was an idea Vance encountered as a student and the memoir that made him famous was his own, and that Thiel controlled him, since no document shows Thiel directing anything Vance said or did. [scene:de14_network_map|Part Fourteen] places these ties on the map of Thiel's wider network.
