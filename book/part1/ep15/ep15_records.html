Who Was Made to Hand Over Records

A name in the files tells you who knew Epstein. Whether anyone has been made to produce records tells you who has been examined. The release shows a good deal of compulsion, aimed in particular directions.

Between 2019 and 2021 federal prosecutors in Manhattan sent grand-jury subpoenas to Epstein's banks (JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank, Bank of America, TD Bank, Citibank, UBS and FirstBank Puerto Rico among them), to his accountant Richard Kahn and lawyer Darren Indyke on 18 October 2019, to the law firm representing his accusers, and to phone, technology and airline companies. [cite:ep15_doj_gjjpm] [cite:ep15_doj_gjdb] [cite:ep15_doj_gjkahn] [cite:ep15_doj_gjbsf] [cite:ep15_doj_gjfirstbank] A large share of the 2019 subpoenas concerned the jail and the guards on duty the night he died. One FBI report shows the limit of the compulsion. On 10 July 2019, four days after the arrest, agents had a subpoena ready for Les Wexner; when his lawyers said he would sit for an interview, "subpoena service was no longer required". [cite:ep15_doj_wexner302]

Others went further on the money. In July 2020 the Virgin Islands attorney general subpoenaed eight financial institutions, including Bank Leumi, Charles Schwab, Citibank and Wells Fargo, for the records of 35 Epstein-related accounts. [cite:ep15_jtn_usvisubpoenas2020] In November 2025 the House Oversight Committee subpoenaed JPMorgan and Deutsche Bank. [cite:ep15_ho_comer2025] Leon Black, subpoenaed by the same committee in 2026, did not appear. [cite:ep7_pbs_black2026]

What the record does not show matters as much. No subpoena or other compulsory request appears in the released files or in public reporting for the Edmond de Rothschild bank, or for Black's family office and the company that lent Epstein $30.5 million. The Treasury's records of some 4,725 wires, which Senator Wyden has described, remain unreleased. [cite:ep7_wyden_blanche2026] Most of the compulsion has fallen on the banks that moved the money and on Epstein's own staff. The private payers have mostly been asked rather than ordered; Wexner, the largest, answered questions under oath for the first time in a congressional deposition in February 2026. [cite:ep15_nbc_wexner2026]

The flight log, mapped

The pilot David Rodgers kept his own passenger log from 1991 to 2006. The FBI obtained it in February 2020, and it became a trial exhibit with minors' names removed. [cite:ep15_doj_flightlogemail] A public transcription covers about 4,700 log entries, nearly all from 1995 onward. [cite:ep15_fle_flightlogs] Matching it to the people on this book's network map gives the following minimum counts. The pages before 1995 are not in the transcription, so earlier flights, including some reported elsewhere, do not appear. A flight to St Thomas, the nearest airport to Little St. James, is not proof of a visit to the island, and being on a plane is not evidence of a crime.

Two things stand out. Every one of Bill Clinton's 25 legs in the log falls in 2002 and 2003, on long trips through Asia, Africa and Europe, and none goes to or from St Thomas. And the people who flew most often to the island airport were not the famous guests but the assistants, two of whom the 2008 agreement named as potential co-conspirators: Sarah Kellen shared 22 of Clinton's legs and 77 of Nadia Marcinko's. The log shows who was on board. It cannot show what any of them knew.
