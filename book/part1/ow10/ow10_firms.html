Cellebrite, Elbit, Cognyte, Black Cube and Carbyne

NSO and Paragon are the spyware firms. Five other Israeli-founded companies reach into American government or American life in different ways: one opens seized phones for federal agents, one builds surveillance towers on the Mexican border, one sells interception systems abroad, one is a private intelligence agency hired by American clients, and one routes 911 calls. They differ in ownership, in how much the Israeli state supervises their exports and in whether any American agency is their customer at all. This section takes them one at a time and then sets them side by side.

Cellebrite. Cellebrite was founded in 1999 in Petah Tikva, Israel, where it is still headquartered. Its largest shareholder is the Japanese company Sun Corporation, with about 44 percent, and it has traded on Nasdaq since August 2021, when it merged with a blank-check company. [cite:ow10_wiki_cellebrite] Its devices extract the contents of locked phones that police already hold, which makes it a forensic tool rather than remote spyware. ICE has bought it repeatedly: contracts worth about $2.2 million in 2017 and $30–35 million in 2019 are recorded, and in September 2025 ICE announced an $11 million contract while redacting the whole of its justification. [cite:ow10_wiki_cellebrite] [cite:ow10_reason_cb] Procurement papers for ICE's cyber crimes centre said there were "no other mobile forensic product suites that offer equivalent capabilities." [cite:ow10_fs_cb] Customs and Border Protection, the Naval Criminal Investigative Service and the Marine Corps' criminal investigators also use it. [cite:ow10_reason_cb] Cellebrite stopped selling to Russian and Belarusian state bodies in March 2021, after its tools were linked to the persecution of activists there. [cite:ow10_gv_cb] In January 2026 the Citizen Lab reported that Jordan had used Cellebrite's extraction device on the phones of activists and journalists detained at protests against the war in Gaza; the company said it does "not comment on specifics" and that the rule of law and privacy are fundamental to it. [cite:ow10_occrp_jordan] The sources used here do not say whether Cellebrite's exports require Israeli Defense Ministry licences.

Elbit Systems of America, now Twenty-Six Defense. The American subsidiary of the Israeli arms maker Elbit Systems is the most deeply embedded of these firms in federal security. In February 2014 the subsidiary won a $145 million contract from DHS for Integrated Fixed Towers, sensor towers with command centres to detect and track movement along the border with Mexico in Arizona. [cite:ow10_globes_ift] In July 2026 it announced further Customs and Border Protection contracts worth more than $370 million, running to May 2029. On 8 September 2026 the subsidiary, headquartered in Fort Worth, Texas, with about 3,300 American employees, renamed itself Twenty-Six Defense; its chief executive, Luke Savoie, said the name "underscores our growth in the American market." Defense News noted that the change came as American opinion divided over the Gaza war; the company did not give that as a reason. It remains the American arm of Elbit Systems. [cite:ow10_dn_26] Its border work is the clearest case in this part of Israeli-owned technology used inside the United States by a federal agency, under a federal contract.

Cognyte. Cognyte, an Israeli company, was spun off from the interception company Verint Systems in February 2021 and is listed on Nasdaq. Reuters reported in January 2023 that it had won a tender to sell interception software to Myanmar's state-backed telecoms company about a month before the February 2021 coup; Reuters could not confirm the sale was completed or whether an Israeli export licence was issued. Israel has said it stopped defence exports to Myanmar after a 2017 Supreme Court ruling; the Defense Ministry declined to comment, and Cognyte did not respond. In 2021 Meta banned Cognyte from Facebook, saying its customers in Kenya, Mexico and Indonesia had targeted journalists and politicians with fake accounts. [cite:ow10_aj_cognyte] The sources used here document no American federal contract with Cognyte.

Black Cube. Black Cube is a private intelligence firm that describes itself as "a select group of veterans from the Israeli elite intelligence units." [cite:ow10_cnbc_bc] It has no documented contract with any American agency; its American work has been for private clients. From late 2016 Harvey Weinstein engaged Black Cube, and in July 2017 the law firm of his lawyer David Boies signed a contract with it, to stop publication of stories about Weinstein's abuse of women, and, as Ronan Farrow reported in The New Yorker in November 2017, two of its operatives, posing as wealth advisers, approached the actress Rose McGowan, one recording her secretly. [cite:ow10_npr_bc] [cite:ow10_cnbc_bc] In May 2018 The Observer and The New Yorker reported that Black Cube had been hired, by people the Observer described as Trump aides, in May 2017 to find damaging information on Ben Rhodes and Colin Kahl, two Obama officials who had worked on the Iran nuclear agreement, and that their wives received approaches from people using false identities. Black Cube said it "has no relation whatsoever to the Trump administration" or to the Iran deal; a source said the client was a private party with commercial interests in Iran sanctions. [cite:ow10_cnbc_bc]

Carbyne. Carbyne, founded in Israel in 2015 as Reporty, sells software that lets emergency dispatchers receive location, video and text from callers; it moved its headquarters to New York in 2019 and has been used by emergency services in 23 states. [cite:ow10_wiki_carbyne] Its early money is documented. Ehud Barak invested $1 million in 2015 and became chairman, holding his stake through a partnership he formed, Sum (E.B.) 2015; Israel's public broadcaster Kan reported that Jeffrey Epstein put $1 million into that partnership in 2016 through a British Virgin Islands company, Southern Trust. Peter Thiel's Founders Fund was among Carbyne's backers. [cite:ow10_ctech_barak] When the Epstein link was reported in July 2019, Barak said "a firm linked to Epstein has been a passive investor" and that he had told his lawyers to explore removing it. [cite:ow10_toi_barak] Barak left the board in 2020. In November 2025 the American company Axon agreed to buy Carbyne for $625 million, and the deal closed on 18 February 2026, making Carbyne wholly American-owned. [cite:ow10_p2_axon] [cite:ow10_wiki_carbyne] The Epstein investment is treated in full in [scene:ow12_ties|Part Twelve].

Reading the table. Two patterns stand out. The firms with the most American government business are those whose ownership or operations sit substantially outside Israel: Cellebrite, listed on Nasdaq with a Japanese largest shareholder; Elbit's American subsidiary, with its workforce in the United States; and Paragon and Carbyne after their purchase by American companies. Their tools are used under American contracts that are public in outline. [cite:ow10_wiki_cellebrite] [cite:ow10_dn_26] [cite:ow10_globes_ae] [cite:ow10_p2_axon] The firms whose tools were turned against Americans, NSO against diplomats and Black Cube against former officials and a woman who had accused Weinstein, were Israeli-controlled, and in NSO's case licensed by Israel's Defense Ministry. [cite:ow10_reu_state] [cite:ow10_cnbc_bc] In every case the American customer can see the contract; what it cannot see, for the firms still based in Israel, is what the Israeli licensing authority knows about the tool and its other buyers.
