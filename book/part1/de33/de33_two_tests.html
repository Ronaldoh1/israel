Two Tests, One Result: Urbit and DOGE

The question this book keeps returning to was put most sharply in [scene:de8_who_enforces_the_charter|Part Eight]: if a country is run by a chief executive, who removes a bad one? Yarvin's own answer is a board. He has said a monarch should answer to a corporate board rather than to citizens, and in his design for a patchwork of small sovereign states a board selects and recalls the chief executive. [cite:de8_cnn_yarvin] [cite:de8_kofman_ny] Between August 2024 and July 2026 the idea of a chief executive who fixes a failing institution by setting aside the usual consultation was tried twice in public. Once it was tried in Yarvin's own network, Urbit, by Yarvin himself. The second time it was tried in the federal government, through the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), which was not his. This section sets the two cases side by side: what each promised, what happened, who checked it and what lasted. It then closes Book Six by testing the book's worst-case reading against the pathway, the force and these two tests together.

Neither case was a test of Yarvin's theory as he wrote it, and the record does not allow either to be presented as one. Urbit is a small voluntary network whose users can leave at no cost. DOGE was an office created by executive order inside a constitutional government. Its savings goals, its public leader and its methods came from the president and Elon Musk, and no document traces it to Yarvin's 2012 proposal to retire all government employees, which it resembles; rhyme is not provenance ([scene:de30_rage_to_schedule|Part Thirty-Two]). [cite:de0_eo14158] [cite:de6_tnr_duran] Yarvin himself disowned it as a model. He complained that DOGE had too much libertarian DNA, and he said the administration was "barely scratching the surface" of the change he wanted. [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] [cite:de6_cnn_2025] What the two cases share is narrower and can be documented: in each, someone took authority without a formal title, in the name of a crisis, over an institution with a body above it.

What each promised

The Urbit Foundation's money was nearly gone in August 2024, with a runway "measurable in months." Its board dismissed the executive director, and Yarvin returned to lead its strategy while holding "no formal position at the foundation." His words were: "For that, you need a wartime CEO," and "We're here to fix this." [cite:de23_coindesk_2024] The phrase comes from a 2011 essay by the investor Ben Horowitz, whose wartime chief executive "neither indulges consensus-building nor tolerates disagreements." [cite:de23_horowitz_2011]

DOGE's promises were larger. Musk said at a rally in October 2024 that he could cut "at least $2 trillion." [cite:de11_fortune_2t] In their November 2024 op-ed he and Vivek Ramaswamy wrote that they would serve "as outside volunteers, not federal officials or employees," and that existing law, not new legislation, would carry the changes. [cite:de17_li_oped] In February 2025 Musk set out the case in the Oval Office: "We don't live in a democracy. We live in a bureaucracy." [cite:de3_dcpd_musk] He called DOGE's actions "maximally transparent." [cite:de21_reason_foia] He too held no formal command. A White House declaration filed in court on 17 February 2025 said he had "no actual or formal authority to make government decisions himself" and could "only advise the President." [cite:de17_audit_fisher]

What happened

In Urbit, the return did not settle the crisis. Several senior staff resigned. In 2025 the Foundation's director removed Yarvin from the board while Yarvin was lobbying the other two members to remove the director. The director then asked the owners of the network's 256 "galaxies," its top-level addresses, to replace the board. [cite:de23_marbl] [cite:de5_wiki_urbit] On 9 October 2025 the Foundation announced that "the Galactic Senate has elected to replace the board" after "multiple votes." The new board named a new executive director to succeed Yarvin's Urbit identity, ~sorreg-namtyv, "who is leaving to found a new application development company." [cite:de23_epoch_2025] The full account is in [scene:de23_wartime_ceo|Part Twenty-Three].

In the federal government, Musk left in May 2025, saying "This is not the end of DOGE. It's really just the beginning." [cite:de21_wt_farewell] DOGE's final claim was $215 billion in savings. The Government Accountability Office (GAO), Congress's auditor, examined the $110 billion of that figure attributed to contracts, grants and leases. It found that DOGE's own stated method had been followed for 27.5 per cent of the contract savings, and that 96.2 per cent of the grant savings could not be checked. [cite:de21_gao_full] Asked under oath whether their grant cancellations had reduced the deficit, a DOGE staffer answered: "No, we didn't." [cite:de18_abc_depos] The civilian workforce of 22 agencies fell by nearly 256,000 between December 2024 and January 2026. [cite:de21_gao_workforce] The temporary organization ended on 4 July 2026, the date its own order set, and the budget director told appropriators there would be no closing report. [cite:de0_eo14158] [cite:de21_fnn_vought] [scene:de21_the_ending|Part Twenty-One] tells that ending in full.

Who checked it

In Urbit the check came from the body Yarvin's own design put on top. Only galaxies vote in the Galactic Senate; a vote needs a quorum of 64 galaxies and passes with an absolute majority of 129. [cite:de23_voting_docs] That body removed the board the founder had fought, and the founder left. In one sense the theory's own remedy worked: the owners recalled the executive. The check also came from a narrow tier. In 2019 an independent count put 185 of the 256 galaxies with the company, its staff and the Foundation, and the network's ordinary users, the holders of "planets," had no vote at all. [cite:de23_compact_2025] [cite:de23_voting_docs] Their remedy was to leave.

DOGE answered to more bodies, and they did not all pull the same way. The courts checked it in part. A federal judge found in October 2026 that the Office of Personnel Management had broken the Privacy Act by giving DOGE access to personnel records, and the Supreme Court agreed that month to decide whether the U.S. DOGE Service must answer requests under the Freedom of Information Act. [cite:de19_bgov_afge] [cite:de21_ncc_cert] The courts also let DOGE proceed: on 6 June 2025 the Supreme Court stayed an injunction that had barred DOGE from Social Security records. [cite:de19_ch_afscme] The auditor checked it after the fact. GAO also reported that agencies refused or limited cooperation and that the extent of DOGE's access to systems "could not be determined." [cite:de21_gao_systems] Congress checked it and ratified it in turn. It never legislated the proposed "DOGE dividend," but on 24 July 2025 a law cancelled about $9 billion in spending, mostly foreign aid and about $1.1 billion for public broadcasting, in programmes DOGE had targeted. [cite:de21_usat_dividend] [cite:de33_wdbj_rescission] The last check was the president, who held the office's legal authority throughout. When he and Musk fell out in June 2025, it was Musk who left. [cite:de11_npr_feud]

What lasted, and the one result

What outlasted each experiment came through ordinary channels. In Urbit, the Foundation went on under the board the owners chose and was still publishing technical work in 2026. [cite:de23_bloglist] In Washington, the lasting changes were a smaller workforce, former DOGE staff in permanent posts and the U.S. DOGE Service itself, which still has a budget request. [cite:de21_gao_workforce] [cite:de21_ee_end] To these came a change DOGE did not make: the Schedule Policy/Career rule of 2026, under which an order of 3 June moved about 8,000 senior career officials into at-will employment. [cite:de30_wh_eo14410] [cite:de30_npr_8000] That change came by rule-making and executive order. It is challenged in court and, as of October 2026, has not been blocked. [cite:de30_afge_lit]

The one result, stated as far as the record carries it, is this. In both cases a leader took exceptional authority without a formal title, in the name of a crisis, and promised a fix. In both, the claimed result could not be verified. In both, the leader left before the work was done. And in both, the body above the institution had the last word: the galaxy owners in Urbit, and in Washington the president, the auditor, the courts and Congress. What lasted was what had been written into ordinary procedure. Two cases make a pattern, not a law. They differ in scale and in kind, since one is voluntary and the other carried the force of a state, and their sequences are rhymes, not one plan.

The strongest counter-reading is Yarvin's own, and it should be stated in full. On his account neither case tested his idea. A start-up foundation is not a sovereign. DOGE was a libertarian cost-cutting office inside a system he holds to be unreformable, run by people who never claimed sovereign power and under a president he judged far too timid. [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] [cite:de6_cnn_2025] The record supports the premise of that reply: the CEO-state as he describes it, with a board holding the keys to the state's weapons, was not tried, and was not proposed by anyone in office. [cite:de8_kofman_ny] A second counter-reading runs the other way. The checks on DOGE were partial and late. Its teams reached systems whose extent the auditor could not establish, the highest court let it into Social Security records, and Congress turned part of its target list into law. [cite:de21_gao_systems] [cite:de19_ch_afscme] [cite:de33_wdbj_rescission] The bodies above both institutions held, and they did not hold everywhere.

The worst-case reading, tested

The hypothesis is the Sophia platform's, not a finding. Immigration enforcement and government cuts are the occasion, and a sovereign executive without a veto is the destination. Book Six asked what removing the remaining brakes would take ([scene:de31_five_patterns|Part Thirty-Three]), who would enforce the result ([scene:de32_ice_as_template|Part Thirty-Four]) and how the chief-executive idea fared when tried. The three can now be graded together.

Documented: real powers moved toward the presidency through rulings, rules and statutes. The Supreme Court's decision in Trump v. Slaughter on 29 June 2026 let the president remove members of independent commissions at will. About 8,000 senior officials became removable at will by an order of 3 June 2026. [cite:de0_slaughter] [cite:de30_wh_eo14410] The force grew as well. The 2025 budget act gave ICE about $75 billion over four years, and Guard troops were federalised in Los Angeles without the governor's consent. The Washington, D.C., deployment was extended in July 2026 to 2029. [cite:us6_brennan_obbba] [cite:us3a_fh_us2026] [cite:us3_audit_dc_guard] In both tests, authority was taken without a title in the name of a crisis. Who gained from each step, and what was blocked, is set out in [scene:de31_who_benefits|Part Thirty-Three] and [scene:de31_what_was_blocked|its third section]. The detention contractors and the military at home are covered in [scene:de32_the_detention_business|Part Thirty-Four] and [scene:de32_the_military_at_home|its third section].

Probable: that changes accumulate through ordinary channels rather than in one break, and that each leaves machinery behind for the next occasion. DOGE's service, its alumni and its rule changes outlived its name. The president said in December 2025 that the Guard would return "perhaps in a much different and stronger form." [cite:de21_ee_end] [cite:de33_pbs_guard] Whether that happens is open; what persists is documented.

Not supported: an executive without a veto. The record runs against it at each point where it was tested. On 23 December 2025 the Supreme Court refused, at an interim stage and over three dissents, to let the administration deploy the Guard in Illinois. It found that the government had "failed to identify a source of authority that would allow the military to execute the laws." Within a week the deployments in Chicago, Los Angeles and Portland were ended. [cite:de33_rollcall_illinois] [cite:de33_stlpr_illinois] [cite:de33_pbs_guard] The Court held in February 2026 that the emergency-powers law the president had used for tariffs does not authorise them. [cite:us11_ks_ieepa] Congress refused the dividend and kept the purse. DOGE ended on the date its own order set, and in Urbit the owners removed the founder. [cite:de21_usat_dividend] [cite:de21_fnn_vought] [cite:de23_epoch_2025] None of this shows a plan, and the vetoes did not hold everywhere: the removal power widened, and no court has blocked the new civil-service schedule ([scene:de32_what_stops_it|Part Thirty-Four] weighs what has actually stopped the force). [cite:de0_slaughter] [cite:de30_afge_lit] Book Six ends where Book Five did: the pathway is documented and partly travelled, and the destination is not shown.
