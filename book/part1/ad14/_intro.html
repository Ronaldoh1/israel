Part Fourteen follows the people for whom the Dream's floor is thinnest. It begins with the official poverty line, built in 1963 from three times the cost of an emergency diet and raised only by inflation since, and sets it beside the Census Bureau's Supplemental Poverty Measure, the United Way's ALICE count of working households that cannot meet local costs, and the economists who argue that the official line overstates poverty. It then adds up the financial bill that the criminal-legal system sends alongside a sentence: bail premiums, fines and fees, the price of a phone call and a bar of soap inside, wages of cents an hour, child support that grows while a parent is locked up, and a record that cuts earnings for life, with the state of each rule as of October 2026. It ends with homelessness: the latest national count, why some regions have far more of it than others, the 2024 Supreme Court ruling on camping bans, and the 2025 turn of federal policy away from Housing First, weighed against the trial evidence on both sides.
