Jordan is a separate state whose own government rejects the idea that it is the Palestinian state. The line starts from a real event: in 1921–23 Britain separated Transjordan from the rest of the Palestine Mandate under Article 25, exempting it from the Jewish national home provisions. [cite:w_transjordan] [cite:wiki_transjordan_memorandum] But the 1947 UN partition plan divided only the land west of the Jordan River. Many Jordanians are of Palestinian origin because they became refugees in 1948 and 1967, not because Jordan was a partition. [cite:w_black_september]

There is a twist: Jordan's own rulers once made a version of the claim. In 1948 King Abdullah I said Palestine and Jordan were one country, backing Jordan's annexation of the West Bank. [cite:meforum_jordan_west_bank] On July 31, 1988, King Hussein formally dissolved Jordan's legal and administrative ties to the West Bank. [cite:springer_jordan_palestine_history] Asked whether Jordan should be the de facto Palestinian state, King Abdullah II answered: "Jordan is Jordan." [cite:davidsinger_jordan_palestinian_state]
