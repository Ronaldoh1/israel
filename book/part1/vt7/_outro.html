The black nobility was real, and it was a court nobility, not a government. Its families held ceremonial and hereditary offices at the papal court, and the Lateran Concordat of 1929 confirmed their papal titles in Italian law rather than abolishing them. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob] [cite:vt7_va_lateran] The Orsini gave the Church three popes, the last of whom died in 1730, not five; their cardinals led opposing blocs in the conclave of 1305; their dukedom of Bracciano was sold before 1698; and no genealogy joins them to the Rothschilds, whose name comes from a house in Frankfurt's Jewish street. [cite:vt7_ce_orsini] [cite:vt7_wiki_conclave1305] [cite:vt7_wiki_orsini] [cite:vt7_ra_name] The family's last formal office, Prince Assistant to the Papal Throne, was taken from Filippo Orsini by Pius XII in January 1958, and Paul VI's Pontificalis Domus of 28 March 1968 declared that no office in the Papal Household is hereditary; the last holder of the title died in 2017. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt7_pd_latin] [cite:vt7_br_torlonia] The claims that the Orsini control the papacy, are one bloodline with the Rothschilds, or act as a "Grey Pope" are myths: no document supports them, and the record of 1305, 1958 and 1968 runs against them. Part Eight turns to the bodies that do hold documented institutional weight around the pope: the Society of Jesus, Opus Dei and the Sovereign Order of Malta.
