What the Closure Did, and What the Law Said

The closure's effects were measured, year after year, by UN agencies, the World Bank and humanitarian organizations working inside the strip. Their figures describe an economy that contracted while its neighbour grew, a water supply that failed, and a population that came to depend on aid. Israel's position throughout was that the restrictions answered a real threat, the rocket fire from Gaza and the arming of Hamas, and that responsibility for the strip's condition lay with the group that governed it. [cite:gz_wiki_blockade]

Work and income. According to the UN Country Team, unemployment in Gaza rose from 34.1 percent in 2006 to 40.6 percent in 2016, and among women from 35.1 to 64.4 percent. Over the same decade GDP per person in Gaza fell by 5.3 percent while in the West Bank it rose by 48.5 percent. [cite:gz_un_gaza10] The World Bank estimated losses to Gaza's GDP from the closure at more than 50 percent, and poverty stood at about 40 percent in 2015. [cite:gz_wiki_blockade] By 2016 food insecurity stood at 47 percent, and about 1.2 million people needed humanitarian aid. [cite:gz_un_gaza10]

Water and power. In 2012 the UN warned, in a report titled "Gaza in 2020: A liveable place?", that the strip's only aquifer could become "unusable as early as 2016, with the damage irreversible by 2020," that 90 percent of its water was already unsafe to drink without treatment, and that the population would reach 2.1 million by 2020. It called the economy, as it stood, "fundamentally unviable." [cite:gz_un_gaza2020] Five years later the UN recorded 96.2 percent of the aquifer's water as unfit for human consumption, and power cuts of up to 20 hours a day. [cite:gz_un_gaza10]

The legal judgments. The closure was judged under international law by several bodies, and they did not agree. The International Committee of the Red Cross said on 14 June 2010 that "the closure therefore constitutes a collective punishment imposed in clear violation of Israel's obligations under international humanitarian law." [cite:gz_icrc_2010] The UN Secretary-General's Palmer panel, a year later, found the naval blockade "a legitimate security measure" lawfully implemented, while recommending that the restrictions be eased toward lifting the closure. [cite:gz_palmer] Israel's Supreme Court held in 2008 that Israel no longer had effective control inside Gaza but remained bound to meet the strip's essential humanitarian needs. [cite:gz_ahmed_hcj]

The three judgments looked at different parts of the closure, and the difference between them is part of the record. The sea blockade had defenders in international law that the closure as a whole did not; the measurements of what it did to Gaza's people were not disputed by the bodies that made them. The wars of the following years, the subject of Part Five, were fought over a strip already in this condition.
