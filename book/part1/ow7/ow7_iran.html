Defending Israel, Fighting Iran: 2024–2026

For forty years the military relationship was one of supply, planning and exercises. In April 2024 it became one of combat: American ships and aircraft shot down weapons fired at Israel, and in 2026 American forces went to war with Iran alongside Israel. This section sets out, from Pentagon statements and named reporting, what US forces did, what it cost, and what the United States said it was for. Because the 2026 war was still not formally ended when this was written, its figures are given as of October 2026.

13–14 April 2024. Iran and its allies launched more than 300 drones and missiles at Israel from Iran, Iraq, Syria and Yemen. The Pentagon said that about 99 per cent were brought down. American forces, including the destroyers USS Arleigh Burke and USS Carney and Air Force and Navy fighters, destroyed more than 80 one-way attack drones and at least six ballistic missiles aimed at Israel, among them a missile shot down near Erbil in Iraq. Austin, the Pentagon said, was "grateful for the professionalism and skill of the brave U.S. troops" who had defended Israel, and the United States would "continue to take all necessary action to defend Israel and U.S. personnel". [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024]

1 October 2024. When Iran fired about 200 ballistic missiles at Israel, the destroyers USS Bulkeley and USS Cole fired about a dozen interceptors; the Pentagon said no American ground-based interceptors were used. [cite:ow7_usni_oct2024] Twelve days later the THAAD battery described in the previous section was ordered to Israel, with its American crew. [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024]

June 2025. In the twelve-day war between Israel and Iran in June 2025, the THAAD batteries carried a large share of the defence. Two sources told CNN that the United States fired more than 100, and possibly as many as 150, THAAD interceptors, about a quarter of its supply, using two of its seven THAAD systems. An analysis by the Jewish Institute for National Security of America (JINSA) estimated that THAAD accounted for almost half of all interceptions of Iranian missiles, and that replacing the stock would take three to eight years at current production rates. [cite:ow7_cnn_thaad2025]

The 2026 war. On 28 February 2026 the United States and Israel launched coordinated strikes on Iran, named "Epic Fury" by the Pentagon and "Roaring Lion" by Israel; the American military described "full synchronization and coordination" between the two forces. Iranian state media confirmed that the Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei, had been killed. President Trump said the aim was to defend Americans by removing imminent threats from the Iranian government; Prime Minister Netanyahu called it the removal of an existential threat. [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] Iran answered with missile and drone attacks on Israel, on American bases and on American-allied states, and closed the Strait of Hormuz. A two-week ceasefire mediated by Pakistan began on 7–8 April, was extended, and was followed by the Islamabad Memorandum, signed by both presidents on 17 June; it collapsed on 8 July, and intermittent fighting between American and Iranian forces continued afterwards. [cite:ow7_wiki_ceasefire2026]

The cost, as of October 2026. A Central Command estimate given to Congress in September 2026 put the American cost of the war at $43.6 billion as of 3 September, more than half of it munitions, with defensive interceptors for the Patriot and THAAD systems the most sought-after; the Congressional Budget Office had put it at $38 billion through 1 August, rising by about $3 billion a month. The figure excludes damage to American bases, which a Pentagon inspector general found had been damaged or destroyed in eight countries. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] By 9 October 2026 the Pentagon's casualty database listed 21 American deaths, hostile and non-hostile, in operations against Iran, and 865 wounded; The Washington Post reported four more deaths than the official figure, which the Defense Secretary disputed. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026]

What the United States said it was for. Each step came with a stated American purpose: deterring a wider regional war (October 2023), protecting Israel and American personnel from Iranian attack (2024), and, in 2026, removing what the President called imminent threats to Americans. [cite:ow7_ap_ford2023] [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024] [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] Trump's further claim that Iran was rebuilding its nuclear programme was disputed at the time by the International Atomic Energy Agency, which said it had no evidence that Iran had resumed enrichment. [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury]

The balance. The 2024–26 record tests the "one-way" thesis directly. Israel's forces also defended themselves, and Israel's Arrow interceptors took part alongside THAAD in 2025. [cite:ow7_cnn_thaad2025] The American radar on Har Keren, the American THAAD batteries and the American destroyers protected American bases and personnel as well as Israeli cities. [cite:ow7_int_site512] [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024] But the costs fell unevenly. A quarter of the American THAAD stock was spent defending Israel in twelve days in 2025, and by September 2026 American and NATO officials were describing the shortage of interceptors in Europe as "beyond critical"; and in the war of 2026, which the United States joined alongside Israel, American service members died and the Treasury carried a bill of tens of billions of dollars. [cite:ow7_cnn_thaad2025] [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026] Whether that was a price paid for Israel or for American aims against Iran is a political judgement, and the two governments' stated aims overlapped; what the record shows is who paid it.
