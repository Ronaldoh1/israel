The Separation Began First

Gaza and the West Bank were cut apart long before Hamas ruled Gaza, and Ron's question has to start there. In 1991 Israel ended the general exit permit that had let Palestinians from Gaza move fairly freely into Israel and through it to the West Bank. The Oslo Accords of 1993 described the West Bank and Gaza as "a single territorial unit, whose integrity will be preserved during the interim period". From 2000 Israel barred students from Gaza from studying at West Bank universities and froze changes of address from Gaza to the West Bank; from 2007 Gazans living in the West Bank needed a "permit to remain". [cite:hm_aj_separation]

The effect can be measured. In September 2000 more than half a million exits from Gaza were recorded at the Erez crossing in a single month; by 2013 Israel was issuing about 3,000 permits a month. Sari Bashi of the Israeli rights group Gisha said of the policy: "Families are separated… It's not about security." Israeli officials such as Uzi Landau, then tourism minister, pointed to the attacks that followed Oslo; Israel's position is that the restrictions are a security measure. [cite:hm_aj_separation]

The separation has two sides in the record. Israel built it as a security policy, in years when bombers did come out of Gaza and the West Bank, as Part Four set out. [scene:hm4_bombs|The bombings] It also meant that by the time Hamas took Gaza in 2007, the strip's people had already been largely cut off from the West Bank for years, and the political split fell along a line that Israel's permit system had already drawn.
