The Broken Vessels and the Sparks

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

In the second movement of Luria's account, divine light is poured into vessels, and most of them shatter. The light scatters as sparks, and disharmony and evil enter the world. In the third movement, tikkun, the sparks are gathered and returned to their source, and human beings take part in the work through prayer, intention and words. [cite:kb_brit_luria]

The inner reading finds in this one of the most hopeful teachings on the shelf. The world is broken, but not because it was made bad: it is broken because it could not hold the light it was given. The sparks are still in it, scattered through ordinary things, and they can be gathered. Every act done with the right intention returns a spark. Read inwardly, the self is also a vessel that broke under more than it could hold, and its repair is not a matter of becoming someone else but of gathering what is already there: the scattered light in one's own history, relationships and failures.

The record of this book adds a warning to the reading. The teaching that the world's repair could be completed soon was the teaching that carried Shabbetai Tzevi. [cite:kb_brit_luria] [cite:kb_brit_sabbatai] A repair that is always near can become a repair that one man claims to complete, and a people can follow him to the sultan's door. The Hasidic answer, Part Five showed, was to place the repair in ordinary life, open to everyone, not in a messiah or a date. [cite:kb_brit_devequt] Read inwardly, the lesson is the same: the gathering of sparks is daily work, not a single event, and anyone who claims to finish it for others should be doubted.

The record limits the reading. In Luria's system the repair is cosmic and is carried out through specific mystical intentions and combinations of words. [cite:kb_brit_luria] The psychological reading is modern and the reader's.
