This part asks how a small body of texts could be adopted by people with money and power, and answers it from the documented record of what those people wrote, said and were appointed to. It follows the turn of part of Silicon Valley to the right in the 2020s through two men's public words: Marc Andreessen, whose 2023 "Techno-Optimist Manifesto" named Nick Land among its patron saints and who, with his firm's co-founder, endorsed Donald Trump in 2024, and Balaji Srinivasan, whose theory of the "network state" turned the idea of exit into a design. It then traces the pipeline of people from venture firms, Thiel's companies and conservative institutions such as the Claremont Institute and Hillsdale College into public office in 2025 and 2026, and separates those who have cited Curtis Yarvin from those who merely know him or worked near him. The last section defines the currents most often confused with the Dark Enlightenment, each from its own texts, from Catholic integralism and national conservatism to effective accelerationism, and keeps the violent "accelerationism" of terrorist groups strictly apart.
