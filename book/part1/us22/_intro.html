Part Twenty-One set every measure side by side and added no total. This part adds one, in the open. It first reproduces the scorecard of the simulation's chapter on the two systems, which scored eleven citizen services at 3.70 of 5 for Israel and 2.49 for the United States, and shows what that scorecard could and could not support. It then grades both systems again on 35 measures from this volume, in eleven areas from health and housing to liberty, the economy and the gaps inside each society, against goalposts printed beside every row, and scores Israel twice: for its citizens, and for everyone it governs. Its last section adds the scores up, tests the totals five ways, answers the questions readers ask most often, from who carries more debt and who works longer to which state is the more oppressive, and gives the volume's final assessment.
