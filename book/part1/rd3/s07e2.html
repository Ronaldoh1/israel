Irgun and Lehi: The Paramilitary Line

A common picture of Mandate Palestine shows the Haganah as the sole Jewish military force, acting under unified political direction. The record shows at least three armed organizations operating at the same time: the Haganah, the Irgun and Lehi. They were divided by documented ideological splits, went through periods of open hostility, and achieved only partial, negotiated coordination even at the moment statehood was declared.

The Irgun, also known as Etzel, was founded in 1931 by officers aligned with Jabotinsky's Revisionist movement, the current whose institutional break is traced in [scene:s07a3|the split that made the Israeli right]. Its founders rejected the Haganah's policy of havlagah, or restraint, in the face of Arab attacks [cite:wiki_irgun]. Through the 1930s and 1940s it conducted an armed campaign against British rule and against Arab civilian targets. From 1943 it was commanded by Menachem Begin, who later became Israel's sixth Prime Minister [cite:wiki_irgun]. An organization founded on a disagreement over military strategy thus produced a head of government.

In 1940 the Irgun itself split. Avraham Stern led a faction out to form Lehi, Lohamei Herut Israel, "Fighters for the Freedom of Israel," often called the Stern Gang. The cause was the Irgun's decision to suspend anti-British operations during the Second World War; Stern's group argued for continuing armed resistance against Britain regardless [cite:wiki_lehi_stern_gang]. It went further. Stern's group is documented as having explored contact with Fascist Italy and, most controversially, Nazi Germany, seeking any power willing to oppose British rule in Palestine. The episode was widely condemned at the time, including within the broader Zionist movement [cite:wiki_lehi_stern_gang]. The record is of exploratory contact; it does not show that any actual cooperation or alliance resulted.

Relations among the three organizations ranged from uneasy cooperation to violence. The sharpest internal conflict came in 1944 and 1945, in the period known as the "Hunting Season." The Haganah, under the direction of the Jewish Agency, moved against members of the Irgun and Lehi, using surveillance, exposure to the British authorities, and detention, in an effort to suppress their independent operations and maintain unified political control [cite:wiki_hunting_season]. This was armed Jewish organizations acting against one another, not a single front.

Statehood did not end the friction at once. With the declaration of independence the Haganah became the core of the new Israel Defense Forces, and the Irgun and Lehi were formally absorbed. The absorption was contested. In June 1948 the Altalena affair brought the new army and Irgun forces into armed confrontation over a weapons shipment. Sixteen Irgun members and three IDF soldiers were killed, and the ship was shelled and burned off Tel Aviv on Ben-Gurion's direct order [cite:wiki_altalena_affair]. Begin's refusal to let the confrontation escalate is documented as decisive in preventing wider armed conflict between the two forces. His words were recorded:

there will be no civil war with the enemy at our gates

The Altalena was the flashpoint, not the whole of the integration. Most of the roughly 940 passengers aboard were inducted into the IDF without incident [cite:wiki_altalena_affair]. The wreck itself was located intact on the Mediterranean seafloor in 2026, seventy-eight years later [cite:jpost_altalena_wreck_2026].

These organizations belong in any account of how the state was built. A history that carefully tracks the internal political divisions of the period, among them the Bund, Reform Judaism and Neturei Karta, but treats the military side as simple and unified would apply an inconsistent standard [cite:jvl_altalena_affair]. The Haganah, the Irgun and Lehi were three organizations, not one, and the paramilitary line that began in 1931 ran directly into the state's politics: Begin went from commanding the Irgun to founding Herut and, eventually, to the office of Prime Minister.
