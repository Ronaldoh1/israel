The "Grey Pope" and the Question of Who Decides

The fullest version of the hidden-pope story has three colours. The pope in white is the public face; the "Black Pope", the Jesuit general, runs the machinery; above both stands a "Grey Pope", usually said to be the head of the Orsini family or of the Roman "Black Nobility", sometimes a senior cardinal, who controls the other two and the money. This section describes the white, black and grey scheme only as a named claim. It traces where the parts of the claim come from, sets out what documents exist, grades the result and then gives the documented answer to the question underneath it: who decides in the Holy See.

Three colours that are documented, and one that is not

Rome does have colour nicknames, and they are on the record. The pope wears white. The Jesuit general is called the "Black Pope" after his order's cassock, as the [scene:vt6_black_pope|previous section] showed. [cite:vt6_wiki_supgen] There is also a "Red Pope": journalists long gave that name to the prefect of the Congregation for the Evangelization of Peoples, the old Propaganda Fide, in allusion to a cardinal's red and to his "almost absolute authority over mission territories." [cite:vt6_philstar_redpope] A 2023 academic biography of Cardinal Willem van Rossum, who ran the mission department in the early twentieth century, carries the nickname as its title. [cite:vt6_rup_redpope] Since 2022 that office has been held by the pope himself: under Praedicate Evangelium the new Dicastery for Evangelization "is presided over directly by the Roman Pontiff." [cite:vt6_vn_pe] Each of these nicknames attaches to a named office with a known holder.

Grey has a different origin, and it is not Roman. Éminence grise, "grey eminence", was the name given at the French court to Père Joseph, François Leclerc du Tremblay, a Capuchin friar in a grey habit who served as right-hand man to Cardinal Richelieu, the "red eminence". From him the phrase came to mean "a powerful decision-maker or advisor who operates covertly in a non-public or unofficial capacity." [cite:vt6_wiki_eminence] The phrase describes a type of adviser; it has never been the name of an office at the Holy See.

The claim chain

The "Grey Pope" in the form that circulates today is a recent internet construction, and its sources can be listed. A German bullion dealer's news page of 26 February 2024 calls "Pepe Orsini" the "grey pope", "chief of the Rothschilds, of the Rockefellers" and of the whole black nobility; it cites no source and concedes that reliable evidence for such power is scarce. [cite:vt6_kettner_pepe] An undated, unattributed web page calls "Pepe Orsini" "the ultimate, unelected power behind the Vatican", then states that no such person holds any official capacity and that the widely shared photograph may show an actor. [cite:vt6_sl_greypope] A third undated page gives the same title to a different man, the Jesuit Superior General, calls it "not an official title recognized by the Vatican" and cites only Aldous Huxley's biography of Père Joseph. [cite:vt6_woc_greypope] None of the three names a document. They do not agree on who the Grey Pope is. Two of them say there is no evidence for him.

The Orsini thread of the claim leans on a real family and a real, ceremonial post. The heads of the Orsini held the hereditary lay title of Prince Assistant to the Papal Throne from 1735 until January 1958, when Pius XII relieved Filippo Orsini of it after a public scandal; Paul VI's reform of the papal household in 1968 ended the title's hereditary character, and it is not now in use. [cite:vt6_wiki_princeasst] The family's documented record, and the claims made about it, are set out in [scene:vt7_orsini_record|Part Seven] and [scene:vt7_prince_assistant_1958|the removal of 1958]. The last formal link between the Orsini and the papal court was cut by a pope, against the family.

The Rothschild link in the 2024 version belongs to a much older line of story. Claims that the Rothschild family stands behind thrones and popes go back to anonymous pamphleteers of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The journalist Mike Rothschild, who is not related to the family, has traced them as some two hundred years of antisemitic conspiracy theory. [cite:vt6_pw_rothschild] Folding the Rothschilds, the Rockefellers and a Roman prince into one hidden ruler repeats that story with a Catholic setting. The documented money between the Rothschild houses and Rome, papal loans from the 1830s on which the money ran from the bankers to the pope, is examined in [scene:vt11_papal_loans|Part Eleven], and the alleged unbroken chain to 2026 in [scene:vt11_chain_tested|the chain tested].

The grade

A claim of hidden office can be tested in three ways: is there an office, is there a holder, is there a document. The Holy See's offices are listed in full in its constitution of 2022 and in the Code of Canon Law, and no office stands above the pope; canon 333 rules out any appeal from his decree. [cite:vt6_va_pe] [cite:vt6_cic_331] No holder is identified consistently even by the pages that spread the claim, and one of them says the photograph shared as "Pepe Orsini" may show an actor. [cite:vt6_sl_greypope] [cite:vt6_woc_greypope] No document is cited by any of them. The broader idea of a hereditary nobility ruling through secret committees comes from conspiracy books such as John Coleman's Conspirators' Hierarchy (1992), for which fact-checkers have found no credible evidence. [cite:vt7_md_c300] A claim with no office, no holder and no document, contradicted by the documented acts of popes against the Orsini and the Jesuits, is graded a myth. The "Grey Pope" is a myth.

Who really decides

The documented answer is less dramatic and more definite. In law the pope decides: canon 331 gives him supreme, full, immediate and universal power, the Fundamental Law gives him the fullness of legislative, executive and judicial power in Vatican City, and the Curia acts "in his name and by his authority." [cite:vt6_cic_331] [cite:vt4_vn_fl2026] In practice he decides among options others prepare. The nuncio and the Dicastery for Bishops choose which three names he sees; the Secretariat of State drafts his documents and runs his diplomacy; the Secretariat for the Economy prepares his budget. [cite:vt6_usccb_bishops] [cite:vt6_ewtn_pe] [cite:vt6_va_spe] Every one of those office holders is named, appointed by him for five years and removable by him, and all of them lose office when he dies. [cite:vt6_ewtn_pe] Between popes, the cardinals under 80 decide who the next one will be, by a two-thirds secret ballot. [cite:vt6_vn_sedevacante]

The record also shows popes using that power against the bodies that hidden-pope stories place above them. Clement XIV suppressed the Jesuits, Pius XII removed the Orsini prince from his court post, John Paul II set aside the Jesuits' own interim leader, and Francis took the Secretariat of State's funds away from it in 2020. [cite:vt6_wiki_suppression] [cite:vt6_wiki_princeasst] [cite:vt6_wiki_dezza] [cite:vt6_vn_apsa2020] The limits on papal power are real, but they are visible ones: canon law and the structures his predecessors built, a Curia whose staff carries over ("popes pass, the Curia remains", in Leo XIV's words), finances that must balance, and outside checks such as the MONEYVAL evaluations described in [scene:vt5_moneyval_euro|Part Five]. [cite:vt6_aleteia_appts] [cite:vt5_mv_2021mer] Who holds those offices, and what each has done, is a matter of record. Influence on a pope, by advisers, donors, governments or orders, is a separate claim and has to be shown case by case with documents. Later parts of this volume do that for money, for the Orders and for Israel.
