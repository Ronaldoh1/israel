Syria: The Buffer Zone, Mount Hermon and Sufa 53

Since the disengagement agreement of 31 May 1974, Israeli and Syrian forces on the Golan have been separated by a strip of land patrolled by the UN Disengagement Observer Force, UNDOF, with zones of limited forces on either side. [cite:gi12_jvl_syrdis] Israel had held the western Golan since 1967 and extended its law there in 1981, a step the Security Council unanimously declared "null and void"; that history is in Part Eight. [cite:gi5_wiki_golanlaw] [cite:gi8_rw_res497] [scene:gi8_golan|The Golan] For fifty years the buffer strip itself stayed outside Israeli control. That changed on the day Bashar al-Assad's government fell. The Hezbollah volume follows the air campaign and the post-Assad talks; [scene:hz10_syria|Syria: the corridor, the campaign and the buffer zone] this section sets out the ground Israel took, what it said that ground was for, and the response of Syria, its neighbours and the UN.

8 December 2024: "temporarily"

On 8 December 2024 Netanyahu declared that the 1974 agreement "has collapsed, the Syrian soldiers have abandoned their positions," and said he had ordered the army to seize the buffer zone "and the commanding positions nearby." [cite:hz10s_newarab_collapse] He gave the reason in one sentence: "We will not allow any hostile force to establish itself on our border." [cite:gi9_wiki_syriainv] Israeli forces took the Syrian summit of Mount Hermon, the highest ground in the area, and over the next two days, by the military's count, carried out about 480 strikes on Syrian military stockpiles and naval facilities. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar said the aim was to keep the weapons from falling "into the hands of extremists," and Israel told the Security Council it had "deployed temporarily in few points." [cite:hz10s_cnn_480] The Biden administration called the incursion "a temporary action." [cite:gi9_wiki_syriainv]

The temporary framing did not last. On 17 December Netanyahu visited the Hermon summit and said the army would stay "until another solution ensuring Israel's security is found"; on 15 December his government had approved a plan to double the population of the Israeli-held Golan, a new plan distinct from, and following, the Bennett government's similar plan of 26 December 2021 described in Part Eight. [cite:gi9_wiki_syriainv] [cite:gi8_cnn_golan2021] On 28 January 2025 Defence Minister Israel Katz said on Hermon that the army would remain there and in the security zone "for an indefinite period," adding, "We will not return to the reality of October 7." [cite:hz10s_ih_hermon] In January 2025 Israeli officials were reported to describe a 15-kilometre "zone of control" inside Syria and a 60-kilometre "sphere of influence" beyond it; these terms come from press reports of officials' briefings, not from a published order. [cite:gi9_wiki_syriainv]

The demand for a demilitarised south

On 23 February 2025, at an officers' graduation ceremony, Netanyahu set out a demand that went beyond the buffer zone: "we demand the full demilitarization of southern Syria, in the Quneitra, Daraa, and As Suwayda provinces," and Israel would not allow the forces of Hayat Tahrir al-Sham, the group that had taken Damascus, or the new Syrian army, into the area south of the capital. By then the army had at least nine posts in Syria, two on Hermon and seven in the buffer zone. Demonstrations against the statement followed in Quneitra, Daraa and Suwayda, and Syria's National Dialogue Conference warned against the partition or "surrender" of any part of the country. [cite:gi9_fdd_demil] In July 2025, during fighting between government forces and Druze militias in Suwayda, Israel bombed the defence ministry in Damascus; Netanyahu said Israel was "acting to save our Druze brothers" and was committed to keeping the south-west demilitarised. [cite:hz10s_npr_sweida]

Sufa 53

The most permanent-looking work is a fortified line inside the buffer zone in Quneitra province known as Sufa 53. Begun in mid-2022, under Assad, near the village of Hadr, it consists of a military road about eight metres wide, earth berms about five metres high, trenches and observation posts; according to Syria's state news agency it runs from near Hadr through Jubata al-Khashab and al-Hamidiya to al-Asha and the southern tri-border point. [cite:hz10s_eb_sufa] [cite:hz10s_aa_sufa] [cite:gi9_an_sufa] Israel resumed work on it on 4 October 2026. Israel says the line is "designed to prevent possible attacks by Iran-backed armed groups." [cite:hz10s_aa_sufa]

The response came the next day. Syria's foreign ministry said completing the project breached the 1974 agreement and UN resolutions. Qatar called it a "flagrant breach of international principles"; Jordan said the Israeli measures "violated the UN Charter and Israel's obligations under the 1974 agreement" and called for an end to the occupation of Syrian territory; Kuwait called the incursions "a dangerous escalation" and urged enforcement of the 1974 agreement. [cite:gi9_an_sufa] [cite:gi9a_an_sufa] The United Arab Emirates, which normalised relations with Israel in 2020, condemned the "resumption of excavation and bulldozing operations" on the "Sufa 53 line" and the construction of fortifications, and reaffirmed its "categorical rejection of any violation of Syria's sovereignty." [cite:gi9_uae_sufa]

The positions of Syria and the UN

The UN's position from the first week was that the 1974 agreement stood. UNDOF said Israel was in "violation of the 1974 disengagement agreement"; Secretary-General António Guterres said he was "deeply concerned by the recent and extensive violations of Syria's sovereignty," insisted the agreement "remains in force" and called for withdrawal. [cite:hz10s_almon_katz] [cite:hz10s_un_guterres] The UN envoy Geir Pedersen said in February 2025 that "the Israelis need to withdraw," and in July 2025 Guterres called the violations of Syria's sovereignty "unacceptable." [cite:gi9_wiki_syriainv] Syria's position under President Ahmed al-Sharaa has been to seek a security arrangement, not war. In Paris on 6 January 2026 the two sides agreed to a US-supervised de-escalation cell; [cite:hz10s_almon_paris] Syria's foreign minister met an Israeli delegation in Jordan on 23 August 2026, and Damascus said its priority was an Israeli return to the lines of 8 December 2024 and that the Golan's final status was not yet on the table. [cite:hz10s_eb_amman] On 23 September al-Sharaa said the talks had reached "almost 90%" agreement before Israel added conditions; that is his account, and no Israeli response to it was reported. [cite:hz10s_jpost_sharaa90] Katz was quoted in June 2026 as saying, "we will not withdraw from the security zone in Syria." [cite:hz10s_jpost_slim]

As of October 2026 there is no signed agreement; Israeli forces hold the buffer zone and the Hermon summit, and the Sufa 53 works have resumed. [cite:hz10s_eb_sufa] The documented facts are the seizure, the stated indefinite duration, the demand for a demilitarised south and the fortifications. The stated purpose, in every Israeli statement found, is security: keeping hostile forces from the Golan frontier. No Israeli government decision or bill has claimed sovereignty over the ground taken in 2024, and the Carnegie Middle East Center, describing a "ring of buffer zones," treated it as a security order "at the expense of the sovereign rights" of Syria rather than an annexation. [cite:gi9_carnegie_ring] The precedent cuts both ways: Israel returned the town of Quneitra to Syrian civil control under the 1974 agreement, after destroying much of it according to a UN-commissioned report, [cite:gi12_wiki_quneitra] [scene:gi12_withdrawals|The withdrawals] and it extended its law to the rest of the Golan seven years later.
