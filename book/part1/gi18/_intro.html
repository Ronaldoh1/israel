Part Seventeen tests an argument rather than a document: that Greater Israel, as a state programme, presupposes the founding of 1948, and that without it there would be no programme to record. It first asks whether every programme found in this volume ran through the institutions of a state, whether the idea of a larger land was older than the state, and how close the founding came to not happening, from the two-thirds vote of November 1947 to the war of 1948. It then revisits the "Uganda Test": the documented alternatives of Argentina, East Africa, the territorialists and Birobidzhan, and what justifications and what kind of "Greater" claim could have survived a move away from the biblical land. Counterfactuals are labelled as arguments throughout. Last, it sets the arguments about legitimacy side by side, each attributed to the state, body or court that makes it, and separates two questions often run together: the legitimacy of the state within its pre-1967 lines, and the holding of land beyond them.
