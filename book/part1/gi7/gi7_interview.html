The "Very Much" Interview

On the evening of Tuesday 12 August 2025 the Israeli channel i24NEWS broadcast an interview with Netanyahu conducted by the presenter Sharon Gal. The exchange that made headlines lasted seconds. Within four days the governments of more than thirty Arab and Muslim-majority states had condemned it in a joint statement. [cite:gi7_na_i24] [cite:gi7_afp_i24] [cite:gi7_oman_joint] This section sets out what was asked and answered, as far as the published reports allow; what the states concerned said; what Israel's government said in reply; and what the remark does and does not establish.

The question and the answer

Gal handed Netanyahu a piece of jewellery, which he said was for Sara Netanyahu, and described it as a "map of the Promised Land." The object was not clearly visible on air. Haaretz reported that Gal's company sells pendants shaped like "Greater Israel," with an outline that takes in parts of Egypt, all of Jordan, Lebanon and parts of Syria and Iraq. [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] [cite:gi7_lbc_i24] Gal then asked whether Netanyahu felt connected to that vision.

The exact words of the exchange are reported differently, and no Hebrew transcript or official English rendering was found for this book. The Media Line, Lebanon's LBCI and The New Arab report the answer as "Very much"; L'Orient Today as "Very much so." [cite:gi7_tml_i24] [cite:gi7_lbc_i24] [cite:gi7_na_i24] [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] A second New Arab report says he felt "very attached" to the vision and described himself as on a "historic and spiritual mission." [cite:gi7_na_outrage] Agence France-Presse and Al Jazeera report a further question, whether he subscribed to a vision of Greater Israel, answered "absolutely"; AFP adds that he said "If you ask me what I think, we're there," then turned to Israel's founding and its "great mission" of ensuring its continued existence. [cite:gi7_afp_i24] [cite:gi7_aje_joint] The common ground is narrow but clear: asked about the vision of Greater Israel after being handed a map of it, Netanyahu affirmed a personal attachment to it and framed his role as a generational mission. In the same interview, speaking of Gaza, he said: "We are not pushing them out, but we are allowing them to leave." [cite:gi7_aje_joint] [cite:gi7_afp_i24]

The neighbours answer

Jordan, whose whole territory lay inside the pendant's outline, was among the first to answer, on 13 August. Its Foreign Ministry called the remarks "a dangerous and provocative escalation, a threat to the sovereignty of states," and its spokesman rejected what he called "delusional claims." [cite:gi7_na_outrage] [cite:gi7_afp_i24] [cite:gi7_tml_i24] Egypt's Foreign Ministry said it had "requested clarification on this matter," that the remarks reflected a rejection of peace, and that "there is no way to achieve peace except through a return to negotiations," restating its call for a Palestinian state on the lines of 4 June 1967 with East Jerusalem as its capital. [cite:gi7_ahram_egypt] [cite:gi7_afp_i24] [cite:gi7_na_outrage] Saudi Arabia, the state whose normalisation Netanyahu had made the centrepiece of his 2023 UN speech, expressed its "complete rejection of the settlement and expansionist ideas and projects adopted by the Israeli occupation authorities." [cite:gi7_ahram_egypt] The Arab League called the remarks "a blatant violation of the sovereignty of Arab states" and "a serious threat to collective Arab national security"; Qatar called them "an extension of the (Israeli) occupation's approach based on arrogance"; Iraq's Foreign Ministry said they revealed "expansionist ambitions"; and the Palestinian Authority called them "a disregard of the legitimate rights of the Palestinian people." [cite:gi7_ahram_egypt] [cite:gi7_na_outrage] [cite:gi7_afp_i24]

The collective answer followed. A joint statement dated 16 August 2025 and published by Oman's Foreign Ministry carried the names of the foreign ministers of 31 states (Oman, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Algeria, Morocco, Palestine, Iraq, Jordan, Egypt, Syria, Lebanon, Libya, Yemen, Mauritania, Sudan, Somalia, Türkiye, Bangladesh, Chad, Comoros, Djibouti, Gambia, Indonesia, the Maldives, Nigeria, Pakistan, Senegal and Sierra Leone) and of the secretaries-general of the Arab League, the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation and the Gulf Cooperation Council. They "strongly condemn" Netanyahu's remarks "on the so-called 'Greater Israel'," calling them "a blatant violation of international law and a direct threat to Arab national security," and warned against the Israeli government's "expansionist approach in the West Bank." [cite:gi7_oman_joint] Al Jazeera reported the statement as released on Friday 15 August. [cite:gi7_aje_joint] The signatories included the UAE, Bahrain and Morocco, which normalised relations with Israel under the Abraham Accords in 2020, and Egypt and Jordan, at peace with Israel by treaty since 1979 and 1994. [cite:gi7_oman_joint] [scene:gi8_jordan|Part Eight: Jordan] [scene:gi8_sinai|Part Eight: Sinai and Egypt] France, according to diplomatic sources quoted by Haaretz, reaffirmed its commitment to Jordan's recognised borders. [cite:gi7_olj_pendant]

The remarks came in the same days as a West Bank decision. On 14 August 2025 the finance minister, Bezalel Smotrich, announced the advance of more than 3,000 housing units in the E1 area between Jerusalem and Ma'ale Adumim, saying it would bury the idea of a Palestinian state. [cite:gi7_tml_i24] [cite:wb_itv_e1] Several Arab statements treated the two together, which is why the joint statement's warning concerned the West Bank as much as the pendant's wider outline. [cite:gi7_oman_joint] [scene:gi6_plans|Part Six: the annexation track]

What Israel said

No public, on-record statement from the Prime Minister's Office clarifying or withdrawing the remark was found. According to Haaretz, as reported by L'Orient Today, the Prime Minister's Office and the Foreign Ministry told Jordan and Egypt, which had asked for clarification, that Netanyahu had not used the words "Greater Israel" and was not responsible for the headlines written after the interview. Haaretz described the replies as an attempt to sweep the matter under the rug. [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] [cite:gi7_afp_i24] The reply is accurate as far as it goes: in every account the phrase was the interviewer's, and Netanyahu answered a question about it. It does not deny the answer.

What the remark establishes

Three claims need to be kept apart. The first is documented: on 12 August 2025 the prime minister of Israel, shown a map described as the Promised Land and asked about the vision of Greater Israel, affirmed his attachment to it on television, and his government did not publicly retract the answer. [cite:gi7_tml_i24] [cite:gi7_afp_i24] [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] The second is also documented: the governments whose territory lay inside the outline took the remark as a threat to their sovereignty and said so, jointly and separately. [cite:gi7_oman_joint] [cite:gi7_na_outrage] The third is an inference: that the remark reveals a policy to take territory from Jordan, Egypt, Lebanon, Syria or Iraq. Netanyahu stated a personal and spiritual attachment, not a policy, and named no border. No law, cabinet decision or coalition guideline found for this volume claims territory in any of those states; Israel's peace treaties with Egypt and Jordan set agreed international boundaries, as Part Eight describes, and the formal annexation track documented in Part Six concerns the West Bank. [scene:gi6_votes|Part Six: the Knesset votes] [scene:gi12_withdrawals|Part Twelve: the withdrawals] Critics, among them the joint statement's signatories, answer that a sitting prime minister's endorsement of the phrase, in the same week as E1, cannot be separated from the government's "expansionist approach in the West Bank"; whether the ground Israeli forces have held in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria since 2024 fits a wider pattern is weighed in Parts Nine and Thirteen. [cite:gi7_oman_joint] [scene:gi9_syria|Part Nine: the buffer belt] [scene:gi13_record|Part Thirteen: settled, contested and open] The remark is evidence of how Netanyahu chose to describe his mission; it is not, by itself, evidence of a plan.
