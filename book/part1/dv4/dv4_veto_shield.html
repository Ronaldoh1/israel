The Veto Shield: Washington at the Security Council, 1972–2026

Aid is the most visible thing Washington gives Israel. The veto is the one no other government can give. The United Nations Security Council is the only UN body whose decisions can bind member states, and any one of its five permanent members can block a draft resolution by voting against it. [cite:unsc_us_vetoes] Since 1972 the United States has used that power, again and again, to stop the Council from adopting drafts critical of Israel. This section counts the vetoes, shows what they blocked, and notes the few times Washington chose not to use them.

The count. The Jewish Virtual Library, which keeps a running list, records fifty-one American vetoes of draft resolutions critical of Israel, from the first, on 10 September 1972, concerning Israeli attacks on Lebanon (draft S/10784), to the most recent on its list, on 18 September 2025 (draft S/2025/583). [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes] A different count by the Chicago Council on Global Affairs found forty-five such vetoes by December 2023, slightly more than half of the eighty-nine vetoes the United States had cast on every subject since 1945. [cite:ccga_vetoes] The two counts differ in their cut-off dates and in which drafts they classify as concerning Israel; both show the same thing, that defending Israel has been the single largest use of the American veto.

What the vetoes blocked. The drafts ranged across every part of the conflict: Israeli operations in Lebanon and Gaza, settlements in the territories occupied since 1967, the status of Jerusalem, and Palestinian statehood. On 18 February 2011 the United States vetoed a draft declaring Israeli settlements established since 1967 illegal; on 18 December 2017 a draft concerning the American decision to recognize Jerusalem as Israel's capital; and on 18 April 2024 a draft that would have admitted Palestine as a full member state of the United Nations. [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes] During the Gaza war it vetoed six drafts calling for a ceasefire. The sixth, on 18 September 2025, demanded an immediate, unconditional and permanent ceasefire, the release of all hostages and the lifting of restrictions on humanitarian aid; the other fourteen members of the Council supported it. The American representative, Morgan Ortagus, said the draft failed to condemn Hamas or recognize Israel's right to defend itself and "wrongly legitimizes the false narratives benefiting Hamas." [cite:dv4_un_veto_sep2025]

When Washington did not veto. The shield has gaps, and they show that each veto was a choice. In 1949, when the General Assembly voted to put Jerusalem under an international regime, the United States voted with Israel against the resolution, but the Assembly passed it anyway; Assembly resolutions bind no one, and it was never carried out. [cite:dv3_wiki_res303] In December 2016, in the last weeks of the Obama administration, the United States abstained on Resolution 2334, which declared that Israeli settlements in territory occupied since 1967, including East Jerusalem, have "no legal validity" and demanded a halt to settlement activity; it passed by fourteen votes to none. [cite:deccan_unsc_2334] Israel's government denounced it, it contained no enforcement mechanism, and the next administration moved the other way. [cite:deccan_unsc_2334] The abstention showed what the veto had been preventing: without it, the Council would have stated, on a range of questions, positions that most of its members held.

The other side of the argument. Israel's defenders argue that the veto corrects an imbalance elsewhere in the UN system. The UN Human Rights Council keeps a permanent agenda item on Israel and the occupied territories, the only country-specific item on its permanent agenda, which Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon criticized; and a pro-Israel monitoring group counted seventeen General Assembly resolutions on Israel in 2024 against seven on all other countries combined. [cite:unwatch_item7] [cite:unwatch_unga2024] Both things are true at once: Israel is singled out by some UN bodies, and it is protected, by Washington, in the one body that can bind.

What the shield means for the parent test. Only the five permanent members hold a veto, and the record of more than fifty vetoes cast for Israel is Washington's. [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes] The veto did not create the state, and it cannot make the General Assembly, the courts in The Hague or other governments agree with Israel. What it has done, for more than half a century, is keep the world's only binding forum from imposing consequences on Israeli policy that most of its members were willing to vote for. In the language of Part One, it is a thing supplied that Israel could not have had from anyone else.
