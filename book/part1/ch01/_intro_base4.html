The two peoples at the center of this history are not uniform blocs, and the land has never held only two. This chapter describes the communities themselves before the political story begins: their lineages, a long shared history in the Muslim world, the minorities who fit neither side of the usual shorthand, the difference between a religion and a political movement, and the arguments each tradition has had with itself. It comes here because the history that follows is full of statements about "the Jews," "the Arabs" or "the Christians," and the record speaks instead of named institutions, movements and individuals.

It begins with the Jewish lineages, Mizrahi, Sephardi and Ashkenazi, and the genetic record: a 2010 study that compared 14 Jewish communities with 69 other populations, the 2013 test of the Khazar claim, and the recognition of Ethiopia's Beta Israel in 1973 and 1975.[cite:behar_2010_nature] [cite:behar_2013_khazar] [cite:w_ethiopian_jews_israel] A section on coexistence runs from the Babylonian exile of 586 BCE through al-Andalus and the expulsion from Spain in 1492 to the Ottoman millet, weighing the "golden age" and "persecution" readings against each other.[cite:huc_farhud] [cite:stillman_jews_arab_lands] The land's other communities follow: the Druze, the Negev Bedouin, the Circassians, the Samaritans and Palestinian Christians. Then comes the distinction between Judaism and Zionism, a movement founded at Basel in August 1897, with the Jewish positions set against it, from Reform's Pittsburgh Platform of 1885 to Satmar's VaYoel Moshe of 1958.[cite:basel_program] [cite:pittsburgh_platform_1885] [cite:wiki_satmar] A section on dissent shows that opposition to Zionism, and critique of the state, came from every Jewish lineage, and sets a 2025 poll of American Jews beside it.[cite:wapo_jews_poll] The chapter ends with Christians and one verse, Genesis 12:3, read in opposite ways, and with the Vatican, which withheld recognition of Israel until 1993.[cite:wiki_fundamental_agreement]

The strongest case for each position is given in its own terms. What the chapter shows throughout is how much variety the collective words conceal.
