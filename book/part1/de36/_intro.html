The Dark Enlightenment was written in English, mostly in San Francisco and Shanghai, and most of what this book has examined happened in the United States. This part looks outward. The first section maps who outside America has documented ties to the texts: where Nick Land lives and publishes, where Curtis Yarvin has been invited to speak and what he said there, and which governments the authors praise, keeping the rule that a writer praising a state is not that state adopting his ideas. The second asks whether publics elsewhere would want the kind of rule the texts propose, using the best cross-national polling on strong leaders and military rule. The third follows foreign and sovereign money, which is documented in the network's companies and not in its theorists. The fourth examines China's social credit system, the one existing system often described as the integrated machine this book's worst case imagines, separating what it is from what it is rumoured to be, and closes by testing the worst-case reading against the material.
