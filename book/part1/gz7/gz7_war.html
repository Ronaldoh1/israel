The Course of the War

The siege. Two days after the attack, on 9 October 2023, Israel's defence minister Yoav Gallant announced: "We are putting a complete siege on Gaza … No electricity, no food, no water, no gas – it's all closed." [cite:gz_aj_siege] Israel described its aims as a "defensive and just war to eliminate Hamas and to secure the release of our hostages," the 251 people taken into Gaza on October 7. [cite:gz_toi_rafah] [cite:gz_hrw_oct7] The air campaign was followed by a ground invasion, and the war went on until January 2025.

The first ceasefire, and its collapse. A ceasefire took effect on 19 January 2025, planned in three stages of forty-two days each. In the first stage Hamas released 33 hostages, and Israel agreed to free up to 1,904 Palestinian prisoners over the whole deal. When Hamas refused an Israeli proposal to extend the first stage instead of moving to the second, Israel stopped all aid from entering Gaza on 2 March. On 18 March it resumed its strikes, citing "Hamas's refusal to release hostages and its rejection of proposals to extend the cease-fire"; Hamas said the second stage should proceed as originally planned. Netanyahu declared that Israel had "resumed combat in full force." [cite:gz_wiki_ceasefire2025]

The second ceasefire. In October 2025 Israel and Hamas accepted the first stage of a plan put forward by the United States and mediated with Qatar, Egypt and Turkey, and a ceasefire took effect on 10 October. [cite:gz_wiki_timeline2025] [cite:gz_aj_year2026] On 13 October Hamas released all twenty remaining living hostages, and Israel freed more than 1,900 Palestinians: about 250 serving life sentences for attacks on Israelis, and about 1,700 taken from Gaza during the war and held without charge. [cite:gz_kt_hostages] The remains of the dead hostages were returned over the following months, some of them disputed, until the army announced that the last had been recovered. The UN Security Council endorsed the plan's "Board of Peace" and authorized an international stabilization force. Israel's forces held a line inside the strip, the "yellow line", and repeatedly fired on people who crossed or approached it, saying they threatened its forces; Gaza health officials reported civilians killed near the line. [cite:gz_wiki_timeline2025]

A year on. When the ceasefire marked its first anniversary, in October 2026, the Gaza health ministry counted 1,493 Palestinians killed and 5,299 wounded since it began. Aid trucks were entering at about a third of the agreed rate, no international stabilization force had deployed, and Israel controlled nearly two-thirds of the strip, against the roughly half it was to keep.
