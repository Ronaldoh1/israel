Who Gains: the Money

Continued conflict with Hezbollah, the Houthis and Iran has been accompanied by record sums moving through documented channels. Israel's military spending rose 65 percent in 2024 to $46.5 billion, 8.8 percent of its economy, by the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute's count. [cite:hm_globes_sipri] Its defence exports reached a record $14.795 billion the same year, up 11.7 percent; 54 percent went to Europe, and air-defence systems made up about 48 percent. [cite:hm_globes_exports] The European share, in the years of Russia's war in Ukraine, is a reminder that demand for Israeli weapons does not come only from Israel's own wars; how much of the record the fighting with Hezbollah and Iran produced is not something the export figures can settle.

On the American side, much of the money returns home. Under the 2016 memorandum of understanding, Israel's permission to spend part of its US military financing on Israeli industry is being phased out, so the aid is spent in the United States. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] Since 2016, US production funding for the David's Sling and Arrow 3 missile-defence systems has been split with American partners, Raytheon and Boeing respectively. [cite:ow8_dn_coprod2016] On 9 October 2026 a Raytheon–Rafael joint venture delivered the first Tamir interceptors, the missile Iron Dome fires, built at a plant in Camden, Arkansas. [cite:ow8_rtx_camden] By April 2025 Israel had 751 active US arms-sale cases worth $39.2 billion. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] And the defence of Israel draws on American stocks directly: in the June 2025 war with Iran the United States fired 100 to 150 THAAD interceptors, about a quarter of its supply. [cite:ow7_cnn_thaad2025]

The money record establishes who receives. It does not establish that anyone prolonged a war in order to receive it. The public record examined for this book contains no document, testimony or court finding showing a decision on war or ceasefire in Lebanon, Yemen or Iran taken to secure aid, contracts or exports. That beneficiaries exist is documented; that they caused the wars is a separate claim, and on the evidence here it is unsupported. Part Twelve sets out the American side of the ledger. [scene:hz12_costs|What it cost America]

Who Gains: the Politics

The political argument centres on Netanyahu. His documented incentives are set out in the Hamas volume: a corruption trial running since 2020, from which he asked President Isaac Herzog for a pardon in November 2025 and sought further delays in April 2026; no state commission of inquiry into 7 October three years on; and a New York Times investigation, based on more than 110 officials, reporting that he shelved a Gaza truce under coalition threat, which his office said "defames Israel." [cite:hm_npr_pardon] [cite:hm_toi_trialdelay] [cite:gz_ynet_inquiry2026] [cite:hm_toi_nyt_prolonged] [scene:hm11_incentives|The Hamas Architecture: why the war lasted] Whether the same incentives operated on the Lebanese front is a separate question.

On the Lebanese front, the record cuts both ways. In November 2024 Netanyahu accepted a ceasefire with Hezbollah that his own national security minister, Itamar Ben-Gvir, called "a historic mistake"; the security cabinet approved it ten to one. Netanyahu gave three reasons: to focus on Iran, to give the army a rest and replenish its stocks, and to separate the fronts and isolate Hamas. [cite:hz7_bg_ceasefire] [cite:hz8_jvl_netanyahu] A leader keeping war going for its own sake would not obviously have signed that truce over a coalition partner's objection. A leader choosing which war to fight next, and when, might have. Both readings fit the document; neither is proved by it.

The Case Against the Thesis

The groups' own acts. Hezbollah opened the 2023 front itself, on 8 October, "in solidarity with the victorious Palestinian resistance," and tied its fire to Gaza: "If there is a cease-fire in Gaza, we will stop," Naim Qassem told AP. [cite:hz7_aj_8oct] [cite:hz7_ml_qassem] On 27 July 2024 a rocket killed twelve Druze children and teenagers on a football pitch in Majdal Shams; Israel and the United States attributed it to Hezbollah, Human Rights Watch found the damage consistent with a Hezbollah Falaq-type rocket, and Hezbollah denied any connection. [cite:hz7_wiki_majdal] [cite:hz7_aj_majdal] About 60,000 residents of northern Israel were displaced. [cite:hz7_afp_north_return] On 2 March 2026 Hezbollah again fired first, after Khamenei's killing. [cite:hz8_am_mar2] In 1995 Qassem had stated the rule plainly: "whenever the Israeli enemy shells and harms civilians in our villages, we will shell northern Palestine." [cite:hz4_hrw_pawns] By a Reuters count of July 2025, the Houthis had targeted more than 100 merchant ships since November 2023, sunk four and killed at least eight sailors; a UN panel found them "indiscriminately targeting vessels." [cite:hz10_olj_factbox] [cite:hz10_un_poe2024]

Iran's own aims. Khomeini declared in March 1980, "We should try hard to export our revolution to the world," naming America and Israel. [cite:hz1_merip_khomeini] An Argentine appeals court ruled in April 2024 that Iran ordered the 1992 and 1994 bombings in Buenos Aires and that Hezbollah carried them out; Iran denies involvement. [cite:hz4_bat_cassation] Analysts who study the network describe a patron that "seeks the product of militia actions, not the process of controlling them," and allies such as the Houthis that are more than mere proxies. [cite:hz10_npr_roule] [cite:hz10_conv_proxies] These are aims and acts of the groups and their patron, not inventions of their enemies.

Restraint and ceasefires. Israel accepted the April Understanding of 1996, which committed both sides not to target civilians; withdrew from Lebanon in May 2000; and accepted the November 2024 truce. [cite:hz4_jvl_april] [cite:hz4_crs_sla] [cite:hz7_bg_ceasefire] The United States paused a shipment of 2,000-pound bombs in May 2024 over a planned assault on Rafah, a hold President Trump lifted in January 2025. [cite:ow8_axios_pause] And in May 2025 the United States agreed a truce with the Houthis that did not cover their conflict with Israel; Netanyahu said Israel "will defend itself by itself." [cite:hz10_aje_truce] [cite:hz10_wiki_truce2025] In that instance Washington stepped out of a war with an "axis" group while Israel's continued, which is the opposite of being drawn in.

Two Readings

The first reading: the axis is the standing justification. On this reading, the threat from Hezbollah and Iran was repeatedly described as nearer or more nearly removed than the record bore out, each campaign renewed the enemy it was meant to end, Israel itself armed proxies when it suited, and the result was record budgets, record exports, tens of billions in American aid and a prime minister whose trial waited while the wars went on. [cite:hz11_rferl_spycables] [cite:hz11_bg_gabbard] [cite:hz4_crs_sla] [cite:hm_globes_sipri] [cite:hm_an_costsofwar] [cite:hm_toi_trialdelay]

The second reading: the axis is the reason. On this reading, Hezbollah, the Houthis and Iran chose to attack, killed civilians, stated their aims openly and were funded by a state that ordered bombings abroad; Israel accepted truces, the United States restrained it and broke from it, and the money followed the threat rather than creating it. [cite:hz7_aj_8oct] [cite:hz7_wiki_majdal] [cite:hz4_bat_cassation] [cite:hz7_bg_ceasefire] [cite:ow8_axios_pause] [cite:hz10_aje_truce]

What survives the test is narrower than the thesis and harder to dismiss. The threats were real, and the groups chose their own wars. The record also shows threats described as closer than they proved, campaigns that renewed the enemy they targeted, an Israeli practice of arming proxies of its own, and documented beneficiaries of every round. Whether those beneficiaries steer the wars, rather than profit from them, is the part of the thesis the evidence does not reach.
