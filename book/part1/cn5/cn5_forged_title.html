The Forged Title: Constantine's Donation and the False Decretals

The institution that had spent four centuries separating genuine scripture from forgery built its own worldly power, in part, on two forgeries. The irony was noticed late, and the man who proved the larger one was rewarded with a job in the papal service. Between those two facts lies the story of how the bishop of Rome became a ruler of territory, and of how the documents that justified the change were themselves inventions.

The territory came first. In 754 Pope Stephen II crossed the Alps to meet Pippin, king of the Franks, and at Quierzy Pippin promised to restore papal lands taken by the Lombard king Aistulf; no record of the promise itself survives. Stephen anointed Pippin and his sons. In 755 and 756 Pippin invaded Italy, defeated Aistulf, and the keys of the surrendered cities, with a list of them, were placed on the altar of Old St Peter's. [cite:vt2_britannica_pippin] Papal accounts say the grant included the exarchate of Ravenna and the Roman duchy, much of it imperial territory to which the Frankish king had no clear legal claim. [cite:vt2_britannica_pippin] From 756 until 1870 the popes ruled a state of their own across central Italy. [cite:vt2_wiki_papalstates]

A territory needs a title, and in the same decades someone supplied one. The document known as the Donation of Constantine presents itself as a decree of the first Christian emperor in favour of Pope Sylvester I. In it Constantine grants the pope supremacy over the four great sees of Alexandria, Antioch, Jerusalem and Constantinople, the Lateran palace, and "the city of Rome, and all the provinces, places and cities of Italy and the western regions". [cite:cn_wiki_donation] It was composed by an unknown writer, probably in the eighth century. [cite:cn_wiki_donation] [cite:vt2_britannica_constantine] Its authenticity was contested as early as 1001, when the chancery of the emperor Otto III denied it, but popes cited it as genuine: Leo IX, in his letter of 1054 to Michael Cerularius, patriarch of Constantinople, was the first to rely on it, and it became a standard weapon in disputes over papal authority for the next four centuries. [cite:cn_wiki_donation]

The second forgery reshaped church law. In the ninth century a writer working in the Frankish province of Reims, almost certainly before 850 and using the library of the monastery of Corbie, produced a collection now known as the False Decretals, under the name of "Isidore Mercator". It contains more than ninety forged papal letters attributed to the bishops of Rome of the first centuries. [cite:cn_wiki_pseudoisidore] Their main object was to give accused bishops an array of legal protections amounting to near-immunity from their superiors and from secular courts, to defend church property, and to widen the jurisdiction of the bishop of Rome, to whom accused bishops could appeal. [cite:cn_wiki_pseudoisidore] The collection was absorbed into the main channels of church law. The final proof that it was forged came only in 1628, from a Calvinist preacher, David Blondel. [cite:cn_wiki_pseudoisidore]

The man who demolished the Donation was a Roman scholar, Lorenzo Valla, and his motive deserves the same scrutiny this book gives everyone else's. Between 1435 and 1445 Valla worked at the court of Alfonso V of Aragon, king of Naples, who was then in a territorial conflict with the Papal States under Eugene IV; Wikipedia's account of Valla says the conflict "possibly motivated his work". [cite:cn_wiki_valla] In 1439 and 1440 he wrote his declamation on the "falsely believed and lying" Donation, showing from its Latin, its titles and its anachronisms that it could not have been written in Constantine's time. [cite:cn_wiki_valla] [cite:cn_wiki_donation] The power that paid him had a reason to want the papacy's title weakened. His proof was sound regardless.

What followed shows how the institution handled exposure. In 1444 Valla was tried before the Inquisition and was saved from prison by Alfonso's intervention. Three years later the new pope, Nicholas V, invited him to Rome, where he became a papal scribe and, in 1455, a papal secretary. [cite:cn_wiki_valla] The treatise was not formally published until 1517, the year Luther posted his theses, and it became popular among Protestants. [cite:cn_wiki_valla] The papacy absorbed the critic and kept the territory; it ruled the Papal States for another four centuries, until the Italian army entered Rome in 1870. [cite:vt2_wiki_papalstates]

The forged Donation never had anything to do with the canon of scripture. Its place in this book is as evidence of a pattern. The guardians of the authentic list were willing, for centuries, to rest their claims to land and jurisdiction on documents that did not pass the tests they applied to gospels. When the forgery was exposed by an outsider with motives of his own, the response was first a trial, then a salaried post.
