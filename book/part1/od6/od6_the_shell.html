The Shell: Propaganda Due

There is one modern case in which a lodge really was used to hide power, and it belongs in this book precisely because it is documented. Propaganda Due, P2, was a Masonic lodge founded in 1877 under the Grand Orient of Italy. The businessman Licio Gelli became its master in the 1970s and turned it into a clandestine network. The Grand Orient suspended the lodge in 1976, and Gelli ran it as an irregular body. [cite:vt30_wiki_p2]

On 17 March 1981, in a police search of Gelli's property, investigators found a list of 962 members: ministers, military officers, intelligence chiefs, journalists and bankers, among them Roberto Calvi of the Banco Ambrosiano. Its publication in May 1981 brought down the government of Arnaldo Forlani, and Law 17 of 25 January 1982 dissolved the lodge. [cite:vt30_wiki_p2] Investigators also seized a document, the "Plan for Democratic Rebirth", setting out a programme to reshape Italy's institutions and media. [cite:vt30_wiki_p2]

The parliamentary commission led by Tina Anselmi concluded in its 1984 majority report that P2 was a secret organisation aimed at undermining Italian democracy; a minority report by Massimo Teodori argued that it was part of the political system rather than an outgrowth of it. [cite:vt30_wiki_p2] The courts took a narrower view: in the 1990s members were acquitted of conspiracy against the state, though Gelli was convicted of misleading investigators in the case of the 1980 Bologna station bombing. [cite:vt30_wiki_p2] Calvi was found dead in London in June 1982; his death was first ruled a suicide and later prosecuted as murder. His bank's dealings with the Vatican's Institute for the Works of Religion are set out in [scene:vt10_ambrosiano_calvi|The Vatican Architecture: Ambrosiano and Calvi].

The case cuts against both versions of the myth. It shows that a lodge's forms, secrecy and oaths can be used as a shell by a network of powerful men, which is what the myth always claimed. It also shows how such a network is found: by a police search, a list of names, a parliamentary commission and the courts, and its own Grand Orient had already disowned it. [cite:vt30_wiki_p2] P2 is evidence about P2. It is not evidence about Freemasonry in general, and it involves no people as a people: it involves 962 named individuals and the institutions they served.
