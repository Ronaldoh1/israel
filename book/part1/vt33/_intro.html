Part Thirty-Two asks whether the Christian empire closed the ancient mystery schools and erased what they taught about the soul, and answers by naming who acted, when, and in what document. The first section sets out what the mystery cults and the philosophical schools actually taught about the soul's descent and ascent: the higher grade of "seeing" at Eleusis, the open initiations of Samothrace, the Orphic gold tablets placed in graves, Pythagoras's doctrine of the soul's rebirth, the seven planetary grades of Mithras, the Isis initiation, and the Neoplatonists from Plotinus to Proclus. The second follows the closings from Theodosius's laws of 391–392 through the destruction of the Serapeum, the end of Eleusis, the killing of Hypatia, the conversion of temples into churches, Justinian's decree against teaching philosophy at Athens and the closing of the Isis temple at Philae, and grades the popular story of the burning of the Library of Alexandria. The third weighs what was lost against what survived, following the doctrine of ascent into Christian mystical theology, into Arabic philosophy and back to Renaissance Florence, and ends with the Sophia platform's reading of the pattern and the strongest objections to it.
