No general Arab order to leave has ever been found, and mainstream scholarship no longer defends this account. In 1961 the Irish writer Erskine Childers reported in The Spectator that he had examined the British and American monitoring records of 1948 Arab broadcasts and found no evacuation orders, but repeated appeals to civilians to stay. [cite:spectator_childers_1961] In 1988 the Israeli historian Benny Morris, working from Israel's own military and state archives, concluded that military attack, expulsion and fear were the main causes of flight. [cite:w_morris_birth]

There is a small kernel: Morris found that some local Arab commanders ordered evacuations of women and children in particular places. But expulsions are documented at Deir Yassin, Lydda, Ramla and elsewhere, and about 500 villages were depopulated as 700,000 to 750,000 Palestinians were displaced. [cite:morris_1948] Historians still disagree about whether the Haganah's Plan Dalet of 10 March 1948 was a blueprint for removal or a military plan that gave local commanders discretion. [cite:wiki_plan_dalet]
