This part follows the tools that can tell where a person is and what is on the devices they carry. The first section explains the three ways a phone reports its location, how app and advertising data became a product that federal agencies buy without a warrant, how the telephone companies sold location and searched call records for police, and what the courts decided in 2018 and 2026. The second section separates three kinds of phone tools that are often confused, remote spyware, forensic extraction and fake phone towers, and traces Pegasus, Paragon's Graphite, Cellebrite, GrayKey and cell-site simulators through American contracts, foreign abuses and the rules that apply. The third turns to cars, wearables and the connected home, distinguishing what such devices could collect from what their makers say they collect and from what the state has documentably obtained, and closes with the book's test of its worst-case reading against the part as a whole.
