The conflict over the land is about a century old, not thousands of years. It began with modern political Zionism in 1897 and the British Mandate, and the first Palestinian national movement was organized jointly by Muslims and Christians. Before that, Jews, Muslims and Christians lived together under Ottoman rule, where the millet system gave Jewish communities their own legal, educational and religious self-governance, though not equality with Muslims.

Across the Muslim-majority world, Jewish communities lived for more than a thousand years, from the Golden Age of Muslim Iberia [cite:menocal_ornament] to Baghdad, where some 140,000 Jews made up about one-third of the city in 1945. Coexistence was never utopian; there was real violence, such as the Granada pogrom of 1066. And the region was not peaceful before 1948: revolts in Iraq, Syria and Palestine followed the European carve-up of 1916–22. But the documented record does not fit a story of ancient, permanent hatred.
