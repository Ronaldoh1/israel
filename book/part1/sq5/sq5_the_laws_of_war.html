The Laws of War

"Israel has the right to defend itself"

Article 51 of the UN Charter recognises every state's "inherent right" of self-defence. In 2004 the International Court of Justice advised that Article 51 did not justify Israel's separation wall, because Israel controls the occupied territory and the threat it cited came from inside it, not from another state; the opinion was advisory and not unanimous, and the US House voted to reject it. [cite:icj_wall_op] [cite:wiki_wall_op] Israel's defenders argue that Gaza was not occupied after 2005 and that the right applies against groups like Hamas; critics answer that the law of occupation governs instead. [cite:justia_selfdef] Both sides accept that, however the right is classed, the laws of war bind every party: distinction between fighters and civilians, proportionality and precautions. Verdict: disputed in its legal basis; never a licence to set aside the laws of war.

"Israel warns civilians before it strikes"

Israel used warnings in Gaza from at least 2006, and "roof-knocking", a small munition fired at a roof before the main strike, in the wars of 2008–09, 2012 and 2014; in May 2021 a tower housing AP and Al Jazeera was evacuated after warnings and no one was killed. [cite:wiki_roofknock] An Israeli security institute argues the practice goes beyond what the law requires; a UN commission found it cannot count as an effective warning. [cite:inss_roofknock] In October 2023 an Israeli official said roof-knocking would no longer be the norm, and mass evacuation orders and leaflets took its place. [cite:wiki_roofknock] Verdict: true of earlier wars; not true as a rule of the war since 2023.

"Only one side uses human shields"

In November 2023 the White House said US intelligence, separate from Israel's, indicated that Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad used some Gaza hospitals, including Al-Shifa, and tunnels beneath them for military purposes; Gaza's doctors and officials denied it. [cite:jns_us_intel_hospitals] [cite:bangkokpost_shifa_evidence] In 2024 and 2025 CNN and CBS News reported accounts from Israeli soldiers and former detainees that Israeli units sent Palestinian civilians and detainees, including a 16-year-old, into buildings and tunnels ahead of troops; Breaking the Silence, a group of Israeli veterans, said the practice had a name, the "mosquito protocol". The army said its orders strictly forbid it. [cite:cnn_mosquito_protocol] [cite:cbs_human_shields] The law forbids shielding by anyone, and the presence of a shield does not lift the attacker's duties. Verdict: false; the accusation is documented against both sides.

"The Gaza death toll is Hamas propaganda"

Gaza's Health Ministry counts deaths from hospitals and morgues, later adding family reports. Israel questions the figures because the ministry answers to Hamas; the UN has generally treated them as reliable. A study in The Lancet in January 2025 estimated 64,260 deaths from traumatic injury by 30 June 2024, against 37,877 then reported, an undercount of about 41 percent. [cite:gulfnews_lancet_toll] [cite:transcend_lancet_toll] A 2026 study in The Lancet Global Health, the first survey not drawn from ministry records, estimated more than 75,000 violent deaths in the first fifteen months, against about 49,000 reported, with women, children and the elderly about 56 percent of them. [cite:cyprusmail_lancetgh_2026] The ministry does not separate fighters from civilians; Israel says it killed about 20,000 fighters, a figure not independently checked. [cite:yahoo_record_dems_fighters]

Status as of 26 November 2026: the ministry's count stood at 74,742 killed and 176,227 wounded since October 2023, including at least 1,675 killed since the ceasefire of 10 October 2025. [cite:sq_abna_toll] [cite:sq_ncf_index] Verdict: false; independent studies find the count low, not high.
