Who Reads It Abroad

An idea's reach abroad is easy to overstate. Commentators have linked the Dark Enlightenment to governments from Budapest to Beijing on the strength of resemblance: a strong executive here, contempt for parliaments there. Resemblance is not influence. This section uses the same standard as the rest of the book, mapping only documented ties: who wrote where, who published whom, who invited whom to speak, and who cited the texts by name. It keeps two further distinctions. A theorist praising a foreign state is a fact about the theorist, not about the state. And the neighbouring ideas that circulate abroad, from Javier Milei's anarcho-capitalism to Nayib Bukele's security state, are different things with their own texts, as [scene:de16_neighbours_not_the_same|Part Sixteen] sets out for the American neighbours.

Shanghai: where Land writes

The Dark Enlightenment's second author has lived outside the West for most of the century. After leaving the University of Warwick in 1998, Nick Land resurfaced in the 2000s as a journalist in Shanghai, where he wrote the 2012 serial whose title named this body of writing and where he still lived in October 2025. [cite:de4_haider] [cite:de4_spectator] [scene:de4_ccru_acceleration|Part Four] tells that story. His published work in China includes two guidebooks issued in 2010 by China Intercontinental Press, The Shanghai World Expo Guide 2010 and Shanghai Basics. [cite:de36_wiki_land] That is commercial publishing, and no document shows Chinese state funding of his political writing.

Land's own words about China are admiring and specific. The serial names Hong Kong, Singapore and Dubai, in a passage he quotes, as the closest equivalents of the neocameral state, and later uses Singapore, Hong Kong, Taipei and Shanghai as its measure of cities safe to walk at night. [cite:de8_land_de] In its final instalments he sketched three futures for modernity and called "Modernity 2.0," modernisation "re-invigorated from a new ethno-geographical core," the "most encouraging and plausible scenario," adding that "if China remains even approximately on its current track it will be assuredly realized." [cite:de36_land_ebook] In a 2025 interview with The Spectator he called China's mix of communism and capitalism "the greatest political engine of social and economic development the world has ever known." [cite:de4_spectator] None of this shows the Chinese state reading or adopting his work. It shows a writer in China treating China as the model.

Land's books travel through small publishers in several countries: Urbanomic in Britain, which issued his academic writing in Fanged Noumena (2011) and Fission (2014); Imperium Press, which published The Dark Enlightenment in print in 2022; and Passage Publishing in the United States, which issued his Xenosystems writings in 2024. [cite:de36_wiki_land] [cite:de4_spectator] He met Yarvin in person for the first time in San Francisco in February 2026. [cite:de36_wiki_land]

Yarvin's platforms abroad

Yarvin's documented engagements outside the United States are few and recent. In April 2025 the Paris-based review Le Grand Continent, which publishes in French, Italian and Spanish, ran a three-part interview with him conducted by Gilles Gressani and Mathéo Malik. [cite:de36_lgc_bukele] In it he said that "Bukele es otra prueba de que el mundo está maduro para la monarquía," that Bukele, El Salvador's president, is further proof the world is ripe for monarchy. [cite:de36_lgc_bukele] In the same interview he criticised China's Communist Party, which he said would rather "censurar y ocultar con mentiras" (censor and hide with lies) an evident reality, while describing the students and workers of Tiananmen Square as "una operación del NED, es decir, de la CIA con otro nombre," an operation of the National Endowment for Democracy, which he called the CIA by another name. He said the Taliban give their country "una gobernanza mucho mejor que la que podría ofrecer USAID" (much better governance than USAID could offer), and that, if it were up to him, he would close "todas las embajadas en Europa," all American embassies in Europe. [cite:de36_lgc_bukele] Publication in a critical review is a platform, not an endorsement; the interviewers framed him as an opponent to be understood.

In October 2025 Yarvin and the historian David Starkey gave the second Oakeshott Lecture of the year at the Sheldonian Theatre in Oxford. The student newspaper Cherwell reported that the series is not affiliated with the university and that the theatre was hired privately, and that Yarvin argued for a "new Platonic guardian class" and called himself a "Trumptard." [cite:de36_cherwell_oxford] [cite:de36_oxtalks]

His most direct contact with a foreign political party came in Brazil. According to the investigative agency Agência Pública, on 23 November 2025 he spoke to members of the Movimento Brasil Livre (MBL) at the group's festival and academy graduation in the Mooca district of São Paulo, and was received at the office of the São Paulo councilwoman Amanda Vettorazzo. [cite:de36_publica] In late November he was a guest of honour at the national convention of Partido Missão, the party the MBL had just registered, where in a speech of about six minutes he argued against racial quotas and American-style racial-equality policy; he held no role in the party. [cite:de5_icl] Pública's reporting also shows what the MBL's leaders said they were studying: representatives travelled to El Salvador in 2024 "to observe Bukele's policies" and attended his second inauguration, with the stated aim of adopting its security practices, while its leader Renan Santos was described as "flirting with" Milei's economics. [cite:de36_publica] Those are the party's documented models; Yarvin was a visiting speaker.

Argentina: a different label

Argentina is often named as the place where these ideas reached a head of state, and the documented tie runs through Peter Thiel, not the texts. Thiel met President Javier Milei at the Casa Rosada in May 2024 and again on 23 April 2026, when Milei called him "an anarcho-capitalist, just as I am" and said both regarded taxes as theft; no investments were discussed, according to Milei. [cite:de9_bat_milei] Thiel moved with his family to Buenos Aires in the summer of 2026. [cite:de9_fortune_ba] Milei's label is anarcho-capitalism, the belief that markets can replace the state, which is a different doctrine from neocameralism, under which the state is kept and run as a company. [scene:de9_politics_and_argentina|Part Nine] covers the relationship. No documented tie links Yarvin or Land to Milei.

What was looked for and not found

Three absences are worth stating, each with the caveat that an absence from the record is not proof that nothing exists. No elected European politician was found citing Yarvin or Land by name in Britain, France, Germany, Italy or Hungary. No invitation of either writer to a Gulf state was found; Dubai appears in the texts only as an example. And no translation of The Dark Enlightenment itself into Portuguese, Italian, French or German was confirmed. The documented foreign footprint is a handful of platforms, one Brazilian party convention, and a long residence in Shanghai.
