The last part gathers what the volume found. It first keeps a ledger of the claims most often made about Christian Zionism, in print, in sermons and online, and judges each one on the evidence set out in Parts One to Thirteen: documented, contested between named sources, unsupported by any source found, or a myth that the record contradicts. The ledger includes the claims about Cyrus Scofield's life and patrons, among them the story that Samuel Untermyer or the Rothschilds paid for his Bible, which rests on an old antisemitic accusation and is named as such; the claims about what his notes said and how far they sold; the history of the phrase "a land without a people"; what Christian Zionist organisations say they want for Jews, beside what their critics say; and whether evangelicals set American policy. The second section sorts the volume's findings into what is settled, what is contested and what remains open as of 21 October 2026, answers the question the volume set out to test, and closes with the inner reading of Part Thirteen, kept as a labelled reading. No new evidence is introduced: every finding rests on documents already cited.
