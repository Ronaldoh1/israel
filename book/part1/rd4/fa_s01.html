Foreign Agents Before There Was a State

One version of this history holds that the American government never noticed, or never questioned, foreign-directed Zionist operations on American soil. The government did notice. It sounded alarms in the 1940s, opened investigations and brought prosecutions. What the record shows is that its attention fell selectively, on one wing of the movement far more than on the other, and that the convictions it did secure were later erased by presidential pardon.

The legal basis existed from 1938, when Congress passed the Foreign Agents Registration Act. Its sharpest wartime application fell on the Bergson group, whose campaigns are described in [scene:bg_s01|the information war]. Its leader, Hillel Kook, was a Lithuanian-born member of the Irgun working in the United States as "Peter Bergson." In 1944 he opened a "Hebrew Embassy" in Washington. In June of that year his Hebrew Committee of National Liberation registered with the Justice Department's foreign agents section, naming its foreign principal as the "Hebrew nation." At the same time, deportation proceedings were opened against him. British officials had already warned the State Department that he was a "bomb-thrower" [cite:djn_bergson_fara_1944] [cite:ushmm_peter_bergson].

The surveillance went further than registration. FBI files obtained under the Freedom of Information Act by the Wyman Institute show that the State Department instigated an FBI investigation of the group. Agents planted informants inside it, opened its leaders' mail and went through their trash. An internal memo observed that Bergson had "been in the hair of" Secretary of State Cordell Hull [cite:jta_bergson_fbi] [cite:jewishpress_fbi_spied_bergson]. The pressure did not come only from officials. Leaders of established Jewish organizations, among them Morris Waldman of the American Jewish Committee and Nahum Goldmann, urged that Bergson be deported or drafted [cite:jewishpress_bergson_part2]. The rivalry between the mainstream organizations and the Irgun's American delegation thus reached into the government's handling of him.

The mainstream apparatus received different treatment. The Jewish Agency and the American Zionist Emergency Council operated openly throughout the 1940s. Their successor, the American Zionist Council, was ordered to register as a foreign agent only in November 1962, and that order was later withdrawn [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order] [cite:jta_fulbright_jewish_agency_hearing]. For more than two decades after the registration law was passed, then, the main organizations building political support for the state in America were not required to register under it.

The clandestine arms operation tells a third story. It was prosecuted. Charles Winters was convicted under the Neutrality Act in 1949 and served 18 months in prison. Herman "Hank" Greenspun and Al Schwimmer were also convicted, but served no prison time. All three were later pardoned, by three different presidents. Kennedy pardoned Greenspun in 1961. Clinton pardoned Schwimmer in 2000. George W. Bush pardoned Winters posthumously in 2008, after an appeal campaign that included a letter from Steven Spielberg [cite:nbc_bush_pardons_winters] [cite:ina_schwimmer_obit] [cite:wilmerhale_winters_pardon].

So the answer to whether anyone noticed is yes. The State Department, the FBI and British officials noticed, and the Neutrality Act was enforced. But the scrutiny fell on the Irgun-aligned fringe and on individual smugglers, often at the urging of the mainstream organizations themselves. The principal state-building apparatus in America operated openly for two decades before it was asked to register. And the criminal convictions that did result from the arms effort were, in time, wiped away by presidential pardon.
