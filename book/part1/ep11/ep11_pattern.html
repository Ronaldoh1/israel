The Pattern the Record Does Show

Is there a pattern of killing? The documents say no. Every death with a finished official process was ruled suicide, natural causes or illness; the one unsolved homicide, Shapiro's, happened in 1985 and touches Epstein only through Wexner's company. Two processes are still open, Giuffre's and Andriesz's, and one qualified dissent, Freeman's, is due in November. The book says this in the same plain voice it uses for everything else.

Is there a pattern at all? Yes, and it is fully documented. In case after case, testimony was lost before it was taken, and the institution responsible for the person, the evidence or the follow-up failed in a way its own records show.

The mechanism can be stated without naming anyone: evidence decays faster than institutions act. People die, of whatever cause, before anyone takes their testimony. Recordings are destroyed as no longer relevant. Custody rules are broken around the one prisoner who most needed them followed. Whistleblowers' tips are logged and not pursued. None of this requires a killer. All of it is in the record, and none of it can be waved away as a theory.

That is the honest form of the suspicion so many people feel when they read these lists. The documents do not support the claim that witnesses were silenced. They do support the claim that the system charged with hearing them was too slow, too careless, or too uninterested to hear them in time.
