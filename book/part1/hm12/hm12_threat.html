The Language of Threat, and the Record

As the ground invasion began in late October 2023, Netanyahu told Israelis: "Remember what Amalek did to you," invoking the biblical enemy the Israelites were commanded to destroy. South Africa cited the phrase at the International Court of Justice as evidence of genocidal intent; Netanyahu's office called the charge "false and preposterous" and said it reflected "deep historical ignorance," and his supporters said he meant Hamas alone. [cite:hm_na_amalek] Throughout the war, Hamas was presented as the reason for each strike and each refusal, as Part Ten set out. [scene:hm11_claims|Claims made in Hamas's name]

The record of capacity runs the other way. Hamas had an estimated 20,000 to 25,000 fighters before the war; Israel mobilized 360,000 reservists in days, on top of 169,500 active troops. [cite:hm_inss_arsenal] [cite:hm_adn_360k] [cite:hm_almon_idf] Israel spent $46.5 billion on its military in 2024; Hamas's whole military budget was estimated at $100 million to $350 million a year. [cite:hm_globes_sipri] [cite:hm_nbc_money] And while the war went on, the aid did not stop: the United States provided at least $21.7 billion in military aid in the two years after 7 October, on top of the $3.8 billion a year it already committed. [cite:hm_an_costsofwar] [cite:hm_dn_mou] The One-Way Mirror sets out how that aid moves. [scene:ow8_pace|The One-Way Mirror: the pace since October 2023]

Two Readings

The first reading: the threat was inflated. On this reading, a movement that Israel outnumbered more than ten to one and outspent more than a hundred to one was presented in the language of an existential enemy because that language justified the scale of the war, the closures and the aid, and because a government that depended on ministers like Ben-Gvir had reasons to keep the war going; the gap between the rhetoric and the record is itself the finding. [cite:hm_na_amalek] [cite:hm_ynet_bg_foiled] [cite:hm_toi_nyt_prolonged]

The second reading: the threat was real. On this reading, Hamas killed about 1,200 people in a single day and took 251 hostages; the threat to Israelis is measured by what an enemy has done and intends, not by the size of its budget; and Israel's government denies that politics or Ben-Gvir's demands determined its decisions. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7] [cite:hm_ynet_bg_foiled]

The record settles the asymmetry, the aid and Ben-Gvir's own words. Whether the language of threat was dishonest or sincere is a judgement about intent, which documents rarely prove; the reader has the evidence for both.
