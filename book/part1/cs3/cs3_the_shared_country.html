The Shared Country

Jews were already in the land. Historians call the community the Old Yishuv: about 24,000 people in 1880, most in Jerusalem, living under Ottoman rule beside Muslim and Christian Arabs, before the immigration that began in 1881 cost it its leading place. [cite:rs_rd0_barin_2014_pop] [cite:rs_rd1_nli_oldyishuv] Under the Ottoman millet system each community ran its own religious law, courts and schools. [cite:w_ottoman_millet]

A shared country was proposed from inside the movement. Brit Shalom argued for a binational state in the 1920s; Jacob de Haan spoke for the Orthodox Old Yishuv against the Zionist leadership until he was shot in 1924. [cite:rs_rd3_jvl_brit_shalom] [cite:w_dehaan] Martin Buber and Judah Magnes, president of the Hebrew University, founded Ihud in 1942 and argued that Arabs and Jews should share one state with political parity; Hannah Arendt warned in 1944 against an exclusively Jewish sovereign state; Buber and Magnes testified against partition before the UN commission in 1947, and in May 1948 Magnes met Truman to press for a federation. [cite:wiki_ihud] [cite:w_aac] [cite:researchgate_arendt_zionism_reconsidered] [cite:britainpalestine_buber_magnes] [cite:rs_rd5_manne_2026]

What the leadership chose

The leadership's own papers show the other road. In 1895 Herzl wrote in his diary of trying to "spirit the penniless population across the border", and in 1901 he drafted a charter for a land company providing for moving the inhabitants to other Ottoman provinces. [cite:hyw_intro] The movement chose a state with a Jewish majority in a country where Jews were a minority, and the dissenters lost. In 1949, when Syria's ruler offered to resettle 250,000 to 300,000 refugees as part of a peace treaty, Ben-Gurion declined. [cite:tf_zaim] [cite:majalla_zaim]

Arab leaders also rejected the partition plans of 1937 and 1947, and Arab violence against Jews, at Hebron in 1929 and in the revolt of 1936, is on the record in the Baseline and the Road to 1948. [cite:wiki_hebron_massacre_1929] [cite:ushmm_arab_revolt] The shared country had enemies on both sides. The finding is that it was proposed by Jews in the land and in the movement, and that the leadership that won set it aside.
