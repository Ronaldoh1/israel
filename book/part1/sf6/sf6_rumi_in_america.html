Rumi in America

In January 2017 the writer Rozina Ali published an essay in The New Yorker on the English Rumi. Rumi is "often described as the best-selling poet in the United States", she noted, and although he was "a lifelong scholar of the Koran and Islam, he is less frequently described as a Muslim." [cite:sf_pf_rumi] The most popular versions are by Coleman Barks, who is an interpreter rather than a translator: "he does not read or write Persian", and he turns nineteenth-century translations into American verse. [cite:sf_pf_rumi] The New Age writer Deepak Chopra has said his own Rumi poems are not Rumi's words but "'moods' we have captured as certain phrases radiated from the original Farsi." [cite:sf_pf_rumi]

Ali traced the separation back to the Victorian period. The scholar Omid Safi of Duke University told her that Western translators settled on the idea that "these people are mystical not because of Islam but in spite of it", and he called the New Age versions "spiritual colonialism". [cite:sf_pf_rumi]

The same thing happened to Kabbalah. Christian scholars of the Renaissance borrowed it to argue for Christianity, and their translator inserted Christian words the originals did not contain; a Los Angeles centre later sold it in red string. [cite:kb_sep_pico] [cite:kb_herald_kc] Rumi's English career follows the same path. An inner tradition is admired, lifted out of the religion that formed it, rewritten in the borrower's terms and sold back as a universal spirituality that owes nothing to its source.

The record should be stated precisely. Ali's essay does not say that Barks's versions are worthless or that only Muslims may read Rumi; it says that the versions most Americans read have removed the Islam from a Muslim poet, and that this removal has a history. [cite:sf_pf_rumi] The reader who wants Rumi can read him in scholarly translations, with the Qurʾan he quoted and the Islam he practised left in. The inner reading in Part Eight does that: it reads Rumi's reed flute as the Sufis read it, as an Islamic teaching about the soul's longing for its source.
