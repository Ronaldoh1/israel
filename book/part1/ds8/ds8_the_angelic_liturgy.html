The Angelic Liturgy and the Temple of People

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

Among the scrolls is a set of thirteen songs, one for each of the first thirteen Sabbaths of the year, known as the Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice or the Angelic Liturgy. Nine copies were found at Qumran and one at Masada. [cite:ds_wiki_sabbath] [cite:ds_loc_library] The songs describe worship around God's throne in heaven. They move from the heavenly priests, to the praises of seven orders of angels, to the heavenly temple, its throne room and the throne itself, drawing on Ezekiel, Isaiah, Exodus and 1 Enoch. [cite:ds_wiki_sabbath] Unlike Enoch's vision, they have no prophet who sees and reports. The vision is given directly, inviting those who recite it to share it; sung together by a congregation, the songs could create "a sense of union with the angels and their heavenly worship on high." [cite:ds_wiki_sabbath]

The community also described itself as a temple. Having broken with the priests in Jerusalem, it called its own council "a Most Holy Dwelling for Aaron with everlasting knowledge of the Covenant of justice". [cite:ds_vermes_1qs] A group of people, not a building, had become the holy of holies.

The inner reading follows the temple inward. The building in Jerusalem was lost to the community; the community became the temple; the songs carried the community into the temple of heaven; and the one who sings the songs, on this reading, is carried there without leaving the room. The songs are a practice, not only a description: a way of entering the heavenly sanctuary by attention and voice. They stand between Enoch's ascent, which Part Nine of The Ethiopian Bible read as an inward journey, and the later Jewish tradition of ascent to the chariot-throne. [cite:ds_wiki_sabbath] [cite:et_wiki_merkabah] And they say, in a community's own words, what Paul would say to the church at Corinth: that a people can be a temple. [cite:cn_bh_1cor316]

The record limits the reading. The community meant a real heaven, real angels and a real temple that it hoped to see restored, and the copy at Masada shows the songs were used beyond Qumran, perhaps by people who did not share the community's views. [cite:ds_vq_nature] The inner reading takes the songs as a map of consciousness; their singers took them as worship.
