What the Leaders Said

The earlier parts of this book set out what Israel and the United States did to Iran and with Iran. This part sets out, in the same register, what the government of the Islamic Republic has said and done. This section covers the statements of its two supreme leaders and of one president about Israel, and the state's sponsorship of Holocaust denial; the next covers its funding and arming of armed groups, plots on American soil and the detention of foreign and dual nationals [scene:ir9_reach|Money, weapons, plots and hostages]; the third covers repression at home. These are acts of a government and its institutions, chiefly the Office of the Supreme Leader, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and its Quds Force, and the Ministry of Intelligence. They are not attributed here to Iranians at large, many of whom, as the third section shows, have been the first victims of the same institutions.

Khomeini and Khamenei

The founder of the Islamic Republic set the line. In his New Year message of 21 March 1980, Ruhollah Khomeini said "we should try hard to export our revolution to the world" and named "America, Israel and Zionism" as enemies; the year before, he had declared the last Friday of Ramadan as Quds Day, a state-organised day of rallies against Israel held every year since. [cite:hz1_merip_khomeini] [cite:ir2_wiki_quds] [cite:ir9_rferl_tumor2020] Part Two traces how this rhetoric sat beside the secret purchase of Israeli arms. [scene:ir2_revolution|The break, February 1979]

His successor, Ali Khamenei, supreme leader until he was killed in the US–Israeli strikes of 28 February 2026, returned to the same image for a quarter of a century. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] He called Israel a "cancerous growth" in 1998 and in 2000 said "this cancerous tumor of a state should be removed from the region," as quoted by The New Republic. [cite:ir9_tnr_khamenei] At a Tehran conference in support of the Palestinians on 4 March 2009 he again called Israel a "cancerous tumor" and proposed a referendum among those with a legitimate claim to Palestine to decide its government. [cite:ir9_cbs_2009] On 3 June 2018 his official account posted that Israel "is a malignant cancerous tumor in the West Asian region that has to be removed and eradicated: it is possible and it will happen." [cite:ir9_toi_tumor2018] A week later, in comments on his website, he denied threatening Israel and repeated the referendum proposal. [cite:ir9_farda_2018] On Quds Day, 22 May 2020, he said "The Zionist regime is a cancerous tumor in the region," and in the same speech praised Iran's supply of arms to Palestinian fighters, his first public confirmation that Iran was arming them. [cite:ir9_rferl_tumor2020] His office had circulated a poster headed "the final solution" in the days before; it was deleted from his Twitter account though it remained on the Persian version of his website, and he said his opposition to Israel was not antisemitic. [cite:ir9_ynet_2020]

The most quoted forecast came on 9 September 2015, two months after the nuclear deal was concluded. At the Imam Khomeini Mosque in Tehran Khamenei said: "God willing, there will be no such thing as a Zionist regime in 25 years." Netanyahu, in London for talks with the British prime minister, referred to "the tyrant in Tehran" and said: "Israel is a strong country and it will become even stronger." [cite:ir9_cnn_25yrs] Khamenei's own gloss, in a November 2014 post, was that the "elimination" of Israel did not mean a "massacre of the Jewish people" but a referendum of the land's "original" inhabitants, a position Part Three examines alongside his statement in March 2014 that the Holocaust was "an event whose reality is uncertain." [cite:ir3_toi_9point] [cite:ir3_brookings_2014] [scene:ir3_facts|The other fact]

"Wiped off the map": what Ahmadinejad said

On 26 October 2005 President Mahmoud Ahmadinejad addressed students at a Tehran conference titled "The World Without Zionism." He was widely reported as calling for Israel to be "wiped off the map." [cite:ir9_rferl_2005] The translation has been argued over ever since, and both sides of the argument rest on the same sentence, which Ahmadinejad attributed to Khomeini. Transliterated, it reads "een rezhim-i eshghalgar-i Qods bayad az safheh-i ruzgar mahv shavad." [cite:ir9_atl_translation]

The historian Juan Cole and the Guardian's Jonathan Steele rendered it as "the regime occupying Jerusalem must vanish from the page of time," and argued that it expressed a wish for the end of a regime, in the way Ahmadinejad in the same speech recalled the fall of the Shah, not a threat of war. [cite:ir9_steele_2006] [cite:ir9_atl_translation] The Middle East Media Research Institute (MEMRI), often cited by critics of Iran, translated it as "must be eliminated from the pages of history," and BBC Monitoring, after first writing "eliminated from the map of the world," said on review that "page of history" was closer. [cite:ir9_steele_2006] On the other side, the New York Times, whose correspondent Nazila Fathi first used "wiped off the map," defended its rendering; translators it consulted argued that the Persian verb is active and transitive, so that "wiped off" or "wiped away" is more accurate than "vanish." Official Iranian outlets, among them the state broadcaster IRIB, later ran the same English translation. [cite:ir9_atl_translation] [cite:ir9_steele_2006] Both readings agree that the president of Iran called for the end of what he called the regime occupying Jerusalem; they differ on whether the words carried a threat of Iranian military action. The dispute is about tense and agency, not about whether the hostility was real.

The record of Holocaust denial under Ahmadinejad is not disputed. On 11 and 12 December 2006 the foreign ministry's Institute for Political and International Studies hosted a conference to "review" the Holocaust, attended by the former Ku Klux Klan leader David Duke, the convicted French denier Robert Faurisson and six members of the anti-Zionist Neturei Karta sect. Foreign Minister Manouchehr Mottaki said it was held "neither to deny nor prove" the Holocaust; Ban Ki-moon, Angela Merkel, the US State Department and Ehud Olmert condemned it.
