The Open Letter

On 16 February 1985 Sheikh Ibrahim al-Amin, Hezbollah's spokesman, read a text aloud at the al-Ouzai mosque in west Beirut. It was published in the newspaper al-Safir as an open letter "to all the Oppressed in Lebanon and the World". [cite:hz2_wiki_ideology] A study for Harvard's Belfer Center gives 22 February 1985 as the date it appeared in print, and notes that it was written after the suicide bombings of American and French forces. [cite:hz2_belfer_cohen] With this letter the movement that had operated under other names, or under no name, announced itself. [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks]

What it says about Israel and the United States. The English text most often reproduced, published by the Council on Foreign Relations in 1988, puts Israel at the centre. "We see in Israel the vanguard of the United States in our Islamic world," it says, and the struggle "will end only when this entity is obliterated". It continues: "We recognize no treaty with it, no cease fire, and no peace agreements, whether separate or consolidated." It warns of an Israel extended "from the Euphrates to the Nile". [cite:hz2_cep_letter] It calls the United States "that arrogant superpower". [cite:hz2_wiki_ideology] There is one caveat about the text. A note in a standard reference account says the line about obliteration appears in the Arabic original but is missing from one English translation and is not on Hezbollah's website. [cite:hz2_wiki_ideology] Israel has relied on the programme ever since. In a letter to the UN Secretary-General in February 2000, Israel's mission cited it as stating "the complete destruction of the State of Israel" as Hezbollah's goal. That letter dates the programme to 16 December 1985, which does not match the February reading. [cite:hz2_un_israel2000]

What it says it wants. The letter set out its aims as expelling "the Americans, the French and their allies definitely from Lebanon", and bringing the Phalangists to justice for crimes against Muslims and Christians. [cite:hz2_wiki_ideology] On government it said two things at once. It allowed that the Lebanese people should "choose in all the liberty the form of government" they wanted. It also said that "only an Islamic regime" could stop future imperialist infiltration. [cite:hz2_wiki_ideology] On authority it was plain: "We obey the orders of one leader, wise and just", meaning Khomeini. [cite:hz2_wiki_ideology] The Belfer study reads the document as rejecting Lebanon's confessional power-sharing outright and aiming at a borderless Islamic republic. It also notes that the letter made obedience to Khomeini a binding religious duty. [cite:hz2_belfer_cohen] The letter therefore pointed in two directions. It made Israel's invasion and the Western forces in Beirut the immediate enemy, and it placed the movement under a foreign cleric's authority. Part Three examines which of the two mattered more. [scene:hz3_question|Part Three: Would Hezbollah exist?]

Violence in Beirut in 1985. Three weeks after the reading, on 8 March 1985, a car bomb exploded in Beirut's Shia southern suburbs. It was aimed at Fadlallah, who escaped unhurt, and it killed 80 civilians and wounded more than 250. The CIA "vehemently denied any role", and a House Intelligence Committee report backed that denial. Bob Woodward later reported that the operation had been run off the books by CIA Director William Casey and Saudi Prince Bandar bin Sultan, with $3 million in Saudi money. Woodward's account remains disputed. [cite:hz2_wilson_fadlallah]

Israel pulls back. On 14 January 1985 Israel's cabinet decided to withdraw in three phases, ending along the border while keeping "a zone in southern Lebanon where local forces (SLA) will operate with IDF backing". [cite:hz2b_jvl_jan85] The army handed Sidon to the Lebanese army on 16 February, left the Bekaa on 24 April and Tyre on 29 April, and by 10 June 1985 most of its troops had left, with a smaller force staying to back the South Lebanon Army. [cite:hz2b_wiki_slc] [cite:hz2b_csm_apr85] [cite:hz2_begin_pfg] The zone was about nine miles deep and covered some 850 square kilometres, roughly a tenth of the country. It held about 150,000 people in 162 villages and towns. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] [cite:hz4_hrw_pawns] Israel held it with the South Lebanon Army. The Congressional Research Service describes the SLA as "an allied militia that it fully funded and equipped". [cite:hz4_crs_sla] Antoine Lahd had taken command of the militia in April 1984. [cite:hz4_olj_sla] The occupation the Open Letter named as its target lasted until May 2000, and Part Four follows it. [cite:hz4_merip_2000] [scene:hz4_zone|Part Four: the security zone]

The 2009 revision. In November 2009 Secretary-General Hassan Nasrallah presented a new political document, 52 pages long by the Belfer study's count, at a press conference, approved by the party's General Congress. [cite:hz2_belfer_cohen] According to the Belfer study, it publicly dropped the immediate demand for an Islamic state in Lebanon and accepted Lebanon's power-sharing among religious communities. It grounded Hezbollah's weapons in a formula of "Army-People-Resistance", on the argument that the Lebanese army was too weak to defend the country alone. [cite:hz2_belfer_cohen] Nasrallah said the party would accept "consensus democracy" until sectarianism in politics was abolished. He also called for a defence strategy relying on "a popular resistance participating in defending the country" alongside the army. [cite:hz2_cp_lamb] Franklin Lamb, an American commentator sympathetic to the party, wrote that the document was drafted not to recant the 1985 letter but to address subjects it had not covered. [cite:hz2_cp_lamb] Reading the 2009 text, Nasrallah said: "Our problem with [the Israelis] is not that they are Jews, but that they are occupiers." [cite:hz2_wiki_ideology] By the sources' own descriptions, the revision changed Hezbollah's programme for Lebanon. It did not change the armed stance toward Israel, and CNN, in coverage Lamb criticised as skewed, reported Nasrallah as saying in a televised speech that "American terrorism is the source of every terrorism in the world". [cite:hz2a_cp_lamb] Israel's letter to the UN in 2000 had relied on the 1985 text. [cite:hz2_un_israel2000]
