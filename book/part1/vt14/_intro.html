Part Fourteen deals with the two kinds of paper that critics of the Holy See most often say are hidden: the books of the Bible and the papers in the pope's archive. The first section follows how Christians came to agree on which books belong in their scripture, from second-century lists to the Council of Trent in 1546, and corrects three claims that recur in popular writing: that the Council of Nicaea chose the Bible, that it did so by a vote of "218 to 2", and that Athanasius ordered rival gospels burned. It sets the Nag Hammadi find of 1945 and the Index of Forbidden Books, which ran from 1559 to 1966, on their documented footing. The second section describes the Vatican Apostolic Archive, which was called the "Secret Archive" until 2019: what the old name meant, how large the archive is, who may use it, and how it opens one pontificate at a time. The third section reports what scholars have found since the papers of Pius XII's pontificate were opened on 2 March 2020, and where they still disagree about what he knew of the murder of Europe's Jews and what he did about it.
