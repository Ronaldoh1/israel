Bishops, Nuncios and Other Churches

The previous sections showed that most Catholic schools, hospitals and universities are run by dioceses, parishes, orders and boards, not by the Holy See. The instrument through which Rome's reach is most direct is a different one: it chooses the bishops who govern those dioceses and supervise those schools. This section sets out how that choice is made, the diplomatic network that carries it, the one major state that has negotiated a share in it, and, by contrast, the links between the Holy See and other churches, which rest on agreement rather than appointment.

The rule: the pope appoints

Canon 377 of the Code of Canon Law states the rule in one sentence: "The Supreme Pontiff freely appoints bishops or confirms those legitimately elected." [cite:vt13_cic_377] The second clause covers the cases where a body elects and Rome confirms. The procedure behind the rule is set out in the same canon. At least every three years the bishops of each province or conference compile, in secret, a list of priests they consider suitable and send it to the Apostolic See. When a diocese falls vacant, the pontifical legate, in most countries the apostolic nuncio, gathers the views of the metropolitan archbishop, the other bishops of the province and the president of the bishops' conference, hears some of the diocese's consultors and cathedral chapter and, if he judges it useful, consults other clergy and lay people individually and in secret; he then sends three names, the terna, to Rome "together with his own opinion". [cite:vt13_cic_377]

In Rome the file goes to the Dicastery for Bishops, which discusses it and votes; its prefect presents the result to the pope, usually at a Saturday audience; the pope decides, and the nuncio asks the candidate whether he accepts. The process often takes six to eight months. [cite:vt6_usccb_bishops] Part Six described this machinery in the Curia as reformed in 2022. [scene:vt6_curia_2022|Part Six: the Curia since 2022] Its importance for this volume is personal as well as structural: Robert Prevost was prefect of the Dicastery for Bishops from 30 January 2023 until his election as Leo XIV on 8 May 2025, and in September 2025 he named Archbishop Filippo Iannone to succeed him. [cite:vt6_vn_leobio] [cite:vt6_aleteia_appts] The pope who now signs the appointments spent two years running the office that prepares them.

Canon 377 also closes a door that earlier centuries had left open to princes. Its fifth paragraph provides that "in the future, no rights and privileges of election, nomination, presentation, or designation of bishops are granted to civil authorities." [cite:vt13_cic_377] The words "in the future" leave existing arrangements in concordats where they are, and each such arrangement has to be read in its own text; but the general rule since 1983 is that a government obtains no new say in choosing Catholic bishops.

The nuncios

The nuncio is therefore two things at once: the Holy See's ambassador to a state and the pope's investigator of the local church. The Holy See Press Office recorded on 9 January 2026 that 184 states had diplomatic relations with the Holy See, together with the European Union and the Order of Malta, and that 93 embassies accredited to it were based in Rome. [cite:vt5_pv_relations2026] I.Media, reporting the same note, counted 128 apostolic nunciatures and 12 states with no formal ties. [cite:vt13_aleteia_dipl2026] Because there are fewer nunciatures than partner states, a number of nuncios are accredited to more than one country. A figure of "approximately 180" nunciatures, which sometimes circulates, confuses the number of states with relations with the number of missions. [cite:vt13_aleteia_dipl2026] [scene:vt5_recognition|Part Five: recognition]

China: a negotiated share

The People's Republic of China, which has no diplomatic relations with the Holy See, is the clearest case of a state negotiating a role in the choice of bishops. The Holy See and China signed a Provisional Agreement on the appointment of bishops on 22 September 2018; it was renewed for two years in 2020 and again in 2022, and on 22 October 2024 it was extended for four years. [cite:vt5_vn_china2024] Its text has never been published; the Holy See describes appointments as made "in accordance with" an agreement whose terms remain secret. [cite:vt13_aleteia_datong]

The record shows both the agreement working and its limits. In April 2023 Bishop Joseph Shen Bin was installed as bishop of Shanghai without the Holy See's approval, a breach of the agreement; on 15 July 2023 Francis confirmed him in the post, and Cardinal Pietro Parolin, the Secretary of State, said the pope wished "to remedy the canonical irregularity" and that his intention was "fundamentally pastoral". [cite:vt13_tablet_shenbin] Under Leo XIV appointments have continued. On 28 July 2026 he named Andrea Ding Yang archbishop of Chongqing "within the Provisional Agreement", and Ding was ordained on 8 September 2026. [cite:vt13_vn_chongqing] On 10 August 2026 he appointed Paul Liu Honggang bishop of Datong, a diocese vacant since 1947; Aleteia counted him as the 19th bishop appointed under the 2018 agreement and the seventh Chinese bishop approved by Leo XIV, and noted that the Catholic Patriotic Association, which Rome does not recognise, had announced his "election" on 28 April 2026. [cite:vt13_aleteia_datong] As of October 2026, then, the sequence in China runs from a local selection under state auspices to papal appointment, with the papal appointment as the final, formal step.

Other churches: agreement, not appointment

The appointing power stops at the Catholic Church's own bishops. Rome's ties with other Christian churches run through the Dicastery for Promoting Christian Unity and take the form of dialogues and signed texts between partners. [cite:vt6_va_pe] The most substantial is the Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification, signed at Augsburg in October 1999 by the Lutheran World Federation and the Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity, represented by Ishmael Noko and Walter Kasper, which the Federation describes as resolving one of the key theological conflicts of the Reformation. [cite:vt13_lwf_jddj] The World Methodist Council joined it in July 2006; the Anglican Consultative Council welcomed and affirmed its substance in April 2016; and the World Communion of Reformed Churches joined in July 2017 at Wittenberg. [cite:vt13_lwf_jddj]

With the Orthodox, the anchor is the joint declaration of Paul VI and Patriarch Athenagoras in 1965. On 29 November 2025, at the Phanar in Istanbul, Leo XIV and Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew I marked its sixtieth anniversary and the 1,700th of the Council of Nicaea in a joint declaration that committed them to keep seeking a common date for Easter, to support the joint theological commission, and stated: "we reject any use of religion and the name of God to justify violence." [cite:vt13_va_phanar2025] With the Anglicans, the limits are older and still standing: Leo XIII's bull Apostolicae curae of 13 September 1896 declared Anglican ordinations "absolutely null and utterly void", and a 1998 commentary of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith cited it as teaching to be held definitively. [cite:vt13_wiki_apcurae] Gestures have moved further than doctrine. On 23 October 2025 Leo XIV and King Charles III prayed together in the Sistine Chapel with the Archbishop of York, which Buckingham Palace called the first such joint prayer by a pope and a British monarch since the Reformation. [cite:vt13_ncr_charles]

The documents give a plain answer to the question of reach. Over its own church the Holy See's reach is strong and formal: a single legal rule gives the pope the choice of bishops, and a network of 128 nunciatures runs the inquiries that feed it. [cite:vt13_cic_377] [cite:vt13_aleteia_dipl2026] Where a state insists, as China does, the Holy See has accepted a negotiated procedure and has at least once regularised an appointment made without it. [cite:vt13_tablet_shenbin] Over other churches it has no appointing power at all, only the texts it has agreed with them. [cite:vt13_lwf_jddj] [cite:vt13_va_phanar2025] How the Holy See uses its standing in a particular conflict, including Israel and Palestine, is the subject of Parts Sixteen to Eighteen.
