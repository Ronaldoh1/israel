The Cases, 2016–2026

Three cases were investigated by the police from 2016 and became one indictment. [cite:ny_wiki_trial]

The attorney general, Avichai Mandelblit, announced the indictment on 21 November 2019. [cite:ny_wiki_trial] [cite:ny_npr_indict2019] Netanyahu sought parliamentary immunity, withdrew the request, and the charges were filed in the Jerusalem District Court on 28 January 2020. The trial opened on 24 May 2020, witnesses began testifying in April 2021, the prosecution rested in July 2024, and Netanyahu took the stand in December 2024; his testimony ended on 24 June 2026 after 98 hearings. [cite:ny_wiki_trial] On 4 May 2026 the attorney general and the state attorney agreed to open plea talks. [cite:us2_ynet_trial]

The law that let him stay

By court precedent an indicted minister is expected to leave office, but a prime minister must resign only after a final conviction for a serious offence, and whether an indicted member could even be asked to form a government went to the Supreme Court in 2020. [cite:pm_indictment_toi] [cite:gov_wiki] Netanyahu stayed. His future coalition partners offered him legal relief unasked: according to NPR, in October 2022 Bezalel Smotrich proposed abolishing the offence of breach of trust, and Itamar Ben-Gvir proposed a law to shield a sitting prime minister from prosecution. [cite:npr_netanyahu_far_right_allies]

The pardon request

In June 2025 Donald Trump wrote that the trial should be cancelled "IMMEDIATELY, or a Pardon given to a Great Hero", and in November 2025 he wrote to President Isaac Herzog asking him to pardon Netanyahu. [cite:ny_wiki_trial] [cite:ny_bloomberg_trumpletter] On 30 November 2025 Netanyahu formally asked Herzog for a pardon, without admitting guilt. [cite:ny_pbs_pardon] Herzog asked for a legal opinion, and in April 2026 declined to rule and pressed for plea talks instead; his office called a plea agreement the "proper and correct solution". [cite:dv5_toi_pardon] [cite:ny_medialine_herzog]

The judges' signal, 25 November 2026

On 25 November 2026, four weeks after the election, the Jerusalem District Court told the parties that two of the three cases did not appear to hold: it indicated that the conduct in Case 2000 did not appear to be a crime and that there was little chance of conviction in Case 4000, where it had already twice voiced doubts about the bribery charge, and it recommended a negotiated agreement on Case 1000, whose fraud and breach-of-trust allegations it reportedly found more convincing. [cite:ny_toi_vindicated] The trial was not over and no verdict had been given. The Times of Israel's editor called it "near-total vindication" for Netanyahu. [cite:ny_toi_vindicated]

Status as of 25 November 2026: the trial continues; no verdict; the pardon request is undecided.
