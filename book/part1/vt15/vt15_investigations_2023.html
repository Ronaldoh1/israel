The Investigations, 1983 to 2026

Four kinds of inquiry have looked for Emanuela Orlandi and Mirella Gregori: Italian criminal investigations, physical searches of graves and bones, a Vatican investigation, and a commission of the Italian Parliament. This section sets out what each did and what it found, to October 2026. The order matters for the theme of this volume, because jurisdiction runs along the border described in [scene:vt4_holy_see_vs_city|Part Four]: Italian prosecutors could not search Vatican territory or compel Vatican officials, and the Vatican's own prosecutor could not act in Rome. Every search inside Vatican ground was made by Vatican authorities at the family's request.

Rome's prosecutors, 1983 to 2016

The first Italian investigation was dismissed in 1997; the dismissal cited, among other things, a lack of cooperation from the Vatican. [cite:vt15_wiki_case] A second opened in 2008 after Sabrina Minardi's testimony and led to the opening of Enrico De Pedis's tomb on 14 May 2012. [cite:vt15_ap_2012] In April 2012 the prosecutor who had led it, Giancarlo Capaldo, was replaced on the file by Rome's new chief prosecutor, Giuseppe Pignatone. [cite:vt15_wiki_case] Pignatone and two colleagues asked for the case to be archived. On 19 October 2015 the preliminary-investigations judge, Giovanni Giorgianni, agreed, for both Orlandi and Gregori. Six people had been under investigation for complicity in kidnapping and murder: Monsignor Piero Vergari, the former rector of Sant'Apollinare; De Pedis's driver Sergio Virtù; Angelo Cassani; Gianfranco Cerboni; Minardi; and Marco Accetti, a photographer who had claimed a part in the events. The judge found that the mass of material gathered had never become coherent, precise or consistent enough for a trial, that Minardi could not say where a body had been disposed of, and that Accetti was unreliable. [cite:vt15_fq_2015] None of the six was charged; each is entitled to the presumption of innocence. The family appealed. By 6 May 2016 the Court of Cassation had rejected the appeal, and Pietro Orlandi said that no power could stop the truth. [cite:vt15_fp_cass2016] Pignatone later became president of the Vatican City State tribunal. [cite:vt15_tablet_2023]

The bones, 2018 and 2019

In late October 2018 builders renovating Villa Giorgina, the seat of the Apostolic Nunciature to Italy in Rome, found human bones in the basement; the Holy See announced the find on 30 October. Rome's prosecutors had them tested. The remains were male, and carbon dating placed them before 1964; they were neither Orlandi's nor Gregori's. [cite:vt15_tablet_2018] [cite:vt15_lng_2018]

In March 2019 the family received a letter with a photograph of an angel on a tomb in the Teutonic Cemetery inside the Vatican, and the words "Look where the angel is pointing." [cite:vt15_cp_2019] On 27 June 2019 the Vatican's Promoter of Justice, Gian Piero Milano, ordered the two tombs opened, at the family's request. On 11 July 2019, in the presence of the family and its lawyers, workers opened the tombs of Princess Sophie von Hohenlohe, who died in 1836, and Duchess Charlotte Frederica of Mecklenburg-Schwerin, who died in 1840. Both were empty. The interim press director, Alessandro Gisotti, said that "no human remains or funerary urns were found." [cite:vt15_cp_2019] [cite:vt15_crux_2019] Two ossuaries found beneath the floor of the neighbouring Teutonic College were opened from 20 July. On 28 July the Holy See Press Office reported that they held several hundred partly intact skeletal structures and thousands of fragments, and that a morphological analysis led by Professor Giovanni Arcudi dated none of them later than the end of the nineteenth century. The family's lawyer asked for laboratory tests on about 70 fragments; Arcudi did not support the request, and the samples were kept by the Vatican Gendarmerie. [cite:vt15_vn_2019]

The Vatican's inquiry, January 2023

On 9 January 2023 the Holy See said that its Promoter of Justice, Alessandro Diddi, had opened a file on the case, based in part "on the requests made by the family in various places." [cite:vt15_npr_reopen] He and the Gendarmerie planned to re-examine the files and testimony from the day of the disappearance onward. [cite:vt15_tablet_2023] Pietro Orlandi called it a "positive step." [cite:vt15_npr_reopen] Diddi said Francis had given him "maximum freedom of action." [cite:vt15_cwr_jp2] He interviewed people who had worked in the Vatican in 1983 and, about six months later, sent his material to Rome's prosecutors. [cite:vt15_cwr_senate] In March 2024 Francis wrote that an inquiry had been opened at the Vatican "to shed light on the story and bring out the truth." [cite:vt15_crux_mar2024] By June 2024 Pietro Orlandi was calling it "a farce", saying that the witnesses he had suggested had not been called after more than a year. [cite:vt15_crux_jun2024] As of October 2026 this research found no announced result.

Rome's third investigation, from May 2023

Rome's prosecutors reopened the case in May 2023 under prosecutor Stefano Luciani, with the Carabinieri's investigative unit. [cite:vt15_agi_dec2025] By the end of 2025 they were following three lines: the family, including a 2024 search of the uncle's former home; friends and schoolmates; and a blogger investigated in 2024 over a supposed eyewitness. The Orlandi family rejects the family line. [cite:vt15_agi_dec2025] In December 2025 a woman who had been a classmate at the music school, and who may have been one of the last people to see Emanuela, was registered as a suspect for allegedly giving false information to prosecutors. Registration is not a charge and does not imply involvement in the disappearance. [cite:vt15_ansa_dec2025] [cite:vt15_agi_dec2025] No one had been charged as of October 2026.

The parliamentary commission

On 23 March 2023 the Chamber of Deputies voted 245 to none for a bill to create a commission of inquiry. [cite:vt15_giornale_2023] At a Senate hearing in June 2023, Diddi said that a new commission would be "a pernicious intrusion" on the integrity of the investigations already under way, and a letter from the Secretary of State, Cardinal Pietro Parolin, objected to the way Diddi had been summoned. Diddi later said this was a request to wait, not an absolute no. [cite:vt15_domani_2023] The Senate approved the bill on 9 November 2023 with almost no votes against. [cite:vt15_cwr_senate] It became Law no. 202 of 4 December 2023, in force from 6 January 2024. The law sets up the commission under Article 82 of the Constitution, which gives it the powers and limits of the judicial authority. It runs for the rest of the legislature. Its tasks are to reconstruct both disappearances, to review judicial and journalistic material, to examine conduct that obstructed or delayed the truth, including by asking foreign states for documents, and to report to both chambers. [cite:vt15_law_202] It has 40 members from both chambers. On 14 March 2024 it elected Senator Andrea De Priamo of Fratelli d'Italia as president. [cite:vt15_fp_2024] [cite:vt15_crux_mar2024]

Pietro Orlandi said in April 2024 that the Vatican "didn't want the commission." [cite:vt15_crux_apr2024] The commission's president said in June 2024 that the Foreign Ministry was helping it obtain the 1983 diplomatic correspondence between Italy's ambassador and the Secretariat of State, and that these papers would help dispel portrayals of the Vatican as "absolute evil." [cite:vt15_qn_jun2024] On 21 November 2024 it heard Domenico Giani, head of the Vatican Gendarmerie from 2006 to 2019, who said he had compiled a file in 2012 that contained nothing new and that "I am not a witness to anything"; the family's lawyer replied that the file must have been the product of investigation. [cite:vt15_crux_nov2024] In early 2025 the commission found empty folders on the case in Italy's Central State Archive and asked the Interior Ministry to check where their contents had gone. [cite:vt15_eco_mar2025] Its hearings continued into 2026; at its 76th and 77th sittings, on 29 January and 5 February 2026, it heard a journalist, an author and a witness from 1984, and commissioners said that one witness's hypotheses had no objective confirmation. [cite:vt15_sen_jan2026] [cite:vt15_sen_feb2026] As of October 2026 this research found no final or interim report from the commission.

The family's position

The Orlandi family has never accepted any of the closures. Its stated view, through Pietro Orlandi and the lawyer Laura Sgrò, is that the truth is known inside the Vatican and that the Holy See has not given it the file compiled in 2012 or named the people who met Capaldo, despite requests since 2017. [cite:vt15_crux_nov2024] [cite:vt15_crux_apr2024] On 2 August 2025 Pietro Orlandi said that Pope Leo XIV had not mentioned Emanuela at the Angelus on 22 June, the anniversary, and that no meeting had been arranged; "a single word would have been enough," he said. [cite:vt15_qn_aug2025] The Holy See's stated position is that the search for the truth is "in the interest of the Holy See and the Orlandi family." [cite:vt15_vn_2019]
