The Rockets

After the suicide bombings came rockets. Human Rights Watch counted more than 4,000 rockets fired from Gaza at Israel between 2001 and 2008. "None of these rockets can be reliably aimed," it wrote; fired at populated areas, they were inherently indiscriminate and their use was a war crime. Hamas's spokesman Abu Obeida had promised, "We will double the number of Israelis under fire." Khaled Mashaal called the rockets "a method and not the goal". Hamas prosecuted no one. [cite:hm_hrw_rockets2009]

The pattern held. In the war of May 2021, rockets from Gaza killed 12 civilians in Israel, and one that fell short killed seven Palestinians in Jabaliya; Human Rights Watch again called the indiscriminate fire a war crime. Hamas rejected the report and said it was developing the ability to "accurately target Israeli military headquarters". [cite:hm_almon_hrw2021] Gaza, Documented sets out the wars of 2008 to 2021 and Israel's conduct in them, which rights groups also found unlawful. 2014: The Kidnapping and the War

On 12 June 2014 three Israeli teenagers, Eyal Yifrach, Gilad Shaer and Naftali Fraenkel, were abducted while hitchhiking in the West Bank; their bodies were found on 30 June. Israel blamed two Hamas members from Hebron and arrested hundreds of Hamas members in the West Bank. On 2 July a Palestinian teenager, Mohammed Abu Khdeir, was abducted and burned alive in Jerusalem in revenge, and Israeli suspects were arrested. [cite:hm_wiki_teens2014] Hamas at first denied involvement. In August Saleh al-Arouri, a senior Hamas leader, told a conference in Turkey that the kidnapping was a "heroic operation" by its armed wing, while saying Hamas "did not have the intention at this time to ignite a large battle". [cite:hm_npr_arouri] Rockets from Gaza and an Israeli air and ground war followed; more than 2,000 Palestinians, most of them civilians, and 67 Israelis, all but three of them soldiers, were killed.
