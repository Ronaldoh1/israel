One Arab state did take most of its Palestinian refugees in, and others chose not to, for stated reasons. Under its 1954 nationality law, Jordan gave citizenship to most Palestinians who arrived after 1948; it hosts more than 2 million registered Palestinian refugees, most of them citizens. [cite:jns_arab_citizenship] [cite:adl_refugees] Lebanon, with about 400,000 registered refugees, has never naturalized them, barring them from dozens of professions and, since a 2001 law, from owning property, a policy widely tied to its sectarian power-sharing balance. [cite:fmr_lebanon] [cite:aj_lebanon_property] Egypt and the Gulf offer residency without a path to citizenship.

The Arab League told members not to grant citizenship, to protect refugees' identity and their right of return; critics, including Israeli and pro-Israel analysts, argue this kept refugees in limbo as a political tool. [cite:fmr_lebanon] After October 7, Egypt and Jordan refused to take Gazans, citing the fear that Palestinians who leave would never be allowed back. [cite:pbs_egypt_refugees]
