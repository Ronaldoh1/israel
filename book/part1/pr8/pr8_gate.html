No Independent Entry

From October 2023 Israel, which controls entry, allowed foreign journalists into Gaza only on visits escorted by its army. [cite:pr_wlrn_ban_full] The Foreign Press Association, which represents the international media in Israel, petitioned Israel's Supreme Court for independent access. The state argued that journalists moving on their own could endanger soldiers and reveal the positions of troops; on 8 January 2024 the court rejected the petition, allowing escorted access to continue. The association said the ban, by then 95 days old, was "unprecedented." [cite:pr_mm_fpa2024]

In September 2024 it petitioned again, with Israeli and international press-freedom groups. After the ceasefire, the state told the court that "there is no basis to allow the entry of journalists – Israeli or foreign – into the Gaza Strip without accompaniment," citing the danger to reporters, interference with military operations and the remains of a hostage still in Gaza. It counted about 25 escorted entries by Israeli journalists and five group visits by foreign journalists since late October 2025. [cite:pr_yahoo_state_hcj]

Three years on. As of October 2026 the court's decision on the second petition had been postponed "numerous times," and no foreign reporter had entered Gaza on their own. Embeds remained "tightly controlled": the army decides what reporters see and where they go, and they do not interview Palestinians. "We haven't really been able to get a straight answer as to why this ban continues," said Joe Federman, the AP's deputy director for the Middle East and a longtime member of the association. "This is a risk that most journalists are willing to take." The Committee to Protect Journalists' Jodie Ginsberg warned that "the longer this goes on, the more normalized that lack of access becomes"; foreign reporters, she said, could "corroborate what is being documented by their colleagues." NPR's producer in Gaza City, Anas Baba, said open access "would mean we are no longer carrying this burden alone." [cite:pr_wlrn_ban_full]

The Censor

Foreign journalists working from Israel must agree to abide by the military censor to receive a press card, and the censor restricted subjects including security cabinet meetings and the hostages. The Al Jazeera Law

In April 2024 the Knesset passed a law allowing the prime minister and communications minister to shut down foreign networks judged harmful to state security. Netanyahu wrote that Al Jazeera had "harmed Israel's security, actively participated in the October 7 massacre, and incited against Israeli soldiers"; the network called these "slanderous accusations." Israel closed Al Jazeera's operations in the country in May 2024. In December 2025 the Knesset extended the law to 2027, allowing its use even outside a state of emergency and with amendments aimed at eliminating judicial oversight. [cite:pr_aj_ajlaw]
