Part Two answers the first set of questions this volume was asked: why there is a Vatican at all, who designed it, who paid for it, and who controlled it then and now. It begins with the grant of Italian territory by the Frankish king Pippin to Pope Stephen II in 754–756, the forged Donation of Constantine that later decorated that grant, and the eleven centuries in which the popes ruled a clerical state across central Italy. It follows the collapse of that state from the Roman Republic of 1849 to the Italian capture of Rome on 20 September 1870, the Law of Guarantees that Pius IX refused, and the fifty-nine years in which the popes would not leave the Vatican. It ends with the Lateran Pacts of 11 February 1929: the motives each side stated or was credited with, the men who drafted the texts, the money Italy paid, the Catholic party that had already been dissolved, and the line of control that runs from Pius XI to Leo XIV.
