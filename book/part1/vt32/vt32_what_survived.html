What Survived, and What Stays Open

A ledger of suppression is also, read the other way, a record of survival. Almost every tradition this book has followed, from the Gnostic schools to the mysteries, the suns of the Americas and the mothers of the Andes and the Caribbean, reached the present, in some form, by one of four routes: buried in the ground, preserved in its enemies' books, kept alive by a community that never stopped using it, or revived by later readers who found it again. This section sets out what came through each route, then the questions the record still leaves open, and ends the volume.

Buried and found

The most famous survivals came out of the Egyptian ground. The thirteen Nag Hammadi codices, with 46 different works, were dug up at the foot of the Jabal al-Tarif in December 1945; they had been made about 350 and buried in the following decades, by whom and why is still argued. [cite:vt25_enc_nh] [cite:vt25_lj2015] [cite:vt25_lewisblount2014] The Berlin Codex, with the only extended text of the Gospel of Mary, was bought in Cairo in January 1896 and reportedly came from a burial site at Akhmim; Codex Tchacos, with the Gospel of Judas, was found near Maghagha in the 1970s and passed through dealers, a theft and a bank box on Long Island before its publication on 6 April 2006. [cite:vt26_wiki_berlincodex] [cite:vt26_natgeo_journey] The Greek fragments of the Gospel of Thomas came out of the ancient rubbish of Oxyrhynchus, and the Aramaic fragments of Enoch out of Qumran's Cave 4. [cite:vt20_ecw_thomas] [cite:vt27_wiki_enoch] Two codices left Egypt earlier, by purchase: the Askew Codex with Pistis Sophia, in the British Museum since 1785, and the Bruce Codex with the Books of Jeu, bought by James Bruce about 1769 and in the Bodleian since 1848. [cite:vt26_wiki_askew] [cite:vt26_wiki_bruce] None of these books was hidden from Rome, and none was found by Rome; most were found by chance, sold through the antiquities trade, and published decades later.

The Americas have their own buried survivors. Of the Maya books, more than a hundred of which are thought to have been destroyed in the colonial period, four survive: codices in Dresden, Madrid and Paris, and the Maya Codex of Mexico, an almanac of Venus declared authentic by Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History in 2018 and dated to between 1021 and 1154. [cite:vt34_arch_mani] [cite:vt34_bas_codices] [cite:vt34_wiki_cmm] About 65 per cent of the Dresden Codex is astronomical tables, the kind of knowledge the fires consumed. [cite:vt35_wiki_mayacodices] Around 600 Andean quipus survive, most taken from graves; they have not been deciphered, and a database built by Gary Urton at Harvard is used to study their patterns of colour and knot. [cite:vt32_hyperallergic_khipu] The Sun Stone, buried on an archbishop's order, came up during repairs to the cathedral of Mexico on 17 December 1790, and the Templo Mayor beneath the colonial city was uncovered after workers struck the Coyolxauhqui disc on 21 February 1978. [cite:vt35_wiki_sunstone] [cite:vt35_wiki_templomayor]

Kept by their enemies

The second route is the strangest. Much of what survives of the condemned traditions survives because their opponents copied it in order to refute it. Ptolemy's Letter to Flora, one of the few complete Valentinian works known before 1945, exists only because Epiphanius quoted it whole in his catalogue of heresies. [cite:vt24_wiki_flora] Irenaeus's summary of the Barbelo myth was the main account of it until the Apocryphon of John came to light in the Berlin Codex and at Nag Hammadi, and the Refutation of All Heresies, recovered from a Mount Athos manuscript in 1842, preserves accounts of groups known from nowhere else. [cite:vt25_iren_129] [cite:vt26_wiki_berlincodex] [cite:vt24_wiki_refutation] The pattern repeats in the Middle Ages. The voices of the last Cathars of the Pyrenees survive in the register of Jacques Fournier, bishop of Pamiers and later Pope Benedict XII, whose 578 interrogations of 1318 to 1325 are kept in the Vatican Library and became the basis of Emmanuel Le Roy Ladurie's Montaillou. [cite:vt28_wiki_fournier] The Chinon parchment of 1308, which records the Templar leaders' absolution, lay in the Vatican's archive until it was identified in 2001. [cite:vt29_wiki_chinon] The archive of the institution that condemned them is now one of the main witnesses to what they believed; the archives themselves are described in [scene:vt14_apostolic_archive|Part Fourteen].

The same is true of the ancient mysteries and of the Americas. The seven planetary gates of the Mithraic ladder are known from the critic Celsus, quoted by his own critic Origen, and Porphyry's burned book against the Christians from the roughly thirty Christian writers who quoted it to refute it. [cite:vt33_harland_celsus] [cite:vt33_wiki_againstchristians] In New Spain the Franciscan Bernardino de Sahagún and his Nahua collaborators, Antonio Valeriano, Martín Jacobita, Alonso Vegerano, Pedro de San Buenaventura and others, compiled the twelve books of the Florentine Codex between 1559 and 1577: 2,446 pages and 2,472 illustrations on the gods, the calendar, the heavens, medicine and the conquest from the Mexica side, confiscated by the crown in 1577 and now in Florence's Laurentian Library. [cite:vt34_whe_florentine] Landa, who burned the Maya books, recorded their calendar and a key to their script in his Relación, which Yuri Knorozov used in the 1950s to show that Maya writing was largely syllabic. [cite:vt34_wiki_landa] The Huarochirí Manuscript, compiled in Quechua under the priest who prosecuted the practices it records, and Arriaga's manual of extirpation preserve Andean religion in its enemies' words. [cite:vt34_ency_extirp] [cite:vt34_wiki_arriaga] And the Codex Borgia, a pre-conquest book of the 260-day calendar showing Tonatiuh receiving offerings, has been in the Vatican Library since 1902, which has digitised it and made it public. [cite:vt35_wiki_borgia]

A few of the groups' own texts came through as well. The Cathar Book of Two Principles, the Occitan ritual of Lyon and the Bogomil Secret Supper, the Interrogatio Iohannis, all survive in manuscript. [cite:vt28_wiki_catharism] [cite:vt28_lyons_traditio]

Kept alive

The third route is continuous use, and here the survivors are living communities rather than manuscripts. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, which has never been under Rome's jurisdiction, keeps a Bible its own website counts at 81 books, including 1 Enoch, Jubilees and the three books of Meqabyan; it became self-governing under its own patriarch in 1959 and counts its members in the tens of millions. [cite:vt27_eotc_books] [cite:vt27_wiki_basilios] [cite:vt27_wiki_eotc] Its canon is its own, not a museum of a lost Bible, and it accepts the Council of Nicaea. [cite:vt27_eotc_chalcedon] The Mandaeans, who honour John the Baptist as their greatest teacher and practise repeated baptism in running water, are the one living community that scholars commonly class among the Gnostic religions; they are not Christians, their origins are disputed, and the war in Iraq after 2003 killed or drove out about nine in ten of the Iraqi community, leaving 60,000 to 100,000 worldwide. [cite:vt24_wiki_mandaeans] In Ethiopia, Rome's one sustained attempt to change a church's practice, the Latinisation of 1622 to 1632, was reversed by the same emperor who had imposed it. [cite:vt27_wiki_mendes]

In the Americas continuous use took Catholic form. In the Andes, Pachamama is honoured with the ch'alla and the offerings of August and joined to the Virgin of Candelaria; the Virgin of Copacabana, carved by the Indigenous sculptor Francisco Tito Yupanqui and enthroned in 1583, stands near a site the Inca held sacred and draws Indigenous and Catholic pilgrims alike; and the pilgrimage of Qoyllurit'i, inscribed by UNESCO in 2011, combines Catholic and pre-Hispanic worship and greets the first rays of the sun. [cite:vt36_wiki_pachamama] [cite:vt36_wiki_copacabana] [cite:vt35_unesco_qoyllur] In Mexico the Tzotzil Maya of Chamula pray to Our Father Sun/Christ, and the Totonac voladores still descend from their pole. [cite:vt35_everyculture_chamula] [cite:vt35_unesco_voladores] In Cuba and Brazil the Yoruba orishas carried by enslaved Africans live on in Santería, Oshún beside Our Lady of Charity of El Cobre and Yemayá beside Our Lady of Regla, and Iemanjá is honoured on Brazil's beaches every 2 February and New Year's Eve. [cite:vt36_wiki_santeria] [cite:vt36_wiki_yemoja] The Plains nations kept the Sun Dance through the years of the ban, and hold it every year today. [cite:vt35_wiki_sundance] In Goa the devotees of Shri Mangesh carried their linga by night across the border in 1560, and the temples rebuilt beyond Portuguese rule still stand. [cite:vt34_goagov_mangueshi] And the counter-case also shows continuous use: Japan's hidden Christians kept their faith behind Buddhist forms for more than two centuries, and about 30,000 came out of hiding when the ban was lifted in 1873. [cite:vt35_wiki_kakure] [cite:vt35_audit_spencer_kosatsu]

Found again

The fourth route is revival, and for the philosophy of the mystery schools it began early. The doctrine of the soul's ascent passed into Christian mystical theology through the writings ascribed to Dionysius the Areopagite, which drew heavily on Proclus and were translated into Latin by Eriugena in 862; it passed into Arabic through the Theology of Aristotle, a ninth-century paraphrase of Plotinus that shaped al-Kindi and Avicenna, and through the Arabic Proclus that became the Latin Liber de causis. [cite:vt33_sep_dionysius] [cite:vt33_wiki_theologyaristotle] [cite:vt33_sep_proclus] Marsilio Ficino translated the Hermetic writings for Cosimo de' Medici in 1462 or 1463, and Renaissance readers took them for a wisdom older than Plato until Isaac Casaubon dated them to the Christian era in 1614. [cite:ts13_wiki_hermetica] The Rosicrucian manifestos of 1614 to 1616 announced a brotherhood for which there is no evidence; real Rosicrucian orders appeared only in the eighteenth century, and AMORC, among the best known today, was founded in the United States in 1915. [cite:ts13_wiki_rosicruc] [cite:ts13_wiki_amorc] Jules Doinel founded the Église Gnostique in Paris in 1890, the first of the modern Gnostic churches. [cite:vt24_wiki_doinel] G. R. S. Mead's English Pistis Sophia of 1896 and Jung's writing carried the word "Gnosis" into the twentieth century, and Elaine Pagels's The Gnostic Gospels of 1979 carried the Nag Hammadi texts to a general readership. [cite:vt26_wiki_mead] [cite:vt24_enc_pagels] Freemasonry, organised in its modern form by the Grand Lodge founded in London on 24 June 1717 and condemned by Rome since 1738, survives as a fraternity whose relations with the Church are set out in [scene:vt30_rosicrucians_masons|Part Thirty]. [cite:vt30_wiki_pgle] [cite:vt30_vn_ddf2023] In Cusco, Inti Raymi, the Inca sun festival whose open celebration ceased under Spanish rule, was restaged at Sacsayhuamán in 1944 by Humberto Vidal Unda and Faustino Espinoza Navarro from Garcilaso's description, and has been performed every 24 June since. [cite:vt35_infobae_inti] [cite:vt35_wiki_intiraymi] Each revival describes itself as the return of something old; the record usually shows a modern founding that drew on old texts, which is itself a kind of survival.

The institution has revived some of what it once set aside. Mary Magdalene, fused with a sinful woman by Gregory I about 591, was separated from her in the Roman calendar in 1969 and given a feast as "apostle of the apostles" on 3 June 2016. [cite:vt26_pearse_greg33] [cite:vt26_va_feast2016] Galileo's case was reopened by a papal commission and closed by John Paul II's address of 31 October 1992. [cite:vt29_coyne_galileo]

Parallels beyond Christianity

The tension between inner knowledge and religious authority is not only a Christian story. In Judaism, the Kabbalah developed a map of the divine in ten sefirot, aspects through which the divine is manifest, as an inner tradition within rabbinic Judaism rather than against it; the readings of Jacob's struggle as an ascent of the soul, from Philo to the Kabbalah, are set out in [scene:ts13_jacob|The Theology Shield]. [cite:ts13_wiki_tiferet] In Islam, the Sufi teacher al-Hallaj was executed in Baghdad in March 922 with the caliph's approval, charged in terms that presented him as a rebel against the Kaaba; one of his sayings put the true pilgrimage around "the Kaaba of one's heart". [cite:vt32_brit_hallaj] [cite:vt32_wiki_hallaj] The ancient mysteries and the ascent pattern they share with these traditions are traced in [scene:ts13_mysteries|The Theology Shield's chapter on the mysteries]. The parallels show a recurring pattern, not a single lineage: no documented line of transmission joins the Valentinians, the Sufis and the Kabbalists, and none is claimed here.

The end of the record

Book One of this volume asked whether the Holy See's refusal of Zionism was a front, and found that it was not. Book Two asked what the institution took, buried and kept, and its answer is less tidy. The institution did build a mediated, obligatory and, from the sixth century, taxed system over teachings that had offered a direct path; it did condemn, exclude and, in the Middle Ages, burn the people who kept such teachings; and from the twelfth century it did so under its own canons; its own leaders acknowledged in 2000 that "men of the church" had sometimes used methods not in keeping with the Gospel. [cite:vt24_ce_tithes] [cite:vt28_fordham_lat4c3] [cite:vt28_america_2000] It did not choose its Bible at Nicaea, did not order the Gnostic gospels burned in any document yet found, did not suppress the Ethiopian Bible, and did not act alone. [cite:vt22_canons] [cite:vt27_hessayon] [cite:vt21_fc_laws] Beyond the Gnostics, emperors and bishops closed the mystery cults; crowns, conquistadors, friars and bishops dismantled the sun cults and burned the books and quipus of the Americas, outside the Inquisition's reach; and the mother goddesses were pressed into Marian forms that their own peoples then made theirs. [cite:vt21_wiki_theodpagan] [cite:vt34_arch_mani] [cite:vt34_wiki_lima3] [cite:vt36_sahagun1576] What it excluded did not disappear. It survived in a jar, in a bookseller's codex, in its enemies' refutations and its own inquisitors' registers, in a church in the Ethiopian highlands and a people baptising in the rivers of Iraq, in four Maya books, a friar's twelve volumes and a pope's library, in the pilgrims who greet the sun at Qoyllurit'i and the devotees of Oshún at El Cobre, and it is now printed, translated and read more widely than at any time since it was written. The record does not say whether that recovery was an accident or a pattern; it says what was done, and what was kept.
