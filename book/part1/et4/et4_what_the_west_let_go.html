Enoch and Jubilees: The Books the West Let Go

The two most famous books in the Ethiopian canon were not invented in Ethiopia. The Book of Enoch and the Book of Jubilees were Jewish writings of the centuries before Christ, read by Jews and early Christians across the Mediterranean world, and fragments of both were found among the Dead Sea Scrolls. [cite:vt27_wiki_enoch] [cite:vt27_wiki_jubilees] Ethiopia kept them in its Bible. Almost everyone else let them go. The question this section asks is who let them go, and on what authority. The contents of both books are set out in [scene:vt27_enoch|The Vatican Architecture, Part Twenty-Seven].

The oldest parts of Enoch, the Book of the Watchers, tell how two hundred angels, led by Shemihazah, descended on Mount Hermon in the days of Jared, took human wives and fathered giants; one of them, Azazel, "taught men to make swords" and other forbidden crafts, and the Lord commands, "to him ascribe all sin". [cite:vt27_ccel_enoch1] [cite:vt27_wiki_enoch] The Parables add a figure called the Son of Man, named before the sun and the signs were created. [cite:vt27_ccel_enoch2] A later section turns on the powerful: "Woe to you, you rich, for you have trusted in your riches", a line echoed in the Letter of James. [cite:vt27_itb_enoch94] Jubilees retells Genesis and Exodus as a revelation given by an angel to Moses on Sinai, organised in periods of forty-nine years, and insists on a calendar of 364 days. [cite:vt27_wiki_jubilees] [cite:vt27_ccel_jub1]

The New Testament itself quotes Enoch. The Letter of Jude cites Enoch, "the seventh from Adam", prophesying that the Lord comes with his holy ones "to execute judgment upon all". [cite:vt27_biblia_jude] [cite:vt27_ccel_enoch1] The Epistle of Barnabas treats it as scripture. [cite:vt27_wiki_enoch] Its rejection in the West was therefore a choice made against a book that an apostolic letter quoted, and the men who made the choice explained it.

Tertullian, around 200, defended Enoch: some reject it, he wrote, because it is "not admitted into the Jewish canon either", but Noah could have preserved it, and it "possesses a testimony in the Apostle Jude". [cite:vt27_na_tert_cultu] Jerome reported that the Letter of Jude itself "is rejected by many" because it quotes Enoch. [cite:vt27_na_jerome_vir] Augustine settled the matter for the Latin West. "We cannot deny that Enoch, the seventh from Adam, left some divine writings", he wrote, but "their antiquity brought them under suspicion", and they were not in the canon "preserved in the temple of the Hebrew people by the diligence of successive priests"; there was "some truth in these apocryphal writings", but they had "no canonical authority". [cite:vt27_logos_augustine] The decisive test was the authority of an institution, the priesthood that kept the Hebrew canon, not the content of the book.

The result was that Europe forgot Enoch almost entirely. The scholar Ariel Hessayon, writing with Annette Yoshiko Reed and Gabriele Boccaccini, records that Joseph Scaliger printed Greek excerpts of the Watchers from a ninth-century Byzantine chronicle in 1606, and that the full book returned to Europe only when the Scottish traveller James Bruce brought Ethiopian manuscripts home in 1773; Richard Laurence published the first English translation in 1821. [cite:vt27_hessayon] Hessayon is careful to state that the record does not support a claim of deliberate suppression: the rabbis and Fathers such as Athanasius and Augustine rejected the books, and copying stopped. [cite:vt27_hessayon] The books were not burned. They were declared non-canonical, and in a world where books survived only by being copied, that was enough.

Ethiopia kept them because its church never accepted that decision. The kingdom's separation from the councils of the West, described in Part One, allowed it to preserve what the Latin and Greek churches discarded. The Ethiopian Bible is, in this sense, the control group for the experiment the Western canon performed: the same early Christian library, without the lists that closed it. What survived in Ethiopia shows how much the Western lists removed, and that the removal was a decision by men, not the verdict of time.
