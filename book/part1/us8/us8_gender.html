Gender, LGBTQ and Disability Rights

Three further measures of equality among citizens apply to both countries in the same way: how the law and the labour market treat women, how the law treats lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender people, and what rights people with disabilities hold. On all three the two systems have moved in different directions at different times. Israel's courts and Knesset widened LGBTQ rights in steps while religious law still governs marriage. The United States legalised same-sex marriage nationwide in 2015 and has narrowed transgender rights since 2025. On the main gender index, Israel's score fell back after 2022 while the American score stayed above it.

Women: the index and the pay gap

The World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Index scores countries from 0 to 1 on economic participation, education, health and political empowerment. Israel scored 0.689 in 2006, 0.719 in 2016, 0.727 in 2022 and 0.709 in 2025, when it ranked 76th of 148 countries. The United States scored 0.704 in 2006, 0.722 in 2016, 0.769 in 2022 and 0.756 in 2025, ranking 42nd. [cite:us8_wiki_ggg] The two were close for the index's first decade. They separated after 2020, and Israel fell from 60th in 2022 to 83rd in 2023, when its political-empowerment rank dropped from 61st to 96th. [cite:us8_ynet_ggg2023] The index is a composite with its own weighting, and small changes in score move ranks a long way, so it is best read as a trend within each country, not as a verdict between them.

The pay gap is measured differently in each country, and the figures here are not strictly comparable. In Israel, the Shoresh Institution found that women's median monthly income was 76.9 per cent of men's in 2021–23. That gap was larger than in 35 of the 38 OECD countries, and larger than about fifteen years earlier. [cite:us8_jpost_shoresh] Monthly income includes the effect of shorter hours. The Taub Center put the gap for non-Haredi Jewish women at 37 per cent in gross wages and 25 per cent per hour, and about 10 per cent once hours, industry and other factors were accounted for. Among Arab women, the gap rose from almost nothing in 2014 to 15 per cent as more of them entered paid work. [cite:us8_taub_women] In the United States, women working full time all year earned 84 cents for every dollar men earned in 2025, three cents more than in 2024, by Census data. [cite:us8_cap_paygap] By the Bureau of Labor Statistics' weekly series the ratio was 82 per cent in the second quarter of 2026, down from a high of 85 per cent in 2023. [cite:us8_usafacts_paygap]

LGBTQ rights: two routes, different end points

Israel's route ran mainly through its courts and through laws on work and the army, not marriage. The Attorney General stopped enforcement of the sodomy law against consenting adults in 1953, and the Knesset repealed it in 1988. A 1992 law barred discrimination in employment by sexual orientation, and the army opened all roles to gay soldiers in 1993. In 2006 the Supreme Court ordered the population registry to record same-sex marriages performed abroad, while saying it was not creating same-sex marriage in Israel. Courts opened adoption in 2008, and surrogacy to same-sex couples from January 2022. [cite:us8_wiki_lgbt_il] Marriage inside Israel remains in the hands of religious authorities. There is no civil marriage for any couple, so same-sex couples, like mixed-faith couples, marry abroad. [cite:us8_wiki_lgbt_il] [scene:us7_israel|Part Seven: personal status law] In July 2015 an attacker stabbed six people at the Jerusalem Pride march; Shira Banki, 16, died of her wounds. [cite:us8_wiki_lgbt_il]

The American route ran through the Supreme Court, and in two directions. The Court struck down sodomy laws in Lawrence v. Texas (2003). It struck down the federal Defense of Marriage Act's definition of marriage in United States v. Windsor (2013), and required every state to license and recognise same-sex marriages in Obergefell v. Hodges (26 June 2015). In Bostock v. Clayton County (15 June 2020) it held that federal job-discrimination law covers sexual orientation and gender identity. The military's "Don't Ask, Don't Tell" policy of 1993 ended in September 2011, and the Respect for Marriage Act of 2022 wrote same-sex marriage into federal statute. [cite:us8_wiki_lgbt_us] Since January 2025 the direction for transgender people has reversed. An executive order of 20 January 2025 defined sex as binary for federal purposes, and transgender people were barred from military service. In United States v. Skrmetti (June 2025) the Court upheld Tennessee's ban on gender-affirming medical care for minors. [cite:us8_wiki_lgbt_us]

Disability: rights laws, and the money behind them

The United States wrote disability rights into federal law first. President George H. W. Bush signed the Americans with Disabilities Act on 26 July 1990. It covers employment by firms of fifteen or more workers, state and local government services and transport, and privately run public places such as shops, hotels and restaurants. Congress widened its definition of disability in 2008. In Olmstead v. L.C. (1999) the Supreme Court held that keeping people in institutions without need is discrimination under the Act. [cite:us8_wiki_ada] Israel passed its Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law in 1998, a move from a welfare model to a rights model. In 2000 it created a Commission for Equal Rights of Persons with Disabilities in the Justice Ministry. [cite:us8_wiki_dis_il] Internationally the order is reversed. Israel signed the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities on 30 March 2007 and ratified it on 28 September 2012. The United States signed on 24 July 2009, but in December 2012 the Senate vote fell six short of the two-thirds needed to ratify. [cite:us8_wiki_dis_il] [cite:us8_wiki_crpd]

In Israel the conflict has been over the size of benefits. In 2017 the full disability pension was NIS 2,342 a month, when the minimum wage was NIS 5,000. [cite:us8_wiki_dis_il] A protest movement that began in March 2017, and blocked roads that summer, demanded benefits linked to the minimum wage. [cite:us8_wiki_dis_il] On 13 February 2018 the Knesset voted 84–0 for increases of NIS 470 to NIS 770 a month from 1 March, taking the top benefit to nearly NIS 4,000, at a cost of NIS 4.3 billion over four years. Protesters said the rise was too small. The welfare minister called it "the first step to bringing it up to minimum wage".
