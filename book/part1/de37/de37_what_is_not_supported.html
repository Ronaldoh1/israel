What Is Not Supported, and the Same Standard for Every State

The rule for this part is the one the book applies everywhere: a tie to Israel is treated as a tie to any other state. It is mapped when a document shows it, typed by what kind of tie it is, and left out when no document does. It keeps out suspicion dressed as a finding, and keeps in documented facts some readers would rather not see. This section tests the claims that circulate about Israel and the network and fail, states one distinction the whole subject depends on, and then sets the network's documented ties to Israel beside its documented ties to other states, measured with the same ruler.

Two words kept apart

Zionism and Judaism are different things. Zionism is a political movement, founded as an organisation at Basel in 1897, that sought a Jewish national home and later a state in the Land of Israel, and since 1948 has supported that state. [cite:fw_brit_basel] Judaism is a religion, and Jews are a people; many Jews are not Zionists, and many Zionists are not Jews. The library sets out the four words most often confused, Hebrew, Jewish, Israeli and Zionist, in [scene:ch01_four_words|its opening volume], and the largest group of non-Jewish Zionists, Protestant Christian Zionists, in [scene:ts1_defined|The Theology Shield]. The distinction governs this part in three ways. Support for Israel's government, such as Karp's, is a political position and is recorded as one; Palantir itself explains its Israeli work as support for "US allies and liberal democracies." [cite:de37_bhrcc_pal] Criticism of Israel's government is a political position too, and is not treated here as hostility to Jews. And hostility to Jews as a people is antisemitism, named as such where the record shows it, whoever its author.

Claims that circulate and fail

"Israel owns or controls Palantir." Control of Palantir is set out in its own filings. A voting trust established by three American founders, Stephen Cohen, Alex Karp and Peter Thiel, holds special shares that let them control up to 49.999999 percent of all shareholder votes, and no customer accounts for more than ten percent of revenue. [cite:de27_10q_q2_2026] Karp has called Israel commercially irrelevant to the company. [cite:ow9_fortune_karp] A customer that is a minor part of the business, and holds no special shares, does not control it. Not supported; the filings run against it.

"Palantir built or runs Lavender." The system was reported as the Israeli army's own; Palantir denies any involvement and says the system predates its partnership, and no document contradicts the denial. [cite:hm_972_lavender] [cite:de37_bhrcc_pal] Not supported. A related error attaches to Palantir the documented case of Israel's signals-intelligence unit storing intercepted Palestinian calls on Microsoft's Azure cloud, which Microsoft confirmed in part and cut off in September 2025; that case concerns Microsoft, not Palantir, as [scene:ow9_israel|The One-Way Mirror] and [scene:ow4_gaza|its account of Unit 8200] show. [cite:ow9_nbc_msft]

"Thiel, or Yarvin, is an agent of a foreign state." In American law an agent of a foreign principal is someone who acts "at the order, request, or under the direction or control" of a foreign government or party. [cite:ow2_doj_faq] No court record, charge or released government document places either man in that position for Israel or any other state. Thiel's documented contacts with the Israeli world run through Epstein's failed introductions and a fund that declined a Barak-linked company, and his recorded reply to a proposed meeting in Saudi Arabia was that he would avoid the Middle East for a decade. [cite:de14_jacobin] [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] No document shows either theorist in any relationship with the Israeli state, and the core text of the ideology, Land's serial, does not mention Israel. [cite:de4_land_de] Not supported.

"Epstein was an Israeli intelligence asset, and through him the network was compromised." The library has examined the intelligence question claim by claim in [scene:ow12_intelligence|The One-Way Mirror]. The strongest version rests on secondary accounts; the Justice Department and FBI said in July 2025 they found "no credible evidence" that Epstein blackmailed prominent people, a finding itself disputed because of withheld files. [cite:ow12_abc_memo] Association is not direction. Not supported, with the withheld files left open.

"ICE bought an Israeli surveillance company." A private-equity-backed American firm, Penlink, bought Cobwebs Technologies in 2023; ICE bought licences to its products. [cite:de28_ffp_penlink] The record runs against the claim as worded.

Claims framed through Jews as a people. Any account that presents Palantir, the technology executives in this book, or American policy as the work of Jews as a people repeats an antisemitic trope with a long history, and the book does not carry it in any form. No document supports it, and the network's own record is plain about where antisemitism has actually appeared. On 15 November 2023 Musk called a post claiming that "Jewish communities have been pushing the exact kind of dialectical hatred against whites" "the actual truth"; the White House called it an "abhorrent promotion of antisemitic and racist hate," and on 29 November he said, "I'm sorry for that post. It was foolish of me." [cite:de37_cbs_musk] [cite:de37_fwd_musk] In July 2025 his company's chatbot, Grok, praised Hitler and blamed people with Jewish surnames for "anti-white" activism, output the Anti-Defamation League called antisemitic. [cite:de11_reuters_grok] Musk's grandfather, Joshua Haldeman, defended the publication of the forged Protocols of the Elders of Zion; that record is the grandfather's, not the grandson's, as [scene:de3_technocracy|Part Three] sets out. [cite:de3_cbc_leo] Land's serial calls the slide from white identity politics into antisemitism "the trap." [cite:de4_land_de] Each of these is recorded at the strength the documents give it, with the speaker's own explanation where there is one.

The same ruler for every state

Measured by the same rule, the network's documented ties to other states are more numerous than its ties to Israel, and larger in money. Saudi Arabia's Kingdom Holding and Prince Alwaleed rolled over shares worth $1.89 billion into Musk's purchase of Twitter in 2022, and Qatar's sovereign fund committed $375 million. [cite:de11_ab_gulf] [cite:de11_cnbc_coinvest] Qatar's fund and Abu Dhabi's MGX were among the investors in xAI's $20 billion round in January 2026. [cite:de11_agbi_xai] On 19 November 2025 Musk announced a 500-megawatt data centre in Saudi Arabia with Humain, the kingdom's state-backed AI venture. [cite:de37_bgov_xai] His 2018 claim of "funding secured" to take Tesla private rested, by his own testimony, on what he believed was a handshake deal with Saudi Arabia's sovereign fund. [cite:de11_nbc_verdict] China's tie to the network is industrial: Tesla's Shanghai plant delivered 52 percent of the company's cars in 2025, according to the city's government. [cite:de37_sh_tesla] Thiel, by contrast, called Google's conduct toward China "seemingly treasonous" in 2019, while Land, who lives in Shanghai, has called China's mix of state and market "the greatest political engine of social and economic development the world has ever known." [cite:de16_bloomberg_natcon] [cite:de4_spectator]

Thiel's other state ties are personal. New Zealand made him a citizen in 2011 at a private ceremony; The New York Times reported in 2022 that he was acquiring a Maltese passport; and in 2026 he met President Milei in Buenos Aires and moved there with his family, while Argentina's lower house held a hearing on his presence and no Palantir contract with Argentina was reported. [cite:de9_ap_nz] [cite:de9_mt_malta] [cite:de9_bat_milei] [cite:de9_fortune_ba] [cite:de9_bat_congress] South Africa's government declined in 2025 to say whether Musk or Thiel hold its citizenship. [cite:de11_bt_citizenship] Ukraine is a customer of Palantir without charge, and Starlink's service there became, in 2022, the clearest case of one man's company setting limits on an allied war. [cite:de27_time_ukraine] [cite:de11_fox_sevastopol]

Every one of these ties runs through a company or a person's residence, and none runs to the theorists: no document shows foreign money reaching Yarvin or Land, a distinction drawn in full in [scene:de36_foreign_money|Part Thirty-Nine]. Israel's place in the table is real and specific, a defence customer of one company and the licensing authority for one service in one territory. It is neither the largest nor the only foreign presence in the network's affairs.

The worst-case reading, tested

The book's working hypothesis, the Sophia platform's and never a finding, reads immigration enforcement as the occasion and a sovereign executive without a veto as the destination. Israel bears on it only indirectly, as the place where the integrating software the hypothesis worries about is documented in wartime use by a foreign government.

Documented: an American company whose platforms run ICE's case systems signed a defence partnership with Israel's Ministry of Defense "in support of war-related missions," with terms that are not public; its chief executive supports that war in plain words; a UN rapporteur alleged more than the company admits; and the United States then sanctioned the rapporteur, citing among other things her letters to American companies. [cite:de37_bbg_partner] [cite:de37_cnbc_karp] [cite:de37_mee_unreport] [cite:de37_state_albanese] Reported, without a stated mechanism: use in Lebanon in 2024. [cite:de37_mee_steinberger] Probable: nothing beyond these facts; two unexplained claims about wartime use abroad do not establish what the software does at home, and capability shown in one country is not intent in another.

Not supported: that Israel controls Palantir, that Palantir built Lavender, that any person in this book acts for a foreign state, or that the American executive's powers are being shaped by Israel through this network. The record runs against the first and the last directly. Control of the company sits with three American founders under a voting trust, and the network's documented foreign ties are mostly Gulf and Chinese, and commercial. [cite:de27_10q_q2_2026] [cite:de11_agbi_xai] [cite:de37_sh_tesla] What the record shows is a commercial and political alignment, stated openly by those who hold it and contested openly by those who oppose it.
