The last part brings the volume's twenty parts together and answers the question it began with. It first sets out a ledger: for each area the book measured, from courts, elections and prisons to wages, housing, health, schools, family benefits and defence, the named measure, the latest value for each country, its direction since about 1980 or 2000, and the part where the source and definition are given, followed by a short reading of each group of measures and the gaps each system leaves. It then sets the American aid against that ledger: what it is, what it frees, where it is spent and what it weighs in Israel's economy and America's budget, then and now. It brings together the common claims the parts tested, says plainly what a comparison of this kind can and cannot show, and lists what is still open as of October 2026, from the two elections of 27 October and 3 November to the court cases pending in both countries and the talks on the agreement that replaces the aid memorandum in 2028. Any judgment it gives is given measure by measure; it adds no total.
