By 380 the arrangement this book traces was complete in its basic form. The bishops in communion with the emperor held tax immunity from 313, public courts from 318, endowed basilicas and land, and scriptures copied at the treasury's expense. [cite:vt21_hist_x7] [cite:vt21_bateman] [cite:vt21_vc4] Dissenting groups, Marcionites and Valentinians among them, lost their meeting houses to the catholic church by law. [cite:vt21_vc3_65] The emperor's creed at Nicaea came with exile and a death penalty for keeping Arius's books, and the edict of 27 February 380 named the faith of Damasus of Rome and Peter of Alexandria as the law of the empire. [cite:vt21_socrates19] [cite:vt21_wiki_thessalonica] Part Four turns to the bishop of Rome whose name the law now carried, and to the lists that were closed in his generation.
