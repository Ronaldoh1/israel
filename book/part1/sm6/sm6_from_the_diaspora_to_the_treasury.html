From the Diaspora to the Treasury

The movement's first bank was a share company. The Jewish Colonial Trust, incorporated in London, raised about £250,000 against a target of £2,000,000, sold to small subscribers, while its first hundred founders' shares carried votes equal to all the rest combined. [cite:jct_wiki] [cite:jewishencyclopedia_colonial_trust] The Jewish National Fund, founded in 1901, bought land that was then held for Jewish use. [cite:jnf_wiki] In 1948 Golda Meyerson's fundraising tour of the United States raised about $50 million through Henry Montor's network, largely for arms. [cite:israelaffairs_italy]

In 1951 the new state opened a permanent channel. Israel Bonds, formally the Development Corporation for Israel, was launched in New York and has raised about $52 billion since. [cite:pm_icij_bonds] What is new is who buys. From October 2023 to July 2024 American states and municipalities bought more than $1.7 billion of the bonds. Palm Beach County in Florida raised the share of its portfolio allowed in them from 10 to 15 percent and by April 2024 held about $700 million of a $4.67 billion portfolio; Ohio's treasurer had bought $357.5 million since 2019. [cite:pm_icij_bonds] The purchases are decided by elected treasurers and county officials and often announced publicly. Israel Bonds answers critics that Israel has never missed a payment of interest or principal. [cite:pm_icij_bonds]

What changed is the source of the money. The Colonial Trust and the fundraising tours drew on private supporters who chose to give or invest. The bond purchases of today draw on public money held for American taxpayers and pensioners, chosen by officials, and that is where the dispute about them lies.
