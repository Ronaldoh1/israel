Sanctioning a Court, 2020–2026

The third shield faces outward. The International Criminal Court in The Hague, created by the Rome Statute, prosecutes individuals for genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes. Neither the United States nor Israel is a party to the statute. [cite:ow11_eo14203] When the court opened investigations that reached American personnel in Afghanistan and Israeli officials in the Palestinian territories, two American administrations responded with economic sanctions against the court's own officials, and the second extended them to judges, prosecutors, a United Nations rapporteur and Palestinian human rights organizations. [cite:ow11_js_revoke2021] [cite:ow11_wiki_eo14203] This section follows the orders, the designations and the lawsuits in sequence, to the latest point reached in October 2026.

The first round, 2020–2021. In June 2020 President Trump signed Executive Order 13928, authorizing sanctions on persons associated with the court, and in September 2020 the Treasury designated the prosecutor, Fatou Bensouda, and a senior official of her office, Phakiso Mochochoko. [cite:ow11_bm_2021] [cite:ow11_js_revoke2021] The Biden administration revoked the order on 1–2 April 2021. Its revocation did not mean agreement with the court: Secretary of State Antony Blinken said "we continue to disagree strongly with the ICC's actions relating to the Afghanistan and Palestinian situations," and the United States maintained its objection to the court asserting jurisdiction over nationals of non-parties such as the United States and Israel. [cite:ow11_js_revoke2021]

Executive Order 14203, 2025. After the court issued arrest warrants for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and the former defence minister Yoav Gallant, on 6 February 2025 President Trump signed Executive Order 14203, "Imposing Sanctions on the International Criminal Court." Its stated rationale names Israel directly: the court had taken "illegitimate and baseless actions targeting America and our close ally Israel," including what the order called baseless warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant, although neither country is a party to the Rome Statute. It invokes the American Servicemembers' Protection Act of 2002, declares a national emergency, and authorizes the blocking of property of designated persons and the suspension of their entry, and that of their families, into the United States. [cite:ow11_eo14203] The court responded the next day that it "condemns the issuance by the US of an Executive Order seeking to impose sanctions on its officials" and "stands firmly by its personnel." [cite:ow11_icc_stmt2025]

The designations. The prosecutor, Karim Khan, was listed under the order in February 2025. On 5 June 2025 the State Department designated four judges, Beti Hohler, Reine Alapini-Gansou, Luz del Carmen Ibáñez Carranza and Solomy Bossa; on 9 July 2025 it designated Francesca Albanese, the UN Special Rapporteur on the Palestinian territories, over statements that included recommending that the court prosecute Israeli officials; [cite:ow11_jurist_albanese2026] in September 2025 it added three Palestinian human rights organizations, Al Haq, Al Mezan and the Palestinian Centre for Human Rights; and on 18 December 2025 two further judges. Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the court as "a national security threat that has been an instrument for lawfare" against the United States and Israel. [cite:ow11_wiki_eo14203] The State Department's own fact sheet of 20 August 2025 shows how each designation was tied to a ruling: Judge Kimberly Prost for the decision authorizing the Afghanistan investigation, Judge Nicolas Guillou for the decision authorizing the Netanyahu and Gallant warrants, and the two deputy prosecutors, Nazhat Shameem Khan and Mame Mandiaye Niang, for "continuing to support illegitimate ICC actions against Israel." [cite:ow11_state_aug2025] A Reuters report of February 2026 cited by Wikipedia said an American official had warned of further sanctions unless the court changed its statute to bar investigations of the President and his aides. [cite:ow11_wiki_eo14203]

The courts, 2026. The sanctions have been challenged in American courts on constitutional grounds. Albanese's husband and daughter sued in February 2026; on 15 May 2026 Judge Richard J. Leon of the federal district court in Washington preliminarily blocked her designation, finding it was imposed solely because she had encouraged the court to investigate Israeli officials and was likely to violate free-speech rights. [cite:ow11_jurist_albanese2026] [cite:ow11_jns_albanese_dc] On 22 May the Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit stayed that order pending the government's appeal, and on 27 May the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control returned her to its list. [cite:ow11_jns_albanese_dc] [cite:ow11_jurist_albanese2026] Rubio said she had engaged in "biased and malicious activities"; she has said the sanctions made her a "non-person." [cite:ow11_jurist_albanese2026] In June 2026 three sitting ICC judges, Kimberly Prost, Solomy Bossa and Reine Alapini-Gansou, sued the President in the Southern District of New York, arguing that the order exceeds the emergency-powers statutes and violates due process; it was the sixth suit against the order and the first by a sitting judge. [cite:ow11_jurist_judges2026] In October 2026 a federal court in Maine refused the government's motion to dismiss a First Amendment challenge brought by two human rights advocates. [cite:ow11_gs_maine2026] As of October 2026 none of these cases had produced a final judgment in the sources reached here, and their status should be checked before it is relied on.

The two cases. The government's case is stated in the order itself: the United States and Israel never accepted the court's jurisdiction, the warrants against Israel's leaders are illegitimate, and the 2002 statute already expresses American opposition to the prosecution of its personnel by the court. [cite:ow11_eo14203] The critics' case is stated by the court and the plaintiffs: that sanctioning judges for their rulings attacks judicial independence, that it punishes speech and advocacy protected by the American Constitution, and that it uses emergency economic powers against an institution with 125 member states. [cite:ow11_icc_stmt2025] [cite:ow11_jurist_judges2026] What is documented beyond dispute is that an American sanctions programme under emergency economic powers was applied to the officials of an international court and a UN rapporteur, and that the State Department's own designations cite rulings and advocacy concerning Israel as the reason. [cite:ow11_state_aug2025]
