Part Twenty-Eight established that the Good Men of Languedoc and Lombardy were a medieval dualist church with bishops, a single sacrament given by the laying on of hands, and men and women perfect who promised not to lie, swear or kill, who by a modern account exacted no tithe from their believers, and whose teachers called the Roman clergy "Pharisees and false prophets". [cite:vt28_lyons_traditio] [cite:vt28_ency_cathars] [cite:vt28_gui_fordham] Innocent III answered with a crusade carrying Holy Land indulgences; at Béziers on 22 July 1209 the legate himself claimed "almost 20,000" dead, a figure historians doubt, and the war's total is unknown, with the popular 200,000 to 1,000,000 range resting on no contemporary count. [cite:vt28_fordham_lat4c3] [cite:vt28_wiki_beziers] [cite:vt28_nwe_crusade] About 210 to 215 people were burned at Montségur on 16 March 1244 after refusing to recant. [cite:vt28_wiki_montsegur] Between 1184 and 1252 the papacy, with councils and lay rulers, built a standing procedure that searched for heretics, barred lay people in Languedoc from owning the Bible, licensed torture and paid its officers from confiscations; its registers show how the last Good Men were found and burned by 1321. [cite:vt28_wiki_adabolendam] [cite:vt28_wiki_toulouse] [cite:vt28_wiki_adextirpanda] [cite:vt28_wiki_fournier] Part Twenty-Nine follows the same machinery as it turned on a military order of the Church itself, the Templars, and on mystics who claimed direct knowledge of God.
