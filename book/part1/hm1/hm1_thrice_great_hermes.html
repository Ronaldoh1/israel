Thrice-Great Hermes

The Hermetic writings arose in Alexandria, in Roman Egypt, around the first century, and they take the form of conversations between Hermes Trismegistus, presented as "an ancient Egyptian priest", and figures named Pimander, Tat and Asclepius. [cite:hm_enc_hermeticism] The best-known collection, the Corpus Hermeticum, opens with the Poimandres, in which a divine figure who calls himself "Man-Shepherd (Poemandres), Mind of all-masterhood", in G. R. S. Mead's old translation, reveals to the narrator how the world was made and how the soul returns to God. [cite:hm_mead_poim] The Gnostics found Hermetic texts in the jar at Nag Hammadi beside the Christian ones, among them the Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth and a version of the Asclepius. [cite:vt25_wiki_eighthninth] [cite:vt25_wiki_asclepius]

The early Christians took Hermes seriously. Lactantius, Clement of Alexandria and Augustine all accepted that he was very ancient, roughly a contemporary of Moses. [cite:hm_enc_hermeticism] Augustine was the most suspicious: in The City of God he described Hermes as "amicably disposed towards [the] mockeries of the demons". [cite:hm_enc_hermeticism] The ancient sage was admitted as a witness to the truth, and kept at arm's length as a dangerous one.

The Emerald Tablet travelled by another road. Its oldest version is Arabic, from the late eighth or early ninth century, and centuries later Newton made his own translation of it. [cite:de39_wiki_emerald] The tradition thus came west by two roads, one through the Greek Corpus and one through Arabic, and the two met again in the Renaissance.

The Hermetica carry two claims that this book follows. The first is a claim about age: that their teaching is older than Moses and Plato, the source from which later wisdom flowed. The second is a claim about the self: that the soul came from God through the spheres of the heavens and can return the same way, by knowledge. The record tested the first claim and found it false; the second is a teaching, and Part Eight reads it as one.
