Lebanon, 1982

In June 1982 Israel invaded Lebanon, then seven years into its civil war, in an operation it called Peace for Galilee, aimed at the Palestine Liberation Organization, which had been based in Beirut since 1970. Israeli forces reached Beirut, and in August the PLO's fighters and leadership were evacuated under international supervision; Yasser Arafat moved to Tunis. [cite:wiki_1982_lebanon_war] [cite:wiki_plo_lebanon]

From 16 to 18 September 1982, Lebanese Phalangist militiamen allied to Israel killed hundreds of Palestinian and Lebanese civilians in the Sabra neighbourhood and the Shatila refugee camp in West Beirut, an area under Israeli military control; estimates of the dead range from several hundred to several thousand. [cite:wiki_sabra_shatila_massacre] Israel's own Kahan Commission found in February 1983 that the defence minister, Ariel Sharon, bore "personal responsibility" for ignoring the danger of bloodshed when the militias were let into the camps, and he resigned as defence minister; he became prime minister in 2001. [cite:jvl_kahan_commission]

Israeli forces stayed in southern Lebanon until 2000. The invasion and occupation were the occasion for the founding of Hezbollah, with Iranian Revolutionary Guards organising Shia militants in the Bekaa Valley; The Hezbollah Architecture follows that history. [cite:wiki_hezbollah]
