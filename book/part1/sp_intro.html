This booklet follows four images that the inner traditions place in the body: a serpent at the base of the spine, a sacred bone and a sacred fluid, the breath, and a small gland at the centre of the head. For each it sets out first what the texts and the science document, then who tried to forbid or correct the practice built on it, and only at the end the reading that ties them together.

The Canon, the first book on this shelf, already drew the map in its last part: the serpent of Eden, the bronze serpent on the pole, the cross and the place of the skull, laid beside the kundalini of the yoga traditions. [cite:cn_esv_gen35] [cite:cn_esv_num21jn3] [cite:cn_brit_kundalini] This booklet goes underneath that map. It asks where each piece comes from, how old it is, and what the record says about it.

Two rules hold throughout. A reading is marked as a reading, and the last part is one. And where a popular teaching claims more than its sources show, the booklet says so, including where the source turns out to be older or stranger than the teaching claims.
