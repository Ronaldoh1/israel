The Ledger: Twenty Parts, Measure by Measure

This section gathers what Parts One to Twenty measured into one ledger. Nothing new is introduced: every figure below is the one a part gave, with the source and the definition that part used, and each row links back to it. The rules are the ones Part One laid down. The ledger compares systems, laws and governments, never peoples; it puts the same source beside both countries wherever one exists; it gives a direction over time wherever a series exists and says so where only a snapshot does; and it states whom each figure covers. Israel's national figures describe its citizens and residents, about 10.3 million people in September 2026, not the Palestinians of the West Bank and Gaza, whom Part Nine counts separately. [cite:us1_jpost_ilpop] [cite:us1_fh_israel2026] [scene:us1_compare|Part One: how this book compares] Where a judgment is given below, it is given for one measure at a time and says which way that measure points. No row is weighted against another here, because the measures are not in the same units and a reader who values them differently would weigh them differently; Part Twenty-Two adds a total in the open, with every goalpost and weight printed so that a reader can recompute it. [scene:us22_method|Part Twenty-Two: the scorecard]

State and rights

On the measures of how each state is governed, both records moved down in this decade, by different routes. Transparency International's index fell nine points for the United States between 2012 and 2025 and rose two for Israel, so on that measure Israel's trend is the better of the two; V-Dem moved both out of its top category, Israel in 2023 and the United States in 2025; and both fell in the 2026 press-freedom index. [cite:us2_wiki_cpi] [cite:us3_vdem2026] [cite:us3_rsf_il] [cite:us3_wboc_rsf] The forms differ. Israel restricts by statute, from a single state of emergency in force since 1948 to a military censor and a boycott tort; the American restrictions of 2025–26 came mainly by executive action, against a constitution that courts used to stop some of it, most visibly in the tariff ruling of 20 February 2026. [cite:us2_acri_emergency2012] [cite:us3_versa_avneri] [cite:us2_wiki_learning] On criminal justice the United States imprisons about two and a half times as many people per head, 542 against 217 per 100,000 in 2023, and executed 47 people in 2025; on those measures its record is the harsher. [cite:us4_wpb_us] [cite:us4_wpb_il] [cite:us4_dpic_2025] On detention without charge the order reverses: Israel held about 4,500 people in security detention in October 2026, almost all Palestinians from the territories, against 15 at Guantanamo, while America's own large category is civil immigration detention. [cite:us4_hamoked_charts] [cite:us4_amnesty_gtmo] [cite:us4_trac_ice] On homicide, the American rate fell to a record low of 4.1 in 2025 while Israel's national rate doubled in 2023; the rate in Israel's Arab communities, about 12 per 100,000 in 2025 on this book's arithmetic, was above any American national rate in the series, and its police solved about one Arab-community killing in ten. [cite:us5_fox_fbi2025] [cite:us5_taub_homicide] [cite:us5_toi_shinbet] On guns, the American toll of 44,447 deaths in 2024, most of them suicides, has no Israeli counterpart series. [cite:us5_pew_gundeaths]

Money and work

Output per person grew at almost the same rate in both countries over forty-five years, 1.86 and 1.75 per cent a year, and Israel's level stood at 62.7 per cent of America's in 2025 in constant dollars, after two years of war took it back from 66.1 per cent. [cite:us11_wb_gdppc_const] The fiscal records run in opposite directions: Israel's public debt fell from about 163 per cent of GDP in the mid-1980s to 61.9 per cent in 2023, America's rose from 26 per cent in 1980 to 99 per cent in 2025, on different definitions. [cite:us11_jvl_melnick] [cite:us11_imf_debt] [cite:us11_omb_debt] [cite:us11_cbo_outlook2026] For workers, Israel's legal floor follows the average wage and rose 174 per cent in shekels from 1997 to 2026, while the federal American floor has stood at $7.25 since 2009; on the measure of a national wage floor, Israel's law protects low earners more. [cite:us13_nii_minwage] [cite:us13_dol_minwage] On average pay the order reverses: the OECD's average American wage grew 27.7 per cent in real terms from 2000 to 2024 against Israel's 10.9 per cent. [cite:us14_oecd_avwage] Union membership fell steeply in both, and American law guarantees no paid vacation while Israel's guarantees twelve days. [cite:us13_bls_union2025] [cite:us13_wiki_histadrut] [cite:us13_dol_vacation] [cite:us13_toi_vacation]

Cost of living and housing

The two systems burden households in different places. Israel's prices for food, dairy and cars are among the dearest in the OECD, its telecoms 30 per cent below the average after a deliberate competition reform; American telecoms are 51 per cent above it. [cite:us14_toi_food52] [cite:us14_globes_kahlon] Housing grew less affordable in both, on measures that cannot be set against each other: from 129 to 155 average monthly salaries per average Israeli home between 2010 and 2022, and from 3.2 to 5.0 years of median household income per American home between the 1990s and 2024. [cite:us14_wiki_j14] [cite:us14_globes_salaries2023] [cite:us14_jchs_pti] American households carry more debt, 69.2 per cent of GDP against 42.3 per cent in 2024. [cite:us14_globes_mortgagegdp] On homelessness the American record is far worse on the figures available: 745,652 people on one January night in 2025, about 218 per 100,000 on Part Fifteen's arithmetic, against Israeli service counts in the low thousands; Israel's gap here is public housing, sold three apartments for every one bought between 2014 and 2019. [cite:us15_hud_pit2025] [cite:us15_ynet_homeless2022] [cite:us15_comptroller_ph2020]

Health, education and family

The clearest single difference in law is health coverage. Israel has covered every citizen and permanent resident since 1995; the United States left 26.7 million people uninsured all year in 2025, and the Congressional Budget Office expects its 2025 law to add about 10 million by 2034. [cite:us16_wiki_ilhealth] [cite:us16_census_2025] [cite:us16_kff_obbba] Spending less than a third as much per person, Israel moved from level life expectancy with the United States in 1980 to about four years ahead in 2024, and from a higher infant mortality rate to half the American rate; on those outcomes its record is the better, though Arab citizens and the periphery fare worse inside it, as Black Americans do inside the American one. [cite:us16_wb_chepc] [cite:us16_wb_le] [cite:us16_wb_imr] [cite:us16_toi_moh_gaps] [cite:us16_cdc_imr2023] In schools the order changes. The United States spends almost twice as much per student and its PISA reading score of 490 in 2025 stood well above Israel's 436, the lowest Israel has recorded. [cite:us17_oecd_eag2023_usa] [cite:us17_oecd_eag2023_isr] [cite:us17_edweek_pisa2025] [cite:us17_ih_pisa2025] Israel holds university tuition down by regulation; America carries $1.651 trillion in student debt. [cite:us14_huji_tuition] [cite:us14_nyfed_q2_2026] For families, Israel guarantees 15 weeks of paid leave by law and spends about three times America's share of GDP on family benefits on the OECD's cash-and-services measure; the United States guarantees none and its share fell from 0.77 to 0.60 per cent of GDP from 1980 to 2023. [cite:us18_nii_maternity2018] [cite:us18_crs_leave] [cite:us18_socx_usa] [cite:us18_socx_isr] On poverty, Israel's rate is higher on the one shared yardstick, 19.5 per cent against an OECD average of 11.6 per cent in 2023, with child poverty second-highest in the OECD on that basis; the common pairing of an American 10 per cent with an Israeli 21 per cent sets two different definitions side by side. [cite:us18_nii_pov2023] [cite:us18_census_p60290]

Security and the aid

Israel conscripts most Jewish citizens and leaned on reservists who averaged 233 days of service by mid-2026; the United States has relied on volunteers since 1973, and veterans fell from 18 to 6 per cent of its adults between 1980 and 2022. [cite:us19_wiki_conscription] [cite:us19_taub_miluim] [cite:us19_pew_veterans] Israel's defence burden, 7.8 per cent of GDP in 2025, is lower than in the mid-1980s and about two and a half times America's 3.1 per cent. [cite:gi16_boi_2025] [cite:us19_sipri_fs2025] The aid, set beside these figures, weighs about 0.6 per cent of Israel's GDP and 0.05 per cent of American federal outlays. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:us1_fred_ilgdp] [cite:us1_crfb_fy2025] The next section takes it up in full. [scene:us21_verdict|The aid, the claims and what is open]

The gaps each system leaves

Every part named both countries' gaps, and the ledger would mislead without them. Israel's largest is the one its own figures leave out: about 5.56 million Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza, roughly a third of the people under its control, who hold no Israeli citizenship and, in the West Bank, are tried in military courts that convicted in 99.74 per cent of trials in 2010. [cite:us9_pcbs_2025] [cite:wb_972_conviction] [scene:us9_westbank|Part Nine] Inside its citizenry, the record killings in Arab communities, 252 dead in 2025, met a police force that solved about one in ten and a development plan cut since 2023. [cite:us5_yahoo_herzog] [cite:us5_toi_shinbet] [cite:us8_idi_550] Housing rose to 155 average monthly salaries per home by 2022. [cite:us14_globes_salaries2023] And the Haredi exemption grew from about 400 yeshiva students in 1949 to about 63,000 in 2023, ruled unlawful in 2024 yet with about 80,000 eligible men not enlisted in January 2026, while Haredi schools are funded without an enforced core curriculum. [cite:us7_globes_draft2024] [cite:us19_toi_evaders2026] [cite:us17_toi_haredi_core] America's largest gaps are health coverage and cost, with 26.7 million uninsured and drug prices 278 per cent of its peers'; guns, with 44,447 deaths in 2024; incarceration, at 542 per 100,000; and the cost of living for those at the bottom, with a federal wage floor frozen since 2009, half of renters cost-burdened and 745,652 people homeless on one night.
