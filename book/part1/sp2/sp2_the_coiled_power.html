The Coiled Power

The yoga traditions describe kundalini as a dormant energy in every person, pictured as "a coiled serpent lying at the base of the spine", which practice raises through a series of centres to the crown of the head. [cite:cn_brit_kundalini] The best-known manual of that practice is the Hatha Yoga Pradipika, compiled by Svatmarama in the fifteenth century from earlier works and tied by him to the Nath lineage of Matsyendranath. [cite:sp_ww_hyp] It has four chapters: postures, then breath control and cleansing, then the seals and locks called mudras, then meditation. [cite:sp_ww_hyp]

The Pradipika pictures kundalini as a small serpent coiled at the base of the sushumna, the central channel of the subtle body. In one of its models the mudras unblock the channel so that kundalini can rise to the crown, where a stored nectar is released; in another, older one, the practice keeps a drop, the bindu, from falling. The text gives both without reconciling them. [cite:sp_ww_hyp] The textbooks of the practice, in other words, were already layered and inconsistent when they were written down.

The fullest map came from Bengal. The Sat-cakra-nirupana, the "Description of the Six Bodily Centres", is the sixth chapter of a larger work by the tantric practitioner Purnananda, composed in the Saka year 1499, which is 1577 CE. [cite:sp_wl_serpentpower] In 1919 it reached English as The Serpent Power, translated with commentary by Arthur Avalon, the pen name of John Woodroffe, a judge of the Calcutta High Court, together with a second short text. [cite:sp_wl_serpentpower] [cite:sp_ia_serpentpower] Woodroffe's books, in the words of his biographers, stimulated a wide-ranging interest in Hindu philosophy and yoga in the West. [cite:sp_wiki_woodroffe]

A witness

The best-known modern account of the rising is a first-person one. In December 1937, meditating in a small room near Jammu, Gopi Krishna (1903–1984) felt a sensation at the base of his spine and then, in his words, "a stream of liquid light entering my brain through the spinal cord." [cite:sp_hds_gopi] His book Kundalini, published in 1967 with a psychological commentary by James Hillman, made him internationally known, and he spent the rest of his life calling for kundalini to be studied in the laboratory as a natural, evolutionary capacity rather than a cult secret. [cite:sp_hds_gopi] His account is one person's testimony, offered as such.
