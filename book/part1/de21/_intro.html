This part closes Book Three. It follows three things that the cuts left behind. The first is a promise: the "DOGE dividend," a cheque of about $5,000 per household that an investor proposed, Elon Musk passed to the president and the president said he loved, and what became of it once the savings it depended on were audited. The second is the conflict at the centre of the office: a man whose companies the government regulated, investigated and paid, serving inside that government, and what happened, matter by matter, to the cases and contracts involving those companies during his service and after it. The third is the ending: Musk's departure and his break with Trump, the fading of DOGE's central office, its legal termination on 4 July 2026, what persisted inside the state, and a ledger of DOGE's public claims against the record. The last section tests the book's worst-case reading against Book Three as a whole.
