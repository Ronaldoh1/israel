"From the Nile to the Euphrates" is not official Israeli policy, but the idea of a larger Israel is held by people in the governing coalition. The phrase comes from Genesis 15:18 and is the most extreme of three versions of "Greater Israel"; it is rejected as literal policy even by most religious Zionists. [cite:genesis_1518_text] The more mainstream version is the Likud platform, which says "between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty." [cite:likud_charter_platform]

Some statements go further. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich spoke in Paris in March 2023 beside a map that included Jordan, and has said Jerusalem's future is to "extend all the way to Damascus." [cite:smotrich_map_paris_2023] In August 2025, asked if he connects to a pendant map reaching into Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and Iraq, Netanyahu replied "very much so," and 31 Arab and Islamic states condemned it. [cite:netanyahu_jewelry_2025] On the ground, Israel annexed the Golan in 1981 but returned Sinai in 1982. "A secret master plan" overstates it; "no one in power wants more land" understates it.
