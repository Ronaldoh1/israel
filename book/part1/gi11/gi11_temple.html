The Temple Mount: The Status Quo and the Third Temple Movements

The walled platform in Jerusalem's Old City is the holiest site in Judaism, where the two ancient temples stood, and, as the Haram al-Sharif with the al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock, the third holiest site in Islam. [cite:gi11_reuters_tishabav26] It is not a border question in the way the Golan or the Jordan Valley are, but it belongs in this part for two documented reasons: the movements that seek a Third Temple on the site draw on the same religious nationalism traced in the previous section, and a serving minister has called for Israeli "sovereignty and governance" over it. [cite:gi11_toi_tishabav25] This section sets out the arrangement that governs the site, who has sought to change it, what changed between 2023 and 2026, and what Israel's government, Jordan and others have said.

The arrangement of 1967

When Israel took the Old City in June 1967 it took control of the site but left its administration with the Jerusalem Islamic Waqf, a religious trust run from Jordan; it barred the Israeli flag from flying over it and did not extend several of its laws on holy places there. The Chief Rabbinate declared that Jews were forbidden to enter, because the ritual purity required to approach the former Temple's grounds could not be attained. [cite:gi11_wiki_entry] The result, known as the status quo, is usually summarised as: Muslims pray, others may visit. It was never written into Israeli legislation. [cite:gi11_jp_aug2024] [cite:gi11_cnn_tishabav25]

Jordan's role has a treaty basis. In Article 9 of the 1994 peace treaty, Israel undertook to respect "the present special role of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan in Muslim Holy shrines in Jerusalem." [cite:gi8_incore_jotreaty] In 2013 King Abdullah II and the Palestinian president, Mahmoud Abbas, signed an agreement recognising the king's custodianship of the Jerusalem holy sites, though neither Jordan nor the Palestinian Authority controls them in practice. After violence in October 2015, the US secretary of state, John Kerry, announced that Israel had accepted King Abdullah's proposal for round-the-clock monitoring, and Netanyahu reaffirmed Israel's "commitment to upholding unchanged the status quo of the Temple Mount, in word and in practise." [cite:gi11_aje_2015] The site has repeatedly been the point at which wider violence began: the Second Intifada followed Ariel Sharon's visit on 28 September 2000, and the Gaza war of May 2021 followed police raids on the compound. [cite:gz_wiki_intifada2] [cite:gz_wiki_2021] [scene:gz3_oslo|Gaza, Documented: Oslo to the intifada]

The Third Temple movements

The best documented of the groups that seek to rebuild the Temple is the Temple Institute, founded in Jerusalem in 1987 by Rabbi Yisrael Ariel with the stated aim of establishing the Third Temple and restoring its rites. It has made many of the more than ninety ritual objects it says the Temple requires, among them the high priest's breastplate and golden crown. Israel's education and culture ministries were reported to have given it between NIS 300,000 and NIS 700,000 over a decade, a sum its directors have described as neither reliable nor significant. [cite:gi11_wiki_templeinst] In September 2022 five red heifers were flown in from Texas, donated by an evangelical farmer, for the purification ritual that some hold must precede the Temple's rebuilding; the Temple Institute and an evangelical group, Boneh Israel, were involved, and the director general of Israel's Ministry of Jerusalem Affairs and Heritage spoke at the airport when they arrived. Hamas's military spokesman cited the heifers in a January 2024 speech as evidence of a plan against al-Aqsa. [cite:gi11_aje_heifers]

These groups are a minority position within Judaism, not its consensus. Orthodox opinion broadly expects the Temple to be rebuilt but divides over whether by human hands or by divine act, and the Temple Institute's reading of Maimonides has, in one reference account's words, "aroused substantial opposition." [cite:gi11_wiki_templeinst] The Chief Rabbis of Israel, Ashkenazi and Sephardi, reaffirmed the ban on Jews entering the site in December 2013, citing Abraham Isaac Kook's ruling and stating that the decision had "absolutely nothing to do with the political situation"; at the time, mainly national-religious groups encouraged visits, partly to assert a Jewish claim. [cite:gi11_toi_rabbis2013] Haredi authorities generally prohibit entry; some Religious Zionist rabbis permit it under purity rules or treat it as a commandment. [cite:gi11_wiki_entry]

What changed, 2023–2026

The change came through practice and through a minister. Ben-Gvir, as national security minister responsible for the police, said on 26 August 2024 that "the policies on the Temple Mount allow prayer, period." [cite:gi11_jp_aug2024] The Prime Minister's Office answered the same day that "there is no change to the status quo" and that "it is the government and the prime minister who determine policy on the Temple Mount"; the defence minister, Yoav Gallant, called his ascent "dangerous, unnecessary and irresponsible." [cite:gi11_jp_aug2024] On 3 August 2025, the fast of Tisha B'Av, Ben-Gvir openly led prayers on the Mount, reported as the first time a minister had done so, and demanded that Israel enforce "sovereignty and governance" there. Netanyahu's office replied that the policy of maintaining the status quo "has not changed and will not change." Jordan called the visit "an unacceptable provocation" and said Israel had "no sovereignty over the blessed Al-Aqsa Mosque"; Saudi Arabia said such acts "fuel conflict in the region." The Times of Israel reported that police enforcement against Jewish prayer had grown more lenient over the previous three years. [cite:gi11_toi_tishabav25] [cite:gi11_cnn_tishabav25]

In January 2026 Netanyahu said that allowing Jewish prayer "does not breach" the status quo and that policy there was coordinated with him; police had by then allowed Jewish prayer in the compound's eastern sector for about two years, and the deputy attorney general, Gil Limon, warned that Ben-Gvir was altering the status quo unilaterally. [cite:gi11_na_netanyahu2026] On 23 July 2026, Tisha B'Av again, Ben-Gvir filmed himself at the site praising Jewish visitors who had prayed: "Jews praying, feeling like they're the owners of the place." He added: "This has never happened before." Jordan's foreign ministry called it "a desecration of the sanctity of the mosque, an escalation, a barbaric act, and unacceptable provocation"; Netanyahu's office did not immediately comment. [cite:gi11_reuters_tishabav26] [cite:gi11_olj_tishabav26] [cite:gi11a_olj_tishabav26]

The consequences are documented in one of Israel's own reports. In March 2025 the Shin Bet listed, among three factors in Hamas's timing of the attack of 7 October 2023, Israel's handling of far-right Jewish religious activity at the al-Aqsa compound, alongside harsher treatment of prisoners and the perception that Israel's social cohesion had weakened during the protests over the judicial overhaul. [cite:gi11_npr_shinbet] That is the security service's assessment of Hamas's reasoning, not a finding that the policy caused the attack, and Hamas's own statements are examined in the Hamas volume. [scene:hm7_knew|The Hamas Architecture: what was known]

As of October 2026, then, the documented position is this. The Israeli government's stated policy is that the status quo stands; no law or written cabinet decision changing it was found for this book. In practice Jewish prayer has been tolerated in part of the compound, a serving minister has led it, and the prime minister has said that this does not breach the arrangement. Jordan, which holds a treaty-recognised role at the site, has called each step a violation. The Third Temple movements remain small and rabbinically contested, with modest documented state support. What links them to the Greater Israel question, on this book's reading, is not a map but a line of argument: Zvi Yehuda Kook held that no government could give up any part of the land, and Ben-Gvir has demanded Israeli "sovereignty and governance" on the Mount.
