Borrowers, Sellers and a Word

The first borrowers were Christian. In late 1486 the young Italian philosopher Giovanni Pico della Mirandola printed nine hundred propositions in Rome and challenged all comers to debate them. Among them was "the claim that magic and Kabbalah proved Christ's divinity". [cite:kb_sep_pico] His kabbalistic texts came from Flavius Mithridates, a convert to Christianity who translated them from Hebrew and Aramaic into Latin, and who inserted Christian wording not found in the originals. [cite:kb_sep_pico] Pope Innocent VIII indicted thirteen of the theses and later denounced the whole collection; Pico fled to France, where he was arrested at the pope's request and briefly held. [cite:kb_sep_pico]

What Pico began became a movement. Christian Kabbalists sought, as one account puts it, to make Kabbalah "a dogmatic weapon to turn back against the Jews to compel their conversion". [cite:kb_wiki_xkab] Its most important follower, the German Hebraist Johannes Reuchlin, published De arte cabalistica in 1517. [cite:kb_wiki_xkab] [cite:kb_jvl_reuchlin] Reuchlin also did something else. When the convert Johannes Pfefferkorn sought, from 1510, to confiscate and destroy Hebrew books, Reuchlin refused to condemn the Talmud, saying it "was not composed for every blackguard to trample with unwashed feet". Pope Leo X decided against him in 1520, and all his works were later placed on the Index. [cite:kb_jvl_reuchlin] The Index that The Canon followed until 1966 held the books of the Christian who defended the Jewish books.

The modern seller is an American organisation. The Kabbalah Centre, based in Los Angeles, has been led by the Berg family since Philip Berg was named head rabbi in 1969. Its red-string bracelets helped bring Kabbalah into popular culture after Madonna began studying there in 1996. [cite:kb_herald_kc] In May 2011 the Los Angeles Times reported that the criminal division of the Internal Revenue Service was examining whether the Centre's nonprofit funds had been used to benefit the Berg family; a federal grand jury in Manhattan issued subpoenas naming the Bergs, Kabbalah Centre organisations and Madonna's charity Raising Malawi. [cite:kb_herald_kc] The Centre's assets "may top $260 million"; its tax status as a church meant it did not have to publish its filings. [cite:kb_herald_kc] The Centre confirmed it had received subpoenas and said it would work with the IRS, and called separate lawsuits against it "meritless". [cite:kb_herald_kc] [cite:kb_nbc_kc] The sources consulted for this book do not report the outcome of the investigation.

The language did the rest. The English word "cabal" comes from Kabbalah, by way of French and medieval Latin. It first appeared around 1575 meaning Kabbalah itself; by 1606 it meant any secret or arcane knowledge; and before the English Civil War it came to mean a secret plot against the crown. [cite:kb_wo_cabal] A popular story says the word comes from the initials of five ministers of Charles II; that story is false, though a historian noted in 1724 that their initials happened to spell it. [cite:kb_wo_cabal] The word kept the secrecy and lost the knowledge.

That drift matters, because the word is now a staple of conspiracy theories, and some use it, and the name Kabbalah, to suggest that a hidden Jewish power runs the world. The record in this book points the other way. Kabbalah was a teaching that rabbis restricted within their own communities, by age and by ban, out of fear of what it did to the few who studied it. [cite:kb_ou_age40] Its most famous mass movement ended with its messiah converting to Islam under threat of torture. [cite:kb_brit_sabbatai] Christian scholars borrowed it to argue for conversion; the Christian who defended Jewish books ended up on the Index; and its best-known modern seller is an American nonprofit investigated by the IRS. [cite:kb_wiki_xkab] [cite:kb_jvl_reuchlin] [cite:kb_herald_kc] A tradition is not a people, and a word for a plot is not a finding.
