Who Could Have Sat in His Place?

Removing Epstein gives nothing at all, but almost by definition: the operation is defined as what he and Maxwell owned and ran, so it falls with him. The harder question is whether the place he held could have been filled. The map asks it two ways, counting only documented ties and naming no one as a real or possible successor.

Maxwell in his place. Each of his ties passes to her only where she had her own documented tie to the same person or institution; the companies, houses and aircraft stay with the seat. The chance falls to about 1 percent. Banking, places and staff partly carry over, but very little of the money does: on this record she had no tie of her own to Wexner, Black or his other payers.

Someone else with the same institutions. Every tie to a bank, company, government or property passes to a newcomer, and every tie to a person is lost. The chance falls to about 8 percent. Banking, places and staff carry over almost whole, because they served any rich client; the money falls to about a tenth of its strength, because it came through personal relationships.

The map's succession test agrees. Asked which documented node sits at or beside the ties Epstein held, it finds no one close to filling them alone: the best placed, Wexner, covers about 42 percent of the lost capacity; two nodes together cover half, and five cover 80 percent. The seat could have been filled. The patron could not.
