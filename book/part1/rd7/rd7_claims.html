The Claims Register

The same few claims about how the state came to be are repeated in every argument. This register sets each one beside two things: the verdict the field guide in Volume I gives it from the documents, and the test on the map that measures how much of the founding depended on the fact the claim disputes. A claim can be false and the fact behind it still matter little to the founding, or true and the fact matter a great deal; the two columns answer different questions, and neither replaces the other.

Three patterns stand out. The claims that make the founding look self-made, from national liberation to David against Goliath, are the ones the map contradicts most: the British sponsor and the arms from abroad are among the few parts without which the founding becomes doubtful. The claims that make it look like one family's or one lobby's project are contradicted the other way: the Rothschilds' documented money mattered, and removing it costs most of the chance, but no single family, lobby or president carried the whole founding. And the claims about 1948 itself, about who left and why, are questions the map cannot settle, because they concern the war and the population after the state was declared; the record on them is in the volumes that follow.
