The Ending: What DOGE Left Behind

DOGE was built to end. The executive order that created it gave its temporary organization a termination date, 4 July 2026, the 250th anniversary of independence. [cite:de0_eo14158] [cite:de21_ee_end] Its public leader left thirteen months before that, quarrelled with the president, and returned to his side as a donor. Its central office faded through the autumn of 2025, and by the time the date arrived there was no final report and no final count. This section follows that ending: Musk's departure and the break, the wind-down, what persisted inside the government, and the ledger of DOGE's public claims against the record. It closes with the test of the book's worst-case reading against Book Three as a whole.

Musk leaves

On 28 May 2025 Musk posted that his "scheduled time as a Special Government Employee" was coming to an end; the 130-day limit on that status could be reached on 30 May. [cite:de13_abc_musk_exit] At a farewell in the Oval Office that day, Trump gave him a golden key and Musk said: "This is not the end of DOGE. It's really just the beginning." The DOGE website then claimed $175 billion in savings, and Musk said he still expected a trillion over time. [cite:de21_wt_farewell] Six days later the two men were at war in public over the Republicans' tax and spending bill. Trump threatened Musk's government contracts; Musk said Trump would have lost the election without him and posted, without evidence, that Trump was in the Epstein files, a post he deleted. [cite:de11_npr_feud] On 11 June he wrote that some of his posts "went too far," and on 5 July he announced an "America Party." [cite:de11_audit_pbs_regret] [cite:de11_ap_party] The party went no further. On 21 September 2025, at the memorial for Charlie Kirk, Musk sat beside Trump and posted a photograph captioned "For Charlie"; Trump said, "I thought it was nice that he came over." [cite:de21_cnn_kirk] In December 2025 Musk said that DOGE had been "a little bit successful" and that he would not do it again. [cite:de21_ee_end] By October 2026 he was the largest known donor of the midterm elections, and on 8 October Trump presented him with the National Medal of Science. [cite:de11_ap_2026]

The office fades

Without Musk, DOGE lost the centre it had in public view. In November 2025 the director of the Office of Personnel Management, Scott Kupor, told Reuters that DOGE "doesn't exist," then wrote that it "may not have centralized leadership" but that "the principles of DOGE remain alive and well." [cite:de21_ee_end] The administration disputed reports that DOGE had disbanded as a central entity. [cite:de21_fnn_vought] Its savings page was not updated after 1 January 2026, and the website later went offline. [cite:de21_ee_end] [cite:de21_fnn_vought] On 30 June 2026 the budget director, Russell Vought, told House appropriators that there would be no closing report and that "the fruits of their labor are sprinkled all across the government." [cite:de21_fnn_vought] The temporary organization terminated on 4 July. The U.S. DOGE Service itself, the renamed U.S. Digital Service, did not: the president's budget sought $35 million for it, a House appropriations report asked "the Administrator of DOGE" for quarterly reports on hires and transfers, and its acting administrator, Amy Gleason, said it was "largely continuing that same mission" as the digital office it replaced. [cite:de21_ee_end]

The people went into permanent posts. GAO counted 206 DOGE personnel in the Executive Office of the President between January 2025 and January 2026, of whom at least 128 had left those positions; ten agencies did not answer its questions about the rest. [cite:de21_gao_personnel] Gleason moved to lead a health-technology office at the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services; Joe Gebbia, a co-founder of Airbnb, became chief design officer of a new National Design Studio reporting to the White House chief of staff, where Edward Coristine and Zachary Terrell also went. [cite:de21_ee_end] Who these operators were is set out in [scene:de18_the_team|Part Eighteen].

What persisted

Three things outlasted the office. The first is the smaller workforce: across 22 agencies the civilian workforce fell by nearly 256,000, more than 11 per cent, between December 2024 and January 2026, on GAO's count, though some agencies had begun rehiring by mid-2026. [cite:de21_gao_workforce] [cite:de21_fnn_vought] (OPM's own figure uses a different definition; both are set out in [scene:de20_the_cuts|Part Twenty].) The second is a change to the terms of the civil service that DOGE did not make but that followed the same direction: OPM's "Schedule Policy/Career" rule, in effect from 8 March 2026, which removes standard removal protections from career positions judged to influence policy, with up to 50,000 employees estimated to be covered; OPM says it forbids loyalty tests and patronage, and its critics say it can be used to remove experts. [cite:us2_opm_schedpc] [cite:us2_govexec_schedf] The third is the legal fight over what DOGE was. The Supreme Court agreed in October 2026 to decide whether the DOGE Service is subject to the Freedom of Information Act, with argument expected early in 2027; the administration's case is that it "does not exercise independent authority." [cite:de21_ncc_cert] That and the other suits are the subject of [scene:de19_the_courts|Part Nineteen], and the line from Yarvin's 2012 proposal to the 2026 rule is examined in [scene:de30_rage_to_schedule|Part Thirty].

The claims ledger

DOGE's public case rested on claims that can now be checked. The headline figure is the clearest. Musk promised "at least $2 trillion" in October 2024, a claim graded in [scene:de11_claims_vs_record|Part Eleven]; DOGE's final figure was $215 billion; and GAO, examining the $110 billion of it attributed to contracts, grants and leases, could not verify most of it. [cite:de11_fortune_2t] [cite:de21_ee_end] [cite:de21_gao_full] Itemised entries failed earlier. In February 2025 the "Wall of Receipts" listed an $8 billion ICE contract worth $8 million and counted one USAID cut three times; DOGE said it had "always used the correct $8M in its calculations." [cite:de21_nyt_receipts] In the same month Musk said the Social Security rolls included people "150 years old." The records were real; a 2023 inspector general's report had found millions of entries with no date of death, and the acting commissioner said such people "are not necessarily receiving benefits." [cite:de21_abc_ssa150] [cite:de21_aa_dudek] Musk's general answer to errors, given that month about another claim, was: "Nobody's going to bat a thousand." [cite:de21_abc_ssa150]

On openness, Musk wrote in November 2024 that "there should be no need for FOIA requests," and said in February 2025 that "all of our actions are maximally transparent." Two weeks later the government told a court that "USDS is not subject to FOIA." [cite:de21_reason_foia] Its explanation is a legal one, that the office only advises the president, and the Supreme Court will rule on it. [cite:de21_ncc_cert]

Five of the seven are ORANGE: claims the official auditor or the agency's own records could not bear out. One is YELLOW, a stated position on openness abandoned in court, with the government's legal explanation; one is GREY, because the administration's own officials describe DOGE's survival differently. None is RED, because no sworn statement or document of the speaker's own contradicts the claim, and the word "lie" is not used: the record shows claims that were wrong or unverifiable, not that the speakers knew them to be false.

The worst-case reading, tested

The hypothesis is the Sophia platform's, not a finding: government cuts as the occasion, a sovereign executive without a veto as the destination. Across Book Three, what the reading calls the occasion, cuts carried out through an office with reach into the machinery of the state, is documented; that the cuts were used as a pretext for something further is not. An executive order alone created the office; its public face was the largest donor of the 2024 election, though in law he did not lead it; its legal form, an adviser's office rather than an agency, is the basis of the government's argument that it owes the public no records under the Freedom of Information Act, and the White House told a court that its public leader was not in charge of it ([scene:de17_money_to_office|Part Seventeen], [scene:de17_what_doge_was_legally|its second section]). [cite:de11_cnn_290m] [cite:de7_os_donors2024] [cite:de21_ncc_cert] [cite:de11_audit_fisher] Its teams reached systems whose extent the auditor could not determine, at agencies that refused to cooperate with the audit ([scene:de19_the_access|Part Nineteen]). [cite:de21_gao_systems] The workforce shrank by a quarter of a million, and the aid cuts carry modelled projections of millions of deaths whose methods are disputed ([scene:de20_who_paid|Part Twenty]). [cite:de21_gao_workforce] [cite:de21_ucla_lancet] Its savings claims and its promises did not survive audit. [cite:de21_gao_full]

Probable: that the opacity was a matter of policy rather than accident, since refusals to the auditor came from the Executive Office and from several agencies over many months, some disputing GAO's authority itself; and that the presence of the 2024 election's largest donor inside the government made enforcement against his companies more cautious. Both rest on pattern and timing, not on a document. [cite:de21_gao_personnel] [cite:de21_gao_systems] [cite:de21_bberg_sec]

Not supported: that DOGE was the Dark Enlightenment's program in operation. Yarvin's 2012 proposal to retire all government employees, RAGE, rhymes with DOGE [cite:de6_tnr_duran], but no document shows it as DOGE's source, and rhyme is not provenance ([scene:de6_the_program|Part Six], [scene:de1_spine_and_overlay|Part One]). Musk's own stated case was the reverse of the sovereign-executive design, "rule of the people — democracy" against "rule of the bureaucracy." [cite:de3_dcpd_musk] And the record runs against the destination at several points. The dividend stalled on the objections of the president's own party in Congress, which holds the purse. Courts blocked parts of the programme ([scene:de19_the_courts|Part Nineteen]), and the Supreme Court will rule on its secrecy. [cite:de21_usat_dividend] [cite:de21_ncc_cert] The donor and the president split in public. DOGE ended on the date its own order set, and agencies began rehiring. [cite:de11_npr_feud] [cite:de21_fnn_vought] What moved durably toward the presidency, the Schedule Policy/Career rule and the removal power the Supreme Court confirmed in Trump v. Slaughter, moved through rule-making and a court ruling, channels that stayed open to challenge; that is a real shift of power toward the presidency, documented as such, but not an executive without a veto. [cite:us2_opm_schedpc] [cite:de0_slaughter] The occasion happened; the destination is not shown.
