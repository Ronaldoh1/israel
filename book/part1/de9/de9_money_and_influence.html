Money and Political Influence

This section is a ledger. It lists the political money from Thiel that is documented in federal filings or in reporting based on them, cycle by cycle, with the recipient, the amount and the standing of the source; then the documented influence, meaning appointments, hires and the people from his firms who came to hold office; then the government contracts of companies he is tied to, with the type of tie. It applies one rule throughout. A donation that comes before an outcome is a sequence, not a cause: that money was given and that a candidate later won, or a former employee was later appointed, are each documented facts, but no filing documents that the first produced the second, and where a causal claim circulates it is named as a claim.

The political money

By the accounts available, Thiel's federal giving was modest and conventional until 2016, then concentrated and very large in 2022, then small again. In the 2015–16 cycle he gave $2 million to a super PAC supporting Carly Fiorina's presidential campaign and, in the general election, $1.25 million to help elect Donald Trump. [cite:de9_bp_thiel] [cite:de9_cnn_transition] In 2018 he gave $1 million to Club for Growth Action and $100,000 to the Congressional Leadership Fund, the House Republicans' super PAC; in 2020 more than $2 million to Free Forever PAC, which backed Kris Kobach in the Kansas Senate primary, and $100,000 to the Senate Leadership Fund. [cite:de9_bp_thiel] The 2022 cycle is the outlier: about $32 million, by CNBC's count of FEC records, almost all of it to two super PACs supporting Vance and Masters. [cite:de9_cnbc_2022] Super PACs are committees that may raise and spend unlimited sums on a candidate's behalf provided they do not coordinate with the campaign, which is why money that could not legally go to a candidate directly could go to them in eight-figure amounts.

Three cautions apply to the table. First, the figures for 2022 differ by source: CNBC's count from FEC records gives $15 million for Vance and $17.5 million for Masters, while Ballotpedia, relying on Forbes, gives $10 million and at least $20 million; the FEC-based figures are preferred here, and neither set is summed with the other. [cite:de9_cnbc_2022] [cite:de9_bp_thiel] Second, in October 2022 Thiel offered the Senate Leadership Fund a matched deal for Arizona of at least $10 million in all, half from him, if it found the other half; no record shows the match completed. [cite:de9_axios_masters] Third, the 2024-cycle figure is the least clear line in the ledger: an FEC-based total from Ballotpedia, without dates or recipients, against two outlets' descriptions of a cycle sat out. [cite:de9_bp_thiel] [cite:de9_mj_2025] Thiel's charitable vehicles, whose grants are reported on tax returns rather than to the FEC, are a separate and more opaque channel, examined in [scene:de14_foundations_and_opacity|Part Fourteen].

The documented influence

Influence is harder to measure than money, and the only kind this ledger records is the kind with a document behind it: a position held, a job given, an introduction made and reported. Thiel himself has held one official role, on the executive committee of Trump's 2016 transition. [cite:de9_cnn_transition] The larger pattern is that people who worked for him or alongside him came to hold office in 2025. The table names the type of each tie, and its degree: direct (worked for or with him), or one step removed (tied to a firm or fund rather than to him personally).

The table is a map of documented ties, not of control. Each person on it has a career and views of his own, and several held positions or relationships with Trump independent of Thiel. Fortune's reporting in December 2024 and May 2025 described the pattern as Thiel's network reaching into the administration; what it documents is employment and partnership histories, not instructions. [cite:de9_fortune_net24] [cite:de9_fortune_net25] The people themselves, and where each sits as of October 2026, are covered in [scene:de13_where_they_sit_now|Part Thirteen]; Musk's role is in [scene:de11_money_and_influence|Part Eleven] and Vance's in [scene:de12_money_and_influence|Part Twelve].

Contracts

Thiel holds no federal contract in his own name. Palantir, which he chairs and in which he is a large shareholder, does. By early May 2025 it had more than $113 million in new and existing federal contracts under the second Trump administration, by the New York Times's count, and a Defense Department contract for artificial-intelligence software worth $795 million, which could rise to $1.3 billion. [cite:de9_mj_2025] In August 2025 the Army consolidated dozens of Palantir agreements into one enterprise contract worth up to $10 billion over ten years. [cite:ow9_bd_army] These are the company's contracts, won by the company, and the tie to Thiel is that of chairman and shareholder: real, direct and financial, but not the same as a personal award. Companies held by Founders Fund, such as SpaceX and Anduril, have their own government contracts; the tie there is one step further removed, through a fund with outside investors. [cite:de9_wiki_ff] The contracts and their history are in [scene:de27_money_and_war|Part Twenty-Seven] and [scene:de28_ice_contracts|Part Twenty-Eight].

What is not documented

Three things often said about this ledger are not in it. No document shows Thiel paying Curtis Yarvin for his writing; the documented ties are Founders Fund's investment in Yarvin's company and a Thiel Fellowship to a future co-founder of it. [cite:de5_wiki_urbit] [cite:de5_audit_tc2011] No document shows that Thiel's 2022 money bought Vance's selection as Trump's running mate in 2024; the claim that Thiel lobbied for it rests on a single report and is treated as contested in [scene:de12_senate_and_the_ticket|Part Twelve]. And no document shows that any contract to Palantir followed from his political giving; the sequence of donations, appointments and awards is documented, and so is the absence of any record connecting them as cause and effect. Where the money runs in loops, from donor to office-holder to contract and back, the candidate loops and their evidence are set out in [scene:de14_loops|Part Fourteen].
