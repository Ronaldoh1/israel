Housing Against Income

Housing is the largest single cost for most households in both countries and the item on which the two systems' records differ most. It is also the item on which their measures differ. Israeli statisticians usually count how many months of the average wage it takes to buy the average home; American researchers usually divide the median home price by median household income. Both are ratios of price to income, but one uses a single wage and the other a whole household's income, and a household often has more than one earner. The two sets of figures below are therefore set side by side over time within each country, not against each other as if they measured the same thing. [cite:us14_bls_ce2024] [cite:us14_reuters_cpiweights] [cite:us14_globes_salaries2023] [cite:us14_jchs_pti] Land, planning law and public housing, which drive these prices, are the subject of Part Fifteen. [scene:us15_housing|Part Fifteen: housing]

Israel: from 129 salaries to 155

Israel's housing measure rose across the 2010s. In 2010, by a survey published by the Housing and Construction Minister, an average-priced apartment cost 129 average monthly salaries. [cite:us14_wiki_j14] The Ministry of Construction and Housing put the figure at 146 salaries for 2014, and the CBS put it at 155 salaries, or 12.9 years of the average wage, for 2022, when the average home cost NIS 1.87 million and the average monthly salary was NIS 12,100. The spread between cities was wide: 26.9 years of the average wage in Jerusalem, 25.4 in Bnei Brak and 22.2 in Tel Aviv, against 10.1 in Haifa and 10 in Beersheba. [cite:us14_globes_salaries2023] The three estimates come from different bodies whose methods may not match exactly; they agree on the direction.

Behind the ratio is a long rise in prices. The CBS index of dwelling prices rose about 226 per cent in nominal terms from 2008 to 2025, and about 136 per cent after inflation, by Global Property Guide's compilation of the index. Real prices rose 15 per cent in 2009 alone and 10 per cent in 2021, and fell in only a few years, among them 2018, 2023 and 2025. [cite:us14_gpg_israel] By late 2025 the market had cooled: the annual rise had slowed to 0.5 per cent after peaking at 7.8 per cent earlier in the year, and in the third quarter the average apartment cost NIS 3.03 million in Tel Aviv and NIS 2.90 million in Jerusalem, against about NIS 1.57 million in the south and north. [cite:us14_toi_housing_nov25] The Bank of Israel cut its policy rate to 4.25 per cent in November 2025, its first cut in almost two years, and in the year to mid-2026 average prices fell about 2 per cent. [cite:us14_toi_housing_nov25] [cite:us14_globes_cpi_june26]

A fuller measure, Tel Aviv University's Alrov Institute Housing Affordability Index, combines the three things that decide whether a household can buy: home prices from Tax Authority transaction records, household income after tax from the CBS, and mortgage rates from the Bank of Israel, for a four-room flat in twelve cities, each quarter, most recently for the first quarter of 2026. [cite:us14_alrov_hai] Its values sit in quarterly reports that this book did not extract; they would allow an Israeli series with interest rates included, comparable in design to America's.

Rents rose with prices. They rose 34 per cent on average from 2005 to 2011, and 49 per cent in the Tel Aviv metropolitan area, by CBS figures; those increases were the immediate cause of the 2011 tent protest. [cite:us14_wiki_j14] The housing component of Israel's consumer price index was still rising faster than prices as a whole in 2026: it was up 3.8 per cent in the year to September against 1.6 per cent for all items. [cite:us14_te_cpi_sep26] The protest and the government's response are the subject of the last section of this part. [scene:us14_protests|The 2011 protests]

The United States: from three years of income to five

Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies (JCHS) tracks the national ratio of the median single-family home price to median household income. It averaged 3.2 in the 1990s, was 4.1 in 2019 and reached 5.0 in 2024, nearly matching the previous record. Between 2019 and 2024 median single-family prices rose 48 per cent while median household income rose 22 per cent. As in Israel, the spread between regions was wide: the ratio exceeded 12 in San Jose and was 7.3 in New York, but under 3 in McAllen, Akron and Toledo. [cite:us14_jchs_pti]

The same centre measures the share of households "cost-burdened", spending more than 30 per cent of income on housing. In 2024, 22.7 million renter households were cost-burdened, 49 per cent of all renters and a record number; the stock of units renting for under $1,000 a month fell from 8.3 million in 2014 to 5.8 million in 2024. [cite:us14_nlihc_jchs2026] Owners were burdened too: about 24 per cent of owner households, 20.7 million, spent 30 per cent or more of income on housing in 2024, four million more than in 2019. For buyers the change after the pandemic was steep: in March 2019, 49 per cent of homes listed for sale were affordable to a household earning $75,000 or less; in March 2026, 23 per cent were. [cite:us14_novoco_jchs2026]

Reading the two records together

A rough translation is possible, with its limits stated. Twelve point nine years of a single average wage, Israel's 2022 figure, is a heavier load than five years of a median household's income, America's 2024 figure, but an Israeli household often has more than one earner, and the Israeli figure uses the average home and the average wage rather than medians. [cite:us14_globes_salaries2023] [cite:us14_jchs_pti] The direction of travel is the firmer finding. In both countries homes became markedly less affordable relative to income: in Israel over the whole period from 2008, with real prices more than doubling, and in the United States most sharply after 2019. [cite:us14_gpg_israel] [cite:us14_jchs_pti] In both, the burden fell hardest on renters and on buyers in the largest cities.
