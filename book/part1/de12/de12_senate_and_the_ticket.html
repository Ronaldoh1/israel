The Senate and the Ticket

Vance went from private investor to Vice President of the United States in under four years, and he did it through two endorsements by Donald Trump: one for the Senate in April 2022 and one for the vice presidency in July 2024. Between and around those endorsements sits the money that made his first race possible, nearly all of it from one man, and a reversal of opinion about Trump that he has explained in his own words. This section sets out the race, the money and the reversal by date, and then examines the most widely repeated claim about his selection in 2024, which the record does not carry.

The money arrives first

On 15 March 2021, more than three months before Vance declared, Fox News reported that Thiel had put $10 million of his own money into a new super PAC, Protect Ohio Values, set up in anticipation of a Vance campaign, and that the family of the hedge-fund investor Robert Mercer had also made what the PAC called a "significant" donation. [cite:de12_foxbiz_pov] A super PAC is a committee that may raise and spend unlimited sums on a candidate's behalf, provided it does not coordinate with the candidate's own campaign; the form exists because of the Supreme Court's 2010 Citizens United decision and the appeals-court ruling that followed it. Vance announced his candidacy for the seat being vacated by Senator Rob Portman on 1 July 2021. [cite:de12_wiki_vance] [cite:de12_pbs_ap]

Thiel's money did more than buy advertising. Politico, as summarised by Forbes, reported that most of Vance's paid advertising was in effect outsourced to the super PAC, and that Thiel recruited about ten other major donors, among them his PayPal colleague David Sacks, who gave $1 million. [cite:de12_forbesau] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] CBS News, citing the Kansas City Star and OpenSecrets, added that Thiel steered at least $200,000 more through Per Aspera Policy, a non-profit he controlled that did not have to disclose its donors. [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] Under federal law a super PAC must operate independently of the campaign it supports. In June 2022 the Campaign Legal Center, a non-partisan watchdog, complained to the Federal Election Commission that Protect Ohio Values had given the campaign polling and voter-targeting data worth more than $1 million through a website hidden from the public; in May 2024 the Commission dismissed the complaint "in light of the public nature of the communications at issue." [cite:de15_clc_complaint] [cite:de15_fec_mur8009] The allegation was made and was dismissed; no finding of illegal coordination exists.

The reversal

The obstacle to the endorsement Vance needed was his own record. In 2016 he had told Charlie Rose he was "a Never Trump guy" who "never liked him"; he wrote in The Atlantic that "Trump is cultural heroin"; in a private message to his former college roommate Josh McLaurin, later made public by McLaurin, he said he went back and forth between thinking Trump a cynic like Nixon and "America's Hitler"; and he posted that Trump was "reprehensible" and that he would vote for the independent Evan McMullin. [cite:de12_axios_trump2016] The Associated Press also recorded that he had called Trump "dangerous" and "unfit." [cite:de12_pbs_ap] Before Trump endorsed him, Vance apologised repeatedly and deleted the critical posts. [cite:de12_axios_trump2016] His explanation, given most fully after he joined the ticket, was that he had been "certainly skeptical" in 2016 but that Trump "was a great president, and he changed my mind," and that he had "bought into the media's lies and distortions." [cite:de12_mediaite_hannity] "When you see somebody, you were wrong about them," he said, "you ought to admit the mistake." [cite:de12_axios_trump2016] The change of position and the timing are both on the record; why he changed is a question about motive that his own account answers one way and his critics another, and [scene:de12_claims_vs_record|the last section] grades it on that basis.

The primary and the general election

Trump endorsed Vance on 15 April 2022, in a crowded primary field. [cite:de12_wiki_oh2022] The following week Thiel gave Protect Ohio Values another $3.5 million. [cite:de12_pw_pov] In May Vance won the Republican nomination with 32.2 per cent of the vote, ahead of Josh Mandel (23.9) and Matt Dolan (23.3). [cite:de12_wiki_oh2022] By the time of his last gift, in April, Thiel had given the PAC about $15 million, according to CNBC and to CBS citing OpenSecrets; NBC, from Federal Election Commission records in July, put it at "over $14 million," and Ballotpedia, citing Forbes, gives $10 million. [cite:de15_cnbc_fundraiser] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de15_nbc_mtp] [cite:de12_ballotpedia_thiel] The figures reflect different counting dates and sources; the larger is the one most widely reported from the filings, and Politico called it the most ever given to a single Senate candidate. [cite:de12_fortune_mithril]

Thiel gave nothing more for the general election. CNBC reported in October that he had signalled he was done spending for Vance, whom he believed was on course to win. [cite:de15_axios_double] [cite:de15_cnbc_results] The money in the general came from elsewhere. In the third quarter Vance's own campaign raised more than $6.9 million against $17.2 million for his Democratic opponent, Tim Ryan, and the Senate Leadership Fund, the super PAC aligned with the Republican Senate leader Mitch McConnell, booked $28 million of advertising in Ohio; outside groups of all kinds spent about $94.8 million on the race. [cite:de12_vindy_q3] [cite:de12_propublica_oh] On 8 November 2022 Vance won by 53.0 per cent to 46.9. [cite:de12_wiki_oh2022] He was sworn in as a senator on 3 January 2023. [cite:de12_britannica]

The ticket, 2024

Trump announced Vance as his running mate on Truth Social on 15 July 2024, the first day of the Republican National Convention. [cite:de12_cnn_vppick] [cite:de12_britannica] CNN's reconstruction of the decision, published the next day, named the people who had pushed for him: Elon Musk, who took a call from Trump at the end; Donald Trump Jr., who told his father at a late dinner, "Listen, I think I've seen him on TV"; the broadcaster Tucker Carlson; Steve Bannon; David Sacks and the property developer Steve Witkoff; and Senators Tommy Tuberville and Jim Banks. [cite:de12_cnn_vppick] Others pushed against him: Rupert Murdoch lobbied for Doug Burgum, the Fox host Sean Hannity for Marco Rubio, and some donors for Glenn Youngkin. [cite:de12_cnn_vppick]

A line widely repeated since then says that Thiel "led the charge to convince Trump to pick Vance." It appeared in The New Republic on 22 July 2024, sourced by a link to a Rolling Stone article. [cite:de12_tnr] CNN's account does not mention Thiel at all, and Thiel's own public statements that summer were about money, not the choice of running mate: at the Aspen Ideas Festival in June he said, "You hold a gun to my head, I'll vote for Trump," but that he would not give to the campaign. [cite:de12_cnn_vppick] [cite:de12_forbesau] On the record as it stands, the claim is single-sourced and contested, and this book does not use it as a finding.

The record of the campaign itself runs, if anything, the other way. In April 2024 Trump asked Thiel for $10 million, and Thiel refused. [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] In November 2023 The Atlantic reported, in its writer's paraphrase, that Thiel wanted it known he "wouldn't be giving money to any politician, including Donald Trump, in the next presidential campaign"; in June 2024 he said of Trump, in his own words, "I'm not going to give any money to his super PAC." [cite:de9_pw_atlantic] [cite:de9_nbc_aspen] In August 2024 Vance himself told the Financial Times that he was "talking to Peter and persuading him" to contribute. [cite:de12_dbeast_ft] No resulting gift to the Trump campaign or its super PACs has been reported; Federal Election Commission records summarised by Ballotpedia show Thiel giving just over $1.7 million to federal candidates and parties in the 2024 cycle as a whole, a total that is not itemised by date and is tested against his pledge in [scene:de9_claims_vs_record|Part Nine]. [cite:de12_ballotpedia_thiel] A running mate publicly lobbying his old employer for money, without visible result, does not fit a picture in which the employer was directing the ticket. Vance and Trump won on 5 November 2024; Vance resigned his Senate seat at midnight on 10 January 2025 and was sworn in as the fiftieth Vice President on 20 January. [cite:de12_wiki_vance]

The race and the ticket establish the core facts on which every later argument about Vance and Thiel rests. Thiel's money was the largest single source of funds behind Vance's primary campaign, and it arrived before the candidacy did. Trump's endorsement, which Vance sought after a public reversal, came next, and Thiel's money followed it again. In the general election the party's money replaced Thiel's, and in 2024 the record shows Vance asking Thiel for money rather than Thiel choosing Vance. [scene:de15_vance_and_masters|Part Fifteen] sets the 2022 race beside the one Thiel financed in Arizona the same year.
