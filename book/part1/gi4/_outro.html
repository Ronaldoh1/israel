Part Four established that the conquests of June 1967 did not produce a single Israeli answer. On 19 June the government voted, ten to nine and then unanimously, to offer Egypt and Syria peace on the basis of the international border. It deferred any decision on the West Bank, leaned toward annexing Gaza and kept East Jerusalem. [cite:gi4_cie_june19] [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex] The whole-land programme was stated outright by one named body, the Movement for the Whole Land of Israel. Its manifesto, published on 22 September 1967 over 57 signatures from Labor activists, writers, Revisionists and Rabbi Zvi Yehuda Kook, declared that "no government in Israel is entitled to give up this wholeness". The full text is held in the Posen Library and in the movement's own 1977 history; only its opening sentences and that conclusion could be read online for this book. [cite:gi4_972_twoads] [cite:gi4_posen_manifesto] [cite:gi4_inss_einav] On the same day the socialist group Matzpen published the opposite demand. [cite:gi4_972_twoads] The movement won no seat in 1969, joined Likud in 1973 and was absorbed by Gush Emunim, the religious settlement movement founded in 1974, whose stated aim was the permanent incorporation of the whole land. [cite:gi4_wiki_mgi] [cite:gi4_lustick_gush] The Labor governments' own design, the Allon Plan, was never adopted. It would have kept about a third of the West Bank and returned the populated hills to Jordan, and Hussein turned it down in 1968.
