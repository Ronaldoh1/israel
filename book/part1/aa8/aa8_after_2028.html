After 2028

The idea of ending the aid came first from Israel's prime minister. In January 2026 Netanyahu said he wanted to taper American military aid off within ten years, and in February he proposed a gradual withdrawal over ten years to zero. [cite:ji_trump_netanyahu_aid] [cite:idn_netanyahu_10_years] Formal talks opened in the first week of June 2026 on a new framework, with the stated aim of moving "from aid to a completely reciprocal partnership" through joint investment and co-production. [cite:dv4_wiki_next_mou] [cite:ji_mou_talks_launch]

Trump's statement of 3 November 2026 that aid would end "by the end of this year" went further than any of this, and further than the law: the current agreement runs to the end of FY2028, and Congress wrote its annual levels into the defence authorization act for FY2021. [cite:aa_abc_trump] On 9 November Rubio said the president "would like for the financing portion to end as soon as possible" and that "we'll negotiate the timeline". [cite:dv4_toi_rubio_nov2026] The same day Ynet reported a draft under negotiation that would keep military financing at about $3.3 billion a year for five years after 2028, about $16.5 billion in all against $33 billion under the current model, and raise missile-defence funding by an amount not yet set; an Israeli official said Trump's announcement was mainly for domestic political reasons. [cite:aa_ynet_draft] On 24 November the ambassador to Israel, Mike Huckabee, said: "I'm not certain there will be a cessation on an abrupt basis by the end of this year." [cite:aa_tl_huckabee]

Status as of 25 November 2026: the current agreement remains in force at $3.8 billion a year; no new agreement has been signed; whether aid ends in 2026, in 2028 or after a further five years is undecided. Any new agreement would still need Congress to appropriate the money each year. [cite:aa_ynet_draft]
