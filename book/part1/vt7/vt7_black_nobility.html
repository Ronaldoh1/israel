The Black Nobility: Who They Were

"Black nobility" (nobiltà nera) is the name given to the Roman aristocratic families who sided with the pope after the Italian army entered Rome on 20 September 1870. While Pius IX and his successors called themselves prisoners in the Vatican, these families kept the doors of their Roman palaces closed in mourning, and the term came to cover them and also the people whom the popes ennobled between 1870 and the settlement of 1929. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob] The label is modern; the group is old. It grew out of the baronial families of medieval Rome and the families who had served the Papal States and the papal court for centuries. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob] How Rome was lost and why the popes refused to accept it is told in [scene:vt2_roman_question|Part Two: the Roman Question].

The first thing to fix about the black nobility is what kind of thing it was. It was a social allegiance, not an organization. It had no charter, no officers, no budget, no membership roll and no seat of its own. A family belonged by choosing the pope's side in a political quarrel with the Kingdom of Italy, and by holding offices and honours at the papal court. The standard list of families still in existence names the Colonna, Massimo, Orsini, Ruspoli, Pallavicini, Theodoli, Sacchetti, Borghese, Odescalchi and Boncompagni-Ludovisi; the Savelli, Caetani, Aldobrandini and Conti are counted among the lines now extinct; members of the Pacelli family, including Eugenio Pacelli, the future Pius XII, are named among its people. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob] Anything this volume says about "the black nobility" is therefore a statement about named families and named individuals, each with its own record.

What these families did for the papacy

Their documented role was service at court, not government. Several court offices passed by inheritance in particular families: before the reform of 1968, the office of Marshal of the Holy Roman Church and of the conclave belonged to the Chigi, the Grand Master of the Sacred Apostolic Hospice was a Ruspoli, and the hereditary Quartermaster General of the Apostolic Palace was a Sacchetti. [cite:vt7_wiki_hered] The highest lay honour was the office of Prince Assistant to the Papal Throne, held by the heads of the Orsini and Colonna; it has its own section below ([scene:vt7_prince_assistant_1958|the Prince Assistant and 1958]). Younger sons served in the Noble Guard, founded by Pius VII on 11 May 1801 and recruited from noble families, which stood beside the pope at ceremonies. [cite:vt7_wiki_nobleguard] These were posts of precedence and display. None of them carried authority over the Secretariat of State, over appointments of bishops or over papal finance.

Five families, five records

Colonna. The Colonna gave one pope, Martin V, elected on 11 November 1417, who reigned until his death on 20 February 1431. Sciarra Colonna took part in the assault on Boniface VIII at Anagni in 1303, and Marcantonio II Colonna commanded the papal fleet at Lepanto on 7 October 1571. Julius II's bull Pax Romana of 1511 ended the long feud between the Colonna and the Orsini. [cite:vt7_wiki_colonna] [cite:vt7_audit_brit_martin5] The record shows a family that fought popes as well as served them.

Massimo. The Massimo held the office of the papal posts: Camillo Francesco Massimo (1730–1801) was a papal postmaster, and Francesco Camillo Massimo (1865–1943) was Superintendent General of the Papal Posts. [cite:vt7_wiki_massimo] The family's claim to descend from the Roman general Fabius Maximus goes back to a book of 1556 and is "generally regarded as legendary"; asked by Napoleon whether it was true, a Prince Massimo is reported to have replied that he could not prove it. [cite:vt7_wiki_massimo]

Torlonia. The Torlonia were the newest family on the list and the one most tied to money. Giovanni Torlonia administered Vatican finances and was rewarded with titles: the family's Wikipedia article says Pius VI made him duke of Bracciano in 1794 and Pius VII made him prince of Civitella Cesi in 1803, while the article on the duchy dates the Torlonia purchase of the ducal title and the castle of Bracciano to 1803. [cite:vt7_wiki_torlonia] [cite:vt7_audit_wiki_duchy] The Torlonia bank was the Roman partner in the first Rothschild loan to the Papal States in the early 1830s, which the historian Daniela Felisini describes as made "in partnership with James de Rothschild". [cite:wa_felisini] What the loan documents show and do not show is the subject of [scene:vt11_papal_loans|Part Eleven]. Villa Torlonia, bought by Giovanni Torlonia in 1797, was Mussolini's residence from 1925 to 1943; the city of Rome bought it in 1977. [cite:vt7_mvt_villa] The one-lira rent repeated in earlier platform material is not stated by the museum and is not confirmed here.

Pacelli. The Pacelli were lawyers, not barons. Marcantonio Pacelli served the papal government, in finance under Gregory XVI and in the interior ministry under Pius IX from 1851 to 1870; his son Filippo was a lawyer of the Rota. [cite:vt7_wiki_pius12early] Filippo's son Francesco Pacelli (1872–1935), dean of the Rota's lawyers, conducted the day-to-day negotiation of the Lateran Pacts with Mussolini's government for Pius XI, alongside Cardinal Gasparri, and was made a marquess by papal brief on 9 June 1929. His three sons were made princes by royal decree in 1941 and 1942, and one of them, Marcantonio, became a brigadier general of the Noble Guard. [cite:vt7_wiki_fpacelli] That Pius XII's brother negotiated the 1929 settlement is documented and worth stating; it does not by itself show any motive for Pius XII's wartime choices, which are examined on their own record in [scene:vt16_war_and_pius_xii|Part Sixteen].

Orsini. The oldest of the families in papal history, and the one around which most of the claims in earlier platform material gather, has its own section: [scene:vt7_orsini_record|the Orsini record].

1929: what the treaty said about nobles

The Lateran settlement of 11 February 1929 touched the nobility in two places, and neither matches what the earlier sources say. Article 42 of the Concordat committed Italy to recognize, by royal decree, noble titles "conferred by the Supreme Pontiffs even after 1870" and those conferred in future. [cite:vt7_va_lateran] Italy then regulated the use of papal titles and decorations by Royal Decree no. 974 of 10 July 1930, limiting applications to Italian and Vatican citizens. [cite:vt7_rd974_1930] Article 10 of the Treaty exempted persons on an agreed list of the Papal Court from Italian military and jury service. [cite:vt7_va_lateran] Vatican citizenship, by contrast, followed residence and office: Article 9 subjects to the Holy See's sovereignty "all persons having stable residence" in Vatican City, and no article names the Roman nobility. [cite:vt7_va_lateran] The simulation's statement that the noble court positions were "formally abolished under the 1929 Lateran Treaty" is wrong: the treaty confirmed the papal nobility's titles in Italian law and left the court intact. The statement on Wikipedia that the black nobility received dual citizenship in 1929 is not supported by the treaty text read here. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob]

1948 to 1970: how the formal standing ended

The end came from two directions. The Italian Republic's Constitution of 1948 provides in its fourteenth transitional provision that the Republic does not recognize titles of nobility; predicates of titles that existed before 28 October 1922 survive only as part of a surname, and the Constitutional Court confirmed that narrow reading on 26 June 1967. [cite:vt7_cc_101_1967] Inside the Vatican, Paul VI's motu proprio Pontificalis Domus of 28 March 1968 turned the Papal Court into the Papal Household, declared that no office in it was to be regarded as hereditary and reduced the Noble Guard to an honorary cohort. [cite:vt7_pd_latin] Perks such as Vatican number plates were withdrawn. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob] In 1970 Paul VI abolished the Noble Guard altogether. [cite:vt7_wiki_nobleguard] Some nobles resented the change, and in 1977 a group led by Princess Elvina Pallavicini began to court the traditionalist Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre, which is to say that part of the old court nobility ended up in open opposition to the reigning pope's line. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob]

The earlier Sophia module's subtitle for these families, "the families that owned the papacy", fails on this record. The documented offices were ceremonial; a pope created them, a pope could remove a holder (as Pius XII did in 1958), and a pope abolished their hereditary basis by a single motu proprio in 1968. The families' real influence was the ordinary influence of old Roman wealth: property, marriages, bank boards and access. That is a finding about a social elite. It is not evidence of control.
