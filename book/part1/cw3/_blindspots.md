## 2. Series blind-spot list (applied)

- **Palestinian political agency:** Kafr Qasim's villagers as actors (refusing to bring the accused to the sulha; the elders' verdict on the trial; the 2016 march; Raz and Issa on the records); the refugees' own account at Khan Yunis and Rafah given beside Israel's; Gaza's councils under occupation. The fedayeen as an Egyptian-run force (Morris) is named, not used to collapse Palestinian agency into Egypt's.
- **The strongest Israeli case:** Ben-Gurion's 15 October and 7 November statements, the blockade and fedayeen record, Article 51, his 17 November reply to Bulganin, the case for keeping Gaza for security, and Oren's argument that Israel won.
- **Arab states' strongest case:** Nasser's claim that Egypt withdrew rather than lost; the 'aggression' framing in Saud's message and Egypt's UN letter; Egypt's resistance at Abu Ageila and Mitla.
- **Jewish dissent:** Leibowitz on the verdict; the Communist MKs Toubi and Vilner breaking the blackout; Avnery's opposition; Morris's use of 'massacre'.
- **Jews of the Arab world:** the Egyptian expulsions (cw3_egypt_jews), from rd6's sourcing; flagged as a gap needing new research.
- **Real antisemitism named plainly:** the 23 November 1956 proclamation that 'all Jews are Zionists and enemies of the state', as a named state act.
- **Palestinian citizens of Israel:** Kafr Qasim across three sections, from the order to the 2021 Knesset vote, with the regime linked to rd6.
- **Terms examined:** 'collusion' (and Eden's denial); 'massacre' (who uses it: Morris, UNRWA's figures, Israel's denial); 'sulha' (a village custom used by the state); 'manifestly unlawful order' / 'black flag'; 'occupation' of Gaza in 1956; 'Third Kingdom'.
- **Convergence, not conspiracy:** three powers with three different aims at Sèvres (Canal, Algeria/Nasser, borders and shipping), and the US government against its own allies; inside Israel, the commanders who did not shoot at Kafr Qasim and the prosecutors who did not charge Shadmi with murder.
- **Money and alignment:** the US used financial pressure on its allies (Hahn; the Humphrey call); Israel received only economic aid then (aid_arms:Overview:2); the Doctrine's money went to states other than Israel.
- **Sources kept independent:** Kafr Qasim counts from five independent bodies of sources; Gaza figures from the UN, an Israeli historian, Palestinian sources and Sacco; casualties from IDF, Keightley, Hansard and the Library of Congress.

## 3. The reader's questions this stage must not leave hanging

Did Israel go to war to defend itself or by arrangement with Britain and France, and what did each want? Who knew about Sèvres, and why did Eden try to destroy it? Who gave the Kafr Qasim order, how many died, and was anyone punished? Was Kafr Qasim part of a plan to expel the Triangle's Arabs? What happened at Khan Yunis and Rafah, and how sure are the numbers? Why did Eisenhower force his allies out, and did the Soviet threat matter? What did Israel get for leaving Sinai and Gaza, and did it keep it? Who won? Each writer lists the section-level questions and answers them in the prose or says the record does not.
