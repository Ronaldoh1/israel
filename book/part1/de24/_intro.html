Book Five turns from leaving the state to the tools that watch the people who stay, and it begins with data. The first section maps the records that already exist about most American adults and can be bought or borrowed: data brokers and the two investigative databases immigration enforcement licenses, fusion centres, licence-plate cameras, and the government's own files opened to new uses since 2025. The second reads what the apps on a phone, the artificial-intelligence assistants and the shops say they keep, and judges the court cases against them by what actually changed. The third tests the promise on which the commercial trade rests, that data stripped of a name stays anonymous, and describes, as a mechanism rather than a documented practice, how an integration platform could turn an unnamed trail into a person. Each section ends by asking what these systems could do if they were joined, graded from documented to not supported, and the part closes by testing the book's worst-case reading.
