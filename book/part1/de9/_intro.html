This part tells the life of Peter Thiel, the investor whose money, companies and protégés run through the rest of this book. It begins with a childhood that moved from Frankfurt to Cleveland to the mining towns of apartheid-era southern Africa and on to a California suburb, and follows him through Stanford, where he founded a student paper and met the philosopher René Girard, into law and finance. It then traces the fortune: PayPal, Palantir, the first outside stake in Facebook, and the funds that hold positions in SpaceX, Anduril and Curtis Yarvin's company, keeping each vehicle's money apart from his own. It sets out three episodes the public learned of late, the secret financing of the lawsuit that ended Gawker, a New Zealand citizenship granted after twelve days in the country, and a retirement account grown to about $5 billion, and then his politics from the 2009 essay that doubted democracy to the Senate campaigns of 2022 and his move to Buenos Aires in 2026. The last two sections are a ledger of his documented political money and influence, and a test of his own public claims against the record.
