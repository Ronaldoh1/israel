This is the fifth and longest book of the Dark Enlightenment Series. It maps the tools that let a government know where people are, what they buy, whom they talk to and where they travel, most of them built or bought on the occasion of immigration enforcement: data brokers and fusion centres, the records the state already holds on benefits, schools, work and money, phones and spyware, cars and homes, airport and border systems, faces, drones and satellites, money rails, Palantir, and ICE's data stack. It sets beside them the times American governments of both parties turned such tools on their own citizens, and the legal changes that have lowered the walls between datasets.

The book's rule is that capability is not intent. For every tool it records what the tool does, who has used it, where it has failed and what the law allows, then asks what would follow if it were connected to the others. Each connection is graded: documented when it exists today, probable when a contract or authority already points to it, possible when it is only technically feasible, and not supported when the record runs against it. The ecosystem map in Part Thirty draws the whole picture in one place.
