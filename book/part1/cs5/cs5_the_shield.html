The Shield

A secular state rests much of its case on sacred ground, and the record shows the shield working in three ways.

Scripture

The first is scripture held by others. In a Pew survey of 2013, 82 percent of white American evangelicals said God gave the land of Israel to the Jewish people, against 40 percent of American Jews. [cite:ts1_pew_2013] The Theology Shield follows how Christian Zionism became a reliably Republican constituency for Israel's government. [cite:ts9_rns_exit2024] On the network map of the Road to 1948, removing the Christian attachment to the land alone leaves the chance of a state at 90 percent; it mattered most later, as a political shield in America.

The second is scripture used by the state's leaders. In October 2023 Netanyahu, a secular man, told troops entering Gaza to remember what Amalek did. South Africa cited it at the International Court of Justice as evidence of genocidal intent; his office called that "false and preposterous", and Israel's counsel said the full quotation identified Amalek with Hamas. [cite:hm_jta_amalek] [cite:hm_na_amalek]

Persecution

The third is the charge of antisemitism. Executive orders since 2019 place the IHRA definition of antisemitism, with its examples about Israel, inside American civil-rights enforcement; the bill to make it law has stalled, and its own drafter has warned against using it this way. [cite:ow11_eo13899] [cite:ow11_eo14188] [cite:ow11_npr_stern] The Information War follows how the definition moved into platform rules and university discipline.

Antisemitism is real and documented, and the memory of the Holocaust is real. The finding is about use: a state's policy is defended as if criticism of it were an attack on a people and a faith. Many observant Jews reject that equation, on religious grounds. [cite:neturei_karta] The government also includes religious parties, Shas, United Torah Judaism and the religious Zionists, whose members are observant. [cite:us7_globes_conversion2021] The shield is the use of the faith for political ends, whatever the observance of the person holding it.
