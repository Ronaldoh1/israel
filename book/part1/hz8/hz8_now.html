Where Things Stand, October 2026

As of 19 October 2026 the 19 June ceasefire was holding in the limited sense that Hezbollah had stopped firing; Israel had not stopped. Israeli strikes and demolitions in the south continued through the summer and autumn, including beyond Israel's declared security zone, and Hezbollah had not responded to them since mid-June. [cite:hz8b_olj_qassem] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] Israeli forces still held a strip behind the Yellow Line covering about 6 percent of Lebanon, in places about eight kilometres deep, with dozens of villages emptied or flattened and their residents barred; NPR reported Israeli tanks in Zawtar al-Gharbieh and fighting on Ali al-Taher hill near Nabatieh in September. [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] [cite:hz8b_olj_salam] Netanyahu's stated position was "as long as it is not disarmed, we will remain in this zone." [cite:hz8b_national_tampa]

Direct talks had become routine. After five rounds in Washington and two in Rome by early August, the two sides had agreed in September to separate a political track from a military one, and military delegations met in Tampa, Florida, in the week of 19 October, with the United States in the middle. Lebanon went in refusing US proposals for its army to coordinate directly with Israel's and demanding third-party verification and a full withdrawal; the pilot-zone process had stalled for months, and the verification mechanism had not been agreed. [cite:hz8b_aj_rome] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] On disarmament, the army's phased plan had reached its second phase, the land between the Litani and the Awali, on 16 February, with four months allotted, two weeks before the war overtook it; Qassem had called the focus on disarmament "a grave sin." [cite:hz8b_aj_phase2] The public record does not show any further cabinet decision or completed phase since then. Hezbollah's position on 6 October was that it was "ready to cooperate" on the state's monopoly on arms on the basis of the constitution, but would accept no "American diktats," and that "Israel cannot remain in southern Lebanon indefinitely." [cite:hz8b_olj_qassem]

About 40 percent of the displaced had gone home by late June; Salam said in September that return "cannot wait until the crisis ends," while the first prefabricated houses reached Zawtar al-Gharbieh, and the finance minister put the war's cost at roughly $20 billion. [cite:hz8b_olj_salam] UNIFIL's mandate, extended "for a final time" by the Security Council in August 2025 under resolution 2790, ends on 31 December 2026, with the force to leave during 2027. Aoun has asked for an extension or a replacement force; none had been agreed by late September. [cite:hz8_scr_nov25] [cite:hz8b_dn_unifil] [cite:hz8b_olj_unifil] [cite:hz8b_olj_salam] What the dated record settles is that the ceasefire of November 2024 did not survive into the spring of 2026: it ended in a war that killed more people in Lebanon in six weeks than the whole truce had in fifteen months, and that left Israeli troops holding ground deeper inside Lebanon than at any time since the withdrawal of 2000.
