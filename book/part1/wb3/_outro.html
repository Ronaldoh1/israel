Part Three followed the first decade of occupation. The war of 1967 drove about 300,000 Palestinians from the West Bank and Gaza, many for the second time, and the Latrun villages were razed. [cite:wb_prrn_1967] [cite:wb_wiki_imwas] Within three weeks Israel brought 70 square kilometres of East Jerusalem and the surrounding villages under its law, a step the Security Council declared invalid. [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex] Resolution 242 called for withdrawal "from territories occupied," in words read two ways ever since. [cite:wb_wiki_242] The government's own legal adviser warned that civilian settlement would breach the Fourth Geneva Convention; the government settled Kfar Etzion under military cover, followed the Allon Plan's lines, and accepted the settlers of Hebron. [cite:wb_gorenberg_meron] [cite:wb_wiki_allon] [cite:wb_wiki_hebron] By 1977 about 4,400 settlers lived outside East Jerusalem. [cite:wb_peacenow_pop] What is settled is the sequence. What is contested is whether the Convention applies, which Part Ten takes up. What changed in 1977 is the subject of Part Four.
