By the end of this stage, the ground had been cleared of several confusions that shape how the later story is usually told. "Semitic" was shown to be a linguist's term of 1781 for a family of languages that includes both Hebrew and Arabic, and "antisemitism" a word made into a movement's banner by Wilhelm Marr in 1879, meaning hatred of Jews and nothing broader. The dispute over whether that word should reach political criticism of a state, from the IHRA definition of 2016 to the Jerusalem Declaration of 2021 and its own critics, was set out without being settled.

The stage also separated Judaism, a religion and a set of lineages, from Zionism, a political movement founded at Basel in 1897, and traced Jewish opposition to that movement, and critique from within it or within the state it produced, through Reform Judaism, the Bund, Satmar, the binationalists and Mizrahi scholars. It placed the Star of David and the menorah in their actual chronology. It established that Ottoman Palestine was a named, administered region of about 600,000 people, and that Jewish continuity, Arab continuity and modern national identity are three separate questions. Finally, it showed that the contest over this history continues today, through paid influence campaigns and through content moderation, with documented conduct on more than one side.
