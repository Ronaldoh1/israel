Part Nine has set the Holy See's money out body by body. The IOR, founded on 27 June 1942, serves about 12,000 Church clients. In 2025 it held 5.9 billion euros of their money and 815.3 million euros of its own capital, earned 51 million euros and paid the pope a dividend of 24.3 million. [cite:vt9_ior_ar2025] APSA, created in 1967 from the office that managed the 1929 payment and given the Secretariat of State's investments from 1 January 2021, reported net equity of 2,686 million euros in 2025 and paid 22.7 million euros to the Curia. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa_mandate] [cite:vt9_mp_2020] [cite:vt9_apsa_bil2025] The Holy See as a whole closed 2024 with a surplus of 1.6 million euros only after investment gains and asset sales covered an operating deficit of 44.4 million, and its pension fund has an imbalance the pope himself acknowledged. [cite:vt9_america_hs2024] [cite:vt9_ap_pension] Peter's Pence sent between about 12 and 22 per cent of its spending directly to charity in the years reported, and the rest to the Holy See's offices, as the Holy See says it intends. [cite:vt9_vn_pp2024] [cite:vt9_vn_pp2025] [cite:vt9_vn_guerrero2021] The famous totals, "73 billion dollars" and "177 million acres", describe Church property held by separate legal owners, or have no stated method, and are not Holy See figures. [cite:vt9_marketplace_2023] [cite:vt9_cic_1254] Today's figures are the product of reforms forced by earlier scandals.
