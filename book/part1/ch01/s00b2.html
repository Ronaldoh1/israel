The Jewish Lineages: Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Mizrahi

If "Semitic" names a family larger than any one people, Jewish identity is itself a family of distinct communities. No one disputes that Ashkenazi Jews are Jewish. Two opposite errors nevertheless circulate. The first holds that Ashkenazi Jews descend from Central Asian Khazar converts and have no real Middle Eastern ancestry. The second holds that all Jewish communities are genetically and historically identical, as if two thousand years of separate histories had left no mark. The genetic record supports neither.

Each community has its own history. Mizrahi Jews, the Jews of Iraq, Yemen, Egypt, Iran, Syria and elsewhere, never left the Middle East. Their presence there goes back millennia; in Iraq it is traced to the Babylonian Exile of 586 BCE. As late as 1945, Baghdad alone held roughly 140,000 Jews, a third of the city. Sephardi Jews trace their roots to Iberia, where their presence predates the Muslim conquest. Ferdinand and Isabella expelled them in 1492, and most resettled across the Ottoman Empire and North Africa, among existing, related Middle Eastern Jewish populations. Salonika became a majority-Sephardic city for centuries under Ottoman rule. Ashkenazi communities formed in the Rhineland in the early medieval period and later moved east into Poland, Lithuania, Russia and Ukraine. By the nineteenth century they were the majority of world Jewry, and they produced most of the Jewish immigration to the United States.

The most thorough genetic study is a 2010 paper in Nature by Behar, Yunusbayev, Metspalu and 18 co-authors. The team compared genome-wide data from 14 Jewish communities across Europe, the Middle East, North Africa and Central Asia against 69 non-Jewish populations. Most Jewish communities, Ashkenazi included, clustered closer to one another and to Middle Eastern populations than to the peoples among whom they had lived. The paper read this as support for common origins in the Middle East, followed by migrations. [cite:behar_2010_nature] It also found structure within that picture: an Ashkenazi, North African and Sephardi cluster, a Caucasus and Middle Eastern cluster, and a distinct Yemenite cluster. The finding, then, is not that every Jewish community is genetically identical. It is that each carries a detectable shared ancestral signal, with real regional differences layered on top. No single study is beyond revision, but this one remains the most comprehensive of its kind, and its core finding has held up under later attempts at replication.

The Khazar hypothesis has been tested directly. It proposes that Ashkenazi Jews descend substantially from the Khazars, a Turkic people of the Caucasus and southern Russia whose ruling elite is reported to have converted to Judaism around the eighth or ninth century CE. That elite conversion is a separately documented event. The claim that it explains the origin of the whole Ashkenazi population is a much larger one. In a 2013 follow-up, Behar and colleagues compared Ashkenazi genetic data with samples from peoples native to the old Khazar region and found, in their words, "no indication" of a Khazar contribution. Ashkenazi ancestry traces primarily to Middle Eastern and European populations. [cite:behar_2013_khazar] The hypothesis matters beyond genetics because it has a documented history of use in antisemitic argument, to claim that Ashkenazi Jews have no real connection to the land and are not "really" Jewish.

The European share in Ashkenazi ancestry is real and should be stated plainly. It is substantial on the maternal line, the product of roughly two thousand years of intermarriage and conversion in European societies. It sits alongside, not in place of, the shared ancient Levantine origin on the paternal line that the same studies document.

Scripture, read on its own terms, makes the same point about scope. In the genealogies of Genesis 10–11 and 25, Shem's line runs through many generations to Abraham, and then Abraham's line divides. Ishmael is traditionally associated with Arab peoples. Through Isaac come Esau, traditionally associated with Edom, and Jacob, later renamed Israel. By the text's own account, all three lines descend from Shem. [cite:genesis_10_11_bible] [cite:genesis_25_esau_ishmael] "Semitic" was never a synonym for the line of Jacob, which is one branch of the tree. The point is scope, not rank: the text presents all three as branches of one root.

An analogy helps, provided its limits are stated. A Salvadoran, a Mexican and a Peruvian are all Latino, yet each carries a different ancestral mix of Indigenous American, Spanish colonial and other ancestry. A different mix makes no one less Latino. In the same way, Mizrahi Jews carry concentrated, continuous Levantine ancestry, while Ashkenazi Jews carry that same documented root plus substantial European admixture. The comparison illustrates one structure: a shared category containing different histories. It does not claim that Jewish and Latino identity work the same way historically, religiously or genetically.

The Khazar claim returned to public view on February 19–20, 2026, when Tucker Carlson interviewed Mike Huckabee, the U.S. Ambassador to Israel. Carlson asked why everyone in the land should not be genetically tested to find out who Abram's descendants are, saying that "we've cracked the human genome." He singled out Benjamin Netanyahu, citing his family's Polish origin. Huckabee, a committed Christian Zionist, pushed back in the room, calling it a "dangerous conspiracy theory" from "some of the darkest realms of the internet." Reporters at JTA, the Times of Israel, the Jerusalem Post and the Forward identified the underlying claim as the Khazar theory. [cite:jta_carlson_khazar_2026] [cite:toi_carlson_khazar_2026] The episode raised a separate question that deserves its own answer: what threshold of ancestry would make an identity claim legitimate? No geneticist, historian or legal body has ever defined one, for this question or any other. Treaties, borders and citizenship law do not run on DNA percentages, for Israel or for any other nation. Keeping the tested and unsupported genetic claim apart from that broader question is how the discredited version is kept from returning by another door.
