Technocracy Inc. and the Grandfather

One inheritance is regularly drawn for the Dark Enlightenment that its own texts never mention. It runs from a Depression-era American movement called Technocracy, through a Canadian chiropractor who led it in his province, to that man's grandson, Elon Musk, and from Musk to the government cuts of 2025. The line has the appeal of a family saga and a real documentary core. This section separates the core from the saga. It sets out what Technocracy was, what is documented about Joshua N. Haldeman, what Musk himself has said, and where the line from the 1930s to 2025 is documented and where it is only drawn.

A rule governs the whole section. A grandfather's views are not his grandson's. Haldeman died in 1974, when Musk was two years old. [cite:de3_cbc_leo] What Haldeman wrote and did is his record; what Musk has said and done is Musk's. The two meet in this book only where Musk's own words make the connection.

What Technocracy was

Technocracy grew out of the work of Howard Scott, an engineer who, with the geologist M. King Hubbert, founded Technocracy Incorporated in 1933 after an earlier collaboration with Columbia University collapsed. [cite:de3_wiki_scott] Its central proposal was to abolish the "price system." Money, wages and markets would be replaced by energy accounting, and engineers and scientists would manage production and distribution. [cite:de3_wiki_scott] The historian Kevin Baker, writing in American Heritage, described the plan's currency: certificates equal to the energy the country used in a year, issued equally to every citizen, which had to be spent within the year or became worthless, so that no one could accumulate capital. [cite:de3_ah_baker]

The territory to be run this way was continental. Technocracy proposed merging Canada, Greenland, Mexico, the United States and parts of Central America into a single unit governed by technical principles, which it called the Technate. [cite:de3_townley] The movement's moment was brief. Baker reports that membership may have reached a quarter of a million Americans in early 1933, and that within days of a disastrous speech by Scott at New York's Hotel Pierre on 13 January 1933 Columbia distanced itself and Congress was mocking the movement; it survived into the Second World War but never regained momentum. [cite:de3_ah_baker] Scott himself, it emerged, had overstated his credentials. [cite:de3_wiki_scott]

Two features matter for comparison with the texts in this book. Technocracy was hostile to markets and private capital; it wanted to abolish money. And by Scott's own account, as the historian Jill Lepore quoted him in 2025, the movement "has no theory for the assumption of power." [cite:de3_lepore]

Joshua N. Haldeman

Haldeman was born in 1902 in Minnesota and raised in Saskatchewan, trained as a chiropractor, and lost his farm in the Depression. [cite:de3_cbc_leo] He was active in Technocracy Inc. from 1936 to 1941; Lepore describes him as its leader in Canada, technocrat No. 10450-1. [cite:de3_wiki_haldeman] [cite:de3_lepore] After Canada banned the organisation under its wartime regulations in 1940, he was arrested on 8 October 1940 and charged with membership in an illegal organisation; a Regina court fined him $200 later that month over a document titled "Statement of Patriotism by Those Who Were Technocrats." [cite:de3_wiki_haldeman] He left the movement in 1941, citing what he saw as its turn toward collaboration with the Soviet Union. [cite:de3_cbc_leo]

His later politics are documented in detail by CBC News, which reported in March 2025 from newspapers and his own pamphlets. He led the Social Credit party in Saskatchewan from 1945 and chaired its national council from 1946. When the party's Quebec wing published the Protocols of the Elders of Zion, a forged text purporting to reveal a Jewish plot to rule the world, Haldeman defended the publication; whether the document was fraudulent, he said, "is not the point." [cite:de3_cbc_leo] In 1950 he moved his family to Pretoria. In 1951 he wrote in the Regina Leader-Post that South Africa's government "knows how to handle the native question," and later pamphlets described an "Invisible Government" serving an international conspiracy. [cite:de3_cbc_leo] He died in a plane crash on 13 January 1974. [cite:de3_cbc_leo] These are the documented views of Haldeman: support for apartheid, and the defence of an antisemitic forgery. His life is told more fully in [scene:de11_pretoria_and_haldeman|Part Eleven].

What Musk has said

Musk's documented references to his grandfather, as collected by CBC, concern adventure rather than politics: "My grandfather had this desire for adventure, exploration — doing crazy things," he told his biographer Ashlee Vance. [cite:de3_cbc_leo] His one documented use of the movement's name came in a post in June 2019: "Accelerating Starship development to build the Martian Technocracy." He gave no explanation of what he meant. [cite:de3_phys_tweet]

His own stated views on government point in more than one direction. Asked at the Code Conference on 2 June 2016 how Mars would be governed, he said it would "most likely" be "a direct democracy, not representative," with people "voting directly on issues." [cite:de3_inc_code] In the Oval Office on 11 February 2025, defending the cuts led by the Department of Government Efficiency, he framed his project as a defence of elected government against officials: "We don't live in a democracy. We live in a bureaucracy," and if the bureaucracy is in charge, "what meaning does democracy actually have?" [cite:de3_dcpd_musk] That is close to the opposite of Technocracy's programme, which wanted elected politicians replaced by unelected experts. Whether the stated reason is the true one is a separate question; the stated reason is documented, and it is not the technocrats' reason.

The line that is drawn, and the line that is documented

The case for a living inheritance has been made most forcefully by Lepore, in a New York Times guest essay of 4 April 2025. Her judgement, offered as opinion, was that "little of what Mr. Musk proposes is new" and that many of his ideas "resemble those championed by" his grandfather. [cite:de3_lepore] Dafydd Townley of the University of Portsmouth, writing in The Conversation in March 2025, found resonances, in the distrust of elected politicians and the drive for efficiency, and also differences: Musk's commerce is rooted in market competition, and the administration he served does not seek to abolish money or wages. [cite:de3_townley] Neither writer claims a document showing transmission of a political programme from grandfather to grandson.

A stronger version circulates widely online: that a continuous plan runs from the 1930s technocrats through later elite bodies to the present administration, and that the 2025 interest in Greenland, which the Technate map included, is that plan being executed. No document supports the claim. The individual facts in it are real; the continuity and the intention are supplied by the teller. A resemblance between a 1930s map and a 2025 policy is rhyme, not provenance.

The comparison with the Dark Enlightenment texts sharpens the point. Land's serial never mentions Technocracy, and its economic wish-list runs the other way: hard money, "abolition of central banking," and the dismantling of "state monetary and fiscal discretion" so that the market can run without managers. [cite:de3_land_de] Technocracy wanted to abolish markets and money and put engineers in charge; Moldbug wanted a state owned by shareholders and run by a chief executive for profit. [cite:de3_land_de] [cite:de3_wiki_scott] They share a contempt for elected politicians and a faith in competence. They do not share a theory of property, of money, or of who should rule.

The documented line, then, is short. A man who led a technocratic movement in Canada and later defended an antisemitic forgery and apartheid was Elon Musk's maternal grandfather; Musk once used the word "Technocracy" in a single, unexplained post about Mars; and serious writers have argued that the family resemblance runs deeper. [cite:de3_cbc_leo] [cite:de3_phys_tweet] [cite:de3_lepore] What is not documented is any statement by Musk taking up his grandfather's programme, or any path by which Technocracy entered the Dark Enlightenment texts, which neither cite nor resemble it in their economics. [cite:de3_land_de] The family tie belongs in Musk's biography; it does not belong in his ideology unless he puts it there.
