The West Bank

In the West Bank, unlike Gaza or Lebanon, a documented programme of territorial expansion stands beside the military operations. That programme is a matter of law and record: settlement written into the Nation-State Law as a national value, Smotrich's civil powers, the votes in the Knesset and the land-registration decision of 15 February 2026. [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis] [scene:gi5_nationstate|Part Five: the Nation-State Law] Part Six of this book and The West Bank, Documented set it out. [scene:gi6_plans|Part Six: the annexation track] [scene:wb9_annex|The West Bank, Documented: record approvals] This section keeps to the other side of the ledger: who was killed, who was driven from their homes, and what was destroyed, with each figure tied to the body that counted it.

Before October 2023

The West Bank had no war of its own in 2006, 2008–09, 2014 or 2021, but violence there never stopped. In the second intifada, from September 2000 to February 2005, B'Tselem counted (to April 2008, across the West Bank and Gaza together) 4,745 Palestinians killed by Israeli security forces and 44 by Israeli civilians. About 1,053 Israelis were killed by Palestinians. [cite:gz_wiki_intifada2] From 2006 to the end of 2022, by Oxfam's analysis of UN data, Israeli forces and settlers killed 1,036 Palestinians in the West Bank, 225 of them children, and Palestinians killed 86 Israeli settlers. [cite:gi14_oxfam_wb] Those numbers serve as a baseline. What happened after 2023 should be measured against them.

The dead since 2023

Three counts cover the years since. They use different periods and are not added together. The UN Human Rights Office counted 1,001 Palestinians killed in the West Bank and East Jerusalem from 7 October 2023 to 17 October 2025, 213 of them children. Israeli forces killed 968 of them, and settlers, alone or with soldiers, killed 33. In the same two years 59 Israelis, 22 of them members of the security forces, were killed by West Bank Palestinians in the West Bank and in Israel. [cite:wb_ohchr_1001] Oxfam's count, which covers whole calendar years from January 2023 and so includes nine months before the war, gives 1,244 Palestinians killed by the end of 2025, 268 of them children. That is more in three years than in the previous seventeen combined. Over the same three years 43 Israeli settlers, ten of them children, were killed. [cite:gi14_oxfam_wb] OCHA's monthly data give 240 Palestinians and 17 Israelis killed in the West Bank in 2025. From January 2025 to the end of August 2026 the totals are 322 Palestinians killed, 74 of them children (284 by forces, 25 by settlers, 13 by one or the other), and 20 Israelis, eight of them members of the security forces. [cite:wb_ocha_snap2025] [cite:gi14_ocha_wbsnap]

The UN office found that 449 of those killed by Israeli forces in the first two years were unarmed and not involved in violence when they were shot. It said force was being used "as a tool to control and repress," and noted that Israel announced investigations only rarely. The army described many of those its airstrikes killed as "terrorists." [cite:wb_ohchr_1001] For the first time since the second intifada, Israel used aircraft in the West Bank on a regular basis: the UN counted 108 airstrikes in the two years, mostly on refugee camps. [cite:wb_ohchr_1001] The West Bank volume gives the names and cases. [scene:wb9_war|The West Bank, Documented: since 7 October 2023]

Displacement: the camps and the hills

Two kinds of displacement ran side by side. The first was military. On 21 January 2025 the army launched Operation Iron Wall in the Jenin refugee camp and extended it to Tulkarm and Nur Shams. Its stated aim was to degrade the armed groups' infrastructure and remove imminent threats. In February tanks entered Jenin, the first in the West Bank since 2002. [cite:wb_wiki_ironwall] UNRWA's count, as of August 2026, was 12,557 people displaced from Jenin camp (a figure that includes people displaced during the Palestinian Authority's own operation there), 12,412 from Tulkarm and 9,207 from Nur Shams, 34,176 from the operations in all. Most had been out of their homes for nineteen months. [cite:gi14_ocha_wbsnap] [cite:gi14a_ocha_wbsnap] By October 2025, 52 per cent of the structures in Jenin camp had been destroyed or damaged. In September 2026 the UN Human Rights Office said the displacement may amount to forcible transfer, a crime against humanity; Israel describes the operation as counter-terrorism. [cite:wb_jurist_ohchr2026]

The second was in the hills of Area C, by settler attack and by demolition. OCHA counted about 1,492 settler attacks in the first year of the war alone (7 October 2023 to 14 October 2024), and 3,459 attacks causing casualties or damage from January 2025 to August 2026. [cite:gi14_ocha_wb230] [cite:gi14_ocha_wbsnap] The Israeli group Kerem Navot counted 57 Palestinian communities displaced between October 2023 and December 2024, 50 of them entirely, while at least 41 new outposts and herding farms were set up. [cite:wb_972_villages] Israeli authorities demolished 2,702 Palestinian structures between January 2025 and August 2026, 94 per cent of them for lack of building permits. [cite:gi14_ocha_wbsnap] [scene:wb5_areac|The West Bank, Documented: living in Area C] In all, UNRWA counted 41,951 Palestinians displaced, about 15,882 of them children, as of August 2026. Oxfam counted nearly 46,000 displaced in three years, against just over 13,000 in the fourteen before. [cite:gi14_ocha_wbsnap] [cite:gi14_oxfam_wb] In the first three months of 2026 Oxfam recorded more than 540 settler attacks and more than 60 water and sanitation structures vandalised. [cite:gi14_oxfam_wb] The West Bank volume documents the attacks and prosecution rates. [scene:wb8_settlers|The West Bank, Documented: settler violence]

Israel's stated reasons, and the attacks beside them

Israel's government gives two reasons for its operations: armed groups in the northern camps, which the Palestinian Authority's own forces had been fighting in Jenin since December 2024, and attacks on Israelis. [cite:wb_wiki_ironwall] The attacks are part of the record. The 59 Israelis killed by West Bank Palestinians in the first two years, and the 20 killed in the West Bank from January 2025 to August 2026, were killed in shootings, stabbings and car-rammings in the West Bank and in Israel. [cite:wb_ohchr_1001] [cite:gi14_ocha_wbsnap] On settler violence, the record is one of rare prosecutions, which the West Bank volume sets out with the answers Israel's leaders gave. [scene:wb8_settlers|The West Bank, Documented: settler violence]

Destabilisation, as of October 2026

The Palestinian Authority, which Israel and the United States rely on for security coordination, was close to collapse. Israel withheld all clearance revenues, the taxes it collects on the Authority's behalf, from June 2025. By March 2026 it had withheld NIS 11.7 billion and deducted NIS 12.5 billion since 2021. The Authority paid half of February's salaries in June, and its debts and arrears reached about 72 per cent of GDP. [cite:wb_mas_jun2026] [scene:wb12_revenue|The West Bank, Documented: the money] In the same years the settlement enterprise grew at a record pace: 27,941 housing units were advanced in 2025, and the cabinet approved 102 new settlements between December 2022 and March 2026. [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis] As of October 2026 the northern camps were still under army control. [cite:wb_wiki_ironwall] Whether the violence and the settlement drive are connected is not left to inference here. The connection is made in documents, in the stated programme of named officials, which Part Six sets out. [scene:gi6_ground|Part Six: on the ground] Whether Iron Wall was a counter-terrorism operation, as Israel says, or part of that programme, is an argument. The UN office's forcible-transfer finding and Israel's rejection of it stand on opposite sides of it.
