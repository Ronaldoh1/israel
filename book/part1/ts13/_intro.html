Part Thirteen turns from the politics of the name Israel to the name itself, and to an older way of reading it. It sets out first what the record documents: the name's earliest appearance, on an Egyptian victory stele of about 1208 BCE, the story of Jacob's night in Genesis 32, and what Hebrew philology says the name means. It then reports the readings that took Jacob's night as a story of the soul, from Philo of Alexandria and Origen to the Kabbalah and modern rabbinic commentary, and the later Christian image of the "dark night". A section headed as an interpretive lens lays out the IS-RA-EL reading this platform holds, beside the philology that does not support it and the little that can be traced of its origin. The part then separates what is documented about the ancient mysteries and the ascent of the soul from the modern claim of an Egyptian mystery-school lineage, and ends with a labelled argument about how the same name has been read as an inner map and as a land deed.
