In the spring of 1936 the Mandate for Palestine was in its second decade, and the forces that would contest it were already organized. Hajj Amin al-Husseini had been Mufti of Jerusalem since 1921, and the Palestinian parties led by his family and by the Nashashibis were rivals. [cite:wiki_amin_al_husseini] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_wiki_ahc] On the Zionist side the Haganah existed without British recognition, Ze'ev Jabotinsky's Revisionists had claimed both banks of the Jordan since 1925, and the Irgun had formed in 1931. [cite:wiki_special_night_squads] [cite:jabotinsky_revisionist_1925] In November 1935 British forces killed Izz al-Din al-Qassam. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_ebsco_strickert] Five months later, on 15 April 1936, two Jews were killed near Tulkarm, and within ten days a general strike had been called from Nablus and local committees across the country had united as the Arab Higher Committee. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_origins_yehudai] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_mee_strike] In the United States there was not yet a law requiring anyone working for a foreign cause to register, and the leaders of American Zionism were citizens acting as citizens. [cite:doj_fara]

This stage asks how the centre of the Palestine question moved, in less than a decade, from a British administration putting down a rural revolt to the committee rooms of the United States Congress and the platforms of both American parties. It asks with equal weight what each party to the question did as the doors closed on Europe's Jews: what Britain decided and why it abandoned what it had proposed, what the Palestinian Arab leadership chose and what those choices cost, what the Zionist movement demanded and by what means, and what the American president said in public, in private and in writing. The answer is not a single turn but a sequence of them, each documented, and several still argued over.

The cast is wide. In London stood the royal commission known after its chairman as the Peel Commission, a technical commission under Sir John Woodhead, Malcolm MacDonald defending the enforcement of the White Paper of 1939, and its critics in the Commons, Leo Amery, Winston Churchill and Herbert Morrison; in July 1945 Clement Attlee formed a Labour government. [cite:rs_rd4_pw_unccp_1949] [cite:rs_rd4_wp_jvl_debate] [cite:rs_rd4_lab_frus_yale] In Palestine and in exile were al-Husseini, Raghib and Fakhri Nashashibi and the peace bands Britain paid, Jamal al-Husseini's delegation in London, and the neighbouring Arab rulers who mediated the strike's end in 1936 and sent delegations of their own to London in 1939. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_hughes_peacebands_full] [cite:rs_rd4_pw_wiki_london1939] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_hcc] The Zionist movement spoke through Chaim Weizmann and David Ben-Gurion, and in America through Abba Hillel Silver and Stephen Wise, while Hillel Kook, calling himself Peter Bergson, ran an insurgent campaign of his own and Lehi offered to fight on Germany's side. [cite:biltmore_medoff] [cite:azec_case] [cite:w_kook] [cite:academickids_lehi] In Washington Franklin Roosevelt, his State and War Departments, and Senators Robert Wagner and Robert Taft each held a lever; aboard the USS Quincy, King Ibn Saud held another. [cite:djn_1944_hearings] [cite:brookings_quincy_75years] Behind all of them stood the Nazi state, which turned to open violence in November 1938 and to the coordinated murder of Europe's Jews. [cite:ushmm_kristallnacht] [cite:ushmm_wannsee_conference]

The sections follow that sequence. They open with [scene:s07a5|the Arab Revolt] from its first killings to its suppression and its disputed death toll, and with the Peel Commission's partition plan, its proposal to move some 225,000 Arabs, and the three answers it drew. Woodhead and the London conference show how partition was dropped. Évian, where 32 countries declined to take more refugees and Palestine was kept off the agenda, and Kristallnacht four months later frame [scene:rd4_white_paper|the White Paper of May 1939] and the fight over whether it broke the Mandate. [cite:wiki_evian] The war years follow: British training of Jewish fighters, the clandestine fleet and the ships the limit turned away, Lehi and Lord Moyne, al-Husseini's road through Iraq to Berlin, the Vatican's wartime record, and the Holocaust from Wannsee to the counting of six million. The American turn runs through the Biltmore Program, the Bergson campaigns, the watchers in Washington, the Emergency Council's letter machine and the resolutions of 1944–45. The stage closes with Roosevelt's two registers, his concealed illness and the choice of Truman, the meeting at Great Bitter Lake, and Labour's arrival in office.
