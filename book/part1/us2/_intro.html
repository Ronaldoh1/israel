Part Two sets out how each state is put together before the rest of the book measures what it delivers. It compares America's single written constitution, amendable only with three-quarters of the states, with Israel's Basic Laws, written chapter by chapter since 1950 and mostly changeable by a Knesset majority; a president elected apart from Congress with a prime minister who governs only through a coalition; and fifty states with reserved powers with a centralised system of local authorities. It then follows the courts in both countries, from Israel's judicial overhaul of 2023 to 2026 to the American Supreme Court's tariff ruling of February 2026, and how far the public trusts them. It traces emergency powers, Israel's single state of emergency declared in 1948 and renewed ever since beside America's 51 standing national emergencies, and ends with the civil service and the corruption measures that track both states from 2012 to 2025.
