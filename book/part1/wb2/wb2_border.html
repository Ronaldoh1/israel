The Border War

Infiltration. The Green Line ran between villages and their fields, and across it lay the homes many refugees had fled. Through the early 1950s thousands crossed it. The Israeli historian Benny Morris estimated that 90 percent or more of these crossings in 1949–1956 were made to return to homes, harvest fields, recover property or see relatives, rather than to attack. Israel's army, border police and police adopted a policy of shooting suspects on sight, and between 2,700 and 5,000 infiltrators were killed in those years, by Morris's estimate "the great majority of them unarmed." [cite:rs_rd6_lrb_shlaim_1994]

Qibya, 1953. After a grenade attack on a house in the Israeli town of Yehud in October 1953 killed a woman and her two children, [cite:rs_cw2_jvl_qibya] Israel's Unit 101, commanded by Ariel Sharon, crossed into the West Bank village of Qibya, blew up 45 houses and killed 69 villagers, "the majority of them women and children." [cite:rs_rd6_lrb_shlaim_1994] Israel's own foreign minister, Moshe Sharett, wrote that had he foreseen "such a slaughter" he "would have raised hell." [cite:rs_cw2_jps_qibya] The UN Security Council expressed "the strongest censure." [cite:rs_cw2_avalon_res101] The library's account of [scene:cw2_unit101_qibya|Unit 101 and Qibya] sets out the raid in full.

Fatah, and Samu, 1966. By the mid-1960s the raids came from organized Palestinian groups, chiefly Fatah, which crossed into Israel from Jordanian territory without the approval of the Jordanian government, which tried to avoid incidents that invited reprisals. [cite:rs_cw6_cia_dec1966] [cite:rs_cw6_gs_jordan_f] On 13 November 1966, after a mine attributed to Fatah killed three Israeli soldiers near the line, Israeli forces with tanks and aircraft entered the West Bank village of Samu, near Hebron. [cite:rs_cw6_idf_archive_samu] [cite:wb_wiki_samu] The UN counted 125 houses destroyed, the Israeli army 40; sixteen Jordanian soldiers and airmen and three civilians were killed, and one Israeli officer. [cite:wb_wiki_samu] President Johnson's adviser Walt Rostow called the raid "out of all proportion to the provocation" and "aimed at the wrong target"; the Security Council, with the United States voting in favour, censured Israel. [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_d333] [cite:rs_cw6_res228]

The raid turned the West Bank against its own government. Riots spread across the territory with demands that Hussein be overthrown, and Jordanian police killed four Palestinians putting them down. [cite:wb_wiki_samu] [cite:rs_cw6_gs_jordan_samu] Six months later, on 30 May 1967, Hussein placed his army under Egyptian command, [cite:rs_cw6_gs_jordan_t] and within a week he had lost the West Bank.
