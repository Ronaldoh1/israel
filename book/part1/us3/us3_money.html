Money in Politics: Campaign Finance and Lobbying

The two systems pay for politics in opposite ways. Israel's parties are financed mainly by the state, and private giving to them is tightly capped. American candidates raise their own money, and since 2010 outside groups have been able to raise and spend without limit. [cite:us3_fh_il2025] [cite:pm_wiki_superpac] This section sets out how each system works, what the American totals show over time, and where pro-Israel money fits within them. It measures that money by dollars spent and by the documented record of what it bought, not by who the donors are.

Israel: public money, small private gifts

Freedom House summarises the Israeli system in two lines: parties "rely mostly on public funding", allocated according to their electoral performance, and private donations "are strictly limited". [cite:us3_fh_il2025] The weak point has been money that reaches politics around the parties rather than through them. In 2017 the Knesset passed what became known as the "V15 law", to stop wealthy donors using nominally separate political organisations to get around the election-finance rules. [cite:us3_toi_ngotax] This book did not verify the current per-seat funding amount, the donation caps in shekels or the State Comptroller's recent audit findings on party finance; these are gaps in the record presented here, not findings that nothing is there. Measures of corruption and the rules on the civil service are covered in Part Two. [scene:us2_integrity|Part Two: the civil service and corruption]

The United States: limits on candidates, none on outside spending

In the 2025–26 cycle an American candidate may accept $3,500 per election from an individual and $5,000 per election from a multicandidate political action committee (PAC). [cite:pm_fec_limits] Those limits apply to money given to a candidate. Since the Supreme Court's Citizens United ruling (January 2010) and the appeals court's SpeechNow.org decision (March 2010), groups that spend independently of candidates, known as super PACs, may raise and spend unlimited sums. They report their donors, but monthly or quarterly filing can delay disclosure until after a vote, and a "pop-up" super PAC formed within 20 days of an election can file its first report after it. [cite:pm_wiki_superpac] Freedom House put the cost of the 2024 cycle at an estimated $16 billion, the most expensive yet, and named Elon Musk as its top individual spender. [cite:us3_fh_us2026] The agency that enforces these rules was weakened at the same time: in 2025 the FEC was left with two members, which Freedom House said limited its ability to carry out its duties. [cite:us3_fh_us2026]

Lobbying, the other main channel, has also grown. Federal lobbying spending reached a record of about $5 billion in 2025, 14 per cent more than in 2024 and the largest one-year rise since 2008, in nominal dollars. [cite:us3_os_lobby2025] The biggest spenders are business and professional groups. In 2018, for example, the National Association of Realtors reported $72.8 million in lobbying, Boeing $15.1 million and Amazon $14.4 million. [cite:pm_tablet_lobbying]

Pro-Israel money in American elections

The spending most often cited in arguments about the two countries is that of the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) and its super PAC, the United Democracy Project (UDP). Measured as lobbying it is modest. AIPAC reported about $3.5 million in federal lobbying in 2018, around 147th among all spenders, and about $3.3 million in 2024 and nearly $3.8 million in 2025. [cite:pm_tablet_lobbying] [cite:pm_cjn_lobby2025] Measured as election spending it is large. Until 2021 AIPAC did not give to candidates. [cite:opensecrets_aipac_2019] By the end of July 2024 its new PAC had spent $44.8 million, mostly earmarked individual donations passed to candidates, and UDP about $55.4 million, about $100.2 million combined. [cite:pm_sludge_2024] By early August 2026 the two had again passed $100 million for the cycle, $104.3 million by Sludge's count from FEC filings. [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] The Library volume Follow the Money sets out the channels and the race-by-race record. [scene:pm1_channels|Follow the Money: the legal channels] [scene:pm5_2026|Follow the Money: the 2026 cycle]

What the money bought is less clear than its size. In 2024 UDP spent almost $9.9 million against Jamaal Bowman in New York's 16th district, and its preferred candidate won. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] In August 2026 it spent more than $30.6 million, its largest sum on a single race, in Michigan's Democratic Senate primary against Abdul El-Sayed, and he won. [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] [cite:pm_itt_elsayed] A Guardian analysis found that members of Congress who received more pro-Israel money were more likely to back military aid to Israel, but that party affiliation best predicted their statements on civilian casualties, and that the data could not show money had changed anyone's position. [cite:guardian_gaza_donors] An analysis of 788 AIPAC-backed candidates in 2022–24 found that about 86 per cent were incumbents and that its success rate in close races, about 79 per cent, was close to those of the NRA, Planned Parenthood and the Sierra Club. [cite:ow2_bahar_forward] The record supports a narrower claim than either side usually makes: the American system lets any organised interest, this one included, spend almost without limit to shape primaries, and that spending sometimes wins and sometimes does not.

Foreign money and its limits

American law bars foreign nationals, including foreign governments, from contributing or spending in any federal, state or local election. [cite:fec_foreign_nationals] Foreign governments may lobby and run public-relations campaigns if their agents register under the Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA), but the Justice Department's inspector general found in 2016 that 62 per cent of new registrants had filed late and called compliance "unacceptable". [cite:ow2_pogo_oig2016] Israel's government spends in this registered space: its 2026 budget set aside about $730 million for public diplomacy, more than four times the 2025 figure, and the Quincy Institute estimated more than $100 million in FARA-registered spending since October 2023. [cite:pm_jta_730m] [cite:pm_quincy_eighth] Enforcement can bite: former Senator Robert Menendez was sentenced in January 2025 to 11 years in prison for secretly working on behalf of the Egyptian and Qatari governments in exchange for bribes. [cite:us3a_fh_us2026] Israel regulates the opposite flow, foreign-government money reaching its own civil society, through disclosure laws and a proposed 80 per cent tax; the next section covers those. [scene:us3_speech|Speech, press, protest and civil society]

The safeguards around the American money changed in the same year. Freedom House reported that the Justice Department's public corruption unit was cut from 36 career lawyers to two and that 18 inspectors general were removed, and it lowered the American anticorruption score from 3 to 2 out of 4. [cite:us3_fh_us2026] The comparison is lopsided in the evidence as well as in the systems: the American record is detailed and public, through FEC filings and lobbying disclosures, while this book's Israeli record rests on summaries. Both are stated as of October 2026.
