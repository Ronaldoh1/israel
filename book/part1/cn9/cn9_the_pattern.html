The Pattern Across Eighteen Centuries

Set side by side, the episodes in this book are not a random set of scandals. The same mechanism appears in each, with different actors and different stakes. Naming it is the main thing this book has to offer, and it can be stated in six steps.

First, someone claims direct access: a teacher with other books, a translator who wants the laity to read, a friar who denies that money can move a soul. Second, an office fixes the list and makes itself the list's authorised reader, as Irenaeus did when he joined four gospels to a line of bishops. [cite:cn_iren_3_3] Third, the state, or the office acting as a state, enforces the boundary: Constantine's edict against the dissenting congregations, the law of 380, the council of Toulouse, the Constitutions of Oxford, the Index. [cite:vt21_vc3_65] [cite:vt21_wiki_thessalonica] [cite:vt28_wiki_toulouse] [cite:cn_wiki_oxford] Fourth, property and revenue move to the office: meeting houses handed to the catholic church, heretics' lands offered to crusaders, the first fruits of benefices, the proceeds of indulgences. [cite:vt21_vc3_65] [cite:vt28_fordham_lat4c3] [cite:vt31_wiki_annates] [cite:cn_brit_albert] Fifth, access is narrowed for everyone outside the office: the Bible kept in Latin, translations destroyed, translators burned. Sixth, much later, the mechanism is exposed, usually by an outsider with interests of his own, and the institution absorbs the exposure: a forger's critic hired as a papal secretary, indulgences cut off from money in 1567, the Index demoted to moral guidance in 1966. [cite:cn_wiki_valla] [cite:cn_nwe_indulgence] [cite:cn_va_index1966]

The pattern also shows where the mechanism failed, and the failures say what it was built to prevent. It failed when copying became cheap. A manuscript could be found and burned; Tyndale's printed New Testaments arrived faster than they could be seized, and his English became the English of the King James Bible. [cite:cn_brit_tyndale] It failed when a rival power had reasons of its own to protect the critic, as Alfonso of Aragon protected Valla. [cite:cn_wiki_valla] It failed when the excluded texts survived outside the institution's reach: in a jar in Upper Egypt, in the canon of an Ethiopian church Rome never ruled, in codices that surfaced in the antiquities trade. [cite:vt25_wiki_nhl] [cite:vt14_wiki_tewahedo] [cite:vt26_wiki_berlincodex] [cite:vt26_wiki_askew] And it failed, finally, when the institution's own revenue system provoked a revolt, as the St Peter's indulgence did in 1517. Each failure point is a point where the monopoly on copying, reading or paying was broken.

Fairness requires two qualifications, and they are part of the finding, not footnotes to it. The first is that the mechanism did not belong to one side. The groups the institution suppressed built offices of their own when they could: the Valentinians claimed a succession of teachers, the Manichaeans had an elect, the Cathars had bishops and lifelong vows, and the Protestant canon was fixed in the end by a Bible society's budget. [cite:vt24_ect_flora] [cite:vt24_wiki_manich] [cite:vt31_wiki_stfelix] [cite:vt14_wiki_protbible] Power over a list tends to accumulate wherever a list exists. The second is that the men who closed the canon were not all the men who profited from it. Irenaeus wrote without any power but persuasion; Athanasius lived as a fugitive for the faith he defended; Jerome doubted books his patrons kept. [cite:vt23_na_irenaeus_3_11] [cite:cn_brit_athanasius] [cite:vt23_ccel_jerome_solomon] The finding is not that the canon was a fraud. It is that the authority to define the canon was, from its first statement, an authority over people, and that this authority attracted land, money, families and force in every century in which it was held.
