How a Government Is Made: Coalitions, a Ceremonial President and the 2026 Election

Israelis do not vote for a prime minister. They vote for a party list, and the 120 members of the Knesset are elected from a single national constituency by proportional representation; any list that clears a threshold of 3.25 percent of the vote wins seats in proportion to its share. [cite:knesset_idi] No party has ever won a majority of 61 seats on its own. Every Israeli government has therefore been a coalition, and the smaller the margin, the more a small party can demand. [cite:knesset_idi] [cite:gov_goodauthority] That arithmetic is the engine of Israeli politics, and in November 2026 it is running in the open.

The formation process. After an election the President, whom the Knesset elects for a single seven-year term and whose role is largely ceremonial, consults the parties and gives the member most likely to command a majority the task of forming a government. That member has 28 days, which the President may extend by 14. If the attempt fails, the President may give another member 28 days, or a majority of the Knesset may nominate someone for 21 days; if all attempts fail, the country votes again. [cite:pm_brookings] [cite:president_time] Between April 2019 and November 2022 Israelis voted in five national elections because no attempt succeeded for long. [cite:gov_wiki]

Small parties, large powers. The coalition formed after the 2022 election, Israel's thirty-seventh government, showed how far the arithmetic can go. Itamar Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit, with six seats, received the National Security Ministry, with authority over the police; Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionist Party, with seven, received control of civilian affairs in the West Bank. [cite:npr_netanyahu_far_right_allies] [cite:smotrich_planning_authority] Two parties with thirteen of the 120 seats thus held two of the levers most important to the decisions examined in the next sections. In January 2025 Ben-Gvir resigned over a Gaza ceasefire and returned in March, hours after Netanyahu announced that strikes on Gaza would resume. [cite:bgv_aljaz_return]

A prime minister on trial. Benjamin Netanyahu was indicted in December 2019 on charges of bribery, fraud and breach of trust in three cases, the first sitting Israeli prime minister to face criminal charges; his trial began in 2020 and he denies wrongdoing. [cite:pm_indictment_toi] [cite:dv5_toi_pardon] Israeli law, as interpreted by the courts, requires ordinary ministers to step down on indictment but requires a prime minister to resign only after conviction for a serious offence and the exhaustion of appeals. [cite:pm_indictment_toi] In November 2025 Netanyahu formally asked President Isaac Herzog for a pardon without admitting guilt. On 26 April 2026 Herzog declined to rule and instead pressed for talks on a plea agreement between Netanyahu's lawyer and the Attorney General; a Justice Ministry paper had said a pardon would be highly problematic while the trial was under way. President Trump publicly pressed Herzog to grant it. [cite:dv5_toi_pardon]

The election of 27 October 2026. The vote left neither camp with a majority. On the official results published on 4 November, Likud won 27 seats; Gadi Eisenkot's new party, Yashar, 21; B'Yachad 12; the Joint List 11; the Democrats 8; Ra'am 8; Yisrael Beiteinu 7; Otzma Yehudit 7; United Torah Judaism 7; Shas 6; and Religious Zionism–Zehut 6. [cite:dv5_israeled_watch] The bloc aligned with Netanyahu held 53 seats, the bloc led by Eisenkot 48, and Arab-led parties a record 19. [cite:dv5_israeled_watch] [cite:dv5_crs_zanotti_nov2026] On 8 November, after consultations in which party leaders representing 67 members recommended him, President Herzog gave Eisenkot the first chance to form a government; his deadline, with the extension, is 20 December 2026. [cite:dv5_israeled_watch] [cite:dv5_pbs_mandate] Ra'am has said it will support him; the Joint List has offered to back him from outside a coalition; Yisrael Beiteinu has said it will not sit in a coalition with Arab-led parties. [cite:dv5_pbs_mandate] Until a government is formed, Netanyahu and his cabinet remain in office as caretakers with limited powers. If Eisenkot fails, Netanyahu may get his turn; if he fails too, Israel votes again in the spring. [cite:dv5_pbs_mandate]

What the machinery shows. The options before Eisenkot, as one analysis set them out, show the system's logic plainly: a national unity government with Likud, blocked by disagreement over Netanyahu's role; a coalition with the ultra-Orthodox parties, requiring a deal on military service; or a minority government kept alive by Arab parties voting with it from outside. [cite:dv5_israeled_watch] For the first time, parties representing Palestinian citizens of Israel may decide who governs, and the party most opposed to that outcome sits in the same opposition bloc that would need their votes. As of 11 November 2026 the outcome is open. Whatever it is, the constitutional order described in the previous section means that a coalition of 61 can change the state's fundamental laws, and the composition of that coalition, not any fixed text, decides how far they will be changed.
