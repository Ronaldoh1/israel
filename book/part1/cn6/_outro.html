By 1536 the clergy's hold on the scriptures had a second layer beneath the canon itself: control of the language. A council at Toulouse in 1229 forbade lay people to possess the Old and New Testaments, except books of prayer, and ordered translations destroyed, at the end of a war in which heretics' lands and crusade indulgences paid the soldiers. [cite:vt28_wiki_toulouse] [cite:vt28_fordham_lat4c3] In England the Constitutions of Oxford required permission for any new translation, Constance condemned Wycliffe, his body was burned in 1428, and Tyndale was strangled and burned on 6 October 1536.
