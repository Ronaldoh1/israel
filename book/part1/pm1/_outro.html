Part One fixed the terms. Pro-Israel money, in this volume, is money that moves through American organizations whose stated purpose is to support Israel or the US–Israel relationship; it is American money, because foreign money is barred from American elections, except in the one channel, foreign-agent registration, where the Israeli government's own contracts are disclosed. [cite:fec_foreign_nationals] [cite:dc_parscale_fara] It moves through six channels with very different limits: capped contributions, uncapped earmarking, unlimited super PAC spending, lobbying, charity-paid travel and registered foreign agents. [cite:pm_fec_limits] [cite:pm_sludge_jeffries] [cite:pm_wiki_superpac]

What is settled is the legal structure and the long record: about $108.6 million in pro-Israel contributions from 1990 to 2012, mostly from individuals, and no candidate money from AIPAC itself until 2021. [cite:pm_jvl_pacs] [cite:opensecrets_aipac_2019] What is contested is how to count, since trackers' totals for the same cycle differ by a factor of two, and what the money does, since the records show money and votes moving together but not which drives which.
