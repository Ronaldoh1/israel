How the Story Is Taught

Before turning to the record, it is worth taking inventory of what most readers already carry. For most Americans the story of Israel arrives in a specific sequence: textbook, teacher, museum, film, news. Each pass reinforces the last. The familiar account holds that this sequence delivers the true story of an ancient people returning home. What it actually delivers is one framing of a documented modern political project. The framing and the project are distinct things, and this book is concerned with the second.

The sequence begins with the textbook. Four publishers, Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, Pearson, McGraw-Hill and Holt McDougal, reach the vast majority of American world-history students in grades 6 through 12. Their coverage of Israel and Palestine follows a pattern that has been studied in peer-reviewed research, including Sean Yom's 2013 study of American world-history textbooks and Nurit Peled-Elhanan's 2012 study of Israeli textbooks, its mirror image.[cite:peled_elhanan_textbooks] The pattern is not one of falsehood. It is one of emphasis. Certain events are named: the Holocaust, the 1948 declaration, the 1967 war. Others are compressed: the Nakba, the 1953 CIA coup in Iran, the 1982 Sabra and Shatila massacre. Certain terms are used, such as "conflict," "war" and "unrest." Others are avoided, among them "occupation" and "apartheid," the latter the subject of published findings by B'Tselem, Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International since 2021.[cite:btselem_apartheid_2021] [cite:hrw_israel_apartheid] [cite:amnesty_israel_apartheid]

A concrete case shows what emphasis can remove. A textbook may say that Jewish settlers purchased land in Palestine, which is true. It need not mention that the Ottoman government passed a Palestine-specific law in 1881–82 barring land purchases by foreign Jews, that it required imperial approval even for Ottoman Jewish citizens from 1892, that purchasers worked around these laws through Ottoman-citizen intermediaries and consular protections, or that in the 1890s the U.S. State Department became involved in a dispute over Russian Jewish emigrants who had naturalized as Americans in order to claim consular protection for such purchases. Both versions cover the topic. Only one describes the mechanism. A reader who knows only that "settlers bought land" has no reason to ask the next question. Whether such omissions are deliberate or simply the product of survey-course compression, the result is the same: not a false belief, but the absence of the facts that would allow a reader to look further.

Emphasis also shapes who counts as the subject. Jews are not a single lineage. The documented traditions include Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Mizrahi, Beta Israel, Bene Israel, Kaifeng, Bukharan, Georgian, Yemenite and Karaite communities, and no one disputes that all are Jewish. American curricula typically compress this variety into a single narrative centered on Ashkenazi experience, an asymmetry Ella Shohat documented from inside Israel in a 1988 essay.[cite:shohat_sephardim_israel] The compression matters. Political Zionism in 1897 emerged in specifically European Jewish settings, and when non-European Jewish traditions vanish from the classroom, it becomes easier to treat "Zionism" and "all Jews" as the same thing, a conflation the record does not support.

The story also travels through the press, and ownership of the press is a matter of record. Robert Maxwell, born Jan Ludvik Hoch in Czechoslovakia in 1923, built a British media empire that by the 1980s included Mirror Group Newspapers, Macmillan, Berlitz and Pergamon Press; he acquired Macmillan in 1988 for $2.6 billion. Seymour Hersh's The Samson Option, Tom Bower's biography and a later biography by Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon describe ties between Maxwell and Israeli intelligence, though the specifics are contested.[cite:hersh_samson] [cite:bower_maxwell] He died at sea on 5 November 1991, near his yacht off the Canary Islands, and was buried on the Mount of Olives with state honors. Spanish and Israeli examinations concluded he fell overboard; the full sequence of events was never settled. The record establishes ownership. It does not show that any particular story about Israel was directed by an intelligence service, and this book makes no such claim.

Ownership matters more because the textbook system has no independent check. Federal law, 20 U.S.C. § 1232a, bars the Department of Education from any "direction, supervision, or control" over curriculum.[cite:usc_20_1232a] Approval falls to the states, and Virginia's own review documentation places responsibility for accuracy on publishers, who certify themselves.[cite:va_textbook_review] In 2015, after a widely publicized error, the Texas board voted down a proposal to add independent university historians to check textbooks for accuracy.[cite:tx_board_rejects_factcheck] No one in the chain checks the checker, and ownership of a publisher is not screened for anything bearing on accuracy. This book has found no source showing that Maxwell altered Macmillan's textbook content, and does not assert it. The point is structural: the opportunity existed. Errors that enter such a system can also compound, through what Randall Munroe named "citogenesis," in which an unverified claim is cited, then cited again, until repetition looks like independent confirmation.[cite:cornell_citogenesis_blog] [cite:xkcd_978_citogenesis]

Alongside this undirected emphasis stands an organized effort. Hasbara, Hebrew for "explanation," was formalized as Zionist policy before the Second World War, a step credited to Nahum Sokolow, and remains the Israeli state's own name for its public diplomacy. Israel quadrupled its 2026 public-diplomacy budget to $730 million, its foreign minister calling the matter an "existential issue." After October 2023 Israel's own National Security Studies institute, INSS, mapped more than 120 civilian "war rooms," 40 organizations building hasbara technology and 100 databases. One scheme reportedly offered roughly $7,000 per pro-Israel social-media post.[cite:nuclearnews_730m_budget] Frank Luntz's 2009 "Global Language Dictionary," commissioned by The Israel Project, is traced by several sources as the origin of many talking points still in circulation.[cite:medium_luntz_kryptonite_hasbara] None of this means that every pro-Israel statement originates there.

For some Jewish students the framing is reinforced in two further stages. Philanthropic funders, the Jim Joseph Foundation among them, support Israel-centered content in Jewish day schools. Later comes Birthright Israel, founded in 1999, a free ten-day trip for those aged 18 to 26, roughly 27 percent funded by the Israeli government, whose first funding guarantee Benjamin Netanyahu made in 1998. More than 800,000 young adults from some 68 to 70 countries have taken it. A 2025 Brandeis University study, funded by Birthright itself, found participants "much more connected to Israel, and much more likely to identify with the political right."[cite:intercept_birthright_2025] [cite:birthright_global_movement] [cite:wiki_birthright_israel]

Repeated exposure to a consistent framing installs a reflex rather than a conclusion: associations between Israel and democracy, Palestinians and terrorism, criticism and antisemitism, the conflict and antiquity. The rest of this book sets those associations beside the record, which rests on archives, court filings, primary documents and named reporting, including court proceedings and federal election data.[cite:icj_south_africa] [cite:fec_gov] Where a claim cannot be sourced, it is not made. Where it is contested, both sides are named.
