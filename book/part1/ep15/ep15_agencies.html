Six Investigations That Never Met

The public story has one investigation that failed, in Florida, and one that succeeded too late, in New York. The released records show at least six. The FBI opened a case in 2006 that was still active in 2015. Immigration and Customs Enforcement opened three: in West Palm Beach (2006 to 2008), in Las Vegas (opened 2009 and listed as pending in January 2010), and in Paris, under the name Operation Angel Watch, opened in June 2013 and closed months later. The Drug Enforcement Administration opened a case in New York on 17 December 2010; its 69-page memo of 2015 names Epstein and fourteen others and describes about $50 million in suspicious wire transfers between 2010 and 2015, "tied to illicit drug and/or prostitution activities". [cite:ep15_cbs_dea_ice2026]

The prosecutors who finally arrested Epstein in 2019 were, according to CBS's sources, not aware of the DEA investigation. [cite:ep15_cbs_dea_ice2026] Nothing released shows these cases being brought together, and nothing shows why the DEA case, listed as "judicial pending" in 2015, produced no charges. Senator Ron Wyden put that question to the DEA in February 2026, asking when the operation ended, whether anyone recommended charges, and why no drug or money-laundering charges were filed; the agency referred his staff to the Deputy Attorney General's office, and the drug task-force programme that had run the case was itself shut down in 2025. [cite:ep15_sfc_wydendea2026] [cite:ep15_sfc_wydenblanche2026] No answer has been made public.

The failure is sharper than it first looks, because a mechanism for joining these cases already existed. The DEA ran its case through the Organized Crime Drug Enforcement Task Forces, a programme created to pool agencies in "concentrated, coordinated, long-term" investigations. Its members include the FBI, which had its own Epstein case open at the same time; Immigration and Customs Enforcement, which opened three; and the IRS's criminal investigators, whose job is following wire transfers. [cite:ep15_doj_ocdetf] [cite:ep15_cbs_dea_ice2026] Nothing released shows which agencies joined Chain Reaction, or whether the FBI was told of it. The CIA was never part of this picture: by law it has "no police, subpoena, or law enforcement powers or internal security functions" at home. [cite:ep15_justia_3036] The agencies whose job it was to connect these investigations were members of the same programme, and the record shows them running side by side for years without meeting.

The blind spot is structural. The Epstein Files Transparency Act covers the Justice Department, which includes the FBI and the DEA. It does not cover the Department of Homeland Security, of which ICE is part, or Treasury, the State Department, the CIA or the NSA. [cite:ep15_cbs_missing2026] The three ICE investigations, and whatever Treasury's financial-crimes unit received from the banks, sit outside the law that forced the release. Senator Wyden has said the Deputy Attorney General stopped the DEA from handing over its unredacted memo; the department has not answered that account. [cite:ep7_wyden_blanche2026] The document that would close this blind spot is a cross-agency index: every open case on Epstein, by agency and date, and how each one ended.
