This volume follows one strip of land, about 360 square kilometres, through a century of the architecture this library documents. It asks what happened in Gaza, to whom, by whose decision and at whose cost, and it answers from the record: censuses and armistice texts, UN resolutions and agency reports, court orders and findings, the work of historians and the testimony of the people who lived it.

It begins before the State of Israel, with a town on the old coast road between Egypt and Syria, and follows it through the war of 1948, which made the strip and filled it with refugees; nineteen years of Egyptian rule; the occupation from 1967; the uprisings, the Oslo years, the settlements and the withdrawal of 2005; the blockade from 2007 and the wars that followed it; the attack of 7 October 2023; and the war since, its dead, the famine findings and the cases in the international courts. Four parts then step back: one follows the money, from the economy of occupation to aid and reconstruction; one asks who decides Gaza's fate; one sets out the plans American and Israeli leaders have made for its future, and who is making them; and one asks how all of this reached, or did not reach, American audiences. The last part sets out what is settled, what is contested and what is still open.

The volume holds to the standard of the library. Every load-bearing claim carries its source. Where sources disagree, especially on the number of the dead, the figures are set side by side with their method and their date rather than reduced to the largest or the smallest. It describes the acts of governments, armies and armed groups, not the character of peoples, and it treats the suffering on every side as a fact to be recorded, not a point to be scored.
