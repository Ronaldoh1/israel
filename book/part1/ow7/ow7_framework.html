The Framework: Planning Groups, a Stockpile and a Radar

The military relationship between the United States and Israel was not created by a single treaty. There is no mutual-defence pact between the two countries. What exists instead is a stack of executive agreements, statutes and command arrangements, laid down over four decades, most of them signed by defence officials rather than ratified by the Senate. Part One sets out the laws; this section follows how the Pentagon built on them.

The 1980s agreements. On 30 November 1981 the Secretary of Defense, Caspar Weinberger, and Israel's defence minister, Ariel Sharon, signed a memorandum on strategic cooperation that provided for joint exercises, the use of Israeli ports by the US Sixth Fleet and the pre-positioning of American supplies in Israel. As an executive memorandum it was not submitted to the Senate. [cite:ow_wiki_sca1981] On 29 November 1983 a further agreement created two standing bodies: the Joint Political-Military Group, first aimed at Soviet involvement in the region and later at chemical weapons and ballistic-missile proliferation, and the Joint Security Assistance Planning Group, which meets each year in Washington to review Israel's procurement needs and plan how its Foreign Military Financing will be spent. [cite:ow7_jvl_strategic] Congress designated Israel a major non-NATO ally in 1987, a status that opened some US defence contracts to Israeli firms on equal terms with NATO members, and in April 1988 a memorandum of agreement folded the earlier arrangements together. [cite:ow7_jvl_strategic] [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

From Europe to the Middle East command. For most of this period Israel sat inside US European Command, an arrangement that kept it administratively apart from the Arab states covered by Central Command. On 15 January 2021, in the last week of the first Trump administration, the Pentagon moved Israel into Central Command's area in the Unified Command Plan. The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump ordered the change; the Pentagon said that "the Abraham Accords paved the way". Israeli liaison officers now sit at CENTCOM's headquarters in Tampa. [cite:ow_inss_centcom] Oversight of the American stockpile in Israel moved with it, from European to Central Command, in September 2021. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa]

WRSA-I: the stockpile. The War Reserve Stockpile Allies–Israel began in the 1980s, when Israel invited the United States to store munitions on its territory for American use in war; a memorandum between the two governments, still classified, governs it. Congress later amended the stockpiling law so that such stocks could be held in major non-NATO allies, not only NATO states. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] The United States owns the stock until it is transferred; Israel pays for storage and upkeep; additions were capped at $200 million a year, and the Congressional Research Service has estimated the stock's value at up to $4.4 billion. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] The 2012 and 2014 Acts extended the authority, and a 2025 law set the cap on additions at $500 million a year through fiscal 2027. [cite:ow_jvl_2012act] [cite:ow_jvl_2014act] [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

The stockpile has been drawn on in several directions. During the 2006 Lebanon war the Pentagon transferred precision-guided munitions from it to Israel. During the Gaza war of 2014 it sold Israel 120mm tank rounds and 40mm illumination rounds from it in a $3 million sale paid for with US grant aid; the Pentagon called the transfer "routine", and the White House was reportedly not told in advance. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] In 2023 the flow ran the other way: the United States moved about 300,000 155mm artillery shells out of the Israeli stockpile to supply Ukraine, promising to replenish it, and by January 2023 about half had reportedly reached Ukraine. Reporting at the time indicated that, although the shells were American property, the final approval rested with Israel's prime minister. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] [cite:ow7_fdd_ukraine] After 7 October 2023 the Biden administration asked Congress to lift the cap on additions and to allow any item in the stockpile to be transferred to Israel at terms set by the Secretary of Defense; Congress did not pass that version. A separate provision of the 2021 defence act already allowed precision-guided munitions to be transferred outside the cap, and it was extended to 2027. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa]

The radar on Har Keren. The one permanent American military presence in Israel is a radar. In late 2008, at the end of the George W. Bush administration, the United States deployed an X-band radar to the Negev with about 120 American personnel, to detect long-range missiles such as Iran's Shahab-3 and link Israeli and American missile-defence systems. [cite:ow7_sat_xband] The site, on Mount Har Keren some 20 miles from Gaza and known as Site 512, is focused on Iran rather than on Gaza. A Pentagon contract announcement of 2 August 2023 described $35.8 million for a "life support facility", barracks for personnel, as part of a "classified worldwide" project. [cite:ow7_int_site512] Secondary accounts say the radar gives Israel several extra minutes of warning of a launch from Iran and feeds data to American missile-defence centres. [cite:ow7_jvl_site512]

What each side gains. The framework is not purely a channel of benefit to Israel. The stockpile is American property held for American use, and in 2023 it supplied Ukraine; the radar watches Iran for the United States as well as for Israel; the CENTCOM move placed Israel in the same command as its Arab neighbours, a step the Pentagon tied to the Abraham Accords. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] [cite:ow7_int_site512] [cite:ow_inss_centcom] The asymmetries are of a different kind: the WRSA-I agreement is classified, the Har Keren project is described only obliquely in public contracts, and the 1981 and 1988 instruments were never put to a vote. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] [cite:ow7_int_site512] [cite:ow_wiki_sca1981] The rule that keeps other regional arms sales from eroding Israel's "qualitative military edge", by contrast, was written into statute in 2008. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]
