The Pope's Bankers: Who Lent to Rome, and What Rome's Money Does Now

A common picture of the Vatican is of a treasury so large that it could finance states. The documented history of the papacy's money points the other way. For most of the nineteenth century the popes were borrowers, and their most important lenders were the Rothschild houses. In the twentieth century the Holy See's capital came largely from a settlement paid by the Italian state. Its bank has been the centre of two of Europe's best-known financial scandals and of a long reform. None of this makes the Holy See a financial parent of anything, but each part of it bears on claims this volume has to test, and the Rothschild part bears directly on the link between Rome and London.

The Rothschild loans, 1831–1850s. On 30 November 1831 the Holy See signed an agreement with the Rothschilds that gave Pope Gregory XVI a loan of £400,000 at 5 percent, paid in 1832. The pope's side was negotiated by the Roman banker Alessandro Torlonia; the Rothschild side by James de Rothschild of Paris, who became the official papal banker, and his brother Carl Mayer von Rothschild, who met the pope in January 1832. [cite:dv3_wiki_rothschild_loans] The contract carried a secret clause. The Holy See had to tell the Rothschilds before seeking a new loan elsewhere, and to prefer them if they offered equal terms; when Cardinal Tosti tried in 1834 to refinance the debt more cheaply, the Rothschilds opposed it. [cite:dv3_wiki_rothschild_loans] A second loan followed in the early 1850s, under Pius IX, after the Roman Republic fell and the Papal States were restored. [cite:dv3_wiki_rothschild_loans]

Two features of the loans are often left out. The first is that the lenders used their position on behalf of Jews living under the pope. Through the Austrian chancellor Metternich, the Rothschilds asked for concessions for the Jews of the Papal States: an end to the extra taxes they paid, to the rule that they could not take property out of the ghetto, to the bars on their professions, and to harsh rules of evidence against them in court. [cite:dv3_wiki_rothschild_loans] The second is the hostility the loans provoked. The poet Alfred de Vigny wrote that "a Jew now reigns over the Pope and Christianity." [cite:dv3_wiki_rothschild_loans] That line belongs to the history of a hostile trope, not to the history of the debt; the documented relationship was a loan contract with a preference clause, and the borrower kept its sovereignty. An earlier version of this research described the clause as an "exclusive right of first refusal on all Vatican sovereign debt." The contract's documented terms were narrower: notice before other borrowing, and preference on equal terms. [cite:dv3_wiki_rothschild_loans]

The Lateran capital, 1929. The next large transfer came from the Italian state. Under the financial convention of 1929, Italy paid the Holy See 750 million lire in cash and 1,000 million lire in 5 percent government bonds. [cite:dv3_wiki_lateran] Pius XI placed the money with a new Special Administration of the Holy See and appointed a lay financier, Bernardino Nogara, to run it; Nogara stayed until 1954, serving Pius XII through the Second World War. [cite:wiki_bernardino_nogara] A later section asks whether this money shaped the Holy See's wartime conduct; the honest answer is that no document connects the two.

The Vatican Bank. On 27 June 1942 Pius XII created the Institute for the Works of Religion, the IOR, absorbing an older body that went back to a commission set up by Leo XIII on 11 February 1887. [cite:dv3_wiki_ior] Despite its common name, it is not a central bank, not a department of the Curia, and not a private bank: it is a canonical foundation with no owners or shareholders. [cite:dv3_wiki_ior] Its modern troubles began after the Holy See lost its exemption from Italian tax on its investments in 1968 and hired the financier Michele Sindona as an adviser; Sindona's Franklin National Bank failed in 1974. [cite:dv3_wiki_ior] The larger scandal came in 1982, when Banco Ambrosiano collapsed. The IOR, which held 10 percent of it and was alleged to have issued letters of support for it under Archbishop Paul Marcinkus, denied legal liability but acknowledged "moral involvement" and paid US$224 million to creditors. Ambrosiano's chairman, Roberto Calvi, was found dead under Blackfriars Bridge in London. [cite:dv3_wiki_ior]

The reform that followed was slow and came mostly after 2010. Benedict XVI created a Financial Information Authority on 30 December 2010. In July 2012 the Council of Europe's Moneyval committee reviewed the Holy See for the first time and found it met 9 of 16 core standards. The IOR published its first annual report on 1 October 2013 and asked about 1,300 customers outside its permitted categories to close their accounts. Statutes issued by Francis in August 2019 set its present structure, and a rescript of 23 August 2022 required all of the Holy See's financial resources to be moved into it. [cite:dv3_wiki_ior]

A London building. The Holy See's largest recent financial loss ran through the city this volume calls the financial parent. The Secretariat of State invested in a building at 60 Sloane Avenue in London; it was sold in 2022 at an estimated loss of about €140 million, and prosecutors alleged a further €15 million extortion payment to regain control of it. [cite:dv3_cbc_becciu_2023] On 16 December 2023 the Vatican tribunal convicted Cardinal Angelo Becciu, formerly a senior official of the Secretariat of State, of embezzlement and sentenced him to five and a half years. [cite:dv3_npr_becciu_2023] The case has not ended. On 17 March 2026 the Court of Appeal found a "relative nullity" in the proceedings, arising from the prosecution's failure to give the defence its full investigative file, and ordered part of the proceedings renewed. At an eleven-minute hearing on 29 October 2026 it set four sessions for 26 to 29 January 2027, to decide whether the prosecution has now complied; the merits of the appeals will not be reached until after that. [cite:dv3_advaticanum_becciu] As of 11 November 2026, then, the convictions stand under appeal and the case remains open.

The bank in 2025. The IOR's report for 2025, released on 11 May 2026, showed net income of €51 million, up 55.5 percent and the highest in ten years, client assets of €5.9 billion, a core capital ratio of 71.9 percent, and a dividend of €24.3 million paid to the pope. [cite:dv3_vn_ior_2025] With the approval of those accounts, Jean-Baptiste de Franssu ended his term as president and François Pauly took office. [cite:dv3_vn_ior_2025] Pauly's appointment was proposed on 12 December 2025, approved by the commission of cardinals on 28 January 2026, announced on 25 March and effective after the board's meeting of 28 April. [cite:dv3_catholicherald_pauly] [cite:dv3_paperjam_pauly] He is a Luxembourg banker who ran Sal. Oppenheim's Luxembourg bank and then the Banque Internationale à Luxembourg, joined the board of Edmond de Rothschild in 2016, became chief executive of the Edmond de Rothschild group in June 2021 and left that post around 2023. [cite:dv3_citywire_pauly] [cite:dv3_paperjam_pauly] Some accounts give his tenure as 2019 to 2023; the trade press that reported his appointment dates it from June 2021. [cite:dv3_citywire_pauly]

What the money record shows, and what it does not. It shows a real thread between the papacy and the Rothschild name: a loan contract in 1831, a second loan in the 1850s, and, nearly two centuries later, a former chief executive of a bank bearing the name at the head of the Vatican Bank's board. Each link is documented. What the documents do not show is a single continuing relationship running through them. The 1830s loans were arranged by James de Rothschild in Paris and his brother Carl for a borrowing state; the Edmond de Rothschild group is a separate modern firm that bears the name of a later Paris Rothschild; and Pauly was hired as an individual banker, by a board on which he had sat as one of several lay members. [cite:dv3_wiki_rothschild_loans] [cite:dv3_citywire_pauly] [cite:dv3_wiki_ior] The record also shows the scale of the Holy See's finances: an operating budget of about €1.2 billion and a bank with about €6 billion of clients' money. [cite:dv3_america_budget_2024] [cite:dv3_vn_ior_2025] That is the size of a regional bank and a large charity, not of an institution able to underwrite a state, and no document in this research shows Rome's money flowing to the Zionist movement or to Israel. The money in Rome's history mostly flowed in, from lenders, from Italy and from the faithful.
