The Question

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

Every book of chance in this volume begins with a question. The person who consults the I Ching casts yarrow stalks or coins and reads the hexagram that answers. [cite:fa_ency_iching] The person who lays out the cards, or makes the marks of geomancy, does the same. [cite:vt30_wiki_taromancy] [cite:vt30_fordham_rule9] The Catechism's objection is that this conceals "a desire for power over time". [cite:vt30_va_ccc2116]

The inner reading takes the objection seriously and offers another way to see the practice. To consult an oracle well, a person must first put the question clearly, and putting a question clearly is already half of the answer. The hexagram, the card or the figure does not have to know the future to do its work; it gives the mind an image to hold its question against, and the person sees in it what they had not let themselves see. Read this way, the book of chance is a mirror for attention, not a window on fate.

The reading draws a line the Catechism also draws. Using an oracle to gain power over others, or to escape responsibility for a choice, is the use both reject. Using it to see one's own question more truly is the use the reading proposes.

The record limits the reading. The I Ching's own tradition treated it as a means of divination as well as reflection, and many who consult cards or stars believe they are reading the future. [cite:fa_ency_iching] The reading is the reader's.
