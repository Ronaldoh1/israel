The Abraham Accords produced normalization, not an end to any war. Between August and December 2020, Israel normalized relations with the UAE, Bahrain, Sudan and Morocco, none of which had fought it or shared a border with it. [cite:wiki_abraham_accords] Each deal carried a US reward: approval of a roughly $23 billion F-35 and drone sale for the UAE, security guarantees for Bahrain, recognition of Morocco's claim to Western Sahara, and Sudan's removal from the terrorism list. [cite:wiki_uae_israel_normalization] [cite:brit_abraham]

The gains are real: a free-trade agreement, over $3.2 billion in annual goods trade, flights that continued through the Gaza war, and, by Emirati account, a suspension of annexation plans in 2020. [cite:ac_accords5] [cite:cnn_redline] But Palestinians were sidelined in all four deals, and there has been no movement toward a Palestinian state. The deals were decided at the top, protests followed in Morocco and Bahrain, and no signatory has left. Supporters call that endurance proof; critics call it the problem.
