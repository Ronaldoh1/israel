As Above, So Below

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The Emerald Tablet's most famous line, in Newton's translation, reads "That which is below is like that which is above". [cite:de39_wiki_emerald] The popular paraphrase, "as above, so below", was popularised by Helena Blavatsky. [cite:de39_wiki_emerald] The Hermetic traditions use it as a rule of knowledge: the pattern of the heavens is repeated in the earth, the structure of the cosmos in the structure of the person, and whoever understands one can understand the other.

The inner reading takes the rule as the key to every reading on this shelf. The ascent of the Poimandres through the spheres above corresponds to a change within; the Kabbalists' ladder of emanation from God corresponds to the stages of the soul's return; the heavenly calendar of Jubilees corresponds to the rhythm of a life. [cite:hm_mead_poim] [cite:gn_brit_sefira] [cite:vt27_je_jubilees] Read through correspondence, outer and inner are two views of one order.

This book's history adds a warning to the rule. The same habit of seeing correspondences everywhere led Ficino to place Hermes before Plato on the strength of a resemblance that Casaubon later showed to be an illusion of dating. [cite:hm_sep_ficino] [cite:hm_enc_hermeticism] [scene:cn10_the_gate_within|The closing part of The Canon] made the point plainly: correspondence finds patterns wherever it looks, which is exactly why it cannot be evidence. The rule is a way of reading, and its value lies in what it shows a reader about their own life, not in what it claims to prove about the world.

The record limits the reading. The Emerald Tablet's line was read for centuries as a key to alchemical operations, the transformation of matter, and its meaning in its own Arabic setting is a question for specialists. [cite:de39_wiki_emerald] The inner reading is one use among many.
