Seas, Zones and Network States

Book Three followed an attempt to change a state from the inside. Book Four follows the older and, in the texts, more central idea: leaving. Exit, the word the economist Albert Hirschman used for walking away from a failing organisation rather than complaining inside it, runs through every core text of the Dark Enlightenment. In Curtis Yarvin's Patchwork series of 2008, small states owned by corporations would compete for residents, whose one remedy was this: "If residents don't like their government, they can and should move." [cite:de2_le] The lineage of that idea, and Peter Thiel's 2009 case for an "escape from politics" through cyberspace, outer space and the sea, are set out in [scene:de3_other_lineages|Part Three] and [scene:de2_core_texts|Part Two]. This section follows what happened when people tried to build it: first on the ocean, then in a Central American country's special zones, then through venture funds and online communities that buy or borrow land. It names who paid for each project, from the documents that show it, and what became of each.

The sea: a memorandum that lapsed

The Seasteading Institute was founded on 15 April 2008 by Patri Friedman, a former Google engineer and grandson of the economist Milton Friedman, and the software engineer Wayne Gramlich, with $500,000 in seed money from Thiel. [cite:de3_wiki_tsi] [cite:de22_bbg_pronomos] [cite:de22_chequeado] Thiel's total giving was put at $1.25 million in a 2011 report and at $1.7 million in a 2018 one; the first is an itemised total as of 2011, the second gives no source of its own, and the institute's public returns do not name donors, so the two cannot be reconciled from filings. [cite:de3_yahoo_tsi] [cite:de3_bi_tsi] [cite:de14_bi_seastead] Thiel left the board in 2011 and later called seasteads "not quite feasible." [cite:de3_bi_tsi]

The institute's one concrete step toward a floating community came a decade after its founding. On 13 January 2017 it signed a memorandum of understanding with a minister of French Polynesia for a platform in a sheltered lagoon. [cite:de3_wiki_tsi] It did not survive local politics. An opposition politician, Valentina Cross, asked the government to withdraw, and residents objected; in February 2018 the governing party, Tapura Huiraatira, said that "nothing had been signed," that the agreement "was not a legal document," and that it had expired at the end of 2017. [cite:de22_rnz_fp] The Floating Island Project ended there. No seastead of the kind the institute was founded to build has been built. [cite:de3_bi_tsi]

Land: Romer's charter cities and Honduras's zones

The move from sea to land had a separate intellectual source, older than the projects that later borrowed from it and unconnected to the Dark Enlightenment texts. In 2009 the economist Paul Romer began presenting the idea of "charter cities": new cities on empty land, governed under a charter written with outside help, which poor countries could use to import good rules quickly. [cite:de22_od_romer] Honduras took up a version of it. In January 2011 its Congress voted 124 to 1 for a constitutional amendment allowing "special development regions," known by their Spanish initials as REDs. [cite:de22_fee_bell] Romer agreed to chair a transparency commission that would oversee them, but in September 2012 the government signed the first RED contract with foreign investors without telling him; he learned of it from a newspaper and resigned. [cite:de22_od_romer] On 18 October 2012 the Supreme Court declared the REDs unconstitutional as a violation of national sovereignty and territory. [cite:de22_fee_bell]

Congress tried again. Its president, Juan Orlando Hernández, announced a new framework, the Zones for Employment and Economic Development, or ZEDEs; the law passed on 12 June 2013 by 102 votes to 26. [cite:de22_od_romer] [cite:de22_cc_ruling] The main difference from the REDs was the land. A RED would have been granted by the state; a ZEDE would be built on land bought from its owners, although the law still allowed expropriation. [cite:de22_od_romer] Three ZEDEs were eventually established; the best known, Próspera on the island of Roatán, is the subject of [scene:de22_prospera_v_honduras|the next section]. Romer had left the Honduran effort the year before. [cite:de22_od_romer]

The fund: Pronomos Capital

The money that linked the seasteading circle to the Honduran zones went through a venture fund. In August 2019 Patri Friedman incorporated Pronomos Capital to invest in semi-autonomous cities on land in developing countries. Bloomberg Businessweek reported in December 2019 that Thiel was its anchor investor, supplying more than half of about $9 million raised, and named Marc Andreessen, the bitcoin investor Roger Ver and Balaji Srinivasan as other seed backers; Erick Brimen, who would run Próspera, was then working informally with Friedman. [cite:de22_bbg_pronomos] [cite:de22_mr_pronomos] Friedman himself has dated the fund a year earlier: speaking on a podcast, he said that in 2018, when Próspera was about to be approved, he created Pronomos "with financing from Peter Thiel." [cite:de22_chequeado] By December 2023 the fund had raised at least $13.3 million, with Thiel and Andreessen among its named investors, and its projects included a planned city in the Mediterranean, a zone on an island in Honduras and an online business hub in Nigeria. [cite:de22_db_pronomos] Michael Gibson, formerly vice-president of grants at the Thiel Foundation, advises it. [cite:de22_giz_praxis] Pronomos's limited partners and stakes have not been published in full, and the named list should be read as what reporters were told.

The online community: Praxis

Praxis began as an online community of would-be founders that, in 2022, described its future city as a "crypto-state"; by 2026 it claimed some 150,000 members. [cite:de22_perfil_bastitta] Pronomos is among its backers, alongside the crypto investment firm Paradigm and the trading firms Alameda Research and Three Arrows Capital. [cite:de22_giz_praxis] [cite:de22_block_praxis] On 15 October 2024 it announced $525 million, which it described as "primarily consisting of a drawdown facility" from lenders including Arch Lending and GEM Digital: a commitment that can be drawn on, by its own account, not cash in hand. [cite:de22_block_praxis]

The search for land became public in Greenland. In November 2024 Praxis's chief executive, Dryden Brown, posted: "I went to Greenland to try to buy it." When President-elect Trump wrote of acquiring the island, the Praxis account reposted him with the words "According to plan." [cite:de22_giz_praxis] Reuters later reported, from unnamed sources, a push for a "freedom city" in Greenland that two of them said Thiel and Andreessen supported; Thiel's spokesperson said that "Peter isn't involved in any plans or discussions regarding Greenland." [cite:de13_newsweek_greenland] The obstacle was law: Greenland's independence requires the approval of its own parliament, a referendum and the Danish parliament, and a Danish MP, Rasmus Jarlov, said there was "no way" Denmark would approve independence so that Greenland could be bought. [cite:de22_giz_praxis] [cite:de22_xataka_praxis] In 2026 Praxis turned to Uruguay, signing a memorandum with +Colonia, a property development near Colonia del Sacramento, which both sides described as mostly non-binding. [cite:de22_xataka_praxis] [cite:de22_perfil_bastitta] Perfil reported that former team members allege an "authoritarian" vision of governance and racist opinions inside the project; these are allegations by unnamed former staff, not findings. [cite:de22_perfil_bastitta]

The network state

Srinivasan's 2022 book The Network State, discussed in [scene:de16_the_valley|Part Sixteen], gave this cluster a theory: an online community with a shared purpose and a founder that crowdfunds territory and eventually seeks recognition. [cite:de16_maverick] His own pilot was a school. Announced in August 2024, the Network School opened on 23 September 2024 at Forest City, a development in the Malaysian state of Johor, with about 128 participants, and launched a permanent programme in May 2025. [cite:de22_newswav_ns] In July 2026, after unverified social-media claims about who had enrolled, the Johor government ordered an inquiry and on 17 July issued a cessation-of-business notice, citing premises operating without a valid local licence and office space used as classrooms without approval. Srinivasan said more than RM100 million had been invested "without government funding" and put a planned RM500 million expansion on hold. [cite:de22_newswav_ns] On 22 July he announced a memorandum of understanding with Kazakhstan, signed for the government by Deputy Prime Minister Zhaslan Madiyev, for a new campus he called "a haven for the global community optimistic about technology." [cite:de22_vibes_ns] Who funds the school, beyond his own statement, is not documented.

What the record shows

Three things follow from these projects taken together. First, the money is real and traceable along a short line: Thiel funded the seasteading institute directly and anchored the fund that invested in both Próspera and Praxis, so his money reaches the zones one step removed; no document shows him investing in either directly, and he denies involvement in the Greenland plans. [cite:de22_bbg_pronomos] [cite:de13_newsweek_greenland] Second, most projects stalled or moved when a government said no: French Polynesia let its memorandum lapse, Denmark's law closed Greenland, Johor shut the school's premises. [cite:de22_rnz_fp] [cite:de22_giz_praxis] [cite:de22_newswav_ns] Third, the idea that went furthest on land came from Romer, an economist outside this circle, and was turned into law by Honduran politicians; it was then taken up by the fund the circle financed. That is borrowing, not authorship. The texts' Patchwork imagined sovereign corporations; what was built was a zone inside a country's legal order, and the following section shows what happened when that country changed its mind.
