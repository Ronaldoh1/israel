The Support Front

8 October 2023. The morning after Hamas's attack on southern Israel, Hezbollah fired guided rockets and artillery at three Israeli army posts in the Shebaa Farms, the strip of land at the foot of Mount Hermon that Lebanon claims and Israel holds. Its statement called them "3 Zionist occupation sites" and said the attack was carried out "in solidarity with the victorious Palestinian resistance and the steadfast Palestinian people." The Israeli army answered with artillery at the launch area and a drone strike on a Hezbollah post, and said it was "prepared for all scenarios." [cite:hz7_aj_8oct] The public record reviewed for this volume does not show Hezbollah taking part in planning the 7 October attack; its own framing, from the first day, was that it was opening a second front to support Gaza. That is why the period that followed is called the "support front": Hezbollah's stated aim was to tie down Israeli forces in the north, not to win a war of its own. Part Six described the organisation that made this choice. [scene:hz6_state|Hezbollah as its own actor]

The stated condition. Hezbollah attached a condition to the front and repeated it for a year: it would stop when the war in Gaza stopped. Its deputy leader, Naim Qassem, told the Associated Press in July 2024: "If there is a cease-fire in Gaza, we will stop without any discussion." [cite:hz7_ml_qassem] Hassan Nasrallah said the same on 19 September 2024, after the pager detonations described in the next section: "the fighting on the Lebanese front will not stop before the war in Gaza ends." [cite:hz7_axios_nasrallah] The condition matters for how the war ended. By late October 2024, the Wall Street Journal reported, Hezbollah was moving away from its demand for a Gaza ceasefire first, and the agreement of 27 November 2024 contained no Gaza clause at all. [cite:hz7_toi_freedom] [cite:hz7_toi_text] Israel's stated position was the reverse of Hezbollah's: that a hostile armed group on its border, firing in support of the group that had carried out 7 October, could not be allowed to set the terms. On 18 September 2024 Defense Minister Yoav Gallant said "The center of gravity is moving northward," and he, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and the army chief all named the return of displaced northern residents as the military's task. [cite:hz7_afp_gravity]

Who fired more. The exchange was not even. The Armed Conflict Location and Event Data project (ACLED), which counts reported incidents of political violence, recorded 8,099 Israeli strike events on Lebanon against 1,623 Hezbollah strike events on Israel between 7 October 2023 and 20 September 2024, about five to one, with 798 deaths in Lebanon linked to Israeli attacks and 28 in Israel linked to Hezbollah's. [cite:hz7_c4_acled] Over the first full year, to 18 October 2024, ACLED counted 11,238 Israeli attacks (about 83 per cent of the total) and 2,370 by Hezbollah and allied groups. [cite:hz7_aj_acled_year] Two cautions apply. ACLED counts events, not munitions: a barrage of dozens of rockets fired at one place at one time is a single event, which narrows the gap in projectiles fired. And the Israeli army disputed the comparison. It called the analysis "baseless," said more than 9,000 Hezbollah projectiles had crossed into Israel by October 2024 and later put the figure at 12,400 for the year, and argued that a casualty comparison ignored how many attacks Israel's air defences stopped. [cite:hz7_c4_acled] [cite:hz7_acled_year_pdf] Both numbers can be true at once: Hezbollah fired thousands of rockets, drones and missiles, and Israel struck Lebanon far more often and killed far more people. ACLED recorded Israeli strikes in 449 Lebanese localities in that first year. [cite:hz7_acled_year_pdf]

Displacement on both sides. On 8 October Israel began emptying its border communities, and about 60,000 residents of northern Israel left their homes, according to official data reported by AFP. They were not cleared to return until 1 March 2025, and about half had still not gone back a week later. [cite:hz7_afp_north_return] On the Lebanese side the International Organization for Migration counted 76,018 people displaced by early January 2024, mostly from the south, and 110,099 by mid-August 2024. [cite:hz7_an_iom_jan24] [cite:hz7_olj_iom_aug24] The Lebanese figure was already nearly twice the Israeli one by the summer of 2024; after September it grew more than tenfold, as the next sections show.

The journalists at Alma al-Shaab. One of the first deaths on the Lebanese side was a journalist. On 13 October 2023 a group of clearly marked reporters filming near the village of Alma al-Shaab was hit by two Israeli tank rounds fired 37 seconds apart, according to a Reuters investigation published that December. The first killed Reuters videographer Issam Abdallah, 37, and badly wounded AFP photographer Christina Assi, who lost a leg; at least five others were wounded. AFP's own investigation, and separate reports by Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch, reached conclusions consistent with Reuters': that Israeli forces knew or should have known the group were civilians. The Israeli army said it was looking into the incident, and its officials repeated that they do not target journalists. [cite:hz7_npr_abdallah] Two Al Mayadeen journalists were killed in a separate Israeli strike on 21 November 2023. [cite:hz7_npr_abdallah]

Majdal Shams. The deadliest single attack on the Israeli-held side came on 27 July 2024. A rocket struck a football pitch in Majdal Shams, a Druze town in the Golan Heights that Israel captured from Syria in 1967 and still holds. Twelve children and teenagers, aged about ten to sixteen, were killed and at least 42 people were wounded. According to the town's council, none of the twelve held Israeli citizenship; most residents of Majdal Shams identify as Syrian. [cite:hz7_wiki_majdal] Israel attributed the strike to Hezbollah. Its military spokesman, Daniel Hagari, said: "Our intelligence is clear. Hezbollah is responsible for the killing of innocent children," and identified the weapon as an Iranian-made Falaq-1 rocket. [cite:hz7_aj_majdal] Secretary of State Antony Blinken said "every indication" was that the rocket came from Hezbollah; Human Rights Watch said the damage was consistent with a Falaq-type rocket, possibly one that overshot an Israeli army position on Mount Hermon. [cite:hz7_wiki_majdal] Hezbollah denied it: "The Islamic Resistance has no connection to this incident." It had claimed several other rocket attacks that day, which it described as retaliation for Israeli strikes at Kfar Kila. [cite:hz7_aj_majdal] The weight of independent assessment points to a Hezbollah rocket; whether the town was the intended target is not established in the public record, and no independent international investigation, which the EU's foreign policy chief called for, has reported. [cite:hz7_wiki_majdal]

Fuad Shukr. Three days later, on the evening of 30 July 2024, an Israeli strike hit a building in Haret Hreik in Beirut's southern suburbs. Israel said it had killed Fuad Shukr, a senior Hezbollah military commander, and held him responsible for Majdal Shams. Lebanon's health ministry said three people, two of them children, were killed in the strike and 74 wounded. Hezbollah confirmed Shukr's death the next day. Hours after the Beirut strike, Hamas's political leader Ismail Haniyeh was killed in Tehran; the UN Secretary-General called the two strikes a "dangerous escalation." [cite:hz7_aj_shukr] The pattern of this period was set: each side's attacks killed civilians; each named the other's attack as the cause; and Israel answered with strikes on commanders in the capital.
