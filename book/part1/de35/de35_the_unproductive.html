"The Unproductive"

Every political order has to decide what it owes people who cannot or do not support themselves. Of all the questions the Dark Enlightenment texts take up, they answer this one most harshly. American law also sorts people by productivity, through work requirements for benefits and the "public charge" test for immigrants. This section sets the two side by side: what the texts say, in their own words; what policies do now; and where the line between them runs. It closes the part by testing the worst-case reading against everything the part has found.

What the texts say

Yarvin's answer is the sharpest, and it is set out in full in [scene:de6_the_hard_posts|Part Six]. In the first Patchwork post of 13 November 2008, a profit-seeking sovereign corporation running San Francisco meets residents who cost more than they pay. Its first step is to "sell their slums out from under them, obviously," with demolition, "possibly a little aerial bombing," and new districts "suitable for Russian oligarchs"; the residents may emigrate if another realm will take them. [cite:de6_le_contra] [cite:de6_audit_le] A week later he raised turning them "into biodiesel," called that "just kidding," and then set out his goal: "a humane alternative to genocide," which was to hold them in "permanent solitary confinement" connected to a virtual-reality interface. [cite:de6_tnr_duran] The joke marker covers the biodiesel line; the confinement proposal is stated as the goal. In plain words, Yarvin proposed the indefinite confinement of poor people a sovereign corporation found unprofitable, and presented it as better than killing them. Defenders call the post satire; no later retraction by him is on record. [cite:de6_le_contra]

Land's treatment is different in kind. He concedes that social disadvantage "in large part – perhaps overwhelmingly large part – … reflects sheer misfortune," and that "dim, impulsive, unhealthy, and unattractive people, reared chaotically in abusive families," have "every reason to curse the gods before themselves." Then he dismisses the point: "In regards to effective incentive structures, however, none of this is of the slightest importance," because "whatever is subsidized is promoted." [cite:de35_land_ebook] Elsewhere he writes that "the war on poverty creates a chronically dysfunctional underclass." [cite:de35_land_ebook] The passage is contemptuous in its description and absolute in its rule, but it is an argument against subsidy, not a call to confine or compel anyone. Yarvin's 2009 Carlyle essay adds a claim about nature, that some people are by heredity "more suited to mastery than slavery." [cite:de6_mm_2021] Thiel's 2009 essay belongs in the record for one sentence: he wrote that the growth of welfare beneficiaries and the extension of the vote to women had made "capitalist democracy" an oxymoron, and later said he did not think any class should be disenfranchised. [cite:de1_cato_thiel] None of the three texts proposes making the unproductive work.

How American law sorts by productivity now

American law has always attached conditions to help. The Immigration Act of 3 August 1882 barred any immigrant "unable to take care of himself or herself without becoming a public charge." Guidance in 1999 limited the test to cash assistance or long-term care at public expense; a 2019 rule widened it to food stamps, Medicaid and public housing used for more than twelve months in three years, and was repealed in March 2021. [cite:de35_wiki_publiccharge] In 2026 DHS rescinded the 2022 public-charge regulations, with effect from 18 September, so that officers may weigh a wider range of benefits and information. [cite:de35_ey_publiccharge] The public-charge test applies to non-citizens seeking admission or permanent residence, not to citizens.

For citizens, the sorting runs through benefits. The 1996 welfare law tied cash aid to work, and the adult caseload fell by about 85 per cent by 2024. [cite:ad16_aei_welfare] The 2025 budget law extended food-stamp work requirements to adults up to 64 and limited the exemption for parents to those with children under 14. [cite:ad11_mn_snap] From 1 January 2027, adults aged 19 to 64 covered by the Medicaid expansion must report 80 hours a month of work, community service, a work programme or half-time study, with exemptions for parents of young children, the medically frail, pregnant women and others. [cite:ad8_ccf_ifr] As of early October 2026 the start date had not moved, though five governors asked for time. [cite:ad8_audit_hfn_gov] The risk the record shows is not the rule but its administration: Arkansas's 2018 work requirement cost about 18,000 people their coverage in under a year, and the private verification service about half of states use, Equifax's The Work Number, called the new requirements "a big positive" for its business. [cite:de24b_warren_equifax]

The line between the two

Three distinctions separate today's policies from the texts' answer, and each can be checked. The first is what a person loses. A work requirement or a public-charge finding takes away a benefit or an immigration status; Yarvin's proposal takes away liberty. The second is process. The Medicaid rule lists exemptions and runs through state fair hearings; the passages in the texts describe no hearing at all. [cite:ad8_ccf_ifr] [cite:de6_tnr_duran] The third is who is sorted. Today's rules apply to defined claimants and applicants by age, ability and status; Yarvin's "surplus" are residents as such, judged by whether they pay their way. The line blurs in two places on the record: where data matching ends cover for people who qualify, as in Arkansas, and where immigration detention holds people without conviction and pays them $1 a day for work ([scene:de35_the_consequence_ladder|the consequence ladder]). [cite:de24b_warren_equifax] [cite:de32_pogo_dollar] The worst case would cross the line entirely by confining or removing citizens for being unproductive. No law, rule or official proposal to do that is on the record.

The worst-case reading, tested

The book's working hypothesis, the Sophia platform's and not a finding, is that immigration enforcement and government cuts are the occasion and a sovereign executive without a veto the destination. This part asked what such a sovereign would do to the people under it. Documented: the texts' own words remove the rights by which people hold rulers to account, the vote, removal, independent courts, the press and due process, and propose confinement for the unwanted. [cite:de35_land_ebook] [cite:de35_nyt_interview] [cite:de6_tnr_duran] Documented too: every rung of the ladder below compelled labour without conviction exists in American law for some group, and for non-citizens the whole ladder has been climbed, from street face-matching to speech-based visa revocation to detention at record scale; sanctions have cut foreign judges and a UN rapporteur off from banking without a hearing. [cite:de26_404_fortify] [cite:de28_ch_aaup] [cite:de32_npr_deaths] [cite:de35_ap_icc] Compelled labour exists through the Thirteenth Amendment's exception and the $1 detainee rate, a century and a half older than the theorists. [cite:de35_lii_13th] [cite:de32_pogo_dollar]

Probable: that levers built for debts and statuses will be used wherever their triggers allow, as the widening of the public-charge test and the automated end of cover in Arkansas show; that is a reading of how such systems behave, not of anyone's stated plan. [cite:de35_ey_publiccharge] [cite:de24b_warren_equifax] Possible, as a join only: one "critic" flag read by every gatekeeper in [scene:de35_the_consequence_engine|the consequence engine]. Not supported: any theorist text prescribing forced labour, fines or purchase bans; any American designated under sanctions for speech; confinement of citizens for their status or productivity; forced labour at CECOT; and any document linking the texts to the machinery. [cite:de35_hrw_cecot] [cite:de30_dla_nspm7]

The record runs against the worst case in plain places. Courts vacated the speech-based deportation policy, the Harvard freeze, the law-firm orders and five food-restriction waivers, and the Supreme Court ruled 9–0 against GEO's shortcut in a detainee forced-labour case. [cite:de28_ch_aaup] [cite:de35_hm_harvard] [cite:de24b_gma_lawfirms] [cite:de35_bd_aragon] [cite:de32_lii_menocal] Eight states struck the punishment exception from their constitutions, and Congress removed conduct conditions from student aid in 2020. [cite:de35_ap_13th2022] [cite:de35_uc_fafsa] The same administration that built much of the 2025 machinery also ordered an end to politicised debanking and barred work on a central bank digital currency, and Yarvin himself said in 2025 that a new regime "can't be" about bayoneting professors. [cite:de26_fr_14331] [cite:de26_fr_14178] [cite:de6_nyt_transcript] The same standard applies to both parties: the private detention estate, the prison-labour exception that California and Louisiana voters kept, the 2013 banking pressure on gun sellers and Canada's 2022 account freezes were the work of governments across the spectrum. [cite:de32_brennan_14006] [cite:de35_cm_prop6] [cite:de26_cfslm_chokepoint] [cite:de35_cp24_scc] What the part leaves open is the question the texts leave open: who would choose, and who could remove, the chief executive at the top of this ladder.
