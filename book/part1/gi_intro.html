This volume is about the idea of a "Greater Israel": a phrase that many people know and few can trace. It was written to answer direct questions. What does the phrase mean, and how did the Hebrew Eretz Yisrael HaShlema, "the Whole Land of Israel," become a map from the Nile to the Euphrates in the telling of its critics? What borders did Herzl, the Zionist Organization and Jabotinsky actually claim, and what did Israel's governments say and do after 1949, 1956 and 1967? Is the claim written into Likud's platform, Israel's Basic Laws or its government's guidelines? What did Netanyahu mean by the maps he held up at the United Nations and by telling an interviewer that he felt "very much" connected to the vision? Are the positions Israel's army has held in Lebanon, Syria and Gaza since 2024 the expansion the phrase describes? And who, by name, holds the programme, if there is one?

The record answers some of these questions and not others. It shows that Herzl noted in 1898 a plan to ask the sultan for land from Egypt's frontier to the Euphrates and never made the request, and that the Zionist Organization's claim at Paris in 1919 stopped south of Sidon and at the Hejaz Railway. [cite:gi1_pipes_calumny] [cite:gi2_bu_zo1919] It shows the claim to "both banks of the Jordan" as the programme of the Revisionist minority, and the claim to the land between the Jordan and the sea passing from a 1967 manifesto into the Likud platform of 1977 and the government guidelines of December 2022, which asserted "an exclusive and inalienable right to all parts of the Land of Israel." [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] [cite:gi4_972_twoads] [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines] It shows the Golan annexed by law in 1981 and declared null and void by a unanimous Security Council, a minister's plan in 2025 to annex 82 per cent of the West Bank, and the Knesset voting for sovereignty in principle. [cite:gi5_wiki_golanlaw] [cite:gi8_rw_res497] [cite:gi6_toi_82pct] [cite:wb_toi_71_13] It also shows the withdrawals from Sinai in 1957 and 1982, from south Lebanon in 2000 and from Gaza in 2005, the treaties with Egypt and Jordan, and the Israelis who opposed expansion, from the reserve officers who founded Peace Now to the majority who told pollsters in 2026 that they opposed annexation. [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza] [cite:gi1_csm_sinai1982] [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] [cite:gi12_pn_officers] [cite:gi12_jvl_polls] It does not show a Knesset inscription, rivers in the flag or a map on a coin. [cite:gi10_csm_1980] [cite:gi10_mfa_flag] [cite:gi10_boi_coins]

It ends with the buffer zones held in Lebanon, Syria and Gaza as of October 2026, the annexation track a week before Israel's election of 27 October, and a verdict on the central question, territory by territory and actor by actor. [scene:gi9_gaza|The buffer belt] [scene:gi6_ground|The annexation track] [scene:gi13_record|Settled, Contested and Open] Its discipline is the library's. Ideology is attributed to named movements, parties, documents and officials, never to Jews, Israelis or Zionists as a people; myths are named as myths, with the evidence; disputed quotations are given in both readings; and the counter-record is set out with the same care as the record it answers. One correction belongs on this page: the Road to 1948 volume called Herzl's 1898 note a fabrication, and the research for this book found the note authentic, while confirming that the request was never made. [scene:gi2_herzl|Herzl's diary] The volume draws on the Israel Architecture simulation, the research files of the Sophia project and the earlier Israel books, and every claim taken from them has been traced to a citable source. It is a companion to The West Bank, Documented, Gaza, Documented, The Hezbollah Architecture and The Iran Architecture.
