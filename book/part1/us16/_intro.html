Part Sixteen compares how the two states pay for and provide health care. It sets out Israel's National Health Insurance Law, in force since January 1995, which obliges every resident to join one of four nonprofit funds offering the same basket of services, beside the American mix of employer insurance, Medicare, Medicaid and Affordable Care Act plans, and it names the people each system leaves out. It then measures what each country spends, on one international definition from 2000 to 2023, what households pay out of pocket, the American burden of medical debt and the disputed figures about it, and how each government sets the price of medicines. Finally it follows the outcomes from 1980 to 2024, life expectancy, infant and maternal mortality and the gaps between groups of citizens inside each country, and the state of mental health care, which the war since October 2023 has put under particular strain in Israel. Figures are given as of October 2026, ahead of Israel's election on 27 October and the American midterms on 3 November.
