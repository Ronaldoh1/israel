## 2. Series blind-spot list (applied)

- **Palestinian political agency:** planned in cw4_palestinian_politics (pending). In researched sections: Lebanon's 1958 crisis is told from its domestic causes as well as the Nasserist reading; the refugee exchange at the Waldorf gives Ben-Gurion's words and the US share of UNRWA's costs, and the Conciliation Commission's plan; writers must say plainly that Palestinian voices are absent from these US–Israeli documents.
- **The strongest Israeli case:** Ben-Gurion's fear of annihilation in his own words ("what Hitler did"); the periphery as an answer to isolation; the case for a deterrent as Alpher states Ben-Gurion's four strategies; Israel's grievance of 1958 (no arms, no written guarantee).
- **The strongest case of Israel's critics and of the US officials who doubted it:** Eisenhower on US aid and unknown funds; Gates and McCone; the "lack of candor" cables; Cohen and Burr on staged visits.
- **Arab states' case:** Nasser's response to the Shah, Egypt's concern over Dimona (Kennedy's renewed assurances to Cairo), Iraq's question at the Arab briefing; the Arab ambassadors' questions in their own words.
- **Jewish dissent:** the Leibowitz–Livne committee against the bomb; Israeli opposition to the Adenauer meeting; Meir's reported unease at misleading the US scientists.
- **Jews of the Arab world and Iran:** Wadi Salib and Morocco (pending); Iran's Jewish community linked (ira_src_s16).
- **Real antisemitism named plainly:** Nazi-era officials in Bonn (Globke, Oberländer) as documented; the Eichmann section (pending) will carry it, linked to rd4.
- **Palestinian citizens of Israel:** Al-Ard and the military government (pending; regime linked to rd6).
- **Terms examined:** "periphery" (Israel's own policy word; Schonmann's "Outer Circle" and "phantom of a pact"); "inspection" vs "visit" (the 1961 and 1962 visits were invitations, not inspections); "peaceful purposes"; "de facto" vs "de jure" recognition (Iran); "defensive" vs "offensive" weapon (Hawk, against s11d).
- **Convergence, not conspiracy:** inside Washington, the State Department, the CIA, the AEC and the Policy Planning Staff disagreed; France split between Perrin and Guillaumat, Bourgès-Maunoury and Gaillard, then de Gaulle; Israel split between Ben-Gurion and the cabinet over overflights and between Ben-Gurion and Meir over the visit. Each power is shown acting for its own interest (France's Algerian war and its own bomb; Germany's reconciliation and its fear of GDR recognition; Turkey and Iran after Baghdad).
- **Money and alignment:** German money flowed while Bonn withheld relations; the claim that it paid for Dimona is disputed and stays disputed; US aid to Israel 1957–62 (pending) set against Eisenhower's question about where Dimona's money came from; the cost estimates kept separate and attributed.
- **Sources kept independent:** Dimona's discovery rests on US documents in two archives (FRUS and the National Security Archive) plus FAS and JVL; the periphery on Schonmann's archival work, Alpher's participant account, Liel and FRUS; Germany on German documents via Bar-Joseph, the Adenauer foundation and Jüdische Allgemeine. Single-source items are flagged in the notes.

## 3. The reader's questions this stage must not leave hanging

How did Israel get a reactor that could make plutonium, and who in France agreed to it? When did the United States know, and why did it take so long? Was Ben-Gurion's "peaceful purposes" statement true? Did the 1961 visit see what was there? What did Kennedy and Ben-Gurion actually say to each other? What was the periphery doctrine, and did Washington back it? Why did Israel close its airspace to Western planes in August 1958? Did German money pay for Dimona? Why did Egypt break with Iran in 1960? Each writer lists the section-level questions and answers them in the prose or says the record does not.
