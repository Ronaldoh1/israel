Deir Yassin, the Hadassah Convoy and Tantura: The Massacres of 1948 and How the Record Changed

Two opposite claims about 1948 circulate widely. One holds that no massacres took place. The other holds that only one side suffered them. The record supports neither. In the spring of 1948 Jewish forces killed Arab villagers, and Arab forces killed Jews, sometimes within days of each other and sometimes in answer to each other. What has changed over the decades is not whether these events happened but how much is known about them, and much of that knowledge came from Israeli archives, an Israeli student and Israeli veterans.

Deir Yassin was a village west of Jerusalem. On 9 April 1948 it was attacked by the Irgun and Lehi, two right-wing Zionist militias, supported by the Haganah and the Palmach. [cite:wiki_deir_yassin] [cite:aljazeera_deir_yassin_75] The attack came on the day after Abd al-Qadir al-Husayni, commander of the Palestinian Army of the Holy War, was killed leading the counter-attack at al-Qastal, which overlooks the road from Jerusalem to Jaffa. [cite:rs_rd5_palquest_abdelqader] For decades the number killed at Deir Yassin was given as 254. Current scholarship puts it at about 110, generally within a range of 107 to 120, many of them women, children and elderly people. A Red Cross representative reported seeing some 150 bodies in a cave. [cite:wiki_deir_yassin]

The higher figure entered the record at the time. In December 1948 a letter to the New York Times, dated 2 December and published on 4 December and signed by 28 people including Albert Einstein, Hannah Arendt and Sidney Hook, protested Menachem Begin's visit to the United States. It said that on 9 April "terrorist bands" had attacked Deir Yassin, a "peaceful village, which was not a military objective," and "killed most of its inhabitants (240 men, women, and children)." [cite:rs_rd5_einstein_nyt] Arendt had already condemned the Irgun's attacks, Deir Yassin among them, in the essay she published in May 1948. [cite:rs_rd5_manne_2026] The letter's figure of 240 is one given in 1948; it is not the figure scholars now give. The letter's argument against Begin's new party is told in [scene:rf_s01|the section on dissent].

The killings had effects well beyond the village. Benny Morris wrote that Deir Yassin was followed by mass Arab flight. [cite:wiki_deir_yassin] Another strand of the research gathered for this book, drawn from Israeli historians, adds two details: that the attackers announced the killings by radio to nearby villages, which the official Haganah history by Yehuda Slutsky records, and that Ben-Gurion later noted in his diary that Deir Yassin "had a decisive effect" on Arab flight. That account gives the toll as about 107 civilians, at the low end of the current range. [cite:morris_1948] Material on Deir Yassin in Israeli military archives remains classified. [cite:wiki_deir_yassin] [cite:aljazeera_deir_yassin_75]

Four days later the violence ran the other way. On 13 April 1948 Arab forces attacked a convoy travelling to Hadassah Hospital on Mount Scopus and killed 78 Jews, most of them doctors and nurses. The attack was a reprisal for Deir Yassin. [cite:wiki_deir_yassin] The victims were medical staff on their way to a hospital, and their deaths belong in the same record, with the same weight, as the villagers killed four days before.

The Hadassah convoy was not the only such killing of Jews in these weeks. Before the Mandate ended, the Arab Legion attacked the Gush Etzion bloc of Jewish settlements; its defenders surrendered, and some of them were massacred, according to Avi Shlaim's account. [cite:rs_rd5_shlaim_seminar_cw] During Abd al-Qadir al-Husayni's command, the Army of the Holy War's explosives unit blew up the Jewish Agency building in Jerusalem; the source does not give the date or the toll. [cite:rs_rd5_palquest_abdelqader] These belong to the civil war that had begun on 1 December 1947, in which attacks ran in both directions. [cite:explaininghistory_un_partition]

Tantura shows most clearly how the record has grown. It was a coastal village captured by the Alexandroni Brigade on 22–23 May 1948, about a week after the state was proclaimed. A master's thesis by Theodore Katz later recorded testimony that villagers had been killed after the village surrendered. Veterans of the brigade sued him for libel, and he retracted. [cite:npr_tantura] [cite:jewishindependent_tantura]

The question did not end with the retraction. In the 2022 Israeli documentary Tantura, veterans then in their nineties described on camera killings and a mass grave near what is now Dor Beach, a popular resort. A document shown in the film acknowledges that soldiers dug a mass grave. Other veterans deny that any massacre took place, and the number killed cannot be established. [cite:npr_tantura] [cite:jewishindependent_tantura]

The Deir Yassin numbers show how a toll can move in both directions. The figure of 254 was repeated for decades; contemporaries in December 1948 wrote 240; scholarship now gives about 110. The Red Cross representative's 150 bodies is a count of what one witness saw in one place, not a total. None of these figures is a final count, and each should be read with its source.

The way these events entered the record is itself part of the history. Much of what is known came from Israelis: historians working in Israeli archives, a student who gathered veterans' testimony, and veterans who decided late in life to speak. [cite:npr_tantura] Atrocities against Jews in the same weeks, at the Hadassah convoy and at Gush Etzion, are equally part of the record. The later expulsions at Lydda and Ramla in July 1948, with their own disputed tolls, are told with the Arab states' war in [scene:rd5_invasion|the invasion and the war].

Much remains unsettled. Israeli military archives on Deir Yassin are still partly closed, so the exact toll and the full chain of command cannot yet be documented. The number killed at Tantura cannot be established, and the veterans who were there disagree about what happened. The research gathered here gives no date for Katz's thesis or for the libel case, and no toll for the bombing of the Jewish Agency building. For Gush Etzion it says that some defenders were massacred but gives no number. What the record does establish is that killings of civilians and prisoners took place on both sides in these weeks, and that the documentary record of them is still growing.
