This opening chapter has cleared away several confusions that shape how the later story is usually told. "Semitic" turned out to be a linguist's term, coined in 1781, for a family of languages that includes both Hebrew and Arabic. "Antisemitism" was a separate word, made into a movement's banner by Wilhelm Marr in 1879, and it means hatred of Jews, nothing broader. Whether that word should also reach political criticism of a state is still argued. The dispute runs from the IHRA definition of 2016, issued by the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance, to the Jerusalem Declaration of 2021 and that declaration's own critics. These pages set it out without settling it.

The chapter also separated Judaism, a religion and a set of lineages, from Zionism, a political movement founded at Basel in 1897. It traced Jewish opposition to that movement, and critique from within it or from within the state it produced, through Reform Judaism, the Bund, Satmar, the binationalists and Mizrahi scholars. It placed the Star of David and the menorah in their actual chronology. It established that Ottoman Palestine was a named, administered region of about 600,000 people, and that Jewish continuity, Arab continuity and modern national identity are three separate questions. Finally, it showed that the contest over this history is still being fought today, through paid influence campaigns and through content moderation, with documented conduct on more than one side. With that ground cleared, the story itself can begin.
