The Autumn War and the November Ceasefire

23 September 2024. Six days after the pagers, Israel turned the support front into a full war. On 23 September its air force carried out about 200 airstrike events across five Lebanese governorates in a single day, roughly three times the previous peak, according to ACLED. Lebanon's health ministry first reported 558 killed over two days and later gave 569 for 23 September alone, among them 50 children and 94 women; ACLED called it the deadliest day for Lebanon in decades. [cite:hz7_acled_sep24] [cite:hz7_ap_558] Over September as a whole ACLED recorded more than 1,700 Israeli strikes in Lebanon, an increase of more than 125 per cent on August. [cite:hz7_acled_sep24]

The scale of the air campaign. Between 16 September and 25 October ACLED recorded more than 3,250 Israeli airstrike events in Lebanon, and found that the number of airstrike events in Lebanon in both September and October was higher than in any single month it had recorded anywhere in the region since 2017, including Syria in April 2017, and higher than any single month in Gaza in the preceding year. An ACLED "event" is one incident in one place on one day and can include several individual strikes. [cite:hz7_acled_qa] A figure of more than 5,700 airstrike events for mid-September to November circulates in secondary accounts and is attributed to ACLED; this volume could not find it in ACLED's published overviews and uses the 3,250 figure for the period ACLED itself reported. ACLED's November overview said the air campaign stayed at "heightened levels" that month, about a fifth lower than in October. [cite:hz7_acled_nov24]

Nasrallah. On the evening of 27 September Israeli aircraft struck a cluster of buildings in Haret Hreik, in Beirut's southern suburbs, where Israel said Hezbollah's leadership was meeting in an underground compound. Israel later released footage of at least eight F-15I jets that took part; experts consulted by the Associated Press said the damage was consistent with 2,000-pound-class bombs designed to penetrate before exploding. Hezbollah confirmed the next day that Hassan Nasrallah, its secretary-general for 32 years, had been killed. [cite:hz7_ap_nasrallah_site] In the last week of September at least seven senior Hezbollah figures were killed, according to ACLED, on top of at least 21 commanders killed between October 2023 and 22 September 2024. [cite:hz7_acled_sep24] His likely successor, Hashem Safieddine, a relative of Nasrallah who headed the group's executive council, was killed in a later Israeli strike; Hezbollah confirmed his death on 23 October. [cite:hz7_dh_safieddine] Netanyahu later said the strike on Nasrallah, like the pager operation, had gone ahead over the opposition of some senior officials. [cite:hz7_toi_netanyahu_pagers]

The ground invasion. On the night of 30 September Israeli paratroopers and commandos of the 98th Division crossed the border. The army described "limited, localized, and targeted ground raids" on villages it said posed "an immediate threat to Israeli communities in northern Israel." It was Israel's first ground invasion of Lebanon since 2006. [scene:hz5_war|The 2006 war] Naim Qassem, Hezbollah's deputy leader, said: "The Islamic resistance will continue to confront the Israeli enemy." President Joe Biden called for a ceasefire and said he was "comfortable" with Israel stopping its operation; the Pentagon announced a few thousand more troops for the region. [cite:hz7_cbc_ground] Hezbollah kept firing: ACLED recorded more than 50 armed clashes in the first four weeks of the ground operation, more than 60 other Hezbollah attacks inside Lebanon, and rocket fire into Israel of hundreds a day. On 13 October a Hezbollah drone killed four soldiers of the Golani Brigade at a base near Binyamina, about 70 kilometres south of the border. [cite:hz7_acled_qa]

UNIFIL. The UN peacekeeping force in the south, UNIFIL, found its positions in the line of fire. On 10 October it said an Israeli tank had fired at an observation tower at its headquarters in Naqoura, injuring two Indonesian peacekeepers, after Israeli soldiers had the day before deliberately fired at and disabled cameras at another position. Israel acknowledged that its forces had fired in the area, said Hezbollah operated near UN posts, and said it had told peacekeepers to take shelter first. Italy's defence minister, Guido Crosetto, said the incidents could amount to war crimes; the White House said it was "deeply concerned." [cite:hz7_almon_unifil] Part Eight follows UNIFIL's records of the truce that followed. [scene:hz8_violations|Violations after the truce]

The toll. On 4 December 2024 Lebanon's health minister, Firas Abiad, said at least 4,047 people had been killed and 16,593 wounded in Lebanon by Israeli attacks since 8 October 2023, 3,402 of the dead after 15 September 2024; he counted 790 women and 316 children among the victims and said the real toll might be higher. The ministry did not separate civilians from fighters. [cite:hz7_olj_abiad_4047] On the day the ceasefire was agreed, Lebanese government figures put the dead at nearly 3,800 and the displaced at more than a million, about a quarter of the population. [cite:hz7_bg_ceasefire] On the Israeli side, the Alma Research and Education Center, an Israeli institute that tracks the northern front, counted 45 civilians and 83 soldiers killed in the northern arena between 8 October 2023 and the ceasefire; AP put Israel's dead at about 100. [cite:hz7_alma_summary] [cite:hz7_bg_ceasefire] Israel's army published claims of Hezbollah fighters killed (more than 800 in the first year, by its count); Hezbollah did not publish a full count, and the public record does not contain an independent figure, so none is given here. [cite:hz7_acled_year_pdf]

The ceasefire of 27 November 2024. The agreement, brokered by the United States and France, took effect at 04:00 on 27 November. Its published text has thirteen points. Lebanon's government would prevent Hezbollah and other armed groups from attacking Israel, and Israel would stop offensive operations against Lebanese targets by land, air or sea. Both affirmed UN Security Council Resolution 1701 of 2006, and both kept "their inherent right of self-defense, consistent with international law." South of the Litani river, only Lebanon's official forces could hold arms; the Lebanese state would control arms sales and production and "dismantle all existing unauthorized facilities involved in the production of arms and related materiel," starting in the south; the US and France would help bring the Lebanese army in the south to 10,000 soldiers. A monitoring "Mechanism," "hosted by UNIFIL, chaired by the US, and including France," would receive complaints from both sides. Israel would withdraw in phases south of the Blue Line as the Lebanese army deployed, over a period "which should not exceed 60 days." [cite:hz7_toi_text]

The side letter. Before the deal Israel asked Washington for a separate written commitment to its freedom of action if Hezbollah broke the truce; Hezbollah told mediators the 60-day arrangement left the door open to "Israeli aggression in Lebanon." [cite:hz7_toi_freedom] Hours after the ceasefire began, Israel's Channel 13 broadcast what it said was the American letter. In it, as reported, the United States "recognizes Israel's right to respond to threats from Lebanese territory, in accordance with international law"; outside southern Lebanon, Israel reserved the right to act against developing threats if Lebanon could not or would not stop them; Israeli flights over Lebanon would be limited to surveillance; and the two governments would share intelligence on violations. [cite:hz7_olj_sideletter] Netanyahu presented the deal in those terms. Israel was "preserving full military freedom of action," he said, and "The length of the cease-fire will depend on what happens in Lebanon"; he gave three reasons for accepting it: to focus on Iran, to rest the army and restock its weapons, and to separate Hamas from its allies. Biden said the arrangement was "designed to be a permanent cessation of hostilities" and that "Israel did not launch this war." The security cabinet approved it 10 to 1; Itamar Ben-Gvir called it "a historic mistake." [cite:hz7_bg_ceasefire] The gap between the published text, which bound both sides, and the reported letter, which gave one side a further right to act, is where the next part begins.
