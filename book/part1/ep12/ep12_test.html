Outlier or Instance?

Set against these cases, Epstein is an instance, not an outlier. Every part of the mechanism that protected him has protected others: the institution that put its reputation first, the early report the FBI did not pursue, the settlements without admission, the helpers who were never charged, the time limits and lost testimony that closed cases before they opened. The precedents come from a church, a broadcaster, a sports federation, a town council, a youth movement and a film studio, across several countries and a century and a half.

Two things set Epstein's case apart. The first is the clientele: the scale and standing of the people around him, across finance, politics, science and royalty on several continents, as Part Seven's map showed. The second is the federal immunity: a written agreement, signed by a United States Attorney, protecting not only him but every unnamed "potential co-conspirator". The Church moved abusers between parishes; it did not obtain a government's promise not to prosecute any of them.

The precedents also show that the pattern can be broken, and how. Every case here that was exposed was exposed by someone outside the institution: a grand jury, a reporter, an independent inquiry, survivors who kept talking. The cases that ended in convictions quickly were the ones where a court or an independent body took the case out of the hands of those with an interest in delay. Nothing in Epstein's case required it to go differently. The tools and the precedents were there. They were not used.
