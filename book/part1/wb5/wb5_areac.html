Building in Area C

The planning system. In Area C, the Israeli Civil Administration is the planning authority for Palestinian villages as well as for settlements. According to the UN's humanitarian office, less than one percent of Area C is covered by a planning scheme for Palestinian communities approved by the Civil Administration, while the outline plans of Israeli settlements cover at least 8.5 percent of it. Palestinian communities have drawn up 123 outline plans and submitted 109; five had been approved, as of 2021. [cite:wb_ocha_areac]

Permits and demolitions. Without an approved plan, a building permit is almost impossible to get. Between 2009 and 2018 the Civil Administration granted 98 of 4,422 Palestinian requests for building permits in Area C, about 2 percent. Families who build without one build under the threat of a demolition order, and between 1988 and 2014 the Civil Administration issued more than 14,000 of them against Palestinian structures in Area C. [cite:wb_ocha_areac] Part Eight follows the demolitions themselves.

Israel's position, set out by its coordinator of activities in the territories in answer to the UN in 2015, is that under the interim agreements construction in Area C requires the approval of the responsible authorities; it disputed the UN's figures as "inconsistent with the situation on the ground," said the Civil Administration was processing thirteen Palestinian master plans, four of them completed, and described a plan to give Bedouin communities land with water, power and sewage. [cite:wb_cogat_2015] The UN's figures, from 2021, and Israel's 2015 statement are the two documented accounts of how the planning law is applied to the two populations that share the area.
