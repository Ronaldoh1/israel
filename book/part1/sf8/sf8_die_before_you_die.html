Die Before You Die

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

A saying often quoted in Sufi writing tells the seeker to "die before you die". It is frequently presented as a saying of the Prophet. It is not established as one: the hadith scholar Ibn Hajar is reported to have said it is not proven, and the later scholar Mulla ʿAli al-Qari called it "a statement of some Sufis". [cite:sf_ha_die] The book records it here as what it is: a Sufi teaching, not a prophetic one.

The teaching names the goal of the path. Fana, in Britannica's words, is "the complete denial of self and the realization of God": the Sufi has "'annihilated' his individual will and 'passed away' from his own existence". After it comes baqa, subsistence, reached when the Sufi regains full consciousness. [cite:sf_brit_fana] To die before dying is to pass through fana while still alive: to let the self that wants, fears and clings pass away, so that what remains is not nothing but a life lived from God.

The reading also sheds light on al-Hallaj. His "I am the Truth" can be heard, as his judges heard it, as a man claiming to be God. It can also be heard as the voice of someone who had passed through fana, in whom the individual self had died and only the Truth spoke. [cite:sf_brit_hallaj] [cite:sf_brit_fana] The tradition has argued about that for a thousand years. The record shows only that the state chose the first hearing and put him on a cross.

The record limits the reading. The saying is not a hadith; fana as Sufis describe it is a state reached within a disciplined path under a teacher, not an idea to be adopted by reading; and Britannica is careful to place baqa after fana, so that the path ends not in disappearance but in a return. [cite:sf_ha_die] [cite:sf_brit_fana] The parallel with the "death of the old self" read at Golgotha in The Canon is the reader's.
