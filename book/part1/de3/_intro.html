Part Three asks where the Dark Enlightenment's ideas came from. It begins with the ancestors the texts name for themselves, in Moldbug's and Land's own words: Hobbes, the Austrian economists Yarvin later renounced, Hans-Hermann Hoppe's case for monarchy over democracy, Thomas Carlyle, James Burnham and Frederick the Great. It then takes a lineage the texts never name but which is often drawn for them, from the 1930s Technocracy movement through Elon Musk's grandfather, Joshua N. Haldeman, and separates what is documented from what is only inferred. It ends with the libertarian idea of exit, the Austrian institutions, the cybernetic strand that Nick Land brought with him, and the places where scholars and the ancestors themselves dispute the family tree.
