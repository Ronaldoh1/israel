Part Eleven tested the documents on Rome's bankers. Part Twelve turns to the record that has done most to shape public trust in the Holy See since 2002: the sexual abuse of children by clergy and its concealment. It sets out, first, the rules written in Rome, from the secret procedure of 1922 to the abolition of the pontifical secret in December 2019 and the papal commission's reports of 2024 and 2025, separating what they required inside the Church from what they said about the police. It then gives each national report on its own terms, with its method, period and scope, without adding figures together; follows the named cases in which the Holy See itself decided, and the bill paid by American dioceses; and ends with inquiries into Anglican, Baptist and German Protestant churches, so that what is particular to the Catholic case can be told apart from what is not.
