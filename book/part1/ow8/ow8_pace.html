The Pace Since October 2023

Before the war in Gaza, the pipeline described in the previous section ran at the steady rhythm set by the 2016 memorandum. After 7 October 2023 it ran much faster, and the emergency power that lets an administration skip congressional review became a routine part of it. This section sets out the record under two administrations, Biden's and Trump's, which differed sharply in what they said and less in what they shipped.

The first emergencies, December 2023. On 8 December 2023 the State Department notified Congress that Secretary of State Antony Blinken had determined that "an emergency exists that requires the immediate sale" of 13,981 120mm tank cartridges to Israel, worth $106.5 million, bypassing the review period. The department said that "the United States is committed to the security of Israel". Such determinations were rare, though at least four administrations had used the power since 1979. [cite:ow8_ap_tank2023] On 29 December 2023 a second emergency sale followed, worth $147.5 million: 4,792 M107 155mm artillery rounds, 52,229 M795 rounds and 30,000 propelling charges, drawn from US Army stock. [cite:ow8_at_155] These were two of the 85 sales notified for Israel in 2023; the rest went through the ordinary process. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] A Senate resolution introduced that month (S.Res. 504), which would have required a human-rights report on Israel's use of American aid under section 502B of the Foreign Assistance Act, later failed. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

Congress adds money, April 2024. In April 2024 Congress passed the Israel Security Supplemental Appropriations Act, which added $3.5 billion in Foreign Military Financing, $4 billion for missile defence and $1.2 billion for the Iron Beam laser. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] Brown University's Costs of War project calculated that the United States spent $21.7 billion on military aid to Israel between October 2023 and September 2025, not counting committed arms sales. [cite:gz_cow_aid]

The pause, May 2024. The only American hold on a shipment came in May 2024, when the administration paused a consignment of 1,800 2,000-pound bombs and 1,700 500-pound bombs to signal concern about a ground assault on Rafah, where more than a million displaced Palestinians were sheltering. [cite:ow8_axios_pause] President Biden explained: "Civilians have been killed in Gaza as a consequence of those bombs." The 500-pound bombs were released weeks later; the 2,000-pound bombs stayed held for eight months. [cite:ow8_ap_unpause] In August 2024 the administration approved more than $20 billion in new sales, including F-15IA fighters. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

The release and the second wave, 2025. Five days after taking office, on 25 January 2025, President Trump released the 2,000-pound bombs, writing that "a lot of things that were ordered and paid for by Israel, but have not been sent by Biden, are now on their way!" [cite:ow8_ap_unpause] In February 2025 the administration notified $8.4 billion in sales. At the end of the month Secretary of State Marco Rubio waived congressional review to fast-track about $3 billion of them, including heavy bombs and armoured bulldozers that the Biden administration had withheld, and on 1 March he announced that he had signed a declaration to expedite "approximately $4 billion in military assistance". He described Biden's policy as a "partial arms embargo" and said the administration had approved nearly $12 billion in major sales to Israel since 20 January. [cite:ow8_toi_rubio2025] [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] Biden had denied withholding anything except the single batch of 2,000-pound bombs. [cite:ow8_toi_rubio2025]

The scale. By April 2025, according to the Congressional Research Service, the United States had 751 active Foreign Military Sales cases with Israel, worth $39.2 billion. By May 2025 Israel's Ministry of Defense said that some 800 aircraft and 140 ships had delivered more than 90,000 tons of American arms and equipment since the war began. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] In fiscal 2025, the Congressional Research Service noted, Israel was among the countries with the highest-value proposed sales in the whole programme. [cite:ow8_crs_fms2026]

The two administrations' accounts. The Biden administration justified the December 2023 emergencies by the United States' commitment to Israel's security and its right to self-defence, and the May 2024 pause by the harm the heaviest bombs did to civilians. [cite:ow8_ap_tank2023] [cite:ow8_ap_unpause] The Trump administration presented its emergencies as ending a "partial arms embargo" and the conditions that Biden had imposed through a national security memorandum, discussed in the last section of this part. [cite:ow8_toi_rubio2025] The record shows that the two differed in one decision, the hold on the 2,000-pound bombs; that both used the emergency power to bypass Congress; and that Congress, which has never blocked an arms sale by a resolution of disapproval, did not block any of these.
