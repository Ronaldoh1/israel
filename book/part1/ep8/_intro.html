Part Eight asks why the law, which convicted Ghislaine Maxwell and could have convicted many others, protected almost everyone above her. It does not begin with Epstein. It begins in 1973, with the first time a president tried to fire his way out of an investigation, because the gaps that protected Epstein's circle were built, closed and reopened long before anyone had heard of him.

The part then follows the case through the Palm Beach police, the state attorney, the federal prosecutors and the defence team to the 2007 agreement; sets out what the courts later found; and lists, rule by rule, the ordinary laws and policies that together decided who would face the law and who would not. It names the tools Congress built for exactly this kind of case and records which were never used. And it holds every claim to one test: whether the record shows a staged show of justice, or a structure that produces the same outcome without anyone needing to stage it.
