## B. The redactions: what was blacked out, and by whom

**B1. Fully redacted pages (the first release, Dec 2025)**
- CBS News found **at least 550 pages entirely blacked out**: every word on every page.
- **Three consecutive documents, 255 pages,** were completely redacted.
- A **119-page document labelled "Grand Jury-NY"** was completely redacted. Senator Schumer said: "all 119 pages of one document were completely blacked out."
- At least **180 more fully blacked-out pages** sat inside files that were mostly redacted.
- **DOJ's answer:** a largely unredacted version of the 119-page transcript (involving Maxwell) was posted the next day. Blanche said: "The only redactions being applied to the documents are those required by law — full stop." DOJ said no politicians' names were redacted.
- **Reaction:**
  - Rep. Massie (R): the release "grossly fails to comply with both the spirit and the letter of the law".
  - Rep. Khanna (D): "incomplete release with too many redactions".
  - This criticism came from both parties.

**B2. Pages still fully redacted in the "unredacted" set (Feb–Mar 2026)**
- Rep. Laura Friedman reviewed the congressional reading-room files on 13 Feb 2026. She found "hundreds of pages" still entirely blacked out, even in the set described as unredacted.
- Examples by document number: **EFTA00006109–6208, EFTA00006209–6308, EFTA00006307–6363** (every page fully redacted), plus **EFTA01743628 and EFTA01743632** (attachments that could not be opened).
- DOJ staff told her this was "how the FBI sent them over."
- She called it "a coverup" and demanded unredacted copies (letter, 6 Mar 2026).

**B3. The Trump photograph removed, then restored (Dec 2025)**
- At least **15 files** were pulled after the first release, including **EFTA00000468**, a photograph of Trump with Epstein, Maxwell and Melania Trump.
- DOJ said SDNY had flagged it "to protect victims". It was reposted "without any alteration" once DOJ found "no evidence that any Epstein victims are depicted".
- Blanche said the removal "has nothing to do with President Trump".
- The sequence is documented. A motive is not.

**B4. The six names read on the House floor, and a warning (Feb 2026)**
- After reading the unredacted files, Massie and Khanna said six men were "likely incriminated", and Khanna read their names on the House floor:
  - Leslie Wexner;
  - Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem;
  - four lesser-known men.
- **DOJ's answer:** the four lesser-known men "have no apparent connection to Epstein whatsoever". They appeared in **a photo lineup assembled by SDNY**, as "completely random people selected years ago for an FBI lineup".
- **The book records this as the clearest warning in the whole release:** reading an unredacted name off a page is not the same as knowing why the name is there. The two real names stand on their own records (see C). **The four do not appear anywhere in the book.**

**B5. Victims exposed, associates redacted (Jan 2026)**
- In the final release, victims' names and nude images were left visible, while the names of possible co-conspirators were redacted.
- Survivors filed an emergency motion, calling it the "most egregious violation of victim privacy". See `ep_OPERATIONS`, Part C.

**B6. What is still withheld**
- About half of the roughly 6M pages reviewed.
- Victim interviews.
- The unredacted DEA memo.
- Documents withheld on grounds of privilege that the Act does not list (`ep_LEGAL_ARCHITECTURE` row 7).
- Foreign-language documents still under court-ordered review.

**What the redaction record shows, stated plainly:**
- The law allowed five grounds for withholding and forbade withholding to spare embarrassment.
- In the release that followed:
  - entire documents were blacked out;
  - a photograph of the President was pulled and then restored;
  - the people the law was meant to protect were exposed;
  - members of Congress found pages blacked out even in the "unredacted" set.

  Each of these is documented. Whether any was *intended* to protect a particular person is not shown, and the book doesn't claim it.
