Service by Service

Each branch of the US armed forces has its own tie to Israel, and they differ in kind: the Army runs radars and air-defence batteries, the Navy sails to Haifa and has shot down missiles aimed at Israel, the Air Force shares its newest fighter, and the Marine Corps is buying an air-defence system built on Israeli technology. They are joined by exercises run first under European Command and now under Central Command. This section takes them in turn.

The Army. The Army's presence is the most permanent. The X-band radar deployed to the Negev in 2008 came with about 120 American personnel, and the Har Keren site has since been expanded with barracks. [cite:ow7_sat_xband] [cite:ow7_int_site512] In 2019 the Army sent a THAAD (Terminal High Altitude Area Defense) battery to Israel for training. [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024] On 13 October 2024, after Iran's missile attacks of 13 April and 1 October, Secretary of Defense Lloyd Austin, "at the direction of the President", authorized a THAAD battery and its crew to deploy to Israel to augment its air defences; a battery of this kind generally needs about 95 soldiers. The Pentagon said the move "underscores the United States' ironclad commitment to the defense of Israel". [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024] [cite:ow7_ap_thaad2024]

The Army also became, for a time, an Iron Dome customer. It announced in August 2018 that it would buy the Israeli system as an interim defence against cruise missiles, bought two batteries in 2019 (delivered in late 2020 and early 2021), and sent one temporarily to Guam. It declined to buy more, partly because it could not easily network them with its other air-defence systems. In October 2023, after the Hamas attack, officials told Congress the Pentagon would lease both batteries back to Israel, and the Pentagon said the Tamir interceptors it was delivering came from Army stocks. [cite:ow7_twz_irondome] [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

The Navy. The 1981 memorandum gave the Sixth Fleet use of Israeli ports, and Haifa now hosts around forty US Navy ships a year; the amphibious ship USS Iwo Jima docked there in March 2018 after a joint exercise. [cite:ow_wiki_sca1981] [cite:ow7_jvl_strategic] On 8 October 2023, the day after the Hamas attack, Austin ordered the USS Gerald R. Ford carrier strike group, with a cruiser and five destroyers, to the eastern Mediterranean and reinforced F-35, F-15, F-16 and A-10 squadrons in the region, saying the aim was to deter a wider war. [cite:ow7_ap_ford2023] Navy destroyers have since fired in Israel's defence, as the next section describes. [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] [cite:ow7_usni_oct2024]

The Air Force and the F-35. On 7 October 2010 Israel signed to buy twenty F-35s for about $3 billion, paid for with American military aid, and by November 2016 it had ordered fifty. [cite:ow7_wiki_f35i] Israel's version is designated F-35I "Adir" because, in the words of a 2010 report, "there will be unique Israeli features installed in them": Elbit electronic-warfare equipment installed under a 2012 contract, an Israel Aerospace Industries command-and-control software layer running on the jet's central computer, Israeli weapons and Israeli-made helmet displays. The United States at first refused to let Israel integrate its own electronic warfare; Israel's answer was a "plug-and-play" architecture for add-on Israeli electronics. [cite:ow7_wiki_f35i] On 22 May 2018 Israel's air force commander said Israel had become "the first country in the world to use the F-35 in combat". [cite:ow7_wiki_f35i] The industrial flow ran partly back to Israel: Lockheed Martin undertook to buy about $4 billion of parts and systems from Israeli firms, and Israel Aerospace Industries makes F-35 wings. [cite:ow7_wiki_f35i] In August 2024 the United States approved more than $20 billion in sales including new F-15IA fighters. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

The Marine Corps. This book found no standing Marine Corps programme with Israel before the 2020s. Since then the Marines have been developing a Medium-Range Intercept Capability that pairs Iron Dome's launcher and Tamir interceptor (called SkyHunter in American service) with Marine radars and command systems; it hit every target in a live-fire test at White Sands, New Mexico, on 30 June 2022. [cite:ow8_dn_mric] In October 2026 the Raytheon–Rafael joint venture delivered the first Tamir interceptors built at a new plant in Camden, Arkansas, the first outside Israel, and said it would build the Marine variant there. [cite:ow8_rtx_camden]

The exercises. The services meet Israel's forces in large joint exercises. Juniper Cobra, a ballistic-missile-defence exercise held every two years, reached its ninth edition in March 2018 with more than 2,500 American and about 2,000 Israeli personnel; European Command said it was not tied to any real-world event. [cite:ow7_eucom_jc2018] Juniper Oak 23.2, which ended on 26 January 2023 under Central Command, was the largest US–Israeli exercise to date: about 6,400 American and more than 1,500 Israeli troops, more than 140 aircraft including B-52 bombers, and the USS George H.W. Bush carrier strike group. CENTCOM's commander said it showed that "the partnership between CENTCOM and the IDF is stronger and continues to grow". [cite:ow7_navy_jo23] Congress has since written joint exercises, including underground warfare, into the 2025 defence act. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

Why the United States says it does this. The official rationales are consistent across administrations: deterrence and regional stability (the purpose Austin gave for the 2023 carrier deployment and CENTCOM gave for Juniper Oak), interoperability between allied forces, and a statutory commitment, written into law in 2008, to preserve Israel's "qualitative military edge" over its neighbours. [cite:ow7_ap_ford2023] [cite:ow7_navy_jo23] [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] The American gains are documented too: the Army and Marines acquired a combat-proven short-range interceptor, and in 2014 the Missile Defense Agency gained full access to Iron Dome's technology under a co-production agreement described in Part Eight. [cite:ow7_twz_irondome] [cite:ow8_dn_mric] [cite:ow8_crs_irondome2014] What is one-sided is the money: the Israeli F-35s, like most of Israel's American weapons, were bought with American grant aid, and part of that spending returned to Israeli companies through offsets. [cite:ow7_wiki_f35i]
