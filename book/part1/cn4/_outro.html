By 419 the New Testament of twenty-seven books had been listed in Alexandria and in Africa, and the Latin text that the West would read for a thousand years had been commissioned in Rome. [cite:vt23_na_festal39] [cite:vt23_na_carthage] [cite:cn_brit_vulgate] The men who did it held offices that had grown rich and powerful under imperial patronage: the Roman see, fought over in 366 and described by Ammianus as a source of carriages and royal banquets, and the patriarchate of Alexandria, held through repeated exiles. [cite:cn_amm_27_3] [cite:cn_brit_athanasius] Whether the Nag Hammadi codices were buried because of Athanasius's letter is still disputed. [cite:vt25_lj2015] [cite:vt25_lewisblount2014] Part Five follows the Roman office over the next thousand years, as it acquired a territory, a forged title to it, and the families who fought to own it.
