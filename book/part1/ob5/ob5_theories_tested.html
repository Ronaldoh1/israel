Theories Tested

Two theories lead the field. Global neuronal workspace theory holds that a content becomes conscious when it is broadcast widely across the brain, with the prefrontal cortex at the centre of the broadcast. Integrated information theory holds that consciousness is integrated information itself, and locates it in a posterior "hot zone" at the back of the cortex. [cite:ob_mcgill_cogitate]

In an unusual step, the supporters of each theory agreed in advance on predictions that would count against their own view, and an international group, the Cogitate Consortium, ran the test. More than 250 participants were studied with fMRI, magnetoencephalography and electrodes placed inside the brain. The results, published in Nature on 1 May 2025, did not crown a winner: "critical predictions of both leading theories were challenged by the empirical findings." [cite:ob_mcgill_cogitate] All data and analysis code were shared openly, and a second study is under way. [cite:ob_mcgill_cogitate]

The pseudoscience letter

Early results were announced in June 2023, and the press coverage that followed set off a public quarrel. On 15 September 2023 a letter signed by 124 scholars was posted to the preprint server PsyArXiv arguing that integrated information theory should be labelled pseudoscience, on the grounds that it is presented as well established without the support to match and that its core assumptions cannot be tested. [cite:ob_sciam_iit] Its defenders answered at once. Christof Koch said the theory may be wrong but states its assumptions clearly; Anil Seth, no partisan of the theory, called the label inflammatory; and Liad Mudrik, who co-led the test, pointed out that it had falsified one of the theory's predictions, which is what testable theories allow. [cite:ob_sciam_iit]

The quantum theory of mind

One theory brings consciousness back to quantum physics. In the 1990s the mathematician Roger Penrose and the anaesthesiologist Stuart Hameroff proposed that consciousness arises from quantum processes in microtubules, the protein scaffolds inside neurons. [cite:ob_wiki_orchor] In 2000 the physicist Max Tegmark calculated that such states would decohere in the warm, wet brain in femtoseconds, far too quickly to matter; and a 2022 underground experiment testing one version of Penrose's collapse idea found none of the radiation it predicted, though Penrose's original proposal was not ruled out. [cite:ob_wiki_orchor] The philosopher Patricia Churchland put the critics' view in a phrase: "Pixie dust in the synapses". [cite:ob_wiki_orchor]

The state of the science, then, is not that consciousness has been explained and is only chemistry, nor that it has been shown to be quantum or beyond the brain. It is that the best theories have been put to a fair test for the first time and none passed whole.
