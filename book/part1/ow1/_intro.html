Part One sets out the legal spine of the relationship between the United States and Israel: every major law, memorandum and executive action from recognition in 1948 to the negotiations of 2026 over what replaces the current aid pledge, with the administration that made it and, where Congress voted, the margin. It follows how arms sales became an emergency statute in 1973, how the Reagan years built standing machinery, how the aid came to be paid and spent, how the partnership acts and the record pledge of 2016 were passed, and what the United States says it receives in return through the cooperative programmes. Each later part takes one strand of this spine into a single agency.
