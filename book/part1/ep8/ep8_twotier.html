Two Tracks, with Names

A justice system with two tracks does not need two sets of laws. It needs one set, applied through discretion, by people who make different choices depending on who is in front of them. Two other cases show the same structure from different sides.

Dennis Hastert, a former Speaker of the House, was sentenced in 2016 to fifteen months in prison. The charge was illegally structuring bank withdrawals, which he had used to pay hush money to a man he had abused as a boy. The abuse itself could not be charged: the statute of limitations had long expired. "I can't sentence you for being a child molester," the judge told him. [cite:ep8_npr_hastert2016] The law reached a powerful man only through the money he used to keep a secret.

In Belgium in 1996, Marc Dutroux was arrested for abducting girls and young women, four of whom were found dead. Inquiries found that police forces had failed to share intelligence and had searched his house without finding the hidden cellar. When the investigating judge was removed from the case for attending a fundraising dinner for the victims' families, more than 300,000 people marched silently through Brussels on 20 October 1996 demanding reform. After Dutroux briefly escaped in 1998, two ministers resigned and the country's police were reorganised. [cite:ep8_brussels_dutroux] It is a resistance point worth remembering: public pressure forced structural change. Persistent claims that powerful people protected Dutroux are a separate matter, and this book does not adopt them.

Maxwell after conviction

Ghislaine Maxwell is the only person convicted in connection with Epstein's network. She was sentenced to twenty years for sex trafficking of a minor and held in a low-security prison in Tallahassee. In late July 2025 the Deputy Attorney General, Todd Blanche, formerly the president's personal defence lawyer, interviewed her himself over two days under a grant of limited immunity. Senators wrote that it was highly unusual, if not unprecedented, for the department's second-ranking official to do so. On 1 August, about a week later, she was moved to Federal Prison Camp Bryan in Texas, a minimum-security camp. The Bureau of Prisons gave no reason. [cite:cnn_maxwell] [cite:pbs_maxwell] [cite:whitehouse_sen]

That move cuts against the Bureau's own default rule: prisoners convicted of sex offences carry a public-safety factor that requires at least a low-security prison, unless the Bureau's central office waives it. Documents said to show such a waiver for Maxwell circulated in August 2025; the Bureau said it could not vouch for them, and who approved any waiver has never been disclosed. [cite:ep8_snopes_bryan2025] Blanche later said the move was justified by threats to her life. [cite:hill_booker] Members of Congress have since relayed whistleblower accounts of special treatment at the camp, and other prisoners who spoke to the press were reportedly moved to harsher prisons; the Bureau says it forbids preferential treatment and does not discuss individual prisoners. [cite:ep8_cnn_raskin2025] [cite:ep8_pln_bryan2026]

The clemency thread runs alongside. In July 2025 the president said he had not considered pardoning her but that "I'm allowed to". [cite:ep8_nbc_trump_pardon2025] In February 2026, subpoenaed by the House Oversight Committee, she refused to answer questions, and her lawyer said she would speak only if granted clemency. [cite:ep8_abc_maxwell2026] On 19 May 2026 Blanche told senators he would not recommend a pardon. [cite:ep8_rollcall_blanche2026] In July 2026 the Senate passed, unanimously, a resolution opposing any clemency. [cite:ep8_lavoce_senate2026] In August a federal judge rejected her bid to overturn the conviction. [cite:ep8_abc_maxwell2026] As of 31 October 2026 no clemency has been reported.

The book records the sequence and the rule, and goes no further. No document shows who decided the transfer or why, and nothing shows a bargain. What the record does show is the structure in miniature: the one person convicted, moved below the floor the rules set for everyone else, by a decision no outside body reviews, with the final say on her sentence resting in an unreviewable power held by a man named in the same files.
