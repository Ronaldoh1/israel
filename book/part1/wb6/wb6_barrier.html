The Barrier

The route. Israel began building the barrier during the second intifada. Its constructed and planned length is 713 kilometres, more than twice the length of the Green Line, because it does not follow the line. Eighty-five percent of its route runs inside the West Bank, and when it is complete it will cut off about 9 percent of the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, on its western side. [cite:wb_ocha_barrier2022]

Israel's case. Israel built the barrier, it told the International Court of Justice, for one purpose: to "effectively combat terrorist attacks launched from the West Bank," in an intifada in which about 1,000 Israelis were killed by Palestinians, [cite:gz_wiki_intifada2] and it described the barrier as temporary. [cite:wb_asil_icj2004]

The Seam Zone. Between the barrier and the Green Line lies what Israel calls the Seam Zone. About 11,000 Palestinians who live there need permits or special arrangements simply to live in their homes, and about 150 Palestinian communities elsewhere own farmland inside it. Farmers reach their land through 69 gates in the barrier, most of them opened only during the olive harvest, for limited hours. The army rejected 31 percent of farmers' permit applications in 2014 and 67 percent in 2020, and the number of applications fell by 77 percent between 2014 and 2021. [cite:wb_ocha_barrier2022]

The World Court. On 9 July 2004 the International Court of Justice, by fourteen votes to one, advised that the barrier's construction inside the occupied territory was contrary to international law, that it should be dismantled and reparation made, that the Fourth Geneva Convention applies to the territory, and that the settlements "have been established in breach of international law." The UN General Assembly endorsed the opinion, 150 to 6. The one dissenter, the American judge Thomas Buergenthal, agreed that the segments built to protect settlements violated humanitarian law, but found the Court's treatment of Israel's right of self-defence legally doubtful. [cite:wb_asil_icj2004] The opinion was advisory; the barrier was not removed, and by December 2022, 65 percent of its route had been built. [cite:wb_ocha_barrier2022]
