Relations with Israel since 1994

When the Holy See and Israel exchanged ambassadors in 1994, the Fundamental Agreement signed in Jerusalem on 30 December 1993 had already set the work that was still to be done. Its Article 12 said negotiations would continue on the agenda agreed in July 1992, and its Article 10(2) committed both sides to negotiate in good faith "a comprehensive agreement" on the Catholic Church's unresolved property, economic and fiscal questions, to begin within three months of entry into force and to aim for a deal within two years. [cite:vt1_va_fa1993] The agreement entered into force on 10 March 1994; the nunciature and the Israeli embassy to the Holy See opened, and the protocol of 15 June 1994 raised relations to full diplomatic level, with Israel's first ambassador, Shmuel Hadas, presenting credentials on 29 September 1994. [cite:vt18_wiki_hsisrael] [cite:vt1_jvl_protocol1994] Thirty-two years later, as of October 2026, those relations are intact, the second agreement promised in 1993 has been signed, and the third has not.

The 1997 Legal Personality Agreement

The first follow-up treaty dealt with a practical problem: under Israeli law it was not clear which Catholic bodies existed as legal persons able to own land, sign contracts or go to court. The Agreement signed in Jerusalem on 10 November 1997 settled it. Israel undertook to give "full effect" in Israeli law to the legal personality of the Catholic Church itself, with the Holy See recognised as its sovereign authority, and of the Eastern Catholic patriarchates, the Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem, the dioceses, the Assembly of Catholic Ordinaries of the Holy Land, the Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land and the religious orders, which were listed in annexes. Ordinary transactions in Israel were to be governed by Israeli law, while questions of internal authority were left to canon law; the Church's entities were deemed to consent to be sued in the proper Israeli court; and the treaty was to enter into force on the later of the two parties' notifications of ratification. [cite:vt18_jvl_lpa1997] Neither the 1993 nor the 1997 agreement has been incorporated by a vote of the Knesset. [cite:vt18_wiki_hsisrael]

The 1997 agreement is not a dead letter. On 31 December 2025, when Israel's new registration rules for aid organisations took effect, the Latin Patriarchate said that Caritas Jerusalem was an "Ecclesiastical Legal Person" whose status Israel had recognised through the 1993 and 1997 agreements, that it had not applied for re-registration with the Israeli authorities, and that it would continue its work in Gaza, the West Bank and Jerusalem. [cite:vt18_caritasjr_2025]

The economic agreement that was never signed

The third treaty has not been concluded. Talks on property, taxes and economic matters opened on 11 March 1999, more than three years after the deadline the 1993 text had set. [cite:vt18_asianews_2009] The issues were reported consistently over the following years: formal recognition of the tax exemptions the Church had long enjoyed in practice; protection of Church property, especially the holy places; and restitution of property lost since 1948, such as a church at Caesarea expropriated and razed in the 1950s. [cite:vt18_asianews_2009] The joint commission met briefly in 1994 and then not again until 2004; talks broke down on 10 December 2009, when Israel's deputy foreign minister Danny Ayalon spoke of a crisis, with the room of the Last Supper on Mount Zion reported as a sticking point. [cite:vt18_wiki_hsisrael] In June 2012 the commission said the talks had reached a "final stage", but the Catholic bishops of the Holy Land said "a signing is not imminent"; the Holy See's negotiator, Msgr Ettore Balestrero, said the talks would not address East Jerusalem or the West Bank, after Palestinian officials objected that a reported draft could concede them. [cite:vt18_ncreg_2012]

The last joint communiqué found for this volume is dated 14 June 2017. It reported a plenary meeting in Vatican City the day before, led by Msgr Antoine Camilleri for the Holy See and Minister Tzachi Hanegbi for Israel, which hoped for a "rapid conclusion of ongoing negotiations". [cite:vt18_press_2017] No signed economic agreement, and no later plenary communiqué, was found in searches made in October 2026; the matter is therefore open as of October 2026.

Three papal visits

Three popes visited Israel after 1994. John Paul II's pilgrimage in March 2000 is remembered for the prayer he left in the Western Wall on 26 March. It said "we are deeply saddened by the behaviour of those who in the course of history have caused these children of yours to suffer", and committed the Church "to genuine brotherhood with the people of the Covenant". [cite:vt18_va_jp2wall2000] Benedict XVI's visit in May 2009 was cooler in its reception. At Yad Vashem on 11 May he said the victims "will never lose their names", but Rabbi Israel Meir Lau, chairman of the Yad Vashem Council, said something was missing: the pope had said "killed" rather than "murdered" and offered no apology. Benedict did not enter the museum hall whose caption then criticised Pius XII's wartime silence, and the visit came a few months after he had lifted the excommunication of a bishop who denied the Holocaust. [cite:vt18_csm_bxvi2009]

Francis's visit of 24–26 May 2014 gave both sides gestures. He entered the West Bank directly from Jordan, spoke of the "state of Palestine" in Bethlehem and prayed with his forehead against Israel's separation barrier on 25 May. The next day he became the first pope to lay a wreath at the grave of Theodor Herzl, stopped at Prime Minister Netanyahu's request at a memorial to victims of terrorism, and at Yad Vashem asked for "the grace to be ashamed". He invited Presidents Shimon Peres and Mahmoud Abbas to pray for peace at the Vatican the following month, and both accepted. [cite:vt18_jta_francis2014] A pope who had refused Herzl's movement in 1904 had now honoured its founder's grave; the meaning of that fact for this volume's question is weighed in [scene:vt19_predictions_tested|Part Nineteen].

Nuncios, ambassadors and friction

The Holy See has had seven nuncios in Israel since 1994. Andrea Cordero Lanza di Montezemolo, who had been apostolic delegate in Jerusalem since 1990, became the first; he was followed by Pietro Sambi (1998–2005), Antonio Franco (2006–2012), Giuseppe Lazzarotto (2012–2017), Leopoldo Girelli (2017–2021) and Adolfo Tito Yllana (2021–2026). [cite:vt18_wiki_nunciature] On 22 January 2026 Pope Leo XIV appointed Archbishop Giorgio Lingua, previously nuncio to Croatia, as nuncio to Israel and apostolic delegate in Jerusalem and Palestine, and he holds the post as of October 2026. [cite:vt1_aleteia_lingua] The nunciature is in Jaffa, in Tel Aviv, not in Jerusalem; the double title of nuncio to Israel and delegate in Jerusalem and Palestine keeps the Holy See's position that the city's status is still to be settled. [cite:vt18_wiki_nunciature]

The frictions are on record on both sides. Israel objected to the Holy See's 2015 treaty with the State of Palestine, discussed in the next section. [cite:ts11_wiki_hspal] It objected to papal and curial words on Gaza from November 2023, set out in [scene:vt18_gaza_words|the third section]. When Francis died on 21 April 2025, Netanyahu's office took three days to issue condolences and only Israel's ambassador to the Holy See attended the funeral; President Isaac Herzog attended Leo XIV's inauguration. [cite:vt6_wiki_conclave2025] [cite:vt18_efe_herzog2025] On Palm Sunday, 29 March 2026, during the US–Israeli war on Iran that had begun on 28 February, Israeli police stopped Cardinal Pierbattista Pizzaballa, the Latin Patriarch, and the Custos of the Holy Land, Fr Francesco Ielpo, from reaching the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, citing the risks of a mass-casualty incident during the war; the Latin Patriarchate said its heads had been prevented from celebrating the Palm Sunday Mass there "for the first time in centuries". [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] [cite:vt18_rte_palmsunday2026] Italy's prime minister and France's president condemned it; the same night Netanyahu said Pizzaballa had "full and immediate access", and Herzog said the incident stemmed from security concerns over Iranian missile attacks. [cite:vt18_rte_palmsunday2026] [cite:vt18_cl_reversal2026] No separate Holy See statement on the incident was reported. [cite:vt18_cl_reversal2026]

Cooperation continued alongside. Leo XIV received Herzog on 4 September 2025, with Cardinal Parolin and Archbishop Gallagher, and the Holy See's statement asked for a return to negotiations, the release of all hostages, a permanent ceasefire and humanitarian access, and called a two-state solution "the only way out of the current war". [cite:vt1_vn_herzog2025] On Good Friday, 3 April 2026, Leo telephoned Herzog; the Holy See's readout said "the need to reopen all possible channels of diplomatic dialogue was reiterated", while Herzog's office said they had discussed Iran's missile threats and the safety of Christians in Lebanon. [cite:vt18_crux_herzog2026] The pattern from 1994 to 2026 is a relationship that both states kept, argued inside, and used.
