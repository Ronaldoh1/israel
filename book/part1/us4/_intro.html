Part Four compares how the two states police, prosecute, imprison and detain. It sets the decentralised American police system, with more than 17,000 agencies, beside Israel's single national force, and asks what each records about the people its police kill and how often officers answer for it. It then follows incarceration rates in both countries from 1980 (Israel from 1972) to the latest data, explains why Israel's series breaks in 2006 and again after 7 October 2023, and looks at how cases end, overwhelmingly in plea bargains in both systems. It compares the American death penalty, executions by year since the 1999 peak, with Israel's last execution, in 1962, and its 2026 law for military-court convictions. Finally it turns to detention without trial: Israel's administrative detention and military courts, the treatment of children, and the American cases of Guantanamo and immigration detention.
