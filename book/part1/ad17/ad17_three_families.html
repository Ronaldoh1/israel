Three Families: What Comes In, What Goes Out

Every earlier part of this book followed one mechanism at a time: the wage, the mortgage, the premium, the loan, the tax. A household meets all of them at once, in one budget. This section runs three real budgets through those mechanisms. They are not invented people. They are the average households in three fifths of the income ladder, as recorded by the Bureau of Labor Statistics' Consumer Expenditure Survey for 2024, the government's own survey of what American households earn and spend: the middle fifth, the fourth fifth (the upper-middle) and, below them, the two lower fifths. For each it asks what comes in, what taxes and essentials take, what is left, and what one ordinary shock would do to what is left. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] [cite:ad17_bls_cesan24] The same budgets appeared once before in this library, set beside Israel's; here they stay on the American side and go further into each line. [scene:us22_families|Three Families (US System vs Israel System)]

Where the figures come from

The survey counts "consumer units": a family, a person living alone and paying their own way, or people sharing major expenses. It sorts them by income before taxes and splits them into five equal groups of about 27 million units each; in 2024 the middle fifth ran from $57,452 to $94,511 and the fourth fifth from $94,512 to $155,925. [cite:ad17_bls_cesan24] [cite:ad17_bls_gloss] The figures are averages (means) within each fifth, not medians, so a few large incomes pull them up; [scene:ad2_how_to_measure|Part Two] explains the difference. Income before taxes includes wages, self-employment income, Social Security and pensions, interest, rent, unemployment benefits and public assistance. [cite:ad17_bls_cesan24]

Three definitions matter for reading what follows. First, spending includes the sales and excise taxes paid at the till, and a homeowner's housing cost includes mortgage interest, property tax and insurance, but not repayments of the mortgage itself: the survey's glossary says principal "is not included in homeowner costs but is repayment of a loan". Repayments on goods already bought on credit are excluded in the same way. [cite:ad17_bls_gloss] So whatever a family has "left over" in this survey must still cover the principal on its mortgage and its other loans. Second, a line called "personal insurance and pensions" holds the worker's Social Security deductions and contributions to retirement plans; this section counts the Social Security part as a tax and the rest as part of what is left over, because it is saving. [cite:ad17_bls_cesan24] Third, the Bureau did not publish income taxes for 2024, because the outside tax model it uses was not updated. [cite:ad17_bls_cesan24] The income tax below is therefore estimated by applying each fifth's 2023 ratio of income tax to income (6.3 per cent for the middle fifth, 10.4 per cent for the fourth), and the payroll tax is computed at the legal 7.65 per cent of wages and 15.3 per cent of self-employment income. [cite:us22_fred_incafttxlb0104m] [cite:us22_fred_incafttxlb0105m]

The middle family

The middle fifth's average household has 2.5 people and 1.4 earners, and took in $74,474 before tax in 2024, $55,348 of it in wages and $3,349 from self-employment. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] The arithmetic of its year runs as follows. Income tax at 6.3 per cent is about $4,665; payroll tax is about $4,234 on the wages and $512 on the self-employment income, together $4,747. Taxes and compulsory contributions therefore take about $9,412, or 12.6 per cent. Spending, leaving out the insurance-and-pension line, comes to $60,652, or 81.4 per cent of income. What remains is $74,474 minus $9,412 minus $60,652: about $4,410, or 5.9 cents of every dollar. About $1,500 of that is already committed to private pensions and life insurance, so the money free for mortgage principal, debt repayment, emergencies and anything else is closer to $2,900 a year, about $240 a month. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] [cite:us22_fred_incafttxlb0104m]

Where the middle family's money goes follows the mechanisms of earlier parts. Housing takes 32.4 cents of each dollar, shelter alone 20.5 cents, in a period when home prices had risen from about 3.2 times median household income in the 1990s to 5.0 times in 2024. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] [cite:us14_jchs_pti] [scene:ad6_price_and_rent|Part Six] Transport takes 15.7 cents, health care 7.6 cents. The health figure counts only what the household pays; behind it sits the employer's share of the premium, $20,832 of an average $28,303 family policy in 2026, paid out of compensation the worker never sees as wages. [cite:ad8_kff_ehbs2026] [scene:ad8_costs_and_coverage|Part Eight]

The taxes the table does not show

The 12.6 per cent above counts only income and payroll taxes. Sales taxes are already inside the spending figures and property taxes inside housing. The Institute on Taxation and Economic Policy, a research group that favours progressive taxation, estimated that in 2024 the middle fifth paid 26.4 per cent of its income in all federal, state and local taxes together, the fourth fifth 27.2 per cent and the lowest fifth 17.1 per cent; counting state and local taxes alone, the lowest fifth paid 11.3 per cent and the top 1 per cent 7.2 per cent, because sales and property taxes take a larger share of small incomes. [cite:ad11_itep_wp2024] [cite:ad11_itep_wp7] The federal side runs the other way. The Congressional Budget Office found that average federal tax rates fell for every income group between 1979 and 2023, most of all for the lowest fifth, whose individual income tax rate was minus 11 per cent in 2023 because refundable credits paid it more than it owed. [cite:ad11_cbo_dist2023] [scene:ad11_who_pays|Part Eleven]

The upper-middle family: where the promise works

The fourth fifth's average household, 2.9 people with 1.8 earners, took in $121,548. Income tax at 10.4 per cent is about $12,661 and payroll tax about $8,561, so taxes take $21,222, or 17.5 per cent; spending outside the insurance-and-pension line is $78,038; about $22,288, or 18.3 per cent, is left, of which about $3,400 goes to private pensions and life insurance. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] [cite:us22_fred_incafttxlb0105m] For this family the taught version largely holds. It saves real money every year, and families in the matching income band had a median net worth of $362,500 in 2025, up from $336,100 in 2022 after inflation; 78 per cent of the Federal Reserve's upper-middle income group owned their home. [cite:ad17_scf25_inc] Several mechanisms also tilt its way: the tax exclusion for employer health insurance saves $347 per $1,000 of premium in the 22 per cent bracket against $254 in the 12 per cent bracket, and 90 per cent of workers earning $100,000 to $150,000 take part in their employer's 401(k) where one is offered, against 74 per cent of those earning $30,000 to $50,000. [cite:ad8_tpc_esi] [cite:ad10_vanguard_has26] The record therefore does not show a machine that leaves every family with nothing; it shows one in which the margin widens sharply as income rises.

The lower fifths

Below the middle, the survey's budgets cannot be balanced at all. The lowest fifth reported $16,658 of income and $35,046 of spending, 2.1 times as much; the second fifth reported $42,925 and spent $50,054, 1.17 times as much. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] [cite:ad17_fred_q2inc] [cite:ad17_fred_q2exp] These are not typical working families: the lowest fifth averages 1.6 people and half an earner, and the second fifth's head of household averaged 56 years old, so both include many retirees and people living alone. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] [cite:ad17_fred_q2age] Spending above income can mean savings being drawn down, help from relatives, borrowing, income in kind or income not fully reported; the survey does not say which, so this book does not compute a "left over" for them. What the figures do show is the weight of essentials: the lowest fifth's housing costs equal 87 per cent of its reported income and its health spending 21 per cent, and the second fifth spends 11.2 per cent of income on health and 19.6 per cent on transport. [cite:us22_bls_ce24_q] [cite:ad17_fred_q2health] [cite:ad17_fred_q2trans] The families in the matching income bands held a median net worth of $14,400 and $77,100 in 2025. [cite:ad17_scf25_inc]

For a lower-income family that works, the federal system adds as well as takes. In 2026 the earned income tax credit pays up to $7,316 to a family with two children, and the child tax credit is $2,200 a child, $1,700 of it refundable; from October 2026 a family of three can receive up to $808 a month in food assistance. [cite:ad11_cftd_rp2532] [cite:ad11_fns_fy27] These supports fall away as earnings rise, which is how a raise can leave a family worse off, the benefits cliff described in [scene:ad11_benefits_cliff|Part Eleven]. [cite:ad11_atl_what]

One shock

A margin of $4,410 a year is the middle family's whole defence against the unexpected. Nationally, 63 per cent of adults said in 2025 that they would cover a $400 emergency expense with cash or its equivalent, down from 68 per cent in 2021, and 8.6 per cent of families were paying more than 40 per cent of their income in debt payments. [cite:ad9_shed2025] [cite:ad17_scf25_inc] Three ordinary shocks show what the margin can and cannot absorb; each uses a documented national figure and states its assumption.

The same shocks land differently up and down the ladder. The upper-middle family's $22,288 margin absorbs the $10,600 maximum with half the year's savings left. The lower fifths have no margin to absorb anything, which is where the medical debt carried by 41 per cent of adults in 2022, on the broadest definition, and the denials and collections of [scene:ad8_medical_debt|Part Eight] begin. [cite:ad8_kff_debtsurvey] A job loss also costs the family its employer health cover; since the enhanced Marketplace subsidies expired at the end of 2025, a couple just above $84,600 a year can again face the full premium. [cite:ad8_astho_ptc] [cite:ad11_hi_cliff] Unemployment insurance is weakest where it is needed: the OECD's modelled replacement for a worker on the average wage, 38 per cent of net income, is near the bottom of the range of 33 to 60 per cent that the series has shown for the United States since 2001. [cite:us22_oecd_nrr_usa100]

Over five years

The middle family's margin has also moved with prices. Measured simply as income before tax minus the survey's total spending, which already includes Social Security deductions but not income tax, the middle fifth kept 13.2 per cent of income in 2020, when pandemic spending fell, 5.6 per cent in 2022, when prices rose fastest, and 10.2 per cent in 2024. The fourth fifth kept between 24.6 and 26.0 per cent in every year. The second and lowest fifths spent more than their reported income in every year. [cite:ad17_fred_q1inc] [cite:ad17_fred_q1exp] [cite:ad17_fred_q2inc] [cite:ad17_fred_q2exp] [cite:ad17_fred_q3inc] [cite:ad17_fred_q3exp] [cite:ad17_fred_q4inc] [cite:ad17_fred_q4exp]

What the record shows and does not. It shows the budgets of the average household in four fifths of the income ladder, from the government's own survey, with every adjustment stated, and the arithmetic of three documented shocks. It does not show any single family: averages blend owners and renters, the young and the retired, and where spending exceeds income at the bottom, the survey does not separate the possible explanations. The 2024 income taxes are estimates, the net-worth bands match the survey fifths only approximately, and the shocks are illustrations built on national figures, not measurements of what happened to a household.
