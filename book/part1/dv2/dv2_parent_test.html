The Parent Test: What London Gave, and What It Withheld

This volume calls London the financial parent of Israel. The sections of this part have now set out the record on which that title has to stand or fall, and it is time to weigh it. The test is simple to state. A parent, in the sense this volume uses, is a power that supplied something the new state could not have had without it, at a time when no one else was supplying it. Measured that way, London has a strong claim. But the claim belongs to some of the four Londons named at the start of this part and not to others, and the record also contains a decade in which London worked against the child it had helped to start.

What the record supports. Four contributions are well documented. First, the promise: the British government issued the Balfour Declaration in November 1917, before it held Jerusalem, and addressed it to Lord Rothschild in London. [cite:wb_bl_addms41178] [cite:w_sinai_palestine] Second, the legal frame: the British government took the Mandate, which wrote that promise into international law and bound the governing power to facilitate Jewish immigration and settlement. [cite:rs_a22_frus] [cite:mandate_palestine_wiki] Third, the legal vehicles: the movement's bank, its land fund and its fundraising arm were each formed as English entities in London, in 1899, 1907 and 1920, and the land fund stayed an English company until 1953. [cite:jct_wiki] [cite:dv2_wiki_jnf] [cite:keren_hayesod_wiki] Fourth, the plumbing: for twenty-one years Palestine's currency was issued by a board appointed in London, backed by reserves held in London, and carried in the country by a London bank. [cite:wiki_palestine_pound] [cite:dv2_unccp_assets] [cite:dv2_unccp_banking] The sources used here record no other power supplying any of the four in those years, though the Mandate itself was conferred through the League of Nations.

What the record limits. Four limits are equally documented. First, the Corporation of the City of London, the medieval body that governs the Square Mile, appears in none of the decisive acts. It did not draft or send the Balfour letter, it did not hold the Mandate, it did not incorporate the movement's companies, and it did not manage Palestine's currency. [cite:wb_jc_rothschild2017] Its documented engagement with Jewish causes before 1948 is humanitarian, at the Mansion House in 1840 and 1882 and at Guildhall in 1890, and its documented engagement with Israel since is a Lord Mayor's trade mission of the kind it sends to many countries. [cite:dv2_je_mansion_house] [cite:dv2_jn_bowman_2018] Second, the Rothschild house's documented role is one of address, finance and civic standing, not command; the gold fix it chaired was a five-firm committee, and its later Israeli philanthropy is told in other volumes. [cite:dv2_ra_goldfix] Third, the British state's support was not continuous: from May 1939 it capped immigration, in 1947 it handed the question to the United Nations and abstained on partition, and it recognized Israel only in January 1949. [cite:rs_rd4_wp_cojs_text] [cite:rs_rd5_un_yearbook_181] [cite:rs_rd5_langham_exit] Fourth, London's financial plumbing was cut, not handed over: the Treasury order of 22 February 1948 blocked Palestine's sterling balances, and the new state's first notes were printed in New York. [cite:dv2_unccp_assets] [cite:jhom_anglo_palestine_notes]

The verdict. The record supports calling London the financial and imperial parent of the Jewish state, on two conditions. The London that did the parenting was the British government and the legal and financial infrastructure of the London market, not the Corporation of the City of London. And the parenting was concentrated in the years 1917 to 1939, when it was decisive, and was followed by a decade of restriction and withdrawal. Both conditions matter. The first rules out the popular claim that a sovereign "Crown corporation" in the Square Mile directed the creation of Israel; [scene:bc_s01|the library's first examination of the City] and [scene:bc_s06|its examination of the three-cities claim] found no document for that, and nothing in this part's newer research changes the finding. The second explains why the volume needs two more parents. By 1948 the child London had launched was seeking money, arms and recognition elsewhere, and found the first and third of them in Washington.

One further point belongs here, because it bears on how the three parents relate to each other. Nothing in the sources used for this part shows the British government, the London market or the Corporation coordinating with the Holy See or the United States government on Palestine as a joint undertaking. What the record does show is Britain handing the question to the United Nations without recommendations in 1947 and abstaining, while Washington voted for partition and recognized the state within minutes. [cite:rs_rd5_cojs_bevin_1947] [cite:trumanlibrary_recognition] That is the record of two governments acting on different judgments, not of a single design. The parts that follow test the same question for Rome and Washington on their own evidence.

The gaps that remain are named so that later research can close them. The sources used here do not show how the blocked sterling balances were settled after 1948, or what share of the Jewish Colonial Trust's capital was raised in Britain as against Eastern Europe. They do not give the founders' own reasons for incorporating in London. And they do not measure how much of the Zionist movement's spending between 1920 and 1948 passed through London at all, as against the American campaigns told in [scene:us_s01|an earlier volume]. Each of those would sharpen the verdict. None of them, on the evidence so far, would reverse it.
