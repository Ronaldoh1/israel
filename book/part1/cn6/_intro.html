Part Six covers 1208 to 1536, when the question shifted from which books were scripture to who was allowed to read them. It begins with the crusade against the Cathars, paid for with confiscated lands and crusade indulgences, and the council at Toulouse in 1229 that forbade lay people to own the Old and New Testaments and ordered vernacular translations destroyed. It then follows the same principle to England: the Constitutions of Oxford, the burning of Wycliffe's bones and the execution of William Tyndale.
