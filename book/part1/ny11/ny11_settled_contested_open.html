The architecture

Read across the whole record, three things recur. The first is the doctrine: a security wall, not a negotiated border, as the basis of the state's relations with the Palestinians, stated by Jabotinsky in 1923 and restated by Netanyahu a century later. [cite:gi2_jvl_ironwall] [cite:ny_ynet_ironwall] The second is the audience: from the embassy in 1982 to the joint session of 2015 and the White House in the Trump years, an American public and an American government cultivated directly, sometimes over the heads of their own leaders. [cite:ny_wiki_bibi] [cite:wiki_netanyahu_2015_congress_address] [cite:ny_aje_2010video] The third is the coalition: in a parliament where no party approaches a majority, small partners on the far right gained ministries and vetoes, and a leader on trial had reasons of his own to keep them. [cite:bgv_toi_foiled] [cite:ny_wiki_trial]

Two cautions apply. The record does not show that every decision was made for personal legal reasons; it shows a leader whose personal and political interests ran together, which his critics and his defenders read differently. And much of what is described here belongs to institutions, the Knesset, the cabinet, the courts, the army and the voters, and not to one man; the same structure that kept him in power for so long also produced the courts that checked him and the election that took away his majority.
