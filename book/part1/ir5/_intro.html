Part Five tests the claim at the centre of this volume: that Benjamin Netanyahu warned for more than three decades that Iran was weeks, months or a few years from a nuclear bomb, and that the bomb never came. It begins with his American years, from MIT and the Boston Consulting Group to the Jonathan Institute's terrorism conferences, the embassy in Washington and the United Nations, and asks what he warned about then. It sets out every dated statement on Iran's nuclear timeline that could be traced, from the reported Knesset remark of 1992 to the war statements of 2025 and 2026, each with its words, date and venue, and notes where versions differ or no source could be found. It then scores them against what followed, from the US intelligence estimates and IAEA findings to the testimony of 2025, and separates the estimates that carried a date from the warnings that were conditional, before setting Israel's argument that pressure and sabotage delayed the programme beside the critics' charge of crying wolf.
