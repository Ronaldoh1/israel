Portugal and Goa

Portugal's Jews were baptised by force. On 5 December 1496 King Manuel I, negotiating a marriage with a daughter of Ferdinand and Isabella, ordered them to convert or leave; in 1497 he stopped them leaving and had them baptised. [cite:vt34_wiki_manuel] Their descendants, the New Christians, were the main target when, after years of lobbying by King John III, Pope Paul III created the Portuguese Inquisition on 23 May 1536. The grand inquisitor was named by the pope but chosen by the king. The first auto da fé was held in 1540. The historian Fortunato de Almeida counted 31,457 sentences between 1536 and 1794, with 1,183 people executed in person. [cite:vt34_wiki_portinq] The tribunal was abolished on 31 March 1821. [cite:vt34_wiki_portinq]

In Goa the destruction began before the tribunal. Portugal took the city in 1510. An order of 1541 directed that the temples be destroyed and their property given to the missionaries, and a royal order of 1546 from John III forbade Hindu worship in Portuguese territory. By 1566 about 160 temples had been razed on the island of Goa itself, and a royal letter of 1569 stated that all the temples in the colony had been burned and razed. [cite:vt34_wiki_goainq] Communities carried their gods out. In 1560, by the government of Goa's own account, devotees of Shri Mangesh carried the linga by night across the border into territory ruled by the sultans of Bijapur, where the Mangueshi temple at Priol stands today. [cite:vt34_goagov_mangueshi]

The Goa tribunal opened in 1560. Its first targets were New Christians, as in Lisbon; over time the largest category became converts and their descendants accused of keeping Hindu customs. Estimates put the share of the accused charged with Hindu practice at about three-quarters. [cite:vt34_wiki_goainq] The historian Célia Tavares notes that the tribunal also often prosecuted people who had never been baptised, which she calls "quite disconcerting at first sight" for a court built to judge Christian heresy. [cite:vt34_alzulaij_tavares] Language came under the same rule: in 1684 the viceroy, at the urging of Franciscan friars, ordered the people of Goa to give up Konkani for Portuguese within three years. [cite:vt34_wiki_goainq]

Most of the tribunal's archive was burned by Portuguese officials after it was abolished in 1812, so every count is a reconstruction. Seventy-one autos da fé are recorded; the most cited count of executions is 57 people burned in person and 64 in effigy. Far more often, Tavares finds, sentences were whipping, compulsory attendance at Mass, or years in the galleys. [cite:vt34_wiki_goainq] [cite:vt34_alzulaij_tavares] The same colonial church acted against an older Christianity too: at the Synod of Diamper in 1599 the archbishop of Goa forbade the Saint Thomas Christians of Kerala to keep or read Syriac books "written by Nestorian heretics", and books judged heretical were burned. [cite:vt34_hmml_diamper] [cite:vt34_gedsh_diamper] The full account is in [scene:vt34_goa|The Vatican Architecture: Goa].
