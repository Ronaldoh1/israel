Gawker, Citizenship and the Roth

Three episodes in Thiel's life became public only because someone else revealed them: a media company destroyed in court by a lawsuit he had secretly paid for, a New Zealand citizenship he had held for six years before a newspaper reported it, and a retirement account worth billions that came to light through leaked tax records. Each is lawful on the record as it stands. Each also shows how a private fortune can be used against a publisher, for a second passport or inside the tax code, in ways the public learns of late. This section sets out what the documents show, his own explanations where he gave them, and what remains disputed.

Gawker, 2007–2016

In 2007 Valleywag, the Silicon Valley gossip site of the New York media company Gawker Media, published a post headlined "Peter Thiel is totally gay, people." [cite:de9_wiki_thiel] [cite:de9_snopes_close] Thiel had not discussed his sexuality publicly. In May 2009, asked about the site, he said: "Valleywag is the Silicon Valley equivalent of Al Qaeda," and "I think they should be described as terrorists, not as writers or reporters." [cite:de9_sfist_aq]

By his own account he decided "several years ago" to act, and his first step was to hire lawyers to look for people with claims against Gawker whose cases he could finance. [cite:de9_tc_gawker] One was Terry Bollea, the wrestler known as Hulk Hogan, who sued after Gawker published parts of a video of him having sex. In March 2016 a Florida jury found Gawker liable for $130 million and its founder Nick Denton personally for $10 million, a total of $140 million. [cite:de9_aba_settle] Gawker Media filed for bankruptcy in June 2016; the Spanish-language broadcaster Univision bid $135 million for its other sites, and Gawker.com itself announced on 18 August 2016 that it would close within a week. [cite:de9_snopes_close]

Thiel's role became public in May 2016, two months after the verdict. He confirmed to Andrew Ross Sorkin of the New York Times that he had paid about $10 million towards Bollea's suit and others. [cite:de9_wiki_thiel] [cite:de9_fora_gawker] "It's less about revenge and more about specific deterrence," he said, and he accused Gawker of a "unique and incredibly damaging way of getting attention by bullying people even when there was no connection with the public interest." [cite:de9_tc_gawker] He called the funding one of the "greater philanthropic things that I've done," and, asked whether there were other cases, said: "It's safe to say this is not the only one." [cite:de9_fora_gawker] [cite:de9_tc_gawker] In a New York Times opinion article on 15 August 2016 he framed the campaign as a defence of privacy: "It is ridiculous to claim that journalism requires indiscriminate access to private people's sex lives." [cite:de9_cnn_oped] On 2 November 2016 Gawker's bankruptcy estate settled with Bollea for at least $31 million and agreed to remove the Hogan story and two others at issue in suits brought by the lawyer Thiel had backed. [cite:de9_aba_settle]

Everything in this sequence was lawful. Paying for another person's lawsuit is permitted in the United States, the verdict was a jury's, and the bankruptcy followed from the size of the judgment. What the episode documents is a capability: a single wealthy person, acting in secret through a third party's case, removed a publisher he had called a terrorist organisation. Forbes wrote that it "could serve as a blueprint for any billionaire who wants to lay siege against a media outlet." [cite:de9_snopes_close] His stated motive, deterrence of a publisher that harmed private people, and the critics' reading, revenge against the site that had outed him, are both on the record; the dispute is graded in [scene:de9_claims_vs_record|the claims against the record].

Citizenship: New Zealand, Germany, Malta

In January 2017 the New Zealand Herald reported that Thiel had become a New Zealand citizen six years earlier. The government then released his file. [cite:de9_ap_nz] It showed that he had been granted citizenship in 2011 under the "exceptional circumstances" clause, which lets a minister waive the residence rules, and had taken the oath at a private ceremony at the New Zealand consulate in Santa Monica, California. [cite:de9_ap_nz] The Department of Internal Affairs later confirmed he had spent "a total of 12 days" in the country, over four trips, before the grant, against a general rule of 1,350 days over five years. [cite:de9_nbr_12days] The minister who approved it, Nathan Guy, cited his investments in New Zealand start-ups through a local venture fund, including the accounting company Xero, and a "million-dollar donation" to relief after the Christchurch earthquake; Guy said afterwards that "exceptional circumstances are there for a reason." [cite:de9_ap_nz] [cite:de9_nbr_12days] The opposition MP Iain Lees-Galloway called the decision "entirely about money." [cite:de9_ap_nz] No document shows a bargain between the donation and the grant; the file shows only that officials weighed both.

In his application Thiel wrote that he had found "no other country that aligns more with my view of the future than New Zealand," and that it would give him "great pride to let it be known that I am a New Zealand citizen." He also said he would need to go on living in the United States to run his companies. [cite:de9_ap_nz] He did not make the citizenship known; it became public through the newspaper. Asked about it in 2017, he declined to comment. [cite:de9_nbr_12days] The 2011 documents also showed that he remained a citizen of Germany, his country of birth; whether he still holds German citizenship is not on the public record since. [cite:de9_ap_nz]

In October 2022 the New York Times reported that Thiel was "in the process of acquiring at least his third passport," from Malta, under its programme selling citizenship to investors, and that the residence he had listed there was a flat advertised as a holiday rental. He declined to comment. [cite:de9_mt_malta] No grant of Maltese citizenship has been documented. Reports in 2026 that Argentina was considering residence or citizenship for him, which Javier Milei's spokesperson denied, are covered in [scene:de9_politics_and_argentina|the next section]. [cite:de9_nn_nyt]

The Roth, 1999–2019

On 24 June 2021 ProPublica, drawing on confidential Internal Revenue Service records, reported how Thiel's retirement account had grown. A Roth IRA lets savers put in money that has already been taxed and then pays no tax on gains; contributions were capped at $2,000 a year when Thiel opened his, and the account is limited to people below an income threshold. [cite:de9_pp_roth] In January 1999, when he earned $73,263, under the $110,000 limit, he bought 1.7 million PayPal founders' shares at $0.001 each through the account. Its custodian reported it at $1,664 at the end of 1999. By the end of 2019 it held about $5 billion across 96 sub-accounts, built from PayPal, Palantir, Facebook and other holdings. [cite:de9_pp_roth] In 2018 he moved it to Rivendell, a family trust company in Las Vegas named after the elves' refuge in Tolkien. [cite:de9_pp_roth]

The legal question is the price. Assets may not be bought into an IRA below their true value, and PayPal's own securities filing said founders' shares had been sold to employees "below fair value." [cite:de9_pp_roth] The tax-law professor Victor Fleischer told ProPublica: "That's a huge scandal." A tax lawyer said the valuation could not be supported as reasonable, while the founder of the account's custodian called the purchase "very legitimate." [cite:de9_pp_roth] The IRS opened an audit of Thiel in 2011; the records do not show whether it covered the Roth, and it closed years later with no additional tax owed. [cite:de9_pp_roth] The account had been part of his case to New Zealand: a letter in his 2005 residency application stated that he had bought his PayPal shares "through his Roth IRA." [cite:de9_pp_roth] His spokesman accepted ProPublica's questions and did not answer them. [cite:de9_pp_roth]

The finding, then, is narrow and should be stated narrowly. No court or tax authority has found the account unlawful, and the one audit on record closed without additional tax. Tax specialists quoted by name dispute whether the founding share price could be defended, and the account turned a savings vehicle meant for middle earners into one of the largest tax-free fortunes on record. Both statements are documented; neither cancels the other.
