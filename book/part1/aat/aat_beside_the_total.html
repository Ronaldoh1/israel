Beside the Total

Several other sums are often quoted next to the aid, and some writers add them in. They are real, and each is documented, but none is government aid given to Israel in the same sense, so this part shows them beside the total and does not add them to it. Each is converted to today's dollars from its own year in the same way.

Loan guarantees

Twice Congress has guaranteed loans that Israel raised from private lenders. In 1992 it authorized $10 billion over five years for absorbing immigrants; after deductions for Israeli spending on settlements, about $9.2 billion was available. In 2003 it authorized $9 billion; Israel has issued about $4.1 billion of bonds under it, and the authority now runs to 2030. Israel paid the fees and has never defaulted, so the guarantees have cost the United States nothing; they would cost it only if Israel failed to pay. [cite:us20_jvl_loans] [cite:tal_crs2025_web] They are a guarantee of borrowing, not money given.

Israel Bonds bought by American states and counties

American state treasuries, counties and cities bought more than $1.7 billion of Israeli government bonds in the nine months after 7 October 2023, as Follow the Money on this shelf describes. [cite:pm_icij_bonds] These are investments of public money, decided by state and local officials, which Israel repays with interest. The searches made for this part found no state or county grant of money to Israel itself; state money reaches Israel's treasury as loans, through these bonds.

The cost of American forces

American forces in the region since October 2023 have been paid for by the United States, not given to Israel. The Costs of War project at Brown University estimated those operations at $9.65 to $12.07 billion from October 2023 to September 2025: the Red Sea and Yemen, the defence of Israel against Iranian missiles, and the strikes on Iran in June 2025. It described them as serving American, Israeli and allied interests, not as spending for Israel alone. [cite:tal_bilmes_2025] The war with Iran that began with joint American and Israeli strikes on 28 February 2026 added more; one analyst, Stephen Semler, estimated from Pentagon budget documents and reporting that American forces fired about 310 interceptors defending Israel between 28 February and 21 May 2026, at a cost of about $5.2 billion. [cite:tal_aj_iranwar] [cite:tal_semler_2026] These are estimates, and how much of the cost of a regional war to count against one ally is a matter of judgement, which is why they stand apart.

Private American giving

Private Americans have given to Israeli causes since before the state existed. In 1946 the United Palestine Appeal, the arm of the United Jewish Appeal that funded the Jewish community of Palestine, spent $52 million; of the $150 million the United Jewish Appeal raised in 1948, $74.7 million went to it. [cite:tal_jta_uja1946] [cite:tal_jta_uja1948] A study of tax records found $2.06 billion raised by 774 American charities for Israeli organizations in 2007; another found more than $2.65 billion in 2021. [cite:us20_brandeis_philanthropy] [cite:us20_ejp_giving2021] This money is private and tax-deductible, not appropriated; no study has counted it year by year, so no total is given.

Why other totals differ

Readers will meet other totals, and the differences have causes. The Research Service's $174 billion is in the dollars of each year and ends in FY2025. [cite:tal_crs2025_web] Its inflation-adjusted figure, about $298 billion for 1946 to 2024 in 2024 dollars, is drawn from State Department and USAID aid data, which the Research Service notes leave out the missile-defence money the Defense Department provides; it does not state the price index used. [cite:tal_crs2025_web] Converted with consumer prices, the same years in 2024 dollars come to about $387 billion; with the price index for the whole economy, which rises more slowly than consumer prices, about $343 billion. [cite:tal_mpls_cpi] [cite:tal_fred_gdpdef] This part uses consumer prices because they measure what a dollar buys for the people who pay the taxes, and gives the other result so that the choice is visible.

What is not counted

Three things are left out because no reliable figure exists. Israel receives its military financing in one sum early in the year and keeps it in an interest-bearing account, as Part Three describes; the interest is a benefit for which this part found no public yearly figure. [cite:aa_crs_2023] Weapons drawn from American stocks are counted only where Congress paid to replace them, as in 2024. And private giving is shown only for the years a study measured it. The total here is therefore a floor for what the United States government has provided, not a ceiling.
