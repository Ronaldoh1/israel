This part follows Peter Thiel's thinking rather than his life, which [scene:de9_formation|Part Nine] tells. It reads his main texts whole and in order: "The Straussian Moment," a long essay on Leo Strauss, Carl Schmitt and René Girard first delivered at a 2004 conference he funded; the 2009 Cato Unbound essay that declared freedom and democracy incompatible; the 2014 case for monopoly in Zero to One; and what he has said and signed about democracy since 2016. It then explains Girard's theory of imitation and the scapegoat, and the old Christian idea of the katechon, the force that holds back the end of the world, which Thiel carried from his 2004 essay to an on-the-record New York Times interview in June 2025 and to private lectures on the Antichrist in San Francisco, Paris, Cambridge and Rome. The last section sets his arguments beside what his companies and his money have done, with his own explanations, and tests the book's worst-case reading against the result.
