Part Ten set out the law. The Fourth Geneva Convention forbids an occupying power to transfer its own civilians into occupied territory, and Israel's own legal adviser warned in 1967 that settlement would breach it. [cite:wb_fmep_art49] [cite:wb_gorenberg_meron] Israel's position is that the territory is not occupied in the classic sense and that the settlements are legal. [cite:wb_cow_israelview] [cite:wb_wiki_levy] The Security Council in 2016, the World Court in 2004 and 2024, and the General Assembly in 2024 said otherwise; the ICC opened an investigation in 2021. [cite:wb_wiki_2334] [cite:wb_asil_icj2004] [cite:wb_jurist_icj2024] [cite:wb_ahram_unga2024] [cite:wb_aj_icc_timeline] Each stopped at a documented point: advisory opinions without enforcement, non-binding resolutions, Israel's refusal to comply, and US positions that changed from Hansell to Reagan, from Pompeo to Blinken, and from settler sanctions to sanctions on the Court. [cite:wb_jvl_hansell] [cite:wb_tablet_forty] [cite:wb_axios_blinken] [cite:wb_jurist_iccsuit] Part Eleven turns to the split between the West Bank and Gaza.
