The Wider Banking Web

JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank and Bank of America are the banks that paid. They were not the only ones that held Epstein's money or money around him, and the others have drawn almost no attention because no victim has sued them.

A suspicious-activity report JPMorgan filed with the Treasury soon after Epstein's death, unsealed in the Virgin Islands' lawsuit after the New York Times and the Wall Street Journal asked for it, listed one account at Goldman Sachs and three at HSBC's private bank in Switzerland. It flagged transfers but gave no amounts. Goldman says it ended the relationship and moved his assets out in 2010; HSBC declined to comment. [cite:ep3_reuters_goldman2025] A report of this kind is a bank's alert, not a finding.

The Wall Street Journal traced the rest. Wells Fargo held Epstein or Epstein-related accounts in his later years and declined to comment. TD Bank says it closed accounts held by his lawyer and his accountant in 2019. FirstBank Puerto Rico called him a "legacy relationship" it inherited when it bought Chase's Virgin Islands operations in 2002. [cite:ep3_wsj_firms]

Where his money went

The same reporting shows money flowing out of Epstein into some of the best-known funds in finance. Each firm says it had no personal relationship with him or that its senior people did not know him. [cite:ep3_wsj_firms]

None of these investments is evidence of wrongdoing by the firms. They matter for a different reason. A man whose income has no documented source was placing tens of millions of dollars in mainstream funds for a decade after his conviction, and the records of where that money came from and where it went next sit with banks that have never been asked about it in public.
