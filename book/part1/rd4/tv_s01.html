How Did They Get Here? Citizens, Ships and a Law of 1938

The clandestine ships ran from Europe to Palestine; the movement's political turn in these years ran across the Atlantic, to the United States, and a practical question comes with it. Who were the people running the American side of the movement, how did leaders from Palestine and Europe reach them, and under what law did any of it operate? A common assumption is that foreign agents entered the country in secret and worked outside the law. The record points the other way on almost every count.

The leaders of American Zionism were Americans. Louis Brandeis, Rabbi Stephen Wise and Rabbi Abba Hillel Silver were United States citizens, and their advocacy was the legal political activity of citizens. Some of the movement's figures from Palestine had American lives of their own. Golda Meir, then Golda Meyerson, grew up in Milwaukee and emigrated in 1921. She later became Israel's prime minister. [cite:w_golda] David Ben-Gurion, expelled from Ottoman Palestine, lived in New York from 1915 to 1918. There he recruited volunteers for the Jewish Legion, and there he married. [cite:w_bengurion]

Travel was by sea. Visiting leaders such as Chaim Weizmann crossed the Atlantic by ocean liner, as most travelers did. Regular transatlantic passenger flights began only in 1939, with Pan Am's flying boats. [cite:w_boeing314]

The legal frame changed in 1938. Before that year no American law required people working for foreign principals to register at all. Congress passed the Foreign Agents Registration Act in 1938. [cite:doj_fara] The gap before it is real and documented. Anyone who acted for a foreign principal before 1938 could not have been breaking a registration law, because none existed.

Whether the law applied, and to whom, became a real question only later. The Bergson group was watched by the FBI, and that story is told in [scene:fa_s01|who Washington watched]. The first test of the act against the movement's main American apparatus came in 1962, with a Justice Department order to the American Zionist Council ([scene:s11a|the FARA order]). Both came after the law existed; before 1938 there was none to apply.

This short account leaves some things open. The record here does not say how often the visiting leaders crossed or who paid for their travel.
