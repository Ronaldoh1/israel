2024: The Shadow War Comes Into the Open

For more than a decade, neither government had attacked the other's territory openly and said so. That changed in 2024, during the Gaza war, in a sequence of strikes and counter-strikes that lasted seven months. Each step was announced, or soon became known, and each side gave its reasons in public.

On 1 April 2024 an air strike destroyed Iran's consulate building in Damascus. Seven Iranian officers were killed, among them Brigadier General Mohammad Reza Zahedi, who had commanded the IRGC's Quds Force in Lebanon and Syria until 2016, and his deputy, Mohammad Hadi Hajriahimi. A member of Hezbollah also died. Israel did not confirm the strike and said it had no comment. The US National Security Council said the United States had played no part and had not known about the strike in advance. Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei said Iran would make the attackers "regretful." [cite:ir8_ap_consulate]

On the night of 13–14 April Iran attacked Israel directly from its own territory, in an operation it called True Promise. The IDF spokesman, Daniel Hagari, said Iran had launched 170 drones, more than 30 cruise missiles and more than 120 ballistic missiles. Israeli officials said 99 per cent had been intercepted; Hagari said none of the drones or cruise missiles had entered Israeli territory, and that the ballistic missiles that got through caused "minor damage only" to a base that stayed operational. [cite:ir8a_ap_apr14] [cite:ir8a_ynet_hagari] The Israeli military credited the United States, Britain and France with help, and Jordan shot down projectiles crossing its airspace. A seven-year-old girl was badly wounded by fragments. [cite:ir8_gs_tp1] The Pentagon said US forces, among them the destroyers USS Arleigh Burke and USS Carney, had destroyed more than 80 one-way attack drones and at least six ballistic missiles. [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] The IRGC called the attack a response to "the crime of the Zionist regime in the attack on the consular section." Iran's UN mission cited the right of self-defence under Article 51 of the UN Charter and declared that "The matter can be deemed concluded," while warning of a "considerably more severe" response to any further Israeli "mistake." [cite:ir8_gs_tp1]

Israel answered on 19 April with a deliberately small strike. A US official told ABC News that Israeli jets had fired three missiles at a radar site in Isfahan province linked to the air defences around Natanz. Satellite images later showed damage to an S-300 radar at an air base there, and the IAEA said Iran's nuclear sites were undamaged. Iran's foreign minister, Hossein Amir-Abdollahian, said that downed "mini-drones" had caused no damage. In Israel the national security minister, Itamar Ben-Gvir, posted a single word, "Weak." Blinken said the United States had not been involved in any offensive operation. President Biden had reportedly told Netanyahu after the April attack to "Take the win." [cite:ir8_usip_apr19]

The next blow fell in Tehran itself. At about 2 a.m. on 31 July 2024 Ismail Haniyeh, the head of Hamas's political bureau, was killed with his bodyguard in Neshat, an IRGC guesthouse compound in the north of the city. He was in Tehran for the swearing-in of the new president, Masoud Pezeshkian. A few hours earlier Israel had killed the Hezbollah commander Fuad Shukr in Beirut. [cite:hz7_aj_shukr] The New York Times reported, from five Middle Eastern officials, that a bomb hidden in the room about two months earlier had been detonated remotely. Three Iranian officials told the paper the killing was a "tremendous embarrassment" for the IRGC, which ran the guesthouse. [cite:ir8_toi_haniyeh] The IRGC's own account, issued on 3 August, was that a "short-range projectile" carrying about seven kilograms of explosives had been fired from outside the building with American support. The United States said it "was not aware of or involved in" the killing. [cite:ir8_aj_haniyeh] Israel said nothing until 23 December 2024, when the defence minister, Israel Katz, told an audience of ministry staff that Israel would behead the Houthis' leaders "just as we did to Haniyeh, Sinwar and Nasrallah in Tehran, Gaza and Lebanon." [cite:ir8_newarab_katz]

Iran's promised retaliation was delayed for two months. During that time Israel detonated thousands of pagers and radios carried by Hezbollah members in Lebanon and, on 27 September, killed Hassan Nasrallah in Beirut. The same strike killed an IRGC general, Abbas Nilforoushan. [cite:hz7_toi_netanyahu_pagers] [cite:ir8_usip_oct1] [scene:hz7_pagers|The pagers] [scene:hz7_war|The autumn war] On 1 October Iran fired at least 180 ballistic missiles at Israel in two waves, in an operation it called True Promise 2. It named the killings of Haniyeh, Nasrallah and Nilforoushan as the reasons, and its UN mission called the attack a "legal, rational, and legitimate response." Air bases at Nevatim and Tel Nof were among the targets. No one in Israel was killed. A Palestinian man in Jericho died, and two Israelis were lightly hurt. The IRGC claimed 90 per cent of its missiles had hit their targets. Biden said the attack "appears to have been defeated and ineffective," and Netanyahu said Iran had "made a big mistake – and it will pay for it." [cite:ir8_usip_oct1] The US destroyers USS Bulkeley and USS Cole fired about a dozen interceptors. [cite:ow7_usni_oct2024] On 13 October the Pentagon announced that it was sending a THAAD missile-defence battery to Israel, with US soldiers to operate it. [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024]

Israel struck back on 26 October in an operation it called Days of Repentance. Aircraft attacked in three waves. The first hit radars and air defences, and the later waves hit missile and drone production sites, including at Parchin, east of Tehran. The IDF said it had struck 20 sites, and Hagari described "precise and targeted strikes." [cite:ir8_lwj_oct26] Israel warned that Iran would "pay a heavy price" if it responded. A US National Security Council spokesman called the strikes "an exercise in self-defence," and Iran reported "limited damage." [cite:ir8_afp_oct26] Iran's army at first reported two air-defence soldiers killed, then four. [cite:ir8_iw_oct26] A senior US official said the strikes "should be the end of this direct exchange of fire." [cite:ir8_lwj_oct26] As of October 2026 that hope had not held: in June 2025 Israel and then the United States struck Iran's nuclear sites. [cite:hz12_cow_2025] Part Ten takes up the war of June 2025. [scene:ir10_strikes|The Twelve-Day War]

The 2024 record allows some findings that the covert years did not. The order of events is documented. Each side said publicly what it was responding to, and each kept its stated reply limited: Iran declared the April matter "concluded," and Israel struck a radar site on 19 April. [cite:ir8_gs_tp1] [cite:ir8_usip_apr19] The United States was not a bystander. Its forces shot down Iranian drones and missiles in April and October and then sent soldiers to Israel to operate THAAD, while it said it had no part in Israel's offensive strikes. [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024] [cite:ir8_usip_apr19] Whether this amounts to the United States being drawn into the conflict is a question of interpretation, not of record, and Part Twelve weighs it. [scene:ir12_question|Does Iran want war with the US?] What the record does show is that it was Israel's strikes on the Damascus consulate and in Tehran, neither acknowledged at the time, that set off Iran's two direct attacks, on Iran's own stated account. [cite:ir8_gs_tp1] [cite:ir8_usip_oct1] Israel's stated position, as Katz put it in December 2024, was that it would strike the leaders of the groups at war with it wherever they were. [cite:ir8_newarab_katz]
