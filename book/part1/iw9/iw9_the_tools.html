The Tools

Three Israeli or Israeli-founded companies supply tools now used by American agencies at the border and inside the country.

Cellebrite, founded in Israel, makes devices that unlock phones and extract their data. In 2019 Immigration and Customs Enforcement awarded it a $28.2 million contract; in 2025 ICE announced an $11 million contract with its justification redacted, and planning documents describe a five-year contract of up to $100 million. [cite:fronteras_ice_cellebrite] [cite:reason_cellebrite_redacted] [cite:fedscoop_cellebrite_100m]

Cobwebs Technologies, founded by former Israeli intelligence officers and bought in 2023 by the Nebraska firm Penlink, makes Tangles, which searches the open, deep and dark web, and Webloc, which tracks phones in an area drawn on a map using advertising and location data. Citizen Lab reported Webloc handling data linked to up to 500 million devices; Meta banned Cobwebs in 2021 for accounts used against activists and politicians, which Cobwebs called false. In September 2025 ICE bought access to both for about $2 million. [cite:citizenlab_webloc] [cite:wiki_penlink] [cite:immpolicy_penlink_ice]

Elbit Systems of America, owned by Israel's largest arms maker, builds the surveillance towers that watch about 200 miles of the Arizona–Mexico border, including land of the Tohono O'odham Nation. [cite:elbit_ift_2019_pr] [cite:globes_elbit_towers]

Pointed at speech

The same period saw government data drawn together: the Internal Revenue Service agreed in 2025 to share taxpayer data with ICE, and Palantir was contracted to build tools that combine agency records. [cite:fedscoop_irs_ice_order] [cite:nextgov_palantir_megadb] Combined with Catch and Revoke, which reviewed social media for political content, the tools of investigation and the targets of enforcement now meet at protest. [cite:axios_catch_and_revoke]

The record shows the tools, the contracts and the policy. It does not show any individual case in which all five steps were used together against a protester, and that is not claimed here; it shows that the capability exists, assembled from commercial products, much of it built for use elsewhere first.
