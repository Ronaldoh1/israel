Rebuilding, and the Refugee Agency

After 2014. On 12 October 2014, seven weeks after the war of that summer ended, donors meeting in Cairo pledged about $5.4 billion, half of it for the reconstruction of Gaza. [cite:gz_un_cairo2014] Israel had barred cement, steel and other construction materials as "dual-use" goods that Hamas could use to build tunnels, and the money could only become buildings through a new arrangement, the Gaza Reconstruction Mechanism, overseen by a steering team of Israel, the Palestinians and the UN. It placed monitors and cameras at the points where materials were handed out, vetted every supplier and buyer, and kept a database of the materials entering Gaza that Israeli intelligence could see; it gave Israel's security concerns priority and the final say on large projects. Israel's foreign ministry said it would stop Hamas from diverting funds and materials "to violence." Critics said it managed the blockade rather than ending it and relieved Israel of its responsibilities, and Oxfam warned that at the rate materials were then entering, rebuilding could take more than fifty years. [cite:gz_aj_grm]

UNRWA. The UN Relief and Works Agency, created for the refugees of 1948 and described in Part One, had in 2023 a budget of $1.47 billion. In January 2024 Israel alleged that twelve UNRWA employees had taken part in the October 7 attacks; it also accused the agency of employing hundreds of staff in Gaza linked to armed groups, which UNRWA disputes. The United States paused its funding on 26 January 2024 and other major donors followed, though the European Union and Germany later resumed. In March 2024 Congress barred US funding to UNRWA until at least March 2025. An independent review led by the former French foreign minister Catherine Colonna reported in April 2024 that the agency had "many mechanisms in place" to keep its neutrality, along with gaps it recommended closing. [cite:gz_crs_unrwa]

The UN's internal investigators examined nineteen staff members in all. In one case they found no evidence, and in nine the evidence was insufficient; in the other nine, they found that the evidence, "if authenticated and corroborated," could indicate the staff "may have been involved," and UNRWA ended their contracts. [cite:gz_unrwa_oios] On 28 October 2024 the Knesset voted 92 to 10 for two laws barring UNRWA from operating in Israel and barring Israeli officials from contact with it.
