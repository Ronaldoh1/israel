The First Intifada and Madrid, 1987–1991

On 8 December 1987 an Israeli army truck collided with vehicles carrying Palestinian workers at the Jabalia refugee camp in Gaza, killing four; many Palestinians believed it deliberate, which Israel denied. The protests that followed became the First Intifada, an uprising of strikes, boycotts, stone-throwing and mass demonstrations against twenty years of military rule. It lasted until the Oslo agreement; about 1,200 Palestinians and about 100 Israelis were killed. [cite:wiki_jabalia] [cite:wiki_first_intifada]

Hamas was founded in Gaza in December 1987, at the start of the uprising, by Sheikh Ahmed Yassin and others from the Islamic association Mujama al-Islamiya, which Israel's military authorities had registered in 1979 while the PLO was their main concern. Its 1988 charter contains explicitly antisemitic passages, including an endorsement of the forged Protocols of the Elders of Zion; its 2017 document of principles dropped that language. [cite:wiki_mujama_al_islamiya] [cite:wiki_1988_hamas_charter] [cite:wiki_2017_hamas_charter] The Hamas Architecture follows its history.

In 1988 Jordan renounced its claim to the West Bank in favour of the PLO. [cite:wiki_israeli_occupation_wb] After the Gulf War of 1991, the United States and the Soviet Union convened a peace conference in Madrid in October 1991, the first time Israel negotiated face to face with Syria, Lebanon, Jordan and the Palestinians, who attended in a joint delegation with Jordan because Israel refused to sit with the PLO.
