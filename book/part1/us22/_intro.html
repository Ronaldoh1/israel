Part Twenty-One set every measure side by side and added no total. This part adds one, in the open. It first sets out how the scorecard grades, then follows three families, lower-income, middle and upper-middle, through each country's household survey, and grades both systems on 43 measures in twelve areas, from health and housing to a family-budget area, liberty, the economy and the gaps inside each society, against goalposts printed beside every row, and scores Israel twice: for its citizens, and for everyone it governs. Its last section adds the scores up, tests the totals under other choices, sets the household score beside what people who have lived in both countries describe, answers the questions readers ask most often, from who carries more debt and who works longer to which state is the more oppressive, and gives the volume's final assessment.
