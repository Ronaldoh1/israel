Part Three established that both systems hold competitive elections but count and finance them very differently: Israel elects national lists behind a threshold raised to 3.25 per cent, which in 2022 left 8.8 per cent of the vote unrepresented, and its turnout has fallen from 86.9 per cent in 1949 to about 70 per cent, while American turnout among citizens ranged from 52.2 per cent in a midterm to 66.8 per cent in a presidential year. [cite:us3_aje_cec2026] The ratings moved down in both: V-Dem demoted Israel to an electoral democracy in 2023 and the United States in 2025, and Freedom House's American score fell from 94 in 2010 to 81, while Israel's stood at 73, a score that excludes the occupied territories. [cite:us3_vdem2026] [cite:us3_fh_fiw2021] [cite:us3_fh_us2026] [cite:us3_fh_il2026] Israel funds its parties publicly and caps private gifts; American campaigns cost about $16 billion in 2024 and lobbying about $5 billion in 2025, with pro-Israel groups one interest among many that spend without limit through super PACs. [cite:us3_fh_il2025] [cite:us3_os_lobby2025] [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] On speech, Israel restricts by statute, through a military censor, a foreign-broadcaster law, a boycott tort and NGO disclosure rules, while American restrictions in 2025 came mainly by executive action that courts partly blocked; both fell in the 2026 press-freedom index.
