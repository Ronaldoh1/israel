Part Twenty-Three tells how Christians came to agree, and still disagree, on which books are scripture. Part Fourteen gave the milestones and showed that the Council of Nicaea did not choose the Bible; this part goes into the documents themselves. The first section reads the lists and letters that did the sorting, from Irenaeus of Lyon around 180 through the Muratorian fragment, Origen, Eusebius, Athanasius's Easter letter of 367, the African councils and Jerome, to the Council of Trent's binding vote of 8 April 1546. The second section names the books that were left out, who excluded each and in what document, and counts what was in circulation against what was kept, to test how much of the claim that the institution "suppressed most of the books" the record supports. The third sets the four Bibles in use today side by side: the Protestant 66, the Catholic 73, the Eastern Orthodox list of about 76 and the Ethiopian 81.
