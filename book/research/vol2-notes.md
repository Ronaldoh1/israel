# Volume II, Stage Two (1917–1922): research notes

Verified 2026-10-09. Every claim in `vol2.json` comes from a page fetched in this session. WebFetch returns a model-written summary of each page, not raw text, so quoted phrases are short. Check any quotation that will be printed against the original page before publication.

## 1. Faisal–Weizmann Agreement (3 Jan 1919)
**Agree:** The sources agree on the date and the signatories. They also agree on the main articles: Balfour guarantees, large-scale Jewish immigration, Muslim control of Muslim holy places, and British arbitration. Faisal made his assent conditional on Arab independence as set out in his 4 January 1919 memorandum.

**Disagree:** Pro-Zionist writers report that Weizmann told the UN in 1947 that the condition had been met. Other sources (EuroClio, the UN study) treat the agreement as void once France took Syria in 1920. Translations of the reservation also differ.

**Not verified:** I did not read the Avalon or UN primary text of Weizmann's 1947 UN testimony. The claim that Faisal later disowned the Frankfurter letter (attributed to Awni Abd al-Hadi and Rustam Haidar) appears only in Wikipedia's notes, so I left it out. I also could not find the 4 January 1919 memorandum itself.

## 2. Muslim-Christian Associations, Arab Congresses, Arab Executive
**Agree:** The Associations formed in late 1918. The First Congress met in Jerusalem in January 1919. The Third Congress (Haifa, December 1920) created the Arab Executive under Musa Kazim al-Husseini. The 1921–22 delegation went to London. Its exchanges with the Colonial Office are in Cmd. 1700, which I read as primary text.

**Disagree:** The sources give different dates for the Fourth Congress: 29 May–4 June, "May", or 25 June 1921. They also give different dates for the Lords vote (20 or 21 June 1922). Palestinian-side and other scholarship also differ on how far the British encouraged the Associations.

**Not verified:** I found no reference work that gives an exact founding date or place for the first Association (Jaffa and Jerusalem are often cited). The only source for the First Congress delegate count (27) is a Jewish Virtual Library page that credits Wikipedia. The 1922 Lords vote count and the Commons figure of 292–35 do not come from Hansard; the Commons figure is from Langworth only. The archive photo of the delegation is dated 1920, which conflicts with the 1921 departure.

## 3. Article 22 of the Covenant
**Agree:** The text of Article 22 (from the League's own 1945 study and the FRUS annotations). The 'A' mandate allocation at San Remo (25 April 1920). The Covenant entering into force on 10 January 1920.

**Disagree:** The UN study, Quigley and the 1922 Arab Delegation say the Palestine Mandate departed from Article 22's provisional independence and its "wishes of these communities" clause. The League itself called the Mandate "of a very special character", and de Blois treats its Jewish-national-home obligations as lawful and central. Wilde's position, which I saw only through a review, is that provisions varying Article 22 had no effect.

**Not verified:** I never loaded the Avalon page for the Covenant itself (blocked; see below). The text of Article 22 comes from the League's 1945 publication and FRUS instead. The UN study ("Origins and Evolution") was prepared under a UN committee on Palestinian rights, according to Wikipedia only, so it should be read as an advocacy-commissioned document.

## 4. Mufti appointment and Supreme Muslim Council
**Agree:** Kamil al-Husseini died in March 1921. Amin was not among the top three candidates. British officials (Samuel and Storrs) brought about Jarallah's withdrawal. Amin was appointed on 8 May 1921. The SMC Order of 20 December 1921 and its powers come from the primary text. Amin became SMC president on 9 January 1922.

**Disagree:** The month of his 1920 pardon (August or autumn). Whether he was "elected" to the SMC for life or "appointed"; the Order sets four-year terms. How to read British motives: securing calm, or manipulating the election.

**Not verified:** The vote figures. "Fourth with about 7%" rests on one opinion article citing Schechtman; I found no scholarly source that gives vote counts.

## 5. Haganah, Jabotinsky, Jewish Legion
**Agree:** I read the Palin Report (primary) for the April 1920 casualties and findings, including that Jabotinsky's prosecution was "ungenerous". Jabotinsky got 15 years and his 19 comrades 3 years each. Samuel's amnesty came in July 1920. The Haganah was founded in June 1920 at the Ahdut ha-Avodah conference at Kinneret. The Legion was down to 300–400 men by spring 1920.

**Disagree:** On the 15-year sentence, the Encyclopaedia Judaica says it was quashed and the Encyclopedia of World Biography says it was commuted with banishment. The Histadrut's control over the Haganah is described as "responsibility" by Slutsky and as "direct control" by Atlas.

**Not verified:** The exact date of Jabotinsky's arrest and sentencing (19 April 1920 is often cited). The final disbandment date of the Legion. Margolin's first name and rank.

## 6. 1922 Legislative Council
**Agree:** The Order in Council (10 August 1922) and its composition, read as primary text: 10 official and 12 elected members, the elected seats being 8 Muslim, 2 Christian and 2 Jewish. The Arab boycott of February 1923, with 213 of 809 secondary electors chosen. The failed Advisory Council. The Arab Agency rejected in October 1923.

**Disagree:** Mostly how each side explained its decision. Sources agree that the Zionist side accepted the framework and the Arab side rejected it.

**Not verified:** The text of the Order in Council of May 1923 that annulled the elections. The exact makeup of the 1923 Advisory Council: my one source says "eight Arabs, two Jews and two Christians", which looks garbled.

## 7. Third Aliyah
**Agree:** Most immigrants came from Russia and Poland and many were He-Halutz graduates. The sources agree on the founding of the Histadrut and Gedud ha-Avodah (1920) and of Ein Harod (1921). Annual figures from the UN study match the Mandatory's 1922 report (7,844 Jewish immigrants in 1922).

**Disagree:** The total size: 35,000 (Jewish Agency) or 40,000 (Jewish Virtual Library). Annual figures add up to about 29,900 from September 1920 to 1923.

**Not verified:** That the 1920 quota of 16,500 counted "heads of household" (COJS wording). Other accounts say immigrants, and this needs a check against Cmd. 1499 or the Albright text. Degania (1910) belongs to the Second Aliyah, not the Third. I found no reliable 1919 immigration figure.

## 8. Sharif Hussein after the war
**Agree:** He declined to ratify Versailles because of the mandates. The treaty negotiations ran from 1921 to 1924 and the British file (IOR/L/PS/10/881) records the main points: Lawrence's revision in November 1921, the abdication threat of 27 February 1922, Curzon's draft of April 1923 and the final offer of £50,000. He was proclaimed caliph in March 1924 and abdicated on 5 October 1924.

**Disagree:** Whether Britain kept its wartime promises. Lawrence (as reported by Richman) and the 1922 White Paper say it did and that Palestine was excluded from McMahon's pledge. The Arab Delegation and the Hashemite Arab Revolt site say it did not.

**Not verified:** I found no British primary text naming Palestine or Balfour as the sticking point. The only source for that is the Jordanian Royal Heritage site. The QDL transcriptions are automated, so check the folio images.

## 9. Mandate text and Transjordan memorandum
**Agree:** I checked Articles 2, 4, 6, 11, 22 and 25 against the Avalon text (summarised) and the UN and League quotations. The Transjordan exclusions of 16 September 1922 come from the League text. The League record of 29 September 1923 states that the mandates entered into force.

**Disagree:** Quigley (2026) argues that the Council's 1923 minute was in the future tense and that Britain lacked title until Lausanne came into force on 6 August 1924.

**Not verified:** WebFetch would not reproduce the full article texts, so the articles are paraphrased. Before publishing any paraphrase, check the wording against the Avalon page.

## 10. Allenby and Storrs
**Agree:** The surrender of 9 December 1917 and Allenby's entry on foot on 11 December. The proclamation's text, from Horne's 1923 *Source Records* via PalQuest. Storrs's offices and dates. The Pro-Jerusalem Society was founded in September 1918 and incorporated in October 1920. The ban on stucco and corrugated iron is from Ashbee's 1921 records, a primary source.

**Disagree:** Allenby's entry is read as "liberation" (Times of Israel) and as "occupation" (Al Jazeera opinion). Storrs is read as a preservationist (Israeli curators) and as personal, Orientalist rule (Murphy, Burnham).

**Not verified:** The "Jerusalem stone" facing rule. Ashbee documents only the stucco and corrugated-iron ban; the stone requirement rests on press reports. The attribution to Allenby of "the wars of the crusades are now complete" is unsourced ("reportedly") and should not be printed as fact.
