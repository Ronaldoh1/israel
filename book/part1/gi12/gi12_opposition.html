Israelis Against Expansion, and the Official Denials

The ideology traced in this volume belongs to named movements, parties and officials. Other Israelis, also named, have opposed it from the start. In 1967, while the Movement for Greater Israel was calling on the government to keep all the captured land, Labor minister Yigal Allon drew up a narrower plan, designed to include as few Arabs as possible in the areas Israel would claim; successive cabinets never formally adopted it. [cite:gi4_wiki_mgi] [cite:gi4_wiki_allon] This section sets out the organised opposition, the security officials and politicians who have argued against annexation, what polling shows, and what Israel's governments have said when accused of seeking a Greater Israel. The opposition is real, but on several points it is narrower than its supporters abroad often assume.

Movements and monitors

Peace Now began with a letter. On 7 March 1978, while Begin was negotiating with Egypt, 348 reserve officers and soldiers wrote to him criticising a government that "prefers settlements beyond the Green Line to terminating the historic conflict." They warned that ruling about a million Arabs would endanger Israel's Jewish and democratic character, and said that "real security can be achieved only when we achieve peace." [cite:gi12_pn_officers] [cite:gi12_ecf_officers] After the Sabra and Shatila massacre in 1982 the movement says it helped bring 400,000 people into the streets to demand an inquiry. [cite:gi12_pn_officers] On 10 February 1983 a grenade thrown at a Peace Now rally outside the prime minister's office in Jerusalem killed one of its activists, Emil Grunzweig. [cite:hz2b_wiki_grunzweig]

Since 1990 Peace Now's Settlement Watch has counted settlements, outposts and housing approvals, and much of the data in this volume comes from it. [cite:gi12_pn_officers] [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis] Other Israeli groups track the same ground and go to court. Yesh Din monitors police files on settler violence; Ir Amim, Bimkom and Peace Now are petitioners against the E1 plan east of Jerusalem. [cite:wb_yeshdin_2025] [cite:gi6_pn_e1tender] The recording of a June 2024 settlers' gathering at which Smotrich described "a separate civilian system" for the West Bank was supplied to the New York Times by a Peace Now researcher. [cite:gi6_bg_nyt_smotrich] Israeli monitors produced the core of the evidence against the policy they oppose. The West Bank volume covers this work in detail. [scene:wb7_watchers|The West Bank, Documented: the watchers]

Security chiefs and prime ministers

Some of the sharpest opposition has come from inside the security establishment. On 27 August 2019, twenty-five former senior security officials, among them former heads of the Mossad and the Shin Bet and three former advisers to Netanyahu, wrote to the US Congress. Any unilateral annexation, they said, "will put Israel's security and safety" at risk, and they backed a negotiated two-state outcome. [cite:gi12_jn_25chiefs] Two prime ministers rejected the idea in plain words. In November 2006 Ehud Olmert recalled Ben-Gurion's choice of 1949: "we chose a Jewish State without the entire land of Israel." [cite:gi3_jvl_olmert2006] At a cabinet meeting in September 2008 he said "the notion of Greater Israel no longer exists, and anyone who still believes in it is deluding themselves." [cite:gi1_ynet_olmert]

Parties and votes

Several Knesset votes on settlement and annexation measures have been close. The 2017 law to legalise settlement homes on private Palestinian land passed 60 to 52. [cite:gi12_ynet_reglaw2018] When the Knesset reopened four northern West Bank sites in March 2023, Benny Gantz said it was "wrong to return to northern Samaria" and Labor called it a "pre-annexation bill." Two of Gantz's own colleagues, Gideon Sa'ar and Ze'ev Elkin, voted for it. [cite:gi12_toi_repeal2023] On 22 October 2025 a bill to apply sovereignty to the settlements passed its preliminary reading 25 to 24. Likud boycotted the vote. Yair Lapid and Gantz voted for a second bill, on Ma'ale Adumim alone, which passed 32 to 9. [cite:gi6_toi_readings] The record of the mainstream opposition is therefore mixed. Its leaders opposed the broad sovereignty bill, and Gantz backed the 2020 court ruling against the settlement law, but Lapid and Gantz voted to annex the largest settlement near Jerusalem. [cite:gi12_toi_reglaw2020] On the eve of the 27 October 2026 election, Al Jazeera reported that neither Naftali Bennett nor Lapid supports a Palestinian state, and that Gadi Eisenkot is not committed to one. Of the leading contenders, only Yair Golan's Democrats treat a Palestinian state as a goal, and then "a distant, unspecified" one. [cite:gi12_aje_contenders] [cite:gi6_aje_dissolve]

What polling shows

Israeli polling separates two questions that are often merged abroad. Annexation does not command a majority. In the Tel Aviv University Peace Index of late July 2026, 59 percent of Israelis opposed annexing the territories to form a single state with limited Palestinian rights, and 28 percent supported it. In December 2024 the Institute for National Security Studies found 34 percent opposed to any annexation, with 21 percent for annexing the whole West Bank and 21 percent for the settlement areas only. [cite:gi12_jvl_polls] But a Palestinian state commands even less support. In the same 2026 poll 59 percent opposed one, and in September 2025 the Israel Democracy Institute found 70 percent calling a Palestinian state "undesirable" for Israel at that time. Asked to choose between the two, Jewish respondents preferred annexation by 42 percent to 26. [cite:gi12_jvl_polls] In April 2020, when annexation was on the cabinet's agenda, 45 percent supported it and 32 percent opposed. [cite:gi12_tml_idi2020]

The official denials

Israel's governments have repeatedly denied territorial aims beyond what they claim as security needs, and the denials vary in what they cover. On 8 November 1956 Ben-Gurion wrote that Israel had "never planned to annex the Sinai desert." [cite:rs_cw3_jvl_ike_bg] In June 2009 Netanyahu called the West Bank "the land of our forefathers" but accepted "a demilitarised Palestinian state" on conditions and said Israel had "no intention of building new settlements." [cite:gi5_aje_barilan] After Smotrich's Paris speech in March 2023 the Foreign Ministry restated Israel's recognition of Jordan's territorial integrity. [cite:gi8_ynet_mfajordan] When Netanyahu told i24NEWS in August 2025 that he was "very" attached to the vision of Greater Israel, his office and the Foreign Ministry, according to Haaretz, stressed that he had not used the words "Greater Israel" and was not responsible for the headlines. No on-record clarification by his office was found. [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] [scene:gi7_interview|Part Seven: the interview]

The denials over Syria have changed over time. On 9 December 2024 Foreign Minister Sa'ar called the move into the Syrian buffer zone "a limited and temporary step we took for security reasons." [cite:gi12_olj_saar] On 28 January 2025 Defence Minister Israel Katz said the army would stay on Mount Hermon and in the security zone indefinitely. [cite:hz10s_ih_hermon] On the West Bank the governing party's line has been a denial of timing, not of aim. Boycotting the October 2025 readings, Likud said "true sovereignty" would not come from "a show-off law for the protocol." [cite:gi6_toi_readings]

The Israeli opposition record establishes that expansion has never been a national consensus. Officers, former security chiefs, a former prime minister, human rights groups and, in the latest poll cited, a majority of respondents have rejected annexation, sometimes at personal risk. It also shows that rejecting annexation and supporting Palestinian statehood are different positions, and that by 2026 most of Israel's leading opposition figures held the first without the second.
