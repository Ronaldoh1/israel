Sovereignty on the Agenda, 2024–2025

After Donald Trump won the American election of November 2024, the annexation track moved from administration back to declaration. Over the following twelve months a minister set a year for it, drew a map for it and asked the cabinet to adopt it; the Knesset voted for it in principle and then advanced two bills; and the two governments whose agreement had suspended it in 2020, the United States and the United Arab Emirates, said publicly that it would not happen. This section sets those statements and votes side by side, each with the position of the government or party concerned.

"The year of sovereignty"

On 11 November 2024, at a meeting of his Knesset faction, Bezalel Smotrich declared "2025: the year of sovereignty in Judea and Samaria." He said he had instructed the Defense Ministry's Settlement Directorate and the Civil Administration, both now under his authority, to begin groundwork: "I have directed the start of professional work to prepare the necessary infrastructure." He said he had "no doubt" that Trump would support the move, and that "the only way to remove this danger from the agenda," meaning a Palestinian state, "is to apply Israeli sovereignty." The foreign minister, Gideon Saar, answered for the government: "A decision has not yet been made on the issue." The spokesman for the Palestinian president said the remarks confirmed Israel's intent to annex. [cite:gi6_aje_2025year]

The Knesset's motion, July 2025

On 23 July 2025 the Knesset voted 71 to 13 for a non-binding motion that declared the West Bank "an inseparable part of the Land of Israel" and called on the government to apply Israeli sovereignty to Judea, Samaria and the Jordan Valley. [cite:wb_toi_71_13] A motion of this kind creates no law and binds no minister; its weight is as a recorded count of members willing to vote for the principle. The West Bank volume gives the vote in its sequence. [scene:wb9_annex|The West Bank, Part Nine: the annexation votes]

The 82 per cent map, September 2025

On 3 September 2025 Smotrich presented the most specific proposal of the period: a map under which Israel would annex 82 per cent of the West Bank. The statement issued with it gave the principle as "maximum territory and minimum Arab population." Six Palestinian population centres, Ramallah, Nablus, Jenin, Tulkarem, Jericho and Hebron, would be left as islands inside the annexed area. Smotrich called on Netanyahu "to convene the government to approve applying sovereignty." The statement said that "preventing a Palestinian state is an Israeli consensus" and that "sovereignty takes the idea off of the global agenda." Asked about the Palestinian Authority's opposition, Smotrich said "we will destroy them just as we do Hamas." [cite:gi6_toi_82pct] The plan was a minister's proposal, not a government decision; no cabinet vote on it has been reported.

The same day the UAE answered. A senior Emirati official told Axios that annexation would be a "red line" for the UAE and "a death knell for the two-State solution," and that "the choice before Israel right now is annexation or integration." Lana Nusseibeh, an adviser to the Emirati foreign minister, said: "We trust that President Trump will not allow the Abraham Accords tenet of his legacy to be tarnished." The Emiratis had made Israel's ruling out of annexation a condition of signing in 2020. Israeli officials, speaking without attribution, replied that the 2020 commitment had been time-bound and had expired, and that no decision had been taken. [cite:gi6_axios_uae2025] The Times of Israel attributed the "red line" phrase to Nusseibeh herself. [cite:gi6_toi_82pct]

"No Palestinian state", September 2025

Netanyahu did not adopt Smotrich's map. On 11 September 2025, at Ma'ale Adumim, he signed a framework agreement to advance construction in E1, the land between that settlement and Jerusalem, and said: "We are going to fulfill our promise that there will be no Palestinian state; this place belongs to us." Smotrich, speaking at the same ceremony, said Israel would soon celebrate the application of sovereignty. [cite:gi6_toi_e1sign] The statement is a documented refusal of Palestinian statehood; it is not, in its words, a decision to annex. Part Seven reads Netanyahu's remark of August 2025 about a "vision" of Greater Israel, which belongs to the same weeks. [scene:gi7_interview|Part Seven: the "very much" interview]

Two weeks later, on 25 September 2025, as Britain, France, Australia and other states recognized a Palestinian state at the United Nations and some Israeli ministers proposed annexation in response, Trump said in the Oval Office: "I will not allow Israel to annex the West Bank. I will not allow it. It's not going to happen." He said he had spoken to Netanyahu: "It's been enough. It's time to stop now." [cite:gi6_ap_trump0925]

The preliminary readings, October 2025

On 22 October 2025, while Vice President JD Vance was visiting Israel, the Knesset gave preliminary approval to two bills. The first, sponsored by Avi Maoz of the Noam party, would require Israel to "apply its laws and sovereignty to the areas of settlement in Judea and Samaria," that is, to all the settlements. It passed by 25 votes to 24, with the backing of Otzma Yehudit, Religious Zionism and part of United Torah Judaism. The deciding vote was cast by Yuli Edelstein of Likud, who broke his party's boycott, saying "Israeli sovereignty in all parts of our homeland is the order of the day"; Likud removed him from the Knesset's Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee. The second bill, sponsored by the opposition leader Avigdor Liberman of Yisrael Beytenu, would apply sovereignty to Ma'ale Adumim alone. It passed by 32 votes to 9, and the opposition leaders Yair Lapid and Benny Gantz voted for it. [cite:gi6_toi_readings]

Likud boycotted both votes and called them opposition "trolling … aimed at damaging our relations with the US," adding that "true sovereignty will be achieved not with a show-off law for the protocol," and said it was working to create the political conditions for it. Each bill needed three further readings to become law. [cite:gi6_toi_readings] Vance called the vote "a very stupid political stunt." Trump, asked about it, said: "It won't happen," because "I gave my word to the Arab countries," and that Israel "would lose all of its support from the United States" if it annexed. [cite:wb_toi_trump_annex]

Who held which position

The record of these months does not show a single programme held by "Israel". It shows named actors in different places. Smotrich and Religious Zionism, Otzma Yehudit and Noam asked for sovereignty over the settlements or over 82 per cent of the land, now. Netanyahu stated that there would be no Palestinian state; Likud said it was working to create the political conditions for sovereignty, and refused the bills put to the Knesset in October. A part of the centre-right opposition, Liberman, Lapid and Gantz, voted for sovereignty over Ma'ale Adumim, the large settlement east of Jerusalem, while Lapid's party opposed the wider bill. [cite:gi6_toi_readings] The United States and the UAE, whose 2020 statement had paused the step, said it would not happen. Palestinian and Israeli opponents of annexation, including Peace Now, are given in Part Twelve. [scene:gi12_opposition|Part Twelve: Israeli opposition]

What the votes established is narrower than the headlines of either side. As a fact, a Knesset majority voted for the principle of sovereignty over the West Bank, and a bare majority of members present advanced a bill to annex every settlement. As a pattern, each formal step met the same check: the two governments whose agreement had suspended annexation in 2020 restated their objection, and the prime minister's party declined to carry the bills forward. [cite:gi6_axios_uae2025] [cite:gi6_ap_trump0925] [cite:gi6_toi_readings] Whether that check stops annexation or only changes its form is an argument, and the next section sets out the measures that answer it in practice.
