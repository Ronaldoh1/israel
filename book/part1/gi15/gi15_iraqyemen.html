Iraq and Yemen: Strikes at a Distance

Neither Iraq nor Yemen borders Israel, and no Israeli government has claimed land in either. [scene:gi8_claims|Part Eight: the claims tested] Both have nonetheless been struck by Israeli aircraft: Iraq in a series of raids on militia bases in 2019, Yemen from July 2024 in a war with the Houthi movement that began with Houthi missiles and drones fired at Israel. This section sets out each campaign with Israel's stated reason, the other side's stated reason, and the tolls as they were reported and by whom. It does not repeat the history of the Iraqi militias or of the Houthis, which the Hezbollah volume covers, along with the American wars against both. [scene:hz10_iraq|The Hezbollah Architecture: the Iraqi militias] [scene:hz10_houthis|The Hezbollah Architecture: Ansar Allah] [scene:hz10_redsea|The Hezbollah Architecture: the Red Sea war]

Iraq: the depot strikes of 2019

Between July and September 2019 a series of explosions hit bases and weapons depots of Iraq's Popular Mobilisation Forces, the umbrella of mainly Shia armed groups, several of them aligned with Iran. The first was on 19 July near Amerli, at a base that a senior militia official said housed Iranian and Lebanese advisers. Others followed: Camp Ashraf on 27 July; southern Baghdad on 12 August, where one civilian was killed and 29 injured; near Balad Air Base on 20 August; and a convoy near al-Qaim on the Syrian border on 25 August, where the militias said six were killed. [cite:gi15_wiki_iraq2019] No one claimed the attacks. On 23 August two American officials told the Associated Press that Israel had carried out at least the Amerli strike, which hit an Iranian weapons depot and, they said, killed two Iranian commanders. [cite:gi15_aje_iraq2019] [cite:gi15a_aje_iraq2019]

Israel's position was stated in hints and then plainly. Netanyahu said, "I don't give Iran immunity anywhere," and that he had given the security forces "a free hand". On 22 August he said, "We are working against Iranian consolidation — in Iraq as well." [cite:gi15_aje_iraq2019] [cite:gi15_wiki_iraq2019] Iraq's government closed its airspace to unauthorised flights on 13 August and ordered munitions depots moved out of cities. A deputy head of the militias blamed Israeli drones but, in the end, held Washington responsible and threatened retaliation. [cite:gi15_aje_iraq2019] [cite:gi15_wiki_iraq2019] The dead were mostly militia members and Iranian personnel. No consolidated count was published, and some tolls given for individual strikes rest on single, partisan sources.

After 2019 the record reviewed shows no further acknowledged or credibly attributed Israeli strike inside Iraq through October 2026. Some accounts say Israel struck Iranian-aligned militia positions in Iraq after 2023; no source documenting such strikes was found, and the claim is not adopted here. The Iraqi militias' war in those years was mainly with the United States, including the drone that killed three American soldiers at Tower 22 in Jordan on 28 January 2024. [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] [cite:hz10_at_farida] The statement sometimes made, that Israel has had no direct military engagement with Iraq since 1991, is therefore wrong for 2019; for the years since, the record found bears it out.

Yemen: the Houthi front

The exchange between Israel and the Houthis began from Yemen. From late 2023 the Houthis fired missiles and drones at Israel, and attacked shipping in the Red Sea, which they said was support for Gaza that would stop when the war on Gaza stopped. [cite:hz10_crs_if12587] [cite:hz10_aje_truce] On 19 July 2024 a Houthi drone killed one person in central Tel Aviv. Israel struck the port of Hodeidah the next day, its first strike on Yemen; an Israeli official called the port the main entry point for Iranian weapons. The Houthi-run health ministry first reported 80 wounded and later at least three killed and 87 injured. Defence Minister Yoav Gallant said the Houthis had attacked Israel more than 200 times. [cite:hz10_nbc_hodeidah2024] [cite:gi15a_aj_hodeidah]

The strikes grew heavier from December 2024. On 19 December Israeli aircraft hit the ports of Hodeidah, Ras Isa and Salif and two power stations near Sanaa, killing at least nine people, according to Houthi sources. On 26 December they struck Sanaa International Airport, ports and power stations while the head of the World Health Organization, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, was at the airport; a member of his plane's crew was injured. Reported tolls for that day ran from three or four dead, the figure given by the Houthi channel al-Masirah, to six dead and about 40 wounded, reported by CNN. [cite:gi15_wiki_yemendec24] Israel said it was answering Houthi missile and drone fire and hitting port and energy infrastructure used for military purposes. [cite:gi15_wiki_yemendec24]

In 2025 the pace rose again. After a Houthi missile struck near Ben Gurion Airport on 4 May, Israel hit Hodeidah and a cement factory on 5 May and disabled Sanaa airport on 6 May; Houthi-linked media reported four and three dead. Later strikes destroyed Yemenia's last airliner at Sanaa on 28 May and hit ports and power plants through the summer. On 24 August strikes on Sanaa killed 10 people and wounded 102, according to the Houthi health ministry, and on 28 August a strike killed the Houthi prime minister, Ahmed al-Rahawi, and several ministers. [cite:gi15_wiki_yemen2025] [cite:hz10_aje_rahawi] On 10 September Israeli strikes on Sanaa and al-Jawf killed 46 people and wounded 165, by the Houthi health ministry's count. Netanyahu linked them to a Houthi drone that had hit the arrivals hall at Ramon Airport. [cite:gi15_gv_ap46] [cite:gi15_gcap_35] Among the dead were 31 journalists and media workers at the offices of the 26 September and al-Yemen newspapers, according to the Committee to Protect Journalists. The IDF said it had struck the Houthis' "Public Relations Department". CPJ, which had confirmed at least nine of the dead as journalists and was investigating the rest, classed the killings as murders and called it the deadliest attack on the press anywhere in sixteen years. [cite:gi15_cpj_31] [cite:gi15a_cpj_31] A Houthi drone injured 22 people in Eilat in late September, and Israeli strikes on Sanaa around 25 September killed eight and injured 142, according to the BBC's report. [cite:gi15_wiki_yemen2025]

Counting the toll, and where it stands

No consolidated count of the people killed by Israeli strikes in Yemen exists in the sources reviewed. Almost every figure comes from the Houthi-run health ministry or Houthi media and could not be independently checked; a running total on one reference page, "61+ killed" for 2025, falls below the sum of its own per-strike entries and should be read as a floor. [cite:gi15_wiki_yemen2025] [cite:gi15_gv_ap46] [cite:gi15a_aj_hodeidah] Adding the lowest per-strike figures above gives at least 86 people killed by Israeli strikes in Yemen between July 2024 and September 2025, before counting the Houthi prime minister and ministers killed on 28 August and the unnumbered dead of other raids. The Houthi attacks on Israel in the same period killed one person in Tel Aviv and injured at least 22 in Eilat; by the count of Israel's defence minister the Houthis had attacked it more than 200 times by July 2024 alone, and most of their missiles and drones were intercepted. [cite:hz10_nbc_hodeidah2024] [cite:gi15_wiki_yemen2025] Israel's strikes were a small part of Yemen's dead. The UN Development Programme projected that the war since 2015 had caused about 377,000 deaths by the end of 2021, from all parties' violence, hunger and disease. In seven weeks of 2025 alone, Airwars counted at least 224 civilians allegedly killed by American strikes. [cite:hz10_aje_undp377k] [cite:hz10_airwars_rr]

The Houthis suspended attacks on Israel after the Gaza ceasefire of October 2025, as their stated condition implied. They resumed on 28 March 2026, a month into the Iran war, paused after that war's April ceasefire and fired briefly again in June. [cite:hz10_usni_crs2026] [cite:hz10_na_houthis2026] [cite:hz10_wiki_houthi2026] No Israeli strike on Yemen in 2026 was found in the sources reviewed. As of October 2026 Yemen's own war has reignited, with the Houthis fighting Saudi Arabia and Saudi-backed government forces. That war does not involve Israel. [cite:hz10_ahram_taiz] [cite:hz10_lwj_saudi2026] The documented Israeli record in Yemen is one of retaliatory and pre-emptive strikes on ports, power plants, an airport and the Houthi leadership, with a stated reason tied to Houthi fire on Israel. The Houthis' stated reason was the war on Gaza. Neither side's record contains a territorial claim.
