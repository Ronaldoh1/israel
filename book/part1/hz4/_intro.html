Part Four covers the fifteen years between Israel's pullback to a strip of southern Lebanon in 1985 and its withdrawal in May 2000, the period in which Hezbollah grew from a cluster of armed cells into the force that pushed Israel out. It sets out how the "security zone" worked and who paid for it, including the Lebanese militia Israel funded and armed and the Khiam prison that militia ran; how Hezbollah fought its Shia rival Amal and came out of the civil war as the only militia allowed to keep its weapons; the two Israeli air and artillery campaigns of 1993 and 1996, the shelling of the UN compound at Qana and the written rules that followed; the withdrawal, the dispute over the Shebaa Farms that kept the conflict alive, and the attacks abroad, in Buenos Aires and later in Bulgaria, that courts attributed to Hezbollah and that Hezbollah denies.
