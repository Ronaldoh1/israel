The Courts and the Inquiries

The genocide case. South Africa brought a case against Israel at the International Court of Justice under the Genocide Convention, alleging that Israel had committed genocidal acts with genocidal intent, shown by its conduct and by the statements of its leaders. Israel answered that it was entitled to defend itself, that the statements cited were not government or military policy, and that it had no genocidal intent. [cite:gz_ch_icj] On 26 January 2024 the Court found that "at least some of the rights claimed by South Africa" were plausible, and ordered Israel to take all measures within its power to prevent acts within the scope of the Convention, to prevent and punish incitement to genocide, and to enable humanitarian aid. It did not order a ceasefire, and it did not decide whether genocide was being committed, a question for the merits, which will take years. [cite:gz_oj_icj2024] [cite:gz_ch_icj] On 24 May 2024, by thirteen votes to two, it ordered Israel to "halt its military offensive and any other action in the Rafah Governorate, which may inflict on the Palestinian group in Gaza conditions of life that could bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part," to keep the Rafah crossing open and to admit UN investigators. [cite:gz_lawfare_rafah]

The arrest warrants. On 21 November 2024 the International Criminal Court issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant, finding reasonable grounds to believe they bore responsibility for the war crime of starvation as a method of warfare, for intentionally directing attacks against civilians, and for the crimes against humanity of murder, persecution and other inhumane acts, from at least 8 October 2023 to at least 20 May 2024. It issued a warrant on the same day for Mohammed Deif, the commander of Hamas's armed wing, for murder, extermination, torture, rape and hostage-taking on and after 7 October. [cite:gz_un_icc] [cite:gz_almon_icc] Netanyahu called the warrants "anti-Semitic" and the accusations "absurd and false"; Hamas welcomed them; President Joe Biden called them "outrageous" and said there was "no equivalence" between Israel and Hamas. [cite:gz_almon_icc]

The UN commission's finding. On 16 September 2025 the UN's independent Commission of Inquiry, chaired by the South African judge Navi Pillay, concluded that "Israel has committed genocide against Palestinians in the Gaza Strip," finding that four of the five acts defined in the 1948 Convention had been committed, and that President Isaac Herzog, Netanyahu and Gallant "have incited the commission of genocide." [cite:gz_un_coi2025] Israel's foreign ministry said it "categorically rejects this distorted and false report," called for the commission to be abolished, and said the report "relies entirely on Hamas falsehoods." [cite:gz_ctv_coi2025]

The genocide case is undecided. The International Court of Justice has issued orders but not a judgment on the merits; the arrest warrants have not been executed; and the UN commission's finding is the conclusion of investigators, not of a court. The record so far is of findings and orders on one side and Israel's rejection of them on the other, with the judgment still to come.
