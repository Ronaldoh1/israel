Part Fourteen closed with the texts and archives. This part turns to the most famous unsolved case connected with the Vatican: the disappearance in Rome of Emanuela Orlandi, a fifteen-year-old Vatican citizen, on 22 June 1983, and of Mirella Gregori, also fifteen, forty days earlier. It sets out what is known of both disappearances, the calls and letters that demanded the release of the pope's would-be assassin, and John Paul II's appeals. It then grades every major theory against the documents: the Ağca exchange, the Magliana gang and Enrico De Pedis's tomb, the Vatican bank and Archbishop Marcinkus, the sexual-abuse claims including the 2023 remarks about John Paul II, the disputed 2017 expense memo and the uncle. It ends with the investigations, from Rome's closures in 2015 and 2016 and the empty tombs of 2019 to the three inquiries opened in 2023, with their status as of October 2026. Theories are reported as theories. The families' statements are given as theirs, and every person named who has not been charged is presumed innocent.
