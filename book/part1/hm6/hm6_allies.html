Patrons, Not Masters

Hamas has taken money from states with different aims, and has not always done what they wanted. Iran, a Shia state, has armed and funded this Sunni movement through its Revolutionary Guards: in 2018 the US State Department said Iran gave "up to $100 million annually in combined support" to Palestinian groups including Hamas, Islamic Jihad and the PFLP-General Command, with training and weapons. [cite:hm_state_iran] Qatar hosted Hamas's political leaders and, from 2018, sent cash into Gaza with Israel's consent, as Part Five set out. [scene:hm5_qatar|The Qatari money] Israel's Shin Bet attributed Hamas's strength before October 7 largely "to the strategic buttress provided by Iran and the use of the funds that came in from Iran and Qatar". [cite:hm_fdd_shinbet]

Hamas also changed its words when it suited it. In 2017 it published a document of principles that spoke of a conflict with the Zionist project, not with Jews, and accepted a Palestinian state on the 1967 lines as a "formula of national consensus", without recognizing Israel or giving up armed struggle; it did not revoke the 1988 charter. Israel called it an attempt to fool the world. [cite:hm_jps_hroub2017] [cite:hm_cnn_2017doc] Part Two sets out both texts. [scene:hm2_charter|The charter]
