Cycle by Cycle, 2016–2026

Career totals favor long careers and expensive states. A fairer measure of what each election cost is the money a member received in a single two-year cycle. This section sets out what the record used here documents cycle by cycle since 2016, separately for each party and chamber, and it states plainly where the record is still being assembled.

How the cycle lists are being built. OpenSecrets publishes per-cycle lists of the largest recipients of pro-Israel money, but its definitions are its own and its pages could not be read for this volume. To avoid relying on any single tracker's classification, the lists for each cycle from 2016 to 2026 are being built directly from the Federal Election Commission's bulk files of committee-to-candidate transactions. The method takes every contribution, earmarked pass-through and independent expenditure made by a defined list of pro-Israel committees, and totals them by candidate, cycle, chamber and party, keeping money given to a candidate separate from money spent for or against one. The list of committees is published with the results so that any reader can check what was counted. Until those lists are complete, this section reports the one cycle for which a published analysis gives a ranked list, 2018, and the current Congress's career totals given in the previous section.

2018. In the 2018 cycle pro-Israel donors and groups gave just under $15 million to candidates for Congress, reaching 269 members of the House and 57 senators, with Democrats receiving about twice as much as Republicans. [cite:pm_guardian_2019] The largest recipients were all senators or Senate candidates.

Several of those names recur across cycles. Ted Cruz, second in 2018, is second among Senate Republicans in the career totals of 2026, with about $1.4 million, and is the senator who has described himself as Israel's "strongest defender." [cite:trackaipac_congress] [cite:cruz_senate_ami] The 2018 list also shows how the money was distributed before AIPAC's own committees: the largest single giver that cycle was J Street's PAC, which gave only to Democrats, and the top recipients were mainly Democrats. [cite:pm_tablet_lobbying] [cite:pm_guardian_2019]

2022 and 2024. For the two cycles after AIPAC entered campaign spending, the record used here documents the largest recipients by a different measure: the money spent for or against candidates in primaries, set out race by race in Part Five. The Guardian's analysis of members' most recent elections, mostly those of 2022, found that the six largest recipients of pro-Israel donor money were all centrist Democrats who had beaten progressive opponents, together receiving about $25 million, some 42 percent of all pro-Israel donor money reaching sitting members. [cite:pm_guardian_2024] In 2024, by AIPAC's own account, its PAC passed money to candidates in both parties, 206 of its 326 winning general-election candidates being Republicans; its largest single earmarked total that the record names was more than $3.1 million to Wesley Bell. [cite:pm_aipac_memo_2024] [cite:pm_sludge_2024]

The cycles still to be filled. Ranked lists by party and chamber for 2016, 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2026, built from the FEC files, will be added here when the run described above is complete and its committee list has been reviewed. Until then, a reader who wants a single cycle's ranking can find it, under OpenSecrets' own definitions, on its pro-Israel recipients pages, and should read it with the five questions of Part One.
