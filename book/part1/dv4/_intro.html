Washington is the third city in this volume and the one with the strongest documented claim to the title of parent. It is also the one most often misdescribed. The District of Columbia is not a sovereign city-state, not a corporation that secretly replaced the United States in 1871, and not one point of a three-city emblem stitched into its flag. It is something stranger in plain sight: a city of nearly seven hundred thousand people whose final lawmaker is a legislature they cannot vote for, whose judges are chosen by the President, whose local crimes are prosecuted by a federal official, and whose streets, in the autumn of 2026, are still patrolled by National Guard troops sent by the President over the objection of its elected government.

This part begins there, with how the district was made and how it is actually run, set beside the way an ordinary American city is run, so that the reader can see exactly where its anomaly lies. Then it turns from the district to what the district houses: the government that recognized Israel eleven minutes after it was proclaimed, that went on to supply most of its foreign military aid, that has vetoed more than fifty draft resolutions at the Security Council on its behalf, and that in November 2026 is negotiating what replaces the aid when the current agreement ends. Each step is set against what Washington also withheld, as the test in Part One requires, and the part ends with the verdict.
