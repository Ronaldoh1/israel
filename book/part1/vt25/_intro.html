Part Twenty-Five opens the jar. In December 1945 a cache of thirteen leather-bound papyrus books was dug up at the foot of a cliff near Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt. It held 52 writings in Coptic, among them gospels attributed to Thomas and Philip, secret books of John and James, and myths of Sophia and the ignorant creator that bishops had argued against since the second century. The first section separates what is documented about the find (its date, its custody, the 348 date in its bindings) from the discovery story as it is usually told. It also weighs the competing explanations of why the books were buried. The next two sections take the texts in groups. First come the sayings gospels, where the "kingdom within" teaching is plainest. Then come the secret cosmologies, set out stage by stage from the unknowable Monad to the spark's return. The last section is a complete catalogue of every tractate in all thirteen codices. Each entry carries a one-line teaching, a note on whether it conflicted with the bishops' doctrine, and the bishop or document, where one is recorded, that condemned the text or its school.
