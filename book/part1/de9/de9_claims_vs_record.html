His Claims Against His Record

Thiel says less in public than most men of his influence, and much of what is said about him comes from others: biographers, former classmates, a former Israeli prime minister in a private email, a theorist boasting to a friend. This section tests only what he or his authorised spokesperson has said, against the documents, using the four grades this book applies to every person it examines. RED marks a claim contradicted by the person's own documents or sworn record. ORANGE marks a promise or claim not delivered, or not verifiable by the official auditor. YELLOW marks a reversal or concealment, shown with the person's own explanation. GREY marks a dispute in which credible accounts conflict, with both shown. The word "lie" is used only where the record shows the person knew a claim was false when making it; nothing below meets that bar, and each flag names a contradiction for the reader to judge.

Flags on what he believed and wrote

Apartheid (1985–86, denied in 2016): GREY. His spokesperson said in 2016: "Peter has never supported apartheid, but he can easily see how a conversation might be misremembered 30 years later." [cite:de9_wnyc_apartheid] The record against it is the recollection of two named Stanford contemporaries in 2016, that he called moral issues "irrelevant" to apartheid's economy and said governments should not be judged by morality, and of two former peers interviewed for Chafkin's 2021 biography (the published summaries do not say whether they are the same two people), that he said "It works" and called the denial of civil rights "economically sound"; the spokesperson denied those encounters. [cite:de9_wnyc_apartheid] [cite:de9_dm_davis] The accounts are consistent with one another and given on the record; they are still recollections of private talk, against a denial, with no document in his hand. The dispute is real and stays open.

The Diversity Myth (1995) to "I regret" (2016): YELLOW. The 1995 book, written with David Sacks, suggested a woman might "realize" she had been "raped" the next day or later. [cite:de9_cnn_apology] In October 2016 he called the book's statements "insensitive" and "crudely argued" and said: "I regret writing passages that have been taken to suggest otherwise." [cite:de9_cnn_apology] This is a reversal, and his explanation is on the record with it. It came more than two decades after publication and three months after his convention speech, and it is limited to the passages on rape; the book's wider argument against campus multiculturalism he has not withdrawn. [cite:de9_indep_dm]

Suffrage (13 April and 1 May 2009): GREY. His 2009 essay named "the extension of the franchise to women" among the developments that had made "capitalist democracy" an oxymoron. [cite:de1_cato_thiel] Two and a half weeks later he wrote: "While I don't think any class of people should be disenfranchised, I have little hope that voting will make things better." [cite:de1_audit_cato_followup] The clarification is consistent with the original sentence read literally, which described a cause of decline rather than proposing a remedy, and he has not advocated taking the vote from anyone. Critics read the original as an argument against women's suffrage; he presents it as an observation. Both readings are on the record.

Flags on what he did

Gawker: "less about revenge and more about specific deterrence" (May 2016): GREY. His stated motive was to deter a publisher that, in his words, bullied people "even when there was no connection with the public interest," and he called the funding philanthropy. [cite:de9_tc_gawker] [cite:de9_fora_gawker] The record also contains his 2009 description of Valleywag as "the Silicon Valley equivalent of Al Qaeda," the 2007 post that outed him, his decision "several years" before 2016 to find cases to fund, and the secrecy of the funding until after the verdict. [cite:de9_sfist_aq] [cite:de9_tc_gawker] [cite:de9_aba_settle] Deterrence and revenge are not exclusive, the funding was lawful, and the outlet's own conduct was found unlawful by a jury. The question is his motive, which only his words can supply; they say deterrence, his earlier words show grievance, and the reader has both.

New Zealand: "great pride to let it be known" (2011): YELLOW. His citizenship application said it would give him "great pride to let it be known that I am a New Zealand citizen." [cite:de9_ap_nz] He did not let it be known: the grant was made at a private ceremony in California in 2011 and became public only when a newspaper reported it in January 2017, after he had spent twelve days in the country. [cite:de9_ap_nz] [cite:de9_nbr_12days] Keeping a citizenship private is lawful. The flag is the gap between the stated wish and the six years of silence that followed. He has offered no explanation; asked in 2017, he declined to comment. [cite:de9_nbr_12days]

The 2024 cycle: no money "to any politician, including Donald Trump" (November 2023): GREY. The pledge reached the public as a paraphrase by The Atlantic's Barton Gellman, after Reuters reported, citing people close to him, that he might still help former employees; in June 2024 he said in his own words: "I'm not going to give any money to his super PAC." [cite:de9_pw_atlantic] [cite:de9_reuters_2023] [cite:de9_nbc_aspen] No Thiel money to Trump or his super PACs is on record, so that part of the pledge was kept. Ballotpedia, citing FEC records, puts his 2024-cycle giving at "over $1.7 million to candidates for federal office and political parties," while Bloomberg and Mother Jones say he sat the cycle out. [cite:de9_bp_thiel] [cite:de9_bgov_cfg] [cite:de9_mj_2025] The cycle began in January 2023, before the pledge, and the total is not itemised; without dated line items the record cannot show whether any of it broke his word. This flag would move to YELLOW only if itemised records show gifts to candidates after November 2023.

Three tests that raise no flag

Valar and Epstein: "a limited partner," not a co-owner (2026): no flag. His spokesman confirmed that Jeffrey Epstein was a limited partner in Valar Ventures and denied that Epstein co-owned the fund. [cite:de9_byline_valar] Against the denial stands one sentence in a private email by Ehud Barak in February 2016 describing "a new Investment Fund owned by Peter Thiel and JE." [cite:de9_byline_valar] Barak's phrase is a third party's characterisation, not a fund document. The records of the investment itself, the fund statements sent to Epstein, the New York Times's account of a $40 million limited partnership and Valar's own spokesperson, all describe an investor, not an owner, and no ownership record published so far shows Epstein as an owner or manager. [cite:de14_nyt_valar] The record supports the spokesman's statement rather than contradicting it, so no flag is raised; the co-ownership claim itself is graded not supported in [scene:de14_epstein_valar|Part Fourteen], which also examines what the documents released in 2026 do and do not show about the two men's contacts.

Yarvin: ideas that would not "work" (2023): no flag. Thiel told The Atlantic he did not think Yarvin's ideas would "work," while calling him an "interesting and powerful" historian. [cite:de6_cnn_2025] The record is a 2013 venture investment by Founders Fund in Yarvin's company, and Yarvin's own 2016 boast of "coaching" him, which Thiel has not confirmed. [cite:de5_wiki_urbit] [cite:de5_pein_baffler] Investing in a company is not adopting its founder's politics, and Yarvin's claim is Yarvin's. Nothing in the record contradicts Thiel's statement.

The Roth: no public claim (2021): no flag. Thiel has made no public statement about the account; his spokesman took ProPublica's questions and did not answer them. [cite:de9_pp_roth] There is nothing of his to test. The record of the account itself is in [scene:de9_gawker_citizenship_and_tax|the third section].

The pattern is not one of statements broken by documents. Thiel has never held an office requiring sworn statements, and he speaks rarely; the gaps in his record are mostly silences, a citizenship not mentioned, a lawsuit funded quietly, questions about a tax account left unanswered, rather than claims proved false. The two YELLOW flags are a reversal he explained and a concealment he did not.

The worst-case reading, tested

The hypothesis this book tests holds that immigration enforcement and government cuts are the occasion, and a sovereign executive without a veto the destination. Thiel's life bears on it in four ways. Documented: that he wrote in 2009 that he no longer believed freedom and democracy compatible, and called for an escape from politics. [cite:de3_cato_thiel] Documented: that he put $15 million into a super PAC backing the 2022 Senate campaign of J.D. Vance, who became Vice President, and that people who had worked for him held or were named to federal posts in 2025. [cite:de9_cnbc_2022] [cite:de9_fortune_net24] Documented: that he used litigation funding, lawfully and in secret, to end a publication he had called a terrorist group, which shows that one private fortune can remove one check, a hostile press outlet, without any act of state. [cite:de9_tc_gawker] [cite:de9_snopes_close] And documented: that the company he chairs holds large and growing federal contracts, including with immigration enforcement. [cite:de9_mj_2025] [cite:ow9_bd_army]

Probable, on this part's material, is nothing beyond those facts. Not supported: that Thiel directs a plan to install an executive without limits. His own words point the other way as often as toward it: he has said Yarvin's ideas would not work, spent most of 2023–24 out of the presidential race, and in 2025–26 moved much of his life beyond the reach of a government his allies help run. [cite:de6_cnn_2025] [cite:de9_nbc_aspen] [cite:de9_fortune_ba] Not supported either: that his donations caused the appointments or the contracts that followed them; the sequence is documented and the causation is not. The record shows a man using both exit and voice, buying influence by lawful means at a scale few can match, and preparing more than one country to live in. Whether those choices serve the destination the hypothesis names is a question about intent that only his own words could settle, and his words, read as he wrote them, are tested in [scene:de10_philosophy_vs_practice|Part Ten]. [scene:de40_worst_case_scorecard|Part Forty] carries these grades into the book's final account.
