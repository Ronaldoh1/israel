By the summer of 1956 the armistice system of 1949 still stood, but, on the argument of this stage, little rested on it any longer. On this reading, each layer of the structure had been rebuilt in these three years around a single fact: the lines could be condemned, negotiated over and financed, but no one outside the region could hold them.

The legal layer was dense and weak. The armistice agreements and the Security Council's resolutions defined what was forbidden, and the Council used them. It found reprisal raids to be violations three times, and in January 1956 it rejected outright Israel's plea that Syrian interference justified the raid at the lake. [cite:rs_cw2_un_res101] [cite:rs_cw2_un_res106] [cite:rs_cw2_un_res111] Its findings ran in both directions: the Commission had called the killings at Yehud "intolerable aggression", and Resolution 101 asked Jordan to stop the crossings. [cite:rs_cw2_jvl_qibya] [cite:rs_cw2_avalon_res101] What the law lacked was a means of enforcement. On the Jordanian commission the chairman's vote decided, and Israel had cut its connection with that commission in March 1954. [cite:rs_cw2_un_s3252] Hammarskjöld's assurances bound every government only while the others complied, and each reserved its right of self-defence. [cite:rs_cw2_unyb1956] At Suez, the Council said its resolution of 1951 kept "continuing validity", and Egypt offered to release the Bat Galim only if it sailed south, away from the Canal. [cite:rs_cw2_frus_batgalim] The one new instrument that changed facts on the ground was bilateral, not international. The base agreement of October 1954 recognized the Canal as "an integral part of Egypt", and on 26 July 1956 Nasser nationalized the Canal Company. [cite:rs_cw2_frus_suez_base] [cite:rs_cw2_state_suez]

The administrative layer is the story of how decisions were made, and in both Israel and Egypt power was drawn into one office. In Israel the record shows force used outside the ordinary chain. Sharett, as prime minister, was "kept out of the loop" on Operation Susannah, and who ordered it is disputed: Gibli said he had a verbal instruction from Lavon, who denied any knowledge. [cite:rs_cw2_mod_lavon] The raid at the lake was authorized by Ben-Gurion alone, in Shlaim's account, while his foreign minister sought arms in Washington. [cite:rs_cw2_shlaim_kinneret] By June 1956 the premiership and the defence ministry were in Ben-Gurion's hands and Sharett was gone. [cite:rs_cw2_kaller_bg] In Egypt the same three years ran from a ruling council to a sole candidate elected with 99.95 percent, as HISTORY.com reports. [cite:rs_cw2_history_nasser] The Free Officers' rival claimants were removed along the way: Naguib from all his functions, the Brotherhood by ban and arrest. [cite:rs_cw2_frus_naguib] [cite:rs_cw2_origins_mb] Gaza was run by a military governor answerable to Cairo. [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza]

The institutional and financial layer showed money following alignment only part of the way. American economic aid to Israel continued, falling from $73.6 million in 1953 to $50.8 million in 1956. [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_usaid_table] It was held back once, over Banat Yaacov, and released the day after the Council's resolution, once Israel had undertaken to suspend the works. [cite:rs_cw2_frus_pr604] The large Western offers were attached to settlements that never came: two-thirds of the Jordan valley's development costs, and, as Parker Hart recalled it, a grant for the dam at Aswan with a conditional loan to follow. [cite:rs_cw2_frus_unified_plan] [cite:rs_cw2_adst_aswan] Elsewhere, interests were settled in kind or in shares. Egypt bartered cotton and rice for Soviet-bloc arms. Iran's oil was divided among the Anglo-Iranian company, Royal Dutch Shell, five American firms and a French participant. Washington paid a sixth of the Baghdad Pact's budget without joining it. [cite:rs_cw2_frus_tel632] [cite:rs_cw2_fo_consortium] [cite:rs_cw2_frus_baghdad_us] One arrangement rested on no documented written agreement: the CIA's Israel account, held by Angleton as what the agency's historian calls "an administrative anomaly". [cite:rs_cw2_robarge]

The Palestinian Arab side appears in this stage chiefly as a subject of other governments' plans: 900,000 refugees in Dulles's address, 75,000 to be resettled in Israel under Alpha, 12,000 families to be settled in Sinai. [cite:rs_cw2_jvl_dulles55] [cite:rs_cw2_ecf_alpha] [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza] Its own voice is recorded in Gaza. In March 1955 a committee of communists and Brotherhood members demanded an end to the Sinai scheme, arms for the camps and civil freedoms. The governor's promise on the scheme was followed by mass arrests. [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza] In September 1955 Egypt formed fida'i units under its own command. [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza] At Bandung, twenty-nine states declared "support for the rights of the Arab people of Palestine". [cite:rs_cw2_enc_bandung] The raids also fell on civilians: the villagers of Qibya, the woman killed at Nahhalin on the Jordanian side of the line, and at Khan Yunis twenty-five refugees by Egypt's count. [cite:rs_cw2_jps_qibya] [cite:rs_cw2_un_s3251] [cite:rs_cw2_un_a3157]

The regional layer was reordered around Cairo. Egypt had secured the British evacuation, a Soviet-bloc supplier and, after Bandung, a leader with standing beyond the Arab world. Iraq had joined Turkey and Britain in the Pact. Jordan had moved toward it, and turned back. [cite:rs_cw2_enc_bandung] [cite:rs_cw2_loc_jordan] The American layer was defined by what Washington declined to do. It refused Israel arms, stayed out of the Pact, withdrew the dam offer and stood apart from Eden's formula. Each refusal left the decision to someone else: France on Israel's aircraft, Britain and Iraq on the Pact, and, as Fraser Wilkins later put it, the Russians on the dam. [cite:rs_cw2_shlaim_arms] [cite:rs_cw2_frus_mystere] [cite:rs_cw2_frus_eden_uk] [cite:rs_cw2_adst_aswan] At home, the same government wrote God into the Pledge and the motto, as a stand against Communism; the sources record no link to Israel. [cite:rs_cw2_ucsb_pledge] [cite:rs_cw2_ffrf_kramnick]

What was settled, then, was the shape of the coming confrontation: Egypt armed from the East and sovereign over the Canal, Israel armed from France, and the United States committed to neither. What was contested was the meaning of retaliation itself. Israel's strongest case was that civilians were being killed across lines that no commission could close, and that the only guarantee offered required giving up the reply. The strongest case against it was made by the UN and inside Israel's own cabinet. UNTSO found "a striking disparity" between reply and provocation, and the Council held that provocation did not justify the raid. Sharett held that avoiding escalation was the way to prevent war. [cite:rs_cw2_shlaim_east_west] [cite:rs_cw2_frus_kinneret] [cite:rs_cw2_un_res111] What the record leaves open is whether any party could have chosen otherwise. Nasser had asked Moscow for arms before the Gaza raid and before the Pact. [cite:rs_cw2_wilson_solod] Dulles, by Shlaim's account, had decided to sell Israel defensive arms on the eve of the raid at the lake. [cite:rs_cw2_shlaim_arms] Whether those were real alternatives or only moments is the question the crisis of the autumn, told in [scene:cw3_sevres|the next stage], would answer by force.
