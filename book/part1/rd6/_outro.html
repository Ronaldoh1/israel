This volume began with a loan. In 1694, 1,268 subscribers lent the English Crown £1.2 million through a newly chartered company that became the Bank of England, and a Crown at war against France found a new lender in private subscribers. [cite:rs_rd0_boe_museum] It ends two and a half centuries later with a state drawing on outside money in its own name: a loan from the Export-Import Bank of the United States, bonds sold to buyers abroad, and reparations from West Germany. Between the two lie the structures this volume has traced, laid by many hands over many decades and never by one design. By the early 1950s they stood in six layers, and each held something the others did not.

The legal layer was the longest in the making. The Ottoman Land Code of 1858 required titles to be registered individually, and under the Mandate those registered titles were the instruments that could be sold. [cite:wiki_ottoman_land_code_1858] Balfour's letter of 1917 had no legal force until the Mandate of 1922 wrote it into an instrument of the League of Nations, recognized a "Jewish agency" in Article 4, and bound Britain in Article 2 to the national home, to self-governing institutions and to safeguarding the civil and religious rights of all inhabitants. [cite:rs_md_avalon] The General Assembly's partition resolution passed on 29 November 1947 by 33 votes to 13. [cite:rs_rd5_un_yearbook_181] After 1948 the new state supplied its own law: the Absentees' Property Law of 14 March 1950, the Law of Return of 5 July 1950, the Nationality Law in force from 14 July 1952, and the Land Acquisition Law of 1953. [cite:un_absentees_property_law_text] [cite:rs_rd6_mjl_lawofreturn] [cite:rs_rd6_refworld_nationality] [cite:rs_rd6_badil_landregime] Its membership of the United Nations, granted by Resolution 273 on 11 May 1949, was settled. [cite:rs_rd6_unga273_text] Its borders were not. The Egyptian and Jordanian armistices of 1949 reserved the final settlement, and the Egyptian text said the line was "not to be construed in any sense as a political or territorial boundary." [cite:rs_rd6_arm_egypt_text] Nor did the state give itself a constitution: the Harari Resolution of 13 June 1950 promised one made up of chapters, each a separate basic law. [cite:rs_rd6_dickerman_2007] What was contested was the force of the older texts. Israel held that the Arab invasion had nullified partition and that Resolution 194 tied return to peace; the Arab states at Lausanne demanded the 1947 lines and return first. [cite:un_ccp_fourth_report] [cite:unispal_194_survey]

The administrative layer showed the most continuity. The Mandate's High Commissioner had governed from 1923 with no elected chamber beside him. [cite:rs_lc_albright] The Defense (Emergency) Regulations that Britain issued in 1945 passed into Israeli law in 1948, and under them a military government ruled most Arab citizens of Israel until 1 December 1966. [cite:rs_rd6_korn_adalah] The strongest case for it, made at the time and endorsed by the Rattner Commission in 1956, was that a state with armistices but no peace needed such controls; the strongest case against, made by Alina Korn, is that its purpose was to control movement and keep Arabs from confiscated land, and it rests on conviction figures: about 95 percent of Arab citizens' convictions under the regulations were for administrative offenses, such as lacking a permit. [cite:rs_rd6_korn_adalah] The record documents both purposes and does not weigh them.

The institutional and financial layer was built first by the movement and then by the state. The Jewish Colonial Trust of 1899, the Jewish National Fund of 1901 and Keren Hayesod, which raised $20.1 million worldwide in 1921–29, made fundraising permanent; by 1948 the United Jewish Appeal raised $150 million from more than a million American contributors, of which $74.7 million went to Palestine and $64.9 million to relief in Europe. [cite:jta_kh_decade] [cite:jta_uja_1948] After 1948 a Custodian, a Development Authority and the Fund itself received the land of those classed as absentees; BADIL puts the transfers at nearly 2.4 million dunams, a figure the scholarly sources here do not confirm. [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] [cite:rs_rd6_badil_landregime] The money became a state's money: $100.0 million of American aid in 1949, all of it economic, more than $52 million in Israel Bonds in their first year, and German reparations that made up 12–14 percent of Israel's imports for a decade. [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_usaid_table] [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_bonds] [cite:rs_rd6_nli_reparations] The Knesset voted to negotiate with Germany by 61 to 50, while opponents led by Menachem Begin called the payments "blood money." [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_reparations] [cite:rs_rd6_nli_reparations] What the record leaves open is a total: no audited sum exists for the pre-state build or the Haganah's budget.

The Palestinian Arab layer was a national movement that never acquired a state's instruments. The Arab Executive under Musa Kazim al-Husseini was never recognized by Britain, and its boycott of 1923 defeated the Legislative Council but won no recognition. [cite:palquest_musa_kazim] [cite:rs_lc_cojs_boycott] In 1948 Palestinian leaders proclaimed a government in Gaza that Avi Shlaim calls "a phantom deliberately created by the Arab states," while the Jericho Congress proclaimed the union of Palestine and Transjordan under Abdullah as king. [cite:rs_rd6_shlaim_apg_1990] The refugees were counted from 520,000 to a million, depending on who counted. [cite:rs_rd6_morris_appendix] They were given relief through UNRWA, left outside the 1951 Refugee Convention by its Article 1D, and promised in paragraph 11 of Resolution 194 that those wishing to return "should be permitted to do so." [cite:unrwa_who] [cite:w_refconv] [cite:unispal_194_survey] Their strongest case is that return is a right, reaffirmed by the Assembly every year since 1949; Israel's is that the resolution made return conditional on peace, and that the Arab states first rejected it. [cite:wiki_res_194] [cite:unwatch_194] Arab citizens, among the 156,000 non-Jews counted inside Israel at the end of 1948 by Israeli figures, voted from the first election in 1949, most of them while living under military rule. [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_population] [cite:rs_rd6_idi_nazareth] [cite:rs_rd6_korn_adalah] Whether the expulsions of 1948 were planned remains disputed; the claim that the refugees left on a general Arab order is no longer defended by mainstream scholarship. [cite:w_morris_birth]

The regional layer was redrawn twice. Britain made conflicting wartime promises in 1915–16, to Sharif Hussein and to France, and at Cairo in 1921 gave Iraq to his son Faisal and Transjordan to his son Abdullah. [cite:w_mcmahon] [cite:w_sykes_picot] [cite:w_cairo1921] In 1948–50 one of them, Abdullah, annexed the West Bank, and the annexation was condemned within the Arab League. [cite:rs_rd6_loc_jordan] Iraq and Saudi Arabia signed no armistice, and the League's boycott carried the conflict into trade. [cite:rs_rd6_mfa_armistice_map] Syria's military ruler, Husni al-Za'im, offered a peace treaty and the resettlement of 250,000–300,000 refugees, and Ben-Gurion declined. [cite:tf_zaim] [cite:majalla_zaim] The war also ended Jewish life in much of the Arab world: Iraq made Zionism a crime in July 1948, and by the end of 1952, by a State Archives count, Israel had received 377,251 immigrants from Muslim countries. [cite:yated_ezra_nechemia] [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_immigrants_4852] Philip Mendes explains that exodus by push and pull together, against the partisan accounts on either side. [cite:rs_rd6_mendes_2010]

The American layer had the deepest roots and the newest weight. In 1839 Lord Shaftesbury, a British evangelical, pressed for a Jewish return to Palestine, and in 1891 an American evangelist petitioned President Harrison for the same. [cite:wiki_christian_zionism] Congress endorsed the Balfour Declaration in 1922, both party platforms adopted the movement's aims in 1944, and the American Zionist Emergency Council worked Congress district by district. [cite:wiki_lodge_fish] [cite:aja_azec_appraisal_1944] [cite:aja_azec_local_1944] Truman recognized the state against his strategists' advice. In the election summer he stopped pressing Israel on territory and refugees, in part, by John Judis's account, because his campaign needed $100,000 from Abraham Feinberg and Ed Kaufmann; then he threatened a "revision" of policy in May 1949 that he never enforced. [cite:mondoweiss_judis_truman] [cite:jvl_truman_note_may1949] The claim that donors bought policy has that one documented case and no other; the claim that money played no role is contradicted by it. Whether money reached members of Congress before 1948 cannot be known, because the law did not require the records until 1971. [cite:wiki_fcpa_1925]

What, then, did the road from 1694 settle? It settled that a Jewish state existed, with an army, a parliament, a land regime and a law of immigration, and that it rested on institutions built over half a century before it was declared. It did not settle where the state ended, what was owed to the people displaced in its founding, or who would govern the rest of the land. The Holocaust gave the build its urgency; it did not create the apparatus. And the war of 1948, not any plan, decided where the lines ran. Those lines were, by their own text, still not borders. Every volume that follows begins from that fact.
