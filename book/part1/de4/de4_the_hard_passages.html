The Hard Passages: Race, Heredity and Exit as Sorting

About half of "The Dark Enlightenment" is about race. Race enters in the second instalment, and from April to June 2012 it is the main subject, as Land writes about white nationalism, crime, intelligence, the civil-rights era and the American South; the serial ends with a vision of human beings engineering themselves into separate species. [cite:de4_land_de] This section quotes those passages exactly and in context, says what Land said they meant where he said it, and names what they are.

Heredity and the charge of heresy

Race first enters in Part 2, through Moldbug. Land quotes Moldbug quoting the Victorian biologist Thomas Huxley on "our dusky cousins," and then Moldbug's question to Richard Dawkins: "In all seriousness, what is the evidence for fraternism?" [cite:de4_land_de] Land marks the moment himself: "This sure ain't vanilla-libertarian reaction anymore — it's getting seriously dark, and scary." [cite:de4_land_de] His own argument is that the belief in equal human potential is held as religion, not science: "it is surely beyond contention that the latter assumption, alone, is tolerated," and to question anti-racism is to commit what "we now call political incorrectness, and once knew as heresy." [cite:de4_land_de]

The claims become his own in Part 4a. Describing the safety of East Asian cities, Land attributes their order to populations who, "due to biological heredity, deep cultural traditions, or some inextricable entanglement of the two," find peaceful life easy, and sets them against Western inner cities where, in his words, "civilization has fundamentally collapsed." [cite:de4_land_de] In Part 4b he calls the belief in substantial human equality "sheer Gnostic delirium," names intelligence as "by far the most troublesome dimension of human bio-diversity," and in Part 4d writes that "stereotypes generally have high statistical truth-value." [cite:de4_land_de] "Human bio-diversity," or HBD, is the label the online right uses for hereditarian claims about differences between races. Haider calls it a euphemism for scientific racism, and cites the anthropologist Jonathan Marks: "the heredity of race is not genetic, but social." [cite:de4_haider]

Said plainly: the serial presents the view that differences in behaviour and ability between racial groups are probably in part inherited, and that stereotypes are statistically sound. That is a racist claim in the ordinary meaning of the word, and the text makes it in Land's own voice. [cite:de4_land_de]

The sentence about genocide

One line from Part 4b is often quoted alone: "Violence on a genocidal scale is required to even approximate to a practical egalitarian program." [cite:de4_land_de] In context it ends a paragraph arguing that "People are not equal, they do not develop equally," and it continues: "if anything less ambitious is attempted, people get around it." [cite:de4_land_de] The sentence is a claim about what forcing equality of outcome would require, offered as an argument against egalitarianism, not a call for violence; it rests on the hereditarian premise above.

What Land rejects

The same instalments reject several things, and the record must carry those too. Part 4 treats white nationalism as a dead end: for those who take it up, the path "demands rapid acceleration to a state of extreme alarm, or racial panic," and its grievance is "the werewolf curse that means it can only ever be a monster." [cite:de4_land_de] He describes the turn from white identity politics to blaming Jews, quoting a blogger who wrote "you do have to violate PC to say: Jews," and calls it "the labyrinth, the trap, with its pitifully constricted, stereotypical circuit." [cite:de4_land_de] In Part 4d he argues that Nazism is self-defeating: "Identity politics is for losers," and "racial Übermenschen make no real sense." [cite:de4_land_de]

His treatment of John Derbyshire, the columnist dismissed by National Review in April 2012 for a column advising his children to avoid black people, is partly critical. Land finds the column's wording "subtly, yet distinctly, menacing," and concedes the central objection made by William Saletan and Noah Millman: "Stereotypes, however rigorously confirmed, are essentially inferior to specific knowledge in any concrete social situation, because nobody ever encounters a population." [cite:de4_land_de] He quotes Millman's verdict in full: "Is Derbyshire's piece racist? Of course it's racist." [cite:de4_land_de] What he does not reject is the premise. Millman's point, which Land reproduces without dissent, was that Derbyshire's conclusions do not follow from his premises; the premises, about group differences, are left standing.

Hitler as the Cathedral's anti-Messiah

The passage most likely to be misread is in Part 4. Land quotes, and praises as reaching "the brink of genius," a Moldbug essay that compares the moral standing of Hitler and Stalin and asks why socialism does not arouse the horror that white nationalism does. [cite:de4_land_de] Land then argues that modern societies treat Hitler as a religious absolute: "it might be more helpful to compare Hitler to the Antichrist, which is to say: to a mirror Messiah, of reversed moral polarity." He calls this "less a pro-Nazi ideology than a universal faith," and mocks it as "the Church of Sacred Hitlerite Abomination." [cite:de4_land_de] The passage does not praise Hitler, whom Land describes as personifying "demonic monstrosity" in the common view he is describing. Its target is the treatment of Hitler as a metaphysical absolute of evil, which it reframes as a sacrament of the Cathedral's faith.

Exit as sorting

The thread that matters most for this book is the one that joins Land's race material to his politics. In Part 4a he takes the American term "white flight," the movement of white residents out of cities, and makes it the model of his idea: "all exit and no voice," the "spontaneous impulse of dark enlightenment." [cite:de4_land_de] He reads Derbyshire's advice to his children as having "converted white flight from a much-lamented but seemingly inexorable fact into an explicit imperative, even a cause. Don't argue, flee." [cite:de4_land_de] In Part 4c the progressive order becomes a "Cracker Factory," and he writes: "Like Hell, or Auschwitz, the Cracker Factory has a simple slogan inscribed upon its gate: Escape is racist." [cite:de4_land_de] By Part 4f secession leads "towards speciation," with elite groups using genetic engineering to leave the human species behind; "For racial nationalists, concerned that their grandchildren should look like them, Campbell is the abyss." [cite:de4_land_de]

Land took the step further two years later. On 4 October 2014 he published a post titled "Hyper-Racism" on the Alternative Right blog; the word does not appear in the 2012 serial. [cite:de4_wiki_land] [cite:de4_land_de] Haider, who quoted the post, reports that Land treated socioeconomic status as "a strong proxy for IQ," wrote of a "genetically self-filtering elite," and argued that "space colonization will inevitably function as a highly-selective genetic filter." [cite:de4_haider] On those quotations the term names not a hatred of other races but a future in which successful groups separate themselves physically and then genetically from everyone else.

The critics

Land's critics come mostly from the left that once read him closely. Haider's 2017 essay in Viewpoint Magazine argues that Land's race theory is not an aberration but mainstream meritocracy taken to its end: Land "simply carried the mainstream ideology to its inexorable result," and, unlike liberals who believe in that ideal, "admits its implications." [cite:de4_haider] Elizabeth Sandifer, whose Neoreaction a Basilisk (first published in 2016) is a book-length critical reading of these writers, said she gave the work "as much intellectual respect as I could before I took it out back and shot it," and that "Their ideas make no sense if you look at them under any scrutiny whatsoever." [cite:de4_ca_sandifer] [cite:de2_sandifer_ep]

The worst-case reading, tested

The working hypothesis, the Sophia platform's and not a finding, reads immigration enforcement and government cuts as the occasion and a sovereign executive without a veto as the destination. This part asked what Land's writing adds to it.

Documented: Land's serial rejects democracy as such and adopts, with credit, Moldbug's model of a state owned like a company and run by a chief executive, with "No voice, free exit" as its principle. [cite:de4_land_de] Documented too are hereditarian claims about race in his own voice, and a line of argument in which exit becomes sorting, first by neighbourhood and then by genome. [cite:de4_land_de] [cite:de4_haider] And documented is a tie of reading: the investor Marc Andreessen counts Land among his "patron saints." [cite:de4_spectator]

Probable: that Land's vocabulary of exit and acceleration reaches readers in technology investing, on the strength of Andreessen's citation; no document shows that any reader took the race passages from him, or which of his ideas anyone took. [cite:de4_spectator] On one point the record runs against the worst case directly: the sorting in Land's examples is the departure of those who can leave, by flight, secession or engineering, not the removal of others by a state. [cite:de4_land_de] Enforcement that removes people resembles a different text, Hoppe's call to expel political opponents from a covenant community, discussed in [scene:de3_other_lineages|Part Three], more than it resembles Land's. [cite:de3_wiki_hoppe]

Not supported: that Land supplies the destination. His text prescribes no American executive, expects no change "short of an existential civilizational cataclysm," places his hopes for growth in China, and imagines liberty only through secession and, in the end, speciation. [cite:de4_land_de] [cite:de4_spectator] That is a programme for leaving states, not for capturing one, and on this part's material the record runs against the idea that Land's engine was built to power a sovereign American executive. The hand-off from writing to government, if there was one, runs through Yarvin and the people around him, the subject of [scene:de5_programmer_to_moldbug|the next parts], and it is graded in [scene:de40_worst_case_scorecard|the final scorecard].
