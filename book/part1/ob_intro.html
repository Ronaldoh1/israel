This book takes the word at the centre of the whole Consciousness shelf, the observer, and asks what the sciences actually say about it. It begins in physics, with the most quoted and most misquoted idea in modern science, the claim that observing a thing changes it. It moves to the study of the mind, where the question of what consciousness is remains open and its leading theories have just failed their first head-to-head test. It then turns to the edges: the out-of-body research of Robert Monroe, the 1983 Army report that took that research seriously, and the hospital studies of people whose hearts had stopped.

The rule of this book is the rule of the shelf, held more tightly because the subject invites looseness. What an experiment showed is kept apart from what has been said about it. Where a popular teaching claims more than the physics allows, the book says so. And the last part, where the readings begin, is marked as a reading from its first line.
