Donald Trump

Born 1946, Queens, New York. At the time of contact: property developer and owner of the Mar-a-Lago club in Palm Beach. Today: president of the United States, in his second term, and head of the executive branch that holds the Epstein files.

Before Epstein

Donald Trump was born in Queens on 14 June 1946, the son of the property developer Fred Trump. He went to the New York Military Academy and graduated from the Wharton School at the University of Pennsylvania, then joined his father's firm, Elizabeth Trump & Son, which he renamed the Trump Organization. His first large Manhattan project turned an old hotel into the Grand Hyatt; his best known is Trump Tower, where he lived. Through the 1980s and 1990s he built hotels, apartments and casinos, and his television show, The Apprentice, made him a household name. He entered presidential politics in June 2015 and won in November 2016. [cite:ep5_whha_trump] In his second term, in November 2025, he signed the Epstein Files Transparency Act. [cite:efta_wiki]

His record apart from Epstein includes two court outcomes, and the book reports both because it reports the court record of every name at the same level. In 2023 a federal jury in New York found him liable for sexually abusing the writer E. Jean Carroll in a department store in the mid-1990s and for defaming her, and awarded her $5 million; in 2026 the Supreme Court declined to hear his appeal, and the verdict is final. A second jury awarded her $83.3 million for later defamation, which he is appealing. He denies her account. [cite:ep5_nbc_carroll2026] In May 2024 a Manhattan jury convicted him on 34 felony counts in the case over a payment to an adult-film actress; two years later the conviction was still on appeal. [cite:ep5_wex_hush2026] Neither case involves Epstein.

The record with Epstein

The documented relationship runs from the late 1980s or early 1990s to about 2004. The two men went to the same parties in Palm Beach and New York, were photographed together at Mar-a-Lago in 1997, and travelled between New York and Florida on Epstein's plane; a federal prosecutor's email of 2020 counted eight such flights in the 1990s. [cite:ep5_pbs_fallingout] [cite:ep4_sbs_trump_flights] In 2002 Trump called Epstein "a terrific guy" in New York magazine. [cite:ep4_nymag2002] In the summer of 2000 Virginia Giuffre was working as a spa attendant at Mar-a-Lago, according to Social Security records filed in her lawsuit against Maxwell; she was recruited from there. [cite:ep5_pbs_fallingout]

In 2003 Epstein's friends filled an album for his fiftieth birthday. In 2025 the Wall Street Journal reported that it contained a sexually suggestive letter bearing Trump's signature. Trump called the story "false, malicious, and defamatory", said he did not write the letter, and sued the newspaper for $10 billion. Congress later released the letter from the records it subpoenaed from Epstein's estate. In April 2026 a federal judge in Florida, Darrin Gayles, dismissed the suit because Trump had not argued that the paper acted with malice, and allowed him to file an amended complaint. [cite:ep5_apr_wsj2026] That leaves the letter in the public record and its authorship disputed by the man whose name it bears.

The break, three versions

The relationship ended, but there is no single documented account of why or when. In November 2004 the two men bid against each other for a bankrupt oceanfront mansion in Palm Beach, Maison de l'Amitié, and Trump won it for $41.35 million; the trustee called it "two very large Palm Beach egos going at it". [cite:ep5_pbs_fallingout] In 2019 Trump said he had not spoken to Epstein for fifteen years, which points to the same year. A book by reporters from the Miami Herald and the Wall Street Journal says that around October 2007 Trump barred Epstein from Mar-a-Lago after Epstein harassed a member's teenage daughter. In July 2025 Trump gave a third reason: that Epstein had "stolen" young women who worked at the Mar-a-Lago spa, Giuffre among them. [cite:ep5_pbs_fallingout] The three accounts are not exclusive, but they are not the same, and none of them is documented by a record from the time.

If the 2025 account is right, Trump knew by the time of the break that Epstein had taken young workers from his club. Nothing in the record shows that Trump told anyone in authority, then or when Epstein was investigated in 2005 and 2006. Nothing shows that anyone asked him.

What he said, and what the files show

When Epstein was arrested in 2019, Trump said: "I was not a fan of his." [cite:ep5_pbs_fallingout] Epstein's own emails, released in November 2025, say something else about him: that Trump "spent hours" at his house with a person whose name is redacted, and that Trump "knew about the girls". The White House said the redacted person was Giuffre, and Trump denies knowing of Epstein's crimes. [cite:ep4_csm_emails2025] Giuffre herself said in a 2016 deposition, "I don't think Donald Trump participated in anything." [cite:ep2_bi_giuffre_trump2024] Part Four sets out these documents side by side. No court proceeding this book has examined contains an allegation that Trump took part in Epstein's crimes.

What the news got wrong

Two opposite claims circulate, and neither is supported. One is that Trump is on a "client list" of Epstein's; no such list exists in the released record, and in July 2025 the Justice Department and the FBI said their review had found no evidence of one. [cite:ep5_npr_memo2025] The other is that the files "clear" him. The files cannot clear or convict anyone: they show contact, flights, a letter and Epstein's own claims, and they show no crime by Trump.

What the coverage tends to lose is the second half of his entry, which has nothing to do with the 1990s. As president he signed the law requiring the files' release, and his Justice Department published about half of the six million pages it reviewed; the law's Republican co-author says the rest is being withheld unlawfully. [cite:ep1_rollcall_release2026] [cite:khanna2] No other person in this dictionary had the power to release the files that mention him. Part Nine examines how it was used.

What remains unestablished: when the two men met; who wrote the birthday letter; which account of the break is true; and what the unreleased files say.
