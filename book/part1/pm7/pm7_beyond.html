Below Congress: Statehouses, City Halls and Police Chiefs

The congressional trips are recorded because federal ethics rules require it. Below Congress, the trips are at least as numerous and much less visible, because no federal body records the travel of state legislators, mayors or police chiefs. What is known comes from state disclosure filings where they exist, from the sponsors' own announcements and from local reporting. This section sets out what that record shows, including the one program in this volume in which the Israeli government itself paid for American officials' travel.

"50 States, One Israel." In September 2025 about 250 state legislators from across the United States attended a conference in Israel called "50 States, One Israel." It was financed by Israel's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, through its consulates, which invited the legislators and paid their expenses. [cite:pm_idcap_50states] [cite:lincolnchron_gomberg] The participants met Israeli leaders, among them President Isaac Herzog, met families of victims of the 7 October attacks and visited communities attacked that day. [cite:pm_idcap_50states] One of the Idaho participants, Representative Bruce Skaug, said the Israeli consulate in San Francisco had invited him and paid all his expenses, and that he believed he had been invited because he supported Israel and had co-sponsored Idaho's 2021 law against boycotts of Israel. [cite:pm_idcap_50states] Not every participant came away in agreement: Idaho's House minority leader, Ilana Rubel, used a state dinner to raise civilian casualties in Gaza directly with President Herzog. [cite:pm_idcap_50states] The program differs from everything else in this part in one respect. Every other trip described here was paid for by an American organization; this one was paid for by a foreign government, openly, for officials who make the state laws and manage the state funds described in Part Eight.

Community sponsors. Most state and local trips are paid for by American organizations, often local Jewish community bodies. In 2024 the Jewish Community Relations Council and the Jewish Federation of Los Angeles paid nearly $82,000 to send seven California legislators to Israel. [cite:calmatters_israel_trips] The Combat Antisemitism Movement led delegations of mayors and of fifteen state legislators the same year. [cite:inn_cam_mayors] In Massachusetts, a bill introduced in 2026 sought to restrict lobby-funded travel by state officials after trips that its advocates say have taken scores of officials to Israel. [cite:masspeace_travel_bill] These sponsors are community organizations, not the national lobby, and their trips are reported, where they are reported at all, only under state rules that vary widely.

Police. A separate set of programs has taken American police officials to Israel since the early 2000s. Three intermediaries ran them: the Anti-Defamation League's National Counter-Terrorism Seminar, the Law Enforcement Exchange Program of the Jewish Institute for National Security of America, founded in 2002, and the Georgia International Law Enforcement Exchange at the University of Georgia, which reports more than 1,100 participants across 290 programs. More than 1,000 American officials, among them police chiefs from Los Angeles, Atlanta and St. Louis County and federal agents, have taken part. [cite:jta_adl_police] [cite:jvl_police] The sponsors describe the trips as briefings and meetings on counterterrorism, not field training. [cite:reason_police] Critics, led by the Jewish Voice for Peace campaign "Deadly Exchange" from 2017, argue that they spread militarized and surveillance-heavy policing; in 2018 Durham, North Carolina became the first American city to bar such exchanges. [cite:wiki_deadlyex] The ADL paused its trips in 2019. [cite:jta_adl_police] No public evidence connects any particular use of force by American police to these programs, and this volume makes no such claim.

What no one records. The gap in the record is structural. Congress requires its members to disclose every privately paid trip; no federal body records the travel of state and local officials, and the states' own rules vary. The people who make decisions about state pension funds, state contracts and state laws on boycotts, the subjects of Part Eight, can therefore travel at a foreign government's or an advocacy group's expense with no record a reader in another state could find. The simulation's account of [scene:cm_s11|the trips below Congress] collects what has been reported. A complete count does not exist.
