The Arms Network in Operation

The network that grew out of the Sonneborn meeting was not a plan on paper. For three years it moved real weapons, real aircraft and real money through a set of front operations and transit countries, and it did so before US authorities had assembled enough of the picture to act.

A common belief holds that American law enforcement knew nothing of any organized Jewish-American effort to arm the Haganah before 1948, and that Israel's founding war was armed only by Israel's own government. The record does not support either part of that belief. Purchases were funneled through "neutral" countries, including Czechoslovakia, Panama, Mexico, Canada and France, before they reached Haganah operatives. [cite:mexico_arms] Academic research on the network documents trucks, radios, aircraft parts and weapons components routed this way specifically to defeat the US and British arms embargo on Palestine. Mexico was among the named transit points, and its role was not incidental to the operation. [cite:mexico_arms] The sources establish the pattern and the countries; they do not provide a full ledger of each shipment's route or quantity.

The most elaborate single operation belonged to Al Schwimmer, a veteran of the US Army Air Transport Command. Schwimmer bought roughly 30 surplus American warplanes. To move them, he built a fictitious Panamanian carrier, Lineas Aereas de Panama, and flew the aircraft via Panama, Brazil and Casablanca toward Israel. For months the scheme evaded scrutiny by both the FBI and the State Department. [cite:schwimmer_wiki] It was not loose, improvised smuggling. One man created an entire airline with a name and a registration in order to carry military aircraft across several countries under the eyes of two federal agencies. How the fictitious airline kept its cover for so long is not detailed in the sources; its existence, scale and outcome are.

The network's cover did not fail because of a breakthrough investigation. It failed because of an accident. By the end of January 1948, a crate labeled "used industrial machinery" broke apart while it was being loaded onto a freighter in New Jersey, and explosives spilled across the dock. It was one of 76 identical crates bound for Palestine. [cite:schwimmer_sdjw] That spill, rather than investigative work, is credited with giving US authorities their first concrete evidence of the smuggling network. [cite:schwimmer_sdjw] This should not be read as proof that the authorities had no suspicions at all beforehand. The narrower point is that this particular accident supplied the first hard evidence.

The legal consequences came later and lasted for decades. Nine men involved in the network were tried in a Los Angeles federal courthouse in 1950. Schwimmer was convicted under the US Neutrality Act and lost his American citizenship. Charlie Winters served 18 months in prison for selling two B-17 bombers to the operation. [cite:schwimmer_wiki] Schwimmer went on to found Israel Aerospace Industries. President Bill Clinton pardoned him in 2001, and President George W. Bush posthumously pardoned Winters in 2008. [cite:schwimmer_wiki] The sources dwell on these two, the best documented of the nine; they do not give the charges or sentences of every defendant.

Taken together, the routes, the airline, the dock accident and the trials show a network that was organized, international and sustained, and that operated against US law rather than with official American support. It also shows that the arming of the future state was well under way long before May 1948. The Haganah's supply lines through the Americas and Europe were one track of preparation. Another track ran through Washington itself, where the question of who would sit in the White House when the decisions came due had already been settled by chance and by a party convention, as the next section describes.
