This part follows the data. Immigration and Customs Enforcement does not run one surveillance system; it buys many, from a software platform that joins records to people-search databases, phone-location trails, social-media monitors, face recognition, phone-cracking devices and, for a time, spyware. The first section lists those contracts one by one, with dates, values and the administration that signed each, because the stack was built across both parties. The second sets out the law that lets an agency buy data it could not seize without a warrant, what Congress has tried and failed to do about it, and how DHS has used subpoenas and visa vetting to identify critics. The third draws the whole ecosystem on one map, six layers and the joins between them, and grades each join as documented, probable, reported, possible or not supported.
