The Land and the People

"A land without a people for a people without a land"

The phrase was not coined by a Zionist. Its earliest published form appears in an 1843 book by Alexander Keith, a Church of Scotland minister who promoted the "restoration" of the Jews; Lord Shaftesbury used similar wording in 1853, and the first Zionist use came in 1901 from the writer Israel Zangwill. Historians argue over whether it meant literally empty or "without a nation". [cite:aijac_slogan_origin] [cite:meq_land_without_people] Either way, the Ottoman and British censuses record a populated country, mostly Arab, Muslim and Christian. Verdict: false as a description of the land.

"The Palestinian people were invented in the 1960s"

The newspaper Filastin was founded in Jaffa in 1911; Palestinian Arab congresses sent petitions from 1919; Britain counted the country's people in 1922. The PLO was founded in 1964, but the people it claimed to represent were already on the record. Historians date Palestinian national identity to the late Ottoman and Mandate years, the same period in which Jordanian, Syrian and Israeli national identities took shape. [cite:w_name_palestine] Verdict: false.

"The keffiyeh, the flag and the name were all stolen"

The keffiyeh is a regional Arab headdress that became a Palestinian national symbol during the revolt of 1936 to 1939. [cite:w_keffiyeh] The Palestinian flag derives from the flag of the Arab Revolt of 1916, as do the flags of Jordan and other Arab states. [cite:w_pal_flag] [cite:w_arab_revolt_flag] Rome did rename Judaea "Syria Palaestina" after the Bar Kokhba revolt, which many historians read as a punishment of the Jewish rebels; but forms of the name appear in Herodotus in the fifth century BCE, and the Arabic Filastin was used for the region from the early Islamic period. [cite:w_syria_palaestina] [cite:w_name_palestine] Shared symbols are normal among neighbouring nations. Verdict: mostly false, with one half-truth about Rome.

"Jordan is the Palestinian state"

Transjordan was set up as a separate emirate in 1921 and excluded from the Jewish national home provisions of the Mandate in 1922. [cite:w_transjordan] The UN partition plan of 1947 divided only the land west of the Jordan River. Many Palestinians in Jordan arrived as refugees in 1948 and 1967. [cite:w_black_september] Verdict: false.

"Ashkenazi Jews are really Khazars"

A 2012 paper by the geneticist Eran Elhaik argued that Ashkenazi Jews descend largely from the Khazars, a Turkic people whose rulers adopted Judaism around the eighth century. A 2013 study by more than twenty researchers, using 1,774 samples from 106 populations, found no particular similarity between Ashkenazi Jews and the populations of the Khazar region and concluded that Ashkenazi ancestry is mainly Middle Eastern and European. [cite:jewishpress_khazar] [cite:nli_behar_khazar] Later genome-wide and ancient-DNA studies model Ashkenazim as a mix of Levantine and European ancestry. A viral post claiming that a study proved 97.5 percent of Israeli Jews have no tie to the land was rated false: the study included no Israeli Jews. [cite:aap_khazar_factcheck] The Khazar theory has been used since the nineteenth century to cast Jews as impostors. [cite:adl_khazar] Verdict: false.

Genetics settles no one's rights. Palestinian claims do not depend on the ancestry of Israelis, and the documented record of any Israeli policy does not depend on the ancestry of the people who carry it out.
