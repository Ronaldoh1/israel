The Neighbours

"Arab countries won't take their own people"

Jordan did. Under its 1954 nationality law it gave citizenship to most Palestinians who arrived after 1948, and it hosts more than two million registered refugees, most of them citizens, though Human Rights Watch has documented arbitrary revocations. [cite:hrvoices_jordan] [cite:adl_refugees] Lebanon hosts about 400,000 registered refugees, about a tenth of its population; Palestinians there were long barred from dozens of professions and, under a 2001 law, from owning property, measures defended as preventing permanent settlement and tied to Lebanon's sectarian balance. [cite:fmr_lebanon] [cite:aj_lebanon_property] Egypt and the Gulf states give residency without a path to citizenship. The Arab League told its members not to naturalise refugees, to protect their identity and their right of return; critics, including Israeli analysts, say this kept refugees in limbo as a political tool. [cite:jns_arab_citizenship]

After 7 October 2023, Egypt and Jordan refused to take in Gazans. Their stated reason was that Palestinians who leave have historically not been allowed back, so a transfer would make displacement permanent; Egypt also cited the risk of militants in Sinai. [cite:pbs_egypt_refugees] A popular version says they refuse because "Palestinians bring terrorism". Jordan did fight the PLO in September 1970 and expelled its leadership, but it did not expel its Palestinian population. [cite:w_black_september] Verdict: partly true; the claim leaves out Jordan's citizens and the stated reason for refusing a transfer.

"Egypt shares a border with Gaza and sends no aid"

Rafah, on the Egyptian border, was the first crossing into Gaza to reopen after the war began, and the UN said fuel for the whole relief operation came through it. [cite:jordannews_rafah] Israeli forces took the crossing on 7 May 2024 and it closed; Egypt refused to operate it until it returned to Palestinian control, and aid was routed through Kerem Shalom. [cite:malaymail_rafah] [cite:dailyalert_rafah_egypt] Rafah reopened under the ceasefire on 2 February 2026, with Israeli troops in control and only a handful of people allowed through at first. [cite:sq_npr_rafah_2026] Status as of 26 November 2026: in the week to 19 November it was open to passengers on three days and closed on four. [cite:sq_ncf_index] Verdict: false.

"Egypt built a wall, so even Muslims fear Muslims"

Egypt began an underground steel barrier on the Gaza border in 2009 and later razed homes in Rafah to make a buffer zone against tunnels. [cite:w_egypt_gaza_barrier] These were security measures by a state fighting militants in Sinai. A border policy does not reveal a religious motive; by the same logic Israel's fence would. Verdict: true fact, false conclusion.

"Israeli passports are banned in a dozen countries. Where is your outrage?"

Several states restrict entry on Israeli passports, among them Iran, Lebanon, Syria, Iraq, Libya, Yemen, Kuwait, Algeria, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Brunei and, with exceptions, Malaysia; counts vary by source and year. [cite:w_isr_passport] The reply often heard, that the West refused Jewish refugees in the 1930s, is also true and documented in this library. Both facts stand. Neither answers a question about the other. Verdict: true, and beside the point.
