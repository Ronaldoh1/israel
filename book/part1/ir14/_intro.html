Part Fourteen counts what the long conflict with Iran has cost the United States and asks how it came to pay it. The Hezbollah volume and The One-Way Mirror have already set out the deaths caused by Iran's allies in Lebanon and Iraq, the defence of Israel in 2024 and 2025, and the two wars of 2025 and 2026; this part does not repeat them. It adds the direct American–Iranian record they leave out, from the embassy hostages of 1979 to the tanker war of 1987–88 and the airliner shot down by an American cruiser, the Iraq war and the killing of Qassem Soleimani, and the price of the 2026 war at American petrol pumps. It then separates three questions the phrase "dragged in" runs together: who argued for war with Iran, who decided on it, and what Congress and the public did, and it tests the thesis against what the record shows about who began each American action.
