Gaza: Control Without Responsibility, and the Question of Who Governs

Gaza's two million people have never held Israeli status of any kind. What has changed over six decades is the form of Israel's control over them, and with it the argument over what Israel owes them in law. This section traces that form from direct military rule to the "Yellow Line" of October 2026, and sets out the legal positions on each side. The Gaza volume holds the full history, the war since 2023 and its toll; this section links to it rather than repeating it. From military rule to "disengagement"

Israel captured Gaza from Egypt in 1967 and governed it directly, as it did the West Bank. The Oslo agreements of the 1990s created the Palestinian Authority: the Gaza–Jericho agreement of 4 May 1994 gave it civil powers in most of the strip, while Israeli troops stayed at the settlements and the Rafah crossing. [cite:gz_wiki_gazajericho] Oslo described the West Bank and Gaza as "a single territorial unit, whose integrity will be preserved". In practice movement between them narrowed in steps. Israel ended general exit permits in 1991; in 2000 it barred Gazans from studying in the West Bank and froze changes of address in the population registry. In September 2000 more than 500,000 exits through the Erez crossing were recorded in one month; by 2013 the figure was about 3,000 permits a month. Gisha, an Israeli group that tracks movement, said "It's not about security." [cite:hm_aj_separation]

In September 2005 Israel withdrew its settlers and soldiers from Gaza. It kept control of Gaza's airspace and territorial waters, of six of its seven land crossings, and of the population registry that decides who counts as a resident of Gaza; it kept the right to re-enter militarily; and the strip remained dependent on Israel for water, electricity and telecommunications. [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] After Hamas won the Palestinian legislative election of January 2006 and took control of Gaza by force in June 2007, Israel imposed an indefinite blockade, citing weapons smuggling and rocket fire, and Egypt closed the Rafah crossing. [cite:hm_wiki_2006elect] [cite:gz_wiki_blockade] [scene:gz4_mechanics|Gaza, Documented: how the blockade worked] Approval of exit permits for medical treatment fell from 90 per cent in 2006 to 62 per cent in 2016. [cite:gz_un_gaza10]

The legal argument over occupation

Whether Gaza remained occupied after 2005 decides what Israel must provide there. Israel's Supreme Court, in the Al-Bassiouni case of 30 January 2008, held that "since September 2005 Israel no longer has effective control over what happens in the Gaza Strip" and therefore has no "general duty to ensure the welfare" of its residents; it found narrower obligations arising from the armed conflict, Israel's control of the crossings, and the residents' essential humanitarian needs, and upheld cuts to fuel and electricity on that basis. [cite:gz_ahmed_hcj] The United Nations, rights groups and most legal scholars took the opposite view, that Gaza remained occupied. [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] The International Court of Justice, in July 2024, took a middle course: Israel's obligations under the law of occupation in Gaza after 2005 were "commensurate with the degree of its effective control". [cite:gz_lieber_icj] [cite:gz_oj_icj] In a further advisory opinion of 22 October 2025 the Court said that Israel, as occupying power, must "ensure the basic needs of the local population" and allow relief schemes by the United Nations, including UNRWA; the opinion is not binding, and Israel's foreign ministry said it "categorically rejects" it. [cite:gz_ji_ao2025] [scene:gz7_courts|Gaza, Documented: the courts]

The war and the people counted

The war that began with the Hamas-led attack of 7 October 2023 changed both the population and the territory. As of 12 October 2026 Gaza's health ministry counted 74,382 people killed since October 2023, 1,493 of them after the ceasefire of 10 October 2025; the ministry's count does not separate civilians from combatants. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] [cite:gz_yahoo_idf71k] Part Thirteen of the Greater Israel volume and the Gaza volume set out the counts and how each body arrives at them. [scene:gi14_gaza|Greater Israel: the ledger in Gaza] [scene:gz7_toll|Gaza, Documented: the toll] The Palestinian statistics bureau estimated Gaza's population at 2.13 million at the end of 2025, about 254,000 or 10.6 per cent below its pre-war estimate. [cite:us9_pcbs_2025] In 2012 the UN had projected 2.1 million by 2020. [cite:gz_un_gaza2020] UN figures for 2026 give the conditions of those who remain: 1.98 million people, 94 per cent, in need of shelter assistance, and 371,888 of 485,361 housing units destroyed or damaged. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap]

Who governs Gaza, as of October 2026

A year after the ceasefire, Gaza had no government chosen by its residents and no single authority in charge. Human Rights Watch reported that reconstruction had not begun, and that the area behind the Yellow Line, which Israeli officials have called a "new border", covered about 64 per cent of the strip. [cite:gz_hrw_anniv] Al Jazeera put Israel's share at nearly two-thirds, against about half it was to keep under the ceasefire, and reported that no international stabilisation force had deployed. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] Hamas said on 6 July 2026 that it had dissolved the body that governed Gaza and was ready to hand power to a technocratic committee headed by Ali Shaath, which had stayed outside Gaza for months, reportedly because of Israeli objections. [cite:gz_aj_hamas_dissolve] Above both sits the Board of Peace, announced by President Donald Trump on 15 January 2026 and welcomed by the UN Security Council, which Trump chairs for life; critics say it gives Palestinians no say. [cite:gz_wiki_bop] [scene:gz11_board|Gaza, Documented: the Board of Peace] Movement out of the strip remains in Israeli hands: when the Rafah crossing reopened for people on 1 February 2026, returns were limited to those who had left during the war, "only after prior security clearance by Israel". [cite:gi15_almon_rafah]

The vote, too, stops at Israel's line. The Palestinian Authority's election commission said in October 2026 that Israel was blocking ballot boxes and voting ink from entering Gaza, and on 10 October Abbas postponed the legislative elections set for 28 November to September 2027. [cite:us9_nat_elect] [cite:us9_kt_postpone] Israeli officials say Hamas keeps violating the ceasefire, and Israel's foreign minister called the Authority "corrupt" over the delay. On disarmament, the accounts differ: the UN's envoy, Nickolay Mladenov, told the Security Council in August 2026 that Hamas had accepted an agreement covering every category of weapon, while a Hamas spokesman said it covered heavy weapons only, and Mladenov said Israeli strikes were delaying the process. [cite:gz_ih_mladenov] [cite:us9_kt_postpone] Whatever the cause, the record for 2026 is that the people of Gaza lived under the military control of a state in which they cannot vote, beside an Authority that has not held an election in twenty years, under a board on which they have no seat.
