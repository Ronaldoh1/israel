The Red Sea War, 2023–2026

After 7 October 2023 the Houthis did what no other member of Iran's network did: they took the Gaza war to a global shipping lane. From November 2023 they attacked commercial and naval vessels in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden, which they said showed solidarity with the Palestinians, and they fired missiles at Israel, many of them intercepted by Israeli or US forces. [cite:hz10_crs_if12587] Their stated condition was consistent throughout: the attacks were support for Gaza and would stop when the war on Gaza stopped. [cite:hz10_aje_truce] [cite:hz10_aje_rahawi]

The first act set the pattern. On 19 November 2023 Houthi forces seized the car carrier Galaxy Leader and took its 25 crew; they were held for more than a year and freed in January 2025. [cite:hz10_un_poe2024] [cite:hz10_olj_factbox] The Houthis said they targeted ships linked to Israel. The UN Panel of Experts, counting at least 134 attacks between 15 November 2023 and 31 July 2024 on merchant ships and US and British warships, found instead that "the Houthis have been indiscriminately targeting vessels navigating in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden." [cite:hz10_un_poe2024] Many shipping companies diverted around Africa, taking longer and costlier routes. [cite:hz10_crs_if12587]

By the count Reuters compiled in July 2025, the Houthis had targeted more than 100 merchant ships, sunk four, seized one and killed at least eight sailors. The Rubymar, struck in February 2024, sank on 2 March 2024, the first ship lost; three seafarers were killed on the True Confidence that March; the Tutor sank in June 2024 with one crew member never found; and in July 2025, after months of quiet, the Houthis sank the Magic Seas on 6 July and the Eternity C on 9 July, killing four of the Eternity C's crew and leaving eleven missing. [cite:hz10_olj_factbox] Counts differ by window and by method, which is why other tallies run higher or lower.

The American and British response

On 18 December 2023 Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin announced Operation Prosperity Guardian, a multinational naval mission "to jointly address security challenges in the southern Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden," naming Britain, Bahrain, Canada, France, Italy, the Netherlands, Norway, the Seychelles and Spain as partners, though some contributed only staff officers. [cite:hz10_dn_opg] On 11–12 January 2024 the United States and Britain began striking Houthi targets in Yemen; President Biden called the strikes a response to the attacks on shipping and said he "will not hesitate" to order more. [cite:hz10_mm_biden2024] [cite:hz10_crs_if12587] The strikes continued through 2024 without stopping the attacks, which ran until December 2024 and paused in early 2025. [scene:hm12_ceasefires|The Gaza ceasefires] [cite:hz10_usni_crs2026]

Israel and the Houthis also fought each other directly. On 19 July 2024 a Houthi drone killed one person in central Tel Aviv near the US embassy; the next day Israel struck the port of Hodeidah, which an Israeli official called the main entry point for Iranian weapons. The Houthi-run health ministry reported 80 wounded. Defense Minister Yoav Gallant said the Houthis had attacked Israel more than 200 times. [cite:hz10_nbc_hodeidah2024]

Rough Rider and the Oman truce

The second Trump administration escalated. Operation Rough Rider began on 15 March 2025. US officials told NBC News it cost more than $1 billion by early May and used roughly 2,000 bombs and missiles, including at least 75 Tomahawks; the Houthis shot down seven US drones, and two US fighter jets were lost at sea. [cite:hz10_nbc_rrcost] The US military said it struck more than 800 targets. [cite:hz10_cfr_iranhouthis]

By the count of the monitoring group Airwars, the civilian toll was the heaviest of any American campaign in Yemen. On 17 April 2025 US strikes on the Ras Isa fuel terminal killed at least 74 people and injured 171, according to the Houthi-run health ministry, which Reuters could not verify; Central Command said the aim was "to degrade the economic source of power of the Houthis." [cite:hz10_gc_rasisa] On 28 April a US strike hit a migrant detention centre in Saada. Houthi authorities told Amnesty International that 61 of 117 detained migrants were killed and 56 injured; Amnesty found no evidence the site was a military objective and called for the strike to be investigated as a war crime. Central Command replied that it was "assessing all reports of civilian harm." [cite:hz10_ai_saada] Airwars documented at least 224 civilians allegedly killed by US strikes in the roughly seven weeks of Rough Rider, against 258 in all US actions in Yemen from November 2002 to January 2025. [cite:hz10_airwars_rr]

On 6 May 2025 President Trump said the Houthis had "capitulated" and the bombing would stop. Oman's foreign minister announced that "neither side will target the other," and the State Department said the deal covered US shipping, not the conflict between the Houthis and Israel. [cite:hz10_aje_truce] Israeli officials said they had no advance notice; Prime Minister Netanyahu said Israel "will defend itself by itself." [cite:hz10_wiki_truce2025] Neither the Congressional Research Service in July 2026 nor later monitoring reported a resumption of direct US–Houthi hostilities. [cite:hz10_usni_crs2026] [cite:hz10_ctp_sep26]

Israel's war with the Houthis, and the pause test

The Houthis said the US truce did not apply to Israel, and the exchange continued. [cite:hz10_aje_truce] On 24 August 2025 Israeli strikes on Sanaa killed 10 people, according to health officials; Israel said it hit military sites and the presidential palace. On 28 August a strike on Sanaa killed the Houthi prime minister, Ahmed al-Rahawi, and several ministers. Israel's military called the target "a Houthi terrorist regime military target," and Defense Minister Israel Katz reportedly said he sought to target the Houthi leadership as Israel had other groups' leaders. [cite:hz10_aje_rahawi]

The record allows one test of the Houthis' stated condition. They paused shipping attacks in early 2025, during the first Gaza ceasefire, and they suspended attacks on Israel after the October 2025 Gaza ceasefire. [cite:hz10_usni_crs2026] When the United States and Israel went to war with Iran on 28 February 2026, the Houthis held back for a month, then fired ballistic missiles at southern Israel on 28 March, saying they targeted only "the Israeli and American enemy." [cite:hz10_na_houthis2026] None of the roughly ten launches recorded between 28 March and 9 June caused reported injuries in Israel, and the attacks paused after the April ceasefire in the Iran war before brief launches in June. [cite:hz10_wiki_houthi2026] The pattern fits both the Houthis' Gaza condition and their alignment with Iran; it does not on its own show which drove the decision.

Their next war was with Saudi Arabia. After strikes on Sanaa airport that they blamed on Riyadh, the Houthis declared a maritime blockade of Saudi Arabia on 20 July 2026, attacked Saudi tankers and energy sites, and forced Aramco to suspend its Jazan refinery; Saudi Arabia formed a Red Sea coalition with 13 other states. [cite:hz10_usni_crs2026] [cite:hz10_lwj_saudi2026] As of early October 2026 the 2022 truce has collapsed: Houthi and Saudi-backed government forces are fighting around Taiz and near the Bab al-Mandab, with 80 combatants reported killed in a single day; the Houthis say Saudi aircraft have carried out 1,350 strikes and a market raid near Taiz that killed or wounded nearly 50, figures not independently verified. [cite:hz10_ahram_taiz] [cite:hz10_dawn_taiz] The World Food Programme says nearly three-quarters of Yemenis cannot meet basic food needs. [cite:hz10_ahram_taiz] [scene:hz12_costs|Part Twelve counts the American costs.]
