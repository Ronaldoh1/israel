From AZEC to AIPAC

AIPAC, the American Israel Public Affairs Committee, is often thought to have appeared suddenly in the 1950s as a new, independent lobbying group with no earlier history. The familiar version stops there. The institutional record does not. It shows an unbroken line of descent from the wartime American Zionist Emergency Council, or AZEC. It also shows a federal foreign-agent case along the way, and that case must be stated precisely, because it concerned a successor body, not the original one.

The American Zionist Emergency Council, built under Abba Hillel Silver in 1943, did not dissolve after statehood. In 1949 it was renamed the American Zionist Council and registered as a tax-exempt nonprofit. [cite:azec_powerbase] This was an ordinary continuation, not the founding of a new organization.

The foreign-agent question arose more than a decade later. On 21 November 1962 the Internal Security Section of the Justice Department, under Attorney General Robert Kennedy, ordered the American Zionist Council to register as an agent of a foreign principal under the Foreign Agents Registration Act. The department alleged that the Council had received more than $5 million from the Jewish Agency over the preceding eight years. [cite:azc_fara_doj] The order was aimed at the 1949 successor, not at the 1943 body. It was an allegation against one organization at one time, later withdrawn, and it should not be read back across the whole lineage as one continuous violation.

The Justice Department did later withdraw its demand. In 1963 the Council's lobbying work was split off into a newly restructured organization, the American Israel Public Affairs Committee. AIPAC was set up to run solely on private American donations, and on that basis it maintained that it was not a foreign agent. [cite:aipac_wiki]

The sequence is short, and every step is documented: a wartime council, a new name in 1949, a foreign-agent order in 1962, its withdrawal, and a restructuring in 1963. Together these steps answer the question of where AIPAC came from. It was not a new creation without a past. It was the lobbying arm of an organization whose roots ran back to the campaign of the 1940s, reorganized after a federal challenge so that its money came from American donors alone.
