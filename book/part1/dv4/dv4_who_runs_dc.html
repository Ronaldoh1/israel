Who Actually Runs Washington: Home Rule and the Federal Hand, 1973–2026

Since 1974 Washington has elected a mayor and a council, and on most days the city looks like any other: it collects taxes, runs schools, hires police and fixes roads. The Home Rule Act gave it a council of thirteen, a chair and four members elected at large and one from each of eight wards, serving four-year terms. [cite:dv4_wiki_homerule] But the Act was a charter granted by Congress, not a constitution adopted by the city, and Congress wrote into it a set of limits that no state imposes on its own cities in the same combination. This section sets out those limits, how they have been used, and the events of 2025 and 2026, when the federal hand closed more tightly on the city than at any time since Home Rule began.

What the council may not do. The Act forbids the council to impose a commuter tax on income earned in the district by people who live in Maryland or Virginia, to change the Height of Buildings Act of 1910, to change the composition or jurisdiction of the local courts, to lend public credit for private projects, or to pass an unbalanced budget. It gives the council no authority over the National Capital Planning Commission, the Washington Aqueduct that supplies the city's water, or the District of Columbia National Guard. [cite:dv4_wiki_homerule] [cite:dv4_dcvote_2025] Each of these is a power that an ordinary city either has or can be given by its state. The commuter tax is the clearest case: the district may not tax income earned inside it by people who live in the neighbouring states. [cite:dv4_dcvote_2025]

Congress's two vetoes. Every act of the council must lie before Congress before it takes effect: thirty days for most laws and sixty for laws on crime and criminal procedure. [cite:dv4_dcvote_2025] [cite:dv1_crs_dc_homerule] Congress can block a law in two ways. The formal route is a joint resolution of disapproval, which has succeeded four times: in 1979 against a law on the location of embassies, in 1981 against a sexual-assault reform, in 1990 against an amendment to the height rules, and in 2023 against the district's Revised Criminal Code Act, disapproved by a resolution signed on 20 March 2023. [cite:dv4_wiki_homerule] [cite:dv1_crs_in12122] The more common route is the budget. The district's budget, even the part paid for entirely by local taxes, must pass through the federal appropriations process, and Congress has used riders in those bills to forbid the city to spend its own money on, among other things, a needle exchange and the commercial sale of marijuana. [cite:dv4_dcvote_2025] The review applies by statute to every act the council passes.

Courts and prosecutors the city does not choose. The district's local courts, the Superior Court and the Court of Appeals, were created by Congress in 1970 under its Article I powers. Their judges are appointed by the President with the consent of the Senate, from three names per vacancy sent up by the District's Judicial Nomination Commission, and serve fifteen-year terms. [cite:dv4_crs_dc_courts] In Atlanta, serious crimes are prosecuted by the Fulton County District Attorney, an officer elected by the county's voters for four-year terms. [cite:dv1_wiki_fulton_da] In Washington, most crimes investigated by the city's own police are prosecuted by the United States Attorney for the District of Columbia, an official appointed by the President; the District's own Attorney General prosecutes only a limited set, such as juvenile cases and violations of municipal regulations. [cite:dv4_crs_dc_courts] People sentenced to more than a year in prison go not to a city or state prison but to the Federal Bureau of Prisons. [cite:dv4_crs_dc_courts] And the courts themselves are funded through the same congressional appropriations subcommittee that funds the Treasury and the White House. [cite:dv4_crs_dc_courts] When the Superior Court had twelve of its judgeships empty in November 2023, nearly a fifth of its bench, only the President and the Senate could fill them. [cite:dv4_crs_dc_courts]

The emergency clause, used. Section 740 of the Home Rule Act lets the President, when he finds that "special conditions of an emergency nature exist," require the mayor to provide the services of the Metropolitan Police for federal purposes; he must notify Congress within forty-eight hours, and the arrangement lapses after thirty days unless Congress approves it. [cite:dv4_wiki_eo14333] For half a century no President used it. On 11 August 2025 President Trump signed Executive Order 14333, declaring a crime emergency in the District, and invoked Section 740 for the first time. [cite:dv4_wiki_eo14333] The source that records the order notes that the district's crime statistics at the time showed a thirty-year low and a decline through 2024 and 2025. [cite:dv4_wiki_eo14333] The District sued on 15 August; Congress did not extend the federal direction of the police, and it expired on 10 September 2025. [cite:dv4_wiki_eo14333]

The troops stayed. The DC National Guard answers to the President, not to the mayor, and in August 2025 he deployed it, joined by Guard units sent by the governors of several states, among them Ohio, South Carolina, West Virginia, Mississippi, Louisiana and Tennessee. [cite:dv4_wiki_eo14333] After two West Virginia Guardsmen were shot in November 2025 he ordered 500 more. [cite:dv4_wiki_eo14333] On 20 November 2025 Judge Jia Cobb of the federal district court ruled that the deployment unlawfully intruded on local officials' authority over law enforcement; the President could protect federal functions and property, she held, but could not use the Guard on his own authority for crime control or bring in troops from other states. [cite:dv4_wbal_guard_2026] The D.C. Circuit stayed her ruling in December 2025, citing the President's "unique power" to mobilize the Guard in Washington. [cite:dv4_wbal_guard_2026] [cite:dv4_wiki_eo14333] When a new panel heard the appeal on 14 October 2026, about 2,900 Guard troops were still in the city, down from a summer peak near 5,000, and the Defense Department had extended the mission to January 2029. [cite:dv4_wbal_guard_2026] As of 11 November 2026 the appeal is undecided and the troops remain. Earlier, in February 2025, a bill had been introduced in Congress, the BOWSER Act, to repeal the Home Rule Act altogether. [cite:dv4_wiki_homerule]

What this shows about the city. Washington is governed in layers that do not exist anywhere else in the country. Its residents choose a mayor and council; the President chooses its judges and its chief prosecutor and commands its militia; and Congress, in which its residents have no vote, can overrule its laws and spend or withhold its tax money. [cite:dv4_wiki_homerule] [cite:dv4_crs_dc_courts] For most of the period since 1973 the upper layers have pressed lightly. In 2025 and 2026 they pressed hard, and the city learned how little of its government it holds by right. That is the anomaly in its plainest form, and it explains why Washington belongs in this volume despite being the least sovereign of the three cities. Its own government is weak because it was built to sit inside a strong one. The power that this volume asks about, the power that recognized, armed and shielded Israel, belongs to that stronger government, which the district exists to house. The rest of this part follows that power.
