Part Eight follows the weapons. The aid figures in Part One are, in practice, a flow of American-made bombs, shells, aircraft and interceptors, and this part shows how that flow works: the two legal routes by which arms leave the United States, the money that pays for them, the shorter congressional review that Israel shares with NATO and a handful of close allies, and the emergency powers that let an administration skip that review altogether. It measures the pace of deliveries since October 2023 and the one occasion on which an American president held a shipment back. It then turns to the weapons the two countries build together, the missile-defence systems funded by Congress and made partly in American factories, and ends with the rules that are meant to govern how the weapons are used: the Leahy laws, a special vetting forum used only for Israel, and a 2024 presidential memorandum that lasted a year.
