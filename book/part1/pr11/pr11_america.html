How the Dead Were Reported

Studies of the coverage have reached opposite conclusions, and both are part of the record. The Intercept analysed more than 1,100 articles in the New York Times, the Washington Post and the Los Angeles Times from early in the war. It found Israelis mentioned eight times for every Israeli death, a rate sixteen times higher per death than for Palestinians; words such as "slaughter," "massacre" and "horrific" were reserved almost entirely for Israelis killed by Palestinians; and only two headlines mentioned the children of Gaza. [cite:gz_aa_intercept] The Henry Jackson Society, a British think tank, analysed 1,378 articles from February to May 2024 and reached the opposite worry: 98 percent cited the health ministry's figures, 84 percent did not distinguish fighters from civilians, and 19 percent gave the ministry's numbers without saying where they came from. It argued that the ministry, run by a party to the war, should be treated as a contested source, and noted that the army's figures for fighters killed received far less attention than the ministry's. Newsroom Rules

An internal New York Times memo first issued in November 2023 told its journalists to restrict the words "genocide" and "ethnic cleansing," to avoid "occupied territory," to use "Palestine" only in rare cases, and not to call areas settled by refugees "refugee camps." The paper said such guidance was "standard practice" for "accuracy, consistency and nuance." [cite:gz_nl_nytmemo] [cite:gz_dawn_nyt] At CNN, according to the Guardian's reporting from six staff and internal memos, stories had to be cleared by the Jerusalem bureau, quoting Hamas was severely restricted, and, staff said, Israeli government statements were taken at face value; CNN said its reporting had "confronted Israel's response to the attacks." [cite:gz_an_cnnstaff] Every foreign reporter based in Israel works under the military censor. [cite:gz_intercept_cnn]

How the Journalists' Deaths Were Reported

The media watchdog FAIR examined how fifteen Western outlets reported the killing of Anas al-Sharif and his colleagues on 10 and 11 August 2025. All fifteen reported Israel's allegation that he was a Hamas member; four put it in a headline or subheading. Six left out his final message. Only two noted that the other journalists killed with him had not been accused of anything. Thirteen mentioned how many Palestinian journalists had been killed, but only four named Israel as the main cause. Reuters first headlined its story "Israel Kills Al Jazeera Journalist It Says Was Hamas Leader," and later changed it. [cite:pr_fair_sharif]

What Americans Came to Think

In February 2026 Gallup found that, "for the first time in Gallup's annual measurement since 2001, Americans' sympathies no longer lie more with the Israelis": 41 percent sympathized more with the Palestinians and 36 percent with the Israelis, a difference Gallup said was not statistically significant, against 31 and 54 percent three years earlier.
