Palestine was a real, named, populated and governed place long before 1948, even though it was not an independent state. The line leans on one true technicality: the Ottomans named provinces after their capital cities, so no Ottoman unit was officially called "Palestine." [cite:wiki_ottoman_palestine] But the region was administered as three districts that map closely onto the later Mandate boundary, and from 1872 the district of Jerusalem reported directly to Constantinople because of its importance. [cite:wiki_mutasarrifate_jerusalem] Roughly 600,000 people lived there by the late 19th century, in cities such as Jerusalem, Gaza, Jaffa, Hebron and Nablus. [cite:ips_ottoman_palestine]

The name itself goes back to 135 CE, when Rome renamed the province Syria Palaestina after crushing the Bar Kokhba revolt. [cite:wiki_syria_palaestina] From 1922 to 1948, "Palestine" was a polity in international law, with its own citizenship order (1925), about 70,000 passports, the Palestine pound (1927) and its own stamps.
