The Gnostic teachers claimed that salvation came through knowledge of one's origin, that ordinary church members were a second rank of "animal" people, and, in the Gospel of Philip's words, that one who saw Christ became Christ. [cite:vt24_ah1_06] [cite:vt24_gphil] That claim placed a source of authority outside the bishops' office, which is why their opponents treated it as a threat. [cite:vt24_enc_pagels] The single religion called "Gnosticism", however, is largely a later construction: the noun dates from the seventeenth century, and Williams and King have shown how poorly it fits the evidence.
