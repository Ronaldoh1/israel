Several words in this history carry arguments inside them. This chapter examines them before the story uses them: the name "Israel," the term "Semitic," the word "antisemitism," and the symbols on the state's flag and seal. It comes after the land and before the peoples because later chapters cannot report what anyone said or did without these words, and because each has a documented history that is shorter, or longer, than people tend to assume.

It opens with Genesis 32, where the name first appears, given to one man, Jacob, at verse 28, and formed from El and a root meaning "to strive."[cite:genesis_32_biblegateway] [cite:wiki_israel_name] A second section offers a symbolic reading of the name, IS–RA–EL, labeled from its first line to its last as a reading and not a finding. Then comes the word "Semitic," coined by Schlözer in 1781 for a family of languages that includes Hebrew, Arabic and Amharic.[cite:wiki_semitic_etymology] Before the history of the word "antisemitism" comes the hatred it names, which is far older: the blood libel from Norwich in 1144, the badge decrees, the expulsion from England in 1290, Damascus in 1840, Kishinev in 1903 and the forged Protocols.[cite:rs_ch01_ushmm_bloodlibel] [cite:rs_ch01_ushmm_protocols] The word itself follows, from Renan's "Semitic race" to Wilhelm Marr's League of Antisemites in 1879.[cite:wiki_marr_1879] A section on symbols sets the menorah against the Star of David and traces the flag from Basel in 1897 to its confirmation on 28 October 1948.[cite:wiki_flag_of_israel] The chapter ends with the word as a shield: the IHRA definition and its rivals, its path from guideline toward law, and the record number of antisemitic incidents the ADL counted in 2024.[cite:peacenow_ihra_faq] [cite:algemeiner_adl_2024]

Two conclusions run through it. The root of "antisemitism" is a misnomer, and its meaning, hatred of Jews, is settled. Whether the word also reaches criticism of a state is the live dispute, and the chapter sets out who holds each position without deciding between them.
