A Map of the Record

Every stage of this book rests on documents: letters, contracts, votes, treaties, budgets, court records and the accounts of the people involved. Read in order, they tell a story. Laid out together, they form a network: people, organizations, governments and documents, each tied to the others by an act the record names. The companion network map of the Road to 1948 and After is that network drawn whole, from the Rothschild houses of the eighteenth century to the Cold War. Every node can be opened to show who it was and what it did, and every tie shows the sentence and the source that establish it.

The map holds only ties with a source. A tie is verified where the cited page states the act directly, and reported where it states it only indirectly or attributes it to someone else's account; reported ties count for a little over half as much. Names that merely appear together on a page are kept for searching but count for nothing, and so do ties that rest on a single source, which the map marks as still to be verified. Ties are never drawn from who someone was, only from what the documents say they did. The same rule applies to every node, whatever its side.

Most of the map is drawn from the stages of this book. In November 2026 eight nodes from the years before Herzl were added from encyclopedias and histories, with nineteen ties: Charles Netter, Adolphe Crémieux, the Alliance Israélite Universelle, the Odessa Committee, the pogroms of 1881, the May Laws, the Damascus affair of 1840 and the Mortara case of 1858. Each of their ties lists its own sources on the map, and the network they belong to is now told in [scene:rd0_network|the first network, 1836–1896]. Checking them corrected two common errors: Crémieux was the Alliance's president from 1863, not one of its founders, and Kalischer's letter of 1836 went to Amschel Mayer Rothschild before he approached Montefiore. Five of the new ties carry weight in the calculations, each at the weight the map gives a reported tie with no stated sum; the rest record causes or links the map cannot weigh. Laid out this way, the record can be questioned in a way a narrative cannot. Take any node out and ask what would have been left. To answer, the map needs a measure of what it took to found a state. It uses seven things, each drawn from the record of the stages before this one:

Money counts twice. Every other channel depended on it: land was bought, arms were bought, and the organizations ran on dues and appeals. The record states the sums for the largest flows, and they are converted to 1948 dollars. Edmond de Rothschild was reported in 1934 to have "invested over sixty million dollars in Palestine," which at 1913 prices is about $146 million of 1948. [cite:f_jta_baron] The Keren Hayesod took in £4,714,440 between April 1921 and July 1932, about half of it from the United States. [cite:f_jta_kh33] The United Palestine Appeal's share of the American campaigns was $52 million in 1946 and $74.7 million in 1948. [cite:f_jta_uja46] [cite:f_jta_uja48] Golda Meyerson raised about $50 million on her American tour of early 1948. [cite:m_jwa_golda] Where a money tie gives no sum, it is counted at a nominal $1 million, an assumption the map discloses wherever it is used.

The other channels are weighed by connection: a tie between two well-connected nodes counts for more than a tie between two isolated ones, up to twice as much. In diplomacy and recognition a great power counts for more than a small state. The five permanent members of the UN Security Council are weighed by the size of their economies in 1950, in Angus Maddison's estimates: the United States at 1,455,916 million 1990 international dollars, the Soviet Union at 510,243, Britain at 347,850, China at 244,985 and France at 220,492. [cite:m_maddison] Relative to France, the United States counts 6.6 times, the Soviet Union 2.3, Britain 1.6 and China 1.1; every other state counts once.

The seventh channel was added in November 2026, because the first six could not register what the first volume of this library, The Baseline, is about: the moral weight of the Holocaust and the public sympathy, religious attachment and political endorsement that made the founding hard to refuse. It holds only documented ties in which that support moved a decision-maker. The Harrison Report on the Jewish survivors in the displaced-persons camps reached Truman on 24 August 1945; he forwarded it to the British prime minister and asked Britain to admit 100,000 of them to Palestine, and Britain set up the Anglo-American Committee of Inquiry, which in April 1946 recommended the same number. [cite:lg_ushmm_harrison] On 19 July 1947 the chairman of the UN's Palestine committee and its Yugoslav member went to Haifa to see the refugee ship Exodus 1947 arrive, and in August three members toured the camps in Europe. [cite:lg_unscop37] [cite:lg_jta_dp] In the United States, Congress endorsed the Balfour Declaration in 1922, and the American Palestine Committee, which by the 1940s counted two-thirds of the Senate among its members, worked for the congressional resolution of 1945. [cite:lg_wiki_lodgefish] [cite:lg_wiki_apc] Eleven such ties carry weight. Two earlier ones, Lord Shaftesbury's appeal to Palmerston in 1839 and William Blackstone's petition to President Harrison in 1891, are on the map but count for nothing, because the record states no route from them to the bodies that founded the state.

The channel is weighed like the others except money, and it is measured against its own full record, so that adding it changed neither the baseline nor any earlier test, except where a test removes one of its ties. How much legitimacy should weigh beside money or arms is a judgment, not a finding, and the limits at the end of this appendix say so. The search for central-bank ties added five more nodes at the same time; it is described in [scene:rd7_central|following the money to the central banks].

With every tie in place the map reads each channel as complete, and the chance of a founding in 1948 as about 93 percent (93.2, unchanged by the additions of November 2026). That figure is a calibration, not a finding: it says only that on the full record the state was founded, with a margin left for what the record does not hold. Every test in this appendix is read against it.
