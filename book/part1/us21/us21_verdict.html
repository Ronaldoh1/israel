The Aid in That Light, the Claims Tested and What Is Open

The question this volume set out to answer is usually asked in one sentence: why does the United States send money to Israel when Americans lack things Israelis have? The ledger in the previous section shows what each system provides and fails to provide, measure by measure. [scene:us21_ledger|The ledger] This section sets the aid against that record, tests the claims most often made about it and about the two systems, states what the comparison can and cannot show, and lists what remains open as of October 2026, days before Israel votes on 27 October and the United States on 3 November.

What the aid is

Under the memorandum of understanding signed on 14 September 2016, the United States committed $38 billion over its fiscal years 2019 to 2028: $33 billion in Foreign Military Financing (FMF) and $5 billion for missile defence, or $3.3 billion and $500 million a year, running to 30 September 2028. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] [cite:ow1_wh_mou2016] It is military money. The economic grants Israel once received, about $1.2 billion a year through the 1990s, fell to $44 million in 2008, and none are listed from 2020. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] It is paid as a lump sum in the first month of the fiscal year into an interest-bearing account at the Federal Reserve. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] And it is spent mostly in the United States: the share Israel may spend on equipment made in Israel, 26.3 per cent under the 2007 agreement, was $450.3 million in fiscal 2025 and ends entirely in fiscal 2028, and Israeli researchers at INSS concluded in February 2026 that the aid now goes almost entirely to procurement in America. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] [cite:gi16_inss_aid] The White House said at signing that ending offshore procurement and fuel purchases would move up to $1.2 billion a year more into American-made goods. [cite:ow1_wh_mou2016] The aid is therefore at once a transfer to Israel's defence budget and a purchase from American industry. [scene:us1_aid|Part One: the aid] [scene:gi16_aid|The aid in detail]

What it frees, and what it weighs then and now

What the aid frees in Israel's budget is the weapons it buys, which Israel would otherwise have had to pay for with its own shekels, delay or do without; to the extent Israel would have bought them anyway, an equal sum of its own money is left free for other uses, military or civilian. The money freed is at most the grant itself, and nothing in the record ties it to any particular Israeli programme. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:gi16_inss_aid] Israel's Finance Ministry books the grant as income: about ₪22 billion of the 2026 defence budget of about $44.8 billion is "expenditure contingent on income, including the U.S. aid grant". [cite:gi16_bd_budget2026] The buy-American rule has a cost of its own, which the Israeli analyst Gazit put at about $600 million a year in 2011, a view the American government has not endorsed. [cite:gi16_cato_bandow] Washington acted on the fungibility argument once, cutting loan guarantees by an amount equal to Israel's settlement spending: by a net $773.8 million in 1994–97, $289.5 million in 2003 and $795.8 million in 2005. [cite:gi16_jvl_loans]

The aid's weight has fallen more than its dollar amount. On Part One's arithmetic from CRS and World Bank data, it equalled 12.7 per cent of Israel's GDP in 1985, when it was partly civilian and helped carry the stabilisation plan, 2.9 per cent in 2000, 1.3 per cent in 2010 and 0.6 per cent in 2025. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:us1_fred_ilgdp] [cite:us11_brookings_peres] Against Israel's defence spending it fell from about 24 per cent of security outlays in 2011, by Gazit's estimate, to "approximately 14%" by the CRS's account and "roughly 15%" by INSS's. [cite:gi16_cato_bandow] [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] [cite:gi16_inss_aid] Fixed in nominal dollars, the $3.8 billion paid in 2026 buys what about $2.7 billion bought in 2016, and about a third of what the aid of 1985 bought, by Part Twenty's conversion. [cite:us20_bls_cpi_annual] [cite:us20_bls_cpi_sep26] In the American budget it is small: about 0.05 per cent of the $7.0 trillion in federal outlays in fiscal 2025, or about $11 for each American resident. [cite:us1_crfb_fy2025] [cite:us1_cbo_fy2025] [cite:us1_wa_uspop] The parts give no figure for its share of the American budget in 1985, and none is estimated here. The wartime money is larger, at least $21.7 billion in military aid in two years by Brown University's Costs of War count, and America's own operations cost more again: Central Command put the 2026 war with Iran at $43.6 billion by 3 September 2026. [cite:gz_cow_aid] [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] [scene:gi16_costs|What the wars cost]

Set beside the ledger, the arithmetic decides the opening question. Every advantage the ledger records for Israel over America, universal health coverage, paid maternity leave, a wage floor tied to the average wage, rests on Israeli laws paid for by Israeli taxes: a dedicated health tax of 3.2 and 5.2 per cent, national insurance contributions, a tax take of 29.8 per cent of GDP. [cite:us1_cmwf_israel] [cite:us18_nii_maternity2018] [cite:us11_oecd_rev_il] Every gap the ledger records for America, from 26.7 million uninsured to a federal wage floor frozen since 2009, is the product of American laws and budgets, and the aid, at 0.05 per cent of federal outlays, is not large enough to explain any of them. [cite:us16_census_2025] [cite:us13_dol_minwage] [cite:us1_crfb_fy2025] Whether the aid is good policy is a separate question of foreign policy and values, which this volume does not answer.

The claims tested

Parts One to Twenty each tested a common claim against the record where one existed. The table brings them together; each finding applies to that claim alone.

The volume also corrected figures carried in earlier material. The simulation's estimate of an Israeli imprisonment rate for ordinary crime of 108 to 122 per 100,000 was its own calculation and is not repeated, because the Prison Service publishes no criminal-only series. [cite:us4_wpb_il] A widely repeated figure for Israel's minimum wage from 1 April 2026 was the 2025 rate; the 2026 rate is NIS 6,443.85. [cite:us13_nii_minwage] Two findings ran against expectation: households in Israel pay a larger share of health costs out of pocket than Americans, 20.5 against 10.9 per cent in 2023, but a smaller amount, because the American bill is so much larger; and Israel's abortion committees, whose existence suggests restriction, approved 99.6 per cent of requests in 2020. [cite:us16_wb_oop] [cite:us18_toi_abortion2022]

What the comparison shows, and what it does not

It compares systems, not peoples. Every gap between groups reported in this volume, Black and white Americans, Arab and Jewish Israelis, Haredi and other pupils, is a measure of how laws, budgets and institutions treat people, with the mechanism named where the record names it, and none describes the people in a group. [scene:us8_israel|Part Eight: Israel] [scene:us8_us|Part Eight: the United States]

It covers different populations unless it says otherwise. Israel's national figures, and Freedom House's score of 73, describe its citizens and residents; the 5.56 million Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza, roughly a third of the people under Israel's control, appear in their own part and in the ledger's last row of gaps, not in Israel's averages. [cite:us1_fh_israel2026] [cite:us9_pcbs_2025] [scene:us9_westbank|Part Nine] The American figures cover a federal country of 341.8 million in which most schooling, policing and much of health and welfare policy are set by fifty states, so national averages hide wide ranges, from $11,060 per pupil in Idaho to $31,918 in New York. [cite:us1_wa_uspop] [cite:us17_census_fy2024]

It depends on definitions. GDP per person stood at 67 per cent of the American level at market exchange rates in 2025 and 62.7 per cent in constant dollars; the poverty rates, the pay gaps (monthly against weekly), union density, the housing ratios and the debt figures are each defined differently in the two countries, and the ledger marks where they cannot be set against each other. [cite:us1_fred_uspc] [cite:ir13_fred_ilpc] [cite:us11_wb_gdppc_const] [cite:us13_oecd_lmf15]

It mixes series and snapshots. Where long series exist for both countries, life expectancy, infant mortality, GDP, incarceration, defence spending, the comparison over time is firm. Where they exist for only one, the American side is often the better documented: this book found no published Israeli series on people killed by police, homicide before 2009, household wealth by group, homeownership, student debt or surveillance counts, and it did not compare unemployment benefits at all. [cite:us4_mpv_2025] [cite:us5_taub_homicide] [cite:us8_epi_wealth] [cite:us10_odni_astr2026] Several Israeli series break, the prison count in 2006 and the poverty survey in 2011–12, and are read on each side of the break. [cite:us4_wpb_il] [cite:us18_nii_pov2018] A missing series is a gap in the record, not evidence that nothing is there.

It describes; it does not by itself explain. Outcomes such as life expectancy depend on far more than a single law, and the volume does not claim that Israel's health law alone explains its longer lives, or that American zoning alone explains its homelessness. It also gives no overall verdict. The composite scorecard in the platform's earlier simulation chapter on the two systems is not reused here; Part Twenty-Two reproduces it, shows what it could not support, and replaces it with a total whose every measure, goalpost and weight is published, because the measures are not in common units and their weights are a reader's judgment. [scene:us22_method|Part Twenty-Two: the scorecard] [scene:us1_compare|Part One: the rules]

What is open as of October 2026

In Israel, the election of 27 October will seat the Knesset under which the Judicial Selection Law of March 2025 takes effect. [cite:us2_toi_selection2025] Petitions against Basic Law: Torah Study and against the March 2026 death-penalty law were pending with no ruling reported, and no replacement conscription law had been reported after the High Court struck down the arrest-ban law on 3 September 2026. [cite:us2_toi_torahlaw] [cite:us4_wiki_deathlaw] [cite:us19a_jpost_struck] Prime Minister Netanyahu's trial had produced no verdict, plea agreement or pardon in the sources checked. [cite:us2_ynet_trial] [cite:us2_almonitor_pardon] The fate of the bill to tax foreign-government donations to NGOs, of the 2003 family-unification law's latest renewal and of the Arab-community development plan due for renewal in 2026 could not be confirmed. [cite:us3_jpost_ngotax] [cite:us6_adalah_cit2023] [cite:us15_idi_550cut] Palestinian elections were postponed to 11 September 2027, and Israel held about 64 per cent of Gaza behind the Yellow Line. [cite:us9_kt_postpone] [cite:gz_hrw_anniv]

In the United States, the midterms of 3 November choose all 435 members of the House and 35 senators. [cite:us1_wjla_midterms] Section 702 lapsed on 12 June 2026 with collection continuing under certifications valid to 17 March 2027; the border asylum case awaited a possible Supreme Court appeal; the tariff regime after the 150-day window had not been established; the expired marketplace credits and the 2025 law were projected to raise the uninsured count; and the Social Security trust funds were projected to be exhausted in 2034. [cite:us10_bcj_702] [cite:us6_raices_dccir] [cite:us11_ks_ieepa] [cite:us16_kff_obbba] [cite:us18_crs_ss2026] The two central banks were moving in opposite directions, the Bank of Israel cutting to 3.25 per cent on 1 September and the Federal Reserve raising its range to 3.75–4 per cent on 16 September. [cite:us11_globes_boi_sep2026] [cite:us11_fed_sep2026]

Between the two, talks on a framework to replace the 2016 agreement opened on 5 June 2026, with Israel's Defence Ministry aiming to "gradually transition from aid to a completely reciprocal partnership" and Netanyahu saying he wanted the grant to fall toward zero; no agreement had been reported. [cite:ow1_mod_mou2026] [cite:gi16_ji_netanyahu] If none is reached and Congress stops appropriating FMF, Israel would have to pay for existing contracts from its own funds, find other financing or cancel them. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] Both governments that will negotiate it may change within a week of each other.

The answer, measure by measure

Asked "what about America?", the record gives an answer by measure rather than a single verdict. On health coverage, infant survival, life expectancy, paid leave after a birth, public spending on families, a national wage floor, household debt and homelessness, Israel's laws serve its citizens and residents better on the figures this volume found. [cite:us16_census_2025] [cite:us16_wb_imr] [cite:us16_wb_le] [cite:us18_crs_leave] [cite:us18_socx_usa] [cite:us13_dol_minwage] [cite:us14_globes_mortgagegdp] [cite:us15_hud_pit2025] On average pay, school results, spending per pupil, civil marriage and government restrictions on religion, press freedom rank, the burden of compulsory military service, and the share of the people it governs who have no citizenship or national vote, about 1 per cent against roughly a third, the American record is the better one. [cite:us14_oecd_avwage] [cite:us17_ih_pisa2025] [cite:us17_oecd_eag2023_usa] [cite:us7_wiki_marriage] [cite:us7_pew_gri2022] [cite:us3_wboc_rsf] [cite:us19_taub_miluim] [cite:us9_pcbs_2025] On prices the record splits: Israel's food and cars are among the OECD's dearest and its telecoms far cheaper than America's. [cite:us14_toi_food52] Israel's poverty rate is high on the OECD's relative scale, but the parts found no American figure on that scale to set beside it. [cite:us18_nii_pov2023] On incarceration, executions, national homicide and guns, America's record is the harsher, while Israel holds far more people in detention without charge and its homicide rate for Arab citizens is above any American national rate on record. [cite:us4_wpb_us] [cite:us4_dpic_2025] [cite:us5_pew_gundeaths] [cite:us4_hamoked_charts] [cite:us5_taub_homicide] On democracy ratings both declined in this decade; on the corruption index America fell and Israel held roughly steady. [cite:us3_vdem2026] [cite:us2_wiki_cpi] None of these differences is paid for by the aid, which is military money, mostly spent in America, now about 0.6 per cent of Israel's economy and 0.05 per cent of America's budget.
