Burned or Absorbed

The old gods met two fates, and the record names the actors of each. Some were destroyed: by emperors' laws and an emperor's general, by a Gothic army, by conquistadors after gold, by a viceroy, by friars and archdioceses burning books and ancestors, and by a United States department banning dances. [cite:vt21_wiki_theodpagan] [cite:vt33_wiki_telesterion] [cite:vt33_procopius_philae] [cite:vt35_wiki_coricancha] [cite:vt35_wiki_tupacamaru] [cite:vt34_ency_extirp] [cite:vt35_tah_teller] Others were absorbed: temples kept and given new altars by stated papal policy, a festival met by a procession at the same season, a mother's name carried into the Virgin's. [cite:vt36_fordham_mellitus] [cite:vt35_dean_muse] [cite:vt36_sahagun1576]

Three findings stand out. The first is that "the Church" is too small a name for the actors. Emperors, armies, crowns, viceroys and a modern secular government did as much of the destroying as bishops and friars, and in Japan, as The Vatican Architecture records, a state that honoured a sun goddess suppressed Christians. [cite:vt35_ebsco_japan] The second is that absorption was a stated method, not a secret one: Gregory's letter of 601 says so in plain words, and John Paul II praised the same approach when he canonized Juan Diego. [cite:vt36_fordham_mellitus] [cite:vt36_va_jd2002] The third is that the absorbing institution kept a boundary. It raised the Mother as high as a creature can go and refused to call her divine; it gave Wisdom's church to Christ; it raised the Magdalene's feast and kept ordination male. [cite:vt36_vn_mpf2025] [cite:vt36_wiki_sophia] [cite:vt26_va_ordsac1994]

The record also refuses the easiest stories. The claim that every Christian figure is a renamed goddess holds as resemblance far more often than as documented transfer. [cite:vt36_higgins_lactans] The claim that the old religions were all paths of gentle wisdom fails for the Mexica sun, which required human sacrifice. [cite:vt35_smith_tzompantli] What holds is narrower and still large: the sacred places of the old gods were taken, by force or by policy, and the people who kept them found ways to carry them on.
