The Parent Test: What Washington Gave, and What It Withheld

This is the third application of the test set out in Part One: name the city's institutions, list what each supplied, ask whether it was needed, list what each withheld, and date the verdict. [scene:dv1_three_functions|The test] London passed it in part, for the years from 1917 to 1939. Rome did not pass it. Washington's record is the longest and the largest, and it changes most over time, so the dates matter more here than anywhere else.

Which Washington. As with the other cities, the name covers several things. The District of Columbia's own government, its mayor, council and police, appears nowhere in the record of Israel's making; it is a city that cannot overrule its own Congress, let alone shape foreign policy. [cite:dv4_wiki_homerule] The power belongs to the federal government the district houses, and inside that government to separate offices that often disagreed: the President, who alone recognizes states; the State Department and the military, which advised against partition and recognition in 1947 and 1948; and Congress, which appropriates the aid and can block or permit it. [cite:scotus_zivotofsky] [cite:dv4_frus_may12_1948] [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] When this part says Washington acted, it means one of these offices, and usually the President acting with Congress's money.

What Washington supplied, and when. Four contributions are documented. First, recognition: eleven minutes after the proclamation of 14 May 1948, by the President alone, over the written objection of his Secretary of State. [cite:truman_recognition] [cite:dv4_frus_may12_1948] Second, money: about $175 billion in current dollars through 2025, built up from 1962 and made large after 1973, fixed since 1999 in ten-year agreements. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] Third, weapons and the law that protects their advantage: arms sales from 1962, Israel's principal supplier from 1968, a statutory qualitative edge from 2008, a stockpile on Israeli soil, and in 2026 American forces fighting alongside Israel's against Iran. [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance] [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] [cite:wiki_iran_war_2026] Fourth, the veto: more than fifty drafts at the Security Council blocked between 1972 and 2025. [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes]

Was it needed? The answer depends on the date, which is the central finding of this part. In 1948 the new state got recognition from Washington, but its weapons from Czechoslovakia, the first full recognition from Moscow, and its survival from its own forces; the United States embargoed its arms. [cite:dv4_herf_embargo] [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] [cite:israeled_gromyko_timeline] By the test's own definition, Washington was not then supplying something no one else supplied. From the late 1960s that changed. After 1968 the United States was Israel's principal arms supplier, and from the 1970s no other government offered aid, advanced weapons and a Security Council veto together. [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance] [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes] For the state as it has existed since then, with its military advantage over its neighbours and its protection from binding UN action, those three were things it could not have had elsewhere.

What Washington withheld. The other side of the ledger is real. It embargoed arms in the founding war. [cite:dv4_herf_embargo] Its Secretary of State backed the Bernadotte plan to give the Negev to the Arab side in 1948, and its President rebuked Israel's position on territory and refugees in 1949. [cite:jvl_clifford_bernadotte] [cite:jvl_truman_note_may1949] Eisenhower forced Israel out of Sinai in 1956. [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance] At the Security Council it abstained in 2016, letting Resolution 2334 declare the settlements without legal validity. [cite:deccan_unsc_2334] Presidents held back money and weapons to get their way on the Lavi, on settlements in 1991–1992, on sales to China, and on bombs for Rafah in 2024. [cite:migflug_lavi_cancelled] [cite:fmep_loan_guarantees_1992] [cite:ynet_phalcon_2000] [cite:dv4_national_bombs_2025] Each of these was a limit, and each ended with the support continuing.

Why it supplied it. The documented reasons are several and they changed over time: in 1948, as Marshall charged, domestic politics; from the 1960s, Cold War competition with the Soviet Union's Arab clients; and throughout, the arguments Israel's supporters make on their own terms, of a democratic ally, shared intelligence and jointly built missile defence. [cite:dv4_frus_may12_1948] [cite:crs_aid_israel_2023] The aid's own text adds another: it is designed to be spent on American industry. [cite:israeled_mou_text] Volume II and the later volumes on lobbying and Congress trace the domestic machinery in detail. What this part has added is the city's own shape. Washington is a capital whose residents have almost no power and whose resident government has almost unlimited power abroad, and the decisions recorded here were made by a handful of officials in that small district, often against the advice of the professionals who worked beside them.

The verdict. Washington was not the parent of the State of Israel at its birth. In 1948 it was the first to recognize the state in fact, and also the government that embargoed its arms. It became Israel's material and diplomatic guardian afterwards, from the 1960s and decisively from 1973, and on the record that role has no rival: no other government has supplied aid, weapons and a veto together, for so long. If the volume's family image is kept, Washington is the parent by adoption, the one who did not bring the child into the world but raised it, armed it, paid for much of its upbringing and has stood between it and the world's courts and councils ever since. In November 2026 the terms of that guardianship are being renegotiated, and the President has said the money should end; the agreement in force runs to 2028.
