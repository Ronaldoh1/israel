A Ceasefire Counted in Violations

Between 27 November 2024 and the end of February 2026 the ceasefire was in force on paper, and the fighting did not stop. What changed was its shape: instead of two armies exchanging fire, one side struck almost every day and the other almost never answered. The counts below come from four different bodies, each with its own method, and they should not be added together.

What the UN counted

UNIFIL, the UN force in the south, published its first-year figures for 27 November 2024 to 27 November 2025. It recorded almost 7,800 Israeli air violations, more than 2,500 Israeli military activities north of the Blue Line, more than 100 air attacks, and more than 1,000 trajectories of fire crossing the Blue Line, "almost all" from the Israeli side. In the same year its patrols found more than 360 caches of unauthorized weapons and ammunition, which it handed to the Lebanese army. It also recorded that Israel remained at five locations inside Lebanon and maintained two buffer zones that kept the Lebanese army from deploying fully. [cite:hz8_un_unifil_year] In its anniversary statement UNIFIL summed this as more than "10,000 Israeli air and ground violations" in twelve months. [cite:hz8_olj_unifil10k] UNIFIL counts what it observes and does not attribute the weapons caches it finds to a named owner.

The UN human rights office counted deaths. On 25 November 2025 it said it had verified that at least 127 civilians had been killed in Lebanon by Israeli forces since the ceasefire began, through 24 November. Its examples included a strike on the Ein el-Hilweh Palestinian refugee camp near Sidon that killed at least 13 civilians, 11 of them children, and a 16 November strike on a cement and asphalt plant in Ansar that, in its words, hindered reconstruction. About 64,000 people were still displaced. High Commissioner Volker Türk called on both parties to comply in good faith. [cite:hz8_ohchr_nov25]

What Lebanon counted

Lebanon's health ministry counts everyone killed by Israeli fire, civilians and fighters together, without separating them. By its count at least 331 people had been killed and 945 injured between the ceasefire and 24 November 2025. That count included the strike of 23 November on Beirut's southern suburbs that killed Haytham Ali Tabatabai, Hezbollah's senior military commander, with four other people; Netanyahu said he had ordered it because Tabatabai led Hezbollah's "buildup and armament." [cite:hz8_aa_tabatabai] L'Orient-Le Jour's own tally for roughly the same year, 27 November 2024 to 14 November 2025, was more than 12,000 Israeli violations and 343 deaths. [cite:hz8_olj_unifil10k] In January 2026 Lebanon's foreign ministry told the Security Council that it had documented 2,036 Israeli violations in the last three months of 2025 alone: 542 in October, 691 in November and 803 in December. [cite:hz8_aj_complaint]

That last figure is sometimes cited as the total for the whole truce "by January 2026," beside a death toll of fifteen. Both readings are wrong: 2,036 is one quarter's count, and the deaths during the truce, on Lebanon's figures, ran into the hundreds. [cite:hz8_aj_complaint] [cite:hz8_aa_tabatabai]

What Israel counted

Israel described its strikes as enforcement of the agreement, not breaches of it. On the first anniversary the Israeli army said that its forces and the air force had "eliminated more than 370 terrorists" from Hezbollah, Hamas and other armed groups in Lebanon during the year, and that a single division had carried out about 1,200 "targeted activities" and found dozens of weapons stores and launch sites. [cite:hz8_idf_year] That figure is the army's own and is not broken down by group or independently checked; it is larger than the health ministry's total of 331 for all deaths, so the two counts cannot both be complete on the same basis, and neither has been independently reconciled. What the UN did verify is that at least 127 of the dead were civilians.

What Hezbollah did

Hezbollah's documented fire during these fifteen months was slight. On 2 December 2024 it fired two projectiles at the Israeli-held Shebaa Farms, which it called a "defensive and warning response" to Israeli violations; they fell in open ground. [cite:hz8_pbs_dec2] On 22 March 2025 six rockets were fired at the Israeli town of Metula; three were intercepted and three fell inside Lebanon. Hezbollah denied firing them and said it remained committed to the ceasefire. Defence Minister Israel Katz ordered a response and said "the fate of Metula is the same as Beirut"; Israeli artillery and air strikes that day killed at least six people in Lebanon, among them a child, according to the health ministry. [cite:hz8_toi_metula] Security Council Report, summarizing UN reporting in late 2025, recorded March 2025 as the last reported rocket fire toward Israel. [cite:hz8_scr_nov25] Israel's case against Hezbollah during the truce therefore rested less on attacks than on rearmament: the weapons caches, the claimed rebuilding of infrastructure, and men like Tabatabai. Hezbollah has not published a count of its own losses during the truce.

How the Mechanism worked

The body meant to settle these disputes met rarely and did not publish findings. Its thirteenth meeting, at Ras al-Naqoura in November 2025, was about a month after its twelfth; Lebanese military sources described it as routine. The Lebanese army complained there that Israel was not notifying the committee in advance of sites it suspected of holding weapons, as the agreed procedure required; Israel had refused to do so. [cite:hz8_olj_mechanism] The Mechanism did not issue public rulings on which side had breached the agreement, and the reporting this book found does not show any Israeli strike being halted or reversed because of a complaint lodged with it. Lebanon took its counts to the Security Council instead, asking it to compel Israel to withdraw "beyond the internationally recognised borders." [cite:hz8_aj_complaint]

The committee changed shape in December 2025. On 3 December Lebanon sent a civilian, the former ambassador Simon Karam, to sit alongside its officers at the Naqoura meeting, and Israel sent civilian representatives too, with the US envoy Morgan Ortagus present. L'Orient Today called it the first direct talks between the two states in more than forty years. [cite:hz8_olj_karam] Whether this was a step toward a settlement or toward the political deal Israel and the United States wanted from Lebanon is the question the next section takes up.
