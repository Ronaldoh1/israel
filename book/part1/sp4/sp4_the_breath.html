The Breath

In the Hatha Yoga Pradipika, breath control is the second step of the practice, after posture. The text teaches that the mind is still while the breath is held in the body, that purifying the channels by breath restraint lets the "wind" enter the central channel, and, of the held breath called kumbhaka, that at its end the practitioner should "make the mind free of support." [cite:sp_ww_hyp] In the yoga manuals the breath is the lever that moves the serpent.

The navel-gazers of Athos

Eastern Christianity had its own method. The hesychasts, monks of Mount Athos, joined the Jesus Prayer to the rhythm of breathing and a lowered gaze. Around 1337 the Calabrian monk Barlaam attacked them, mocking them as omphalopsychoi, "people with their souls in their navels", and called their claim to see an uncreated light heretical. [cite:sp_wiki_hesychast] The Athonite monk Gregory Palamas answered in nine treatises, the Triads. A synod in Constantinople condemned Barlaam in 1341; he recanted, left for Calabria and later became a bishop of the Latin Church. After years of rival synods, a council of 28 May 1351 upheld Palamas and made his teaching Orthodox doctrine. [cite:sp_wiki_hesychast] The breath method was attacked, defended and, in the East, won.

In the West the response was more cautious. A letter to the bishops from the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, dated 15 October 1989 and signed by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, addressed "eastern methods" such as Zen, Transcendental Meditation and yoga. It allowed that Christians may "take from them what is useful" so long as the Christian idea of prayer is not obscured, and it named the Jesus Prayer, "which adapts itself to the natural rhythm of breathing", as a real help to many. [cite:sp_va_orationis] It also warned that to take the feelings produced by bodily exercises "for the authentic consolations of the Holy Spirit" would be "a totally erroneous way of conceiving the spiritual life", and could amount to "a kind of mental schizophrenia". [cite:sp_va_orationis] The letter did not forbid the methods. It drew a line around what they may be taken to mean.

Breath in the laboratory

The psychiatrist Stanislav Grof had run research on LSD in Prague and at Spring Grove in Maryland. After legal use of LSD was suppressed in the early 1970s, he and his wife Christina developed a method without drugs, combining fast, deep breathing with music, bodywork and drawing, first called Holotropic Breathwork; from 1973 he taught at the Esalen Institute in California. [cite:sp_wiki_grof] When the drug was forbidden, the breath took its place.

Measured effects are now on record. In a study published in PNAS in 2014, Matthijs Kox and colleagues trained 12 healthy volunteers for ten days in meditation, cold exposure and a breathing technique of cyclic hyperventilation followed by breath retention, and compared them with 12 untrained volunteers. All were then injected with a bacterial toxin. The trained group showed a marked rise in adrenaline, an earlier and higher anti-inflammatory response and fewer flu-like symptoms. [cite:sp_pmc_kox] The study shows that a breathing practice can measurably change the body's stress and immune response. It says nothing about kundalini or the soul.
