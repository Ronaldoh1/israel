Part Fifteen turns from what housing costs to the systems underneath it: who owns the land, who may build on it, what each state does for those the housing market leaves out, and the public works on which daily life depends. In Israel the state manages almost all land and leases it to households, through an authority whose governing council includes the Jewish National Fund; the part follows the court rulings and laws on who may receive that land, and the planning and permit record of Arab towns and unrecognised Bedouin villages. In the United States most land is private and the decisive power is local zoning. The second section compares homeownership, public housing and homelessness, and the third compares water and Israel's turn to desalination, electricity and climate policy, public transport, and the response to disasters, including war damage in Israel. Status lines are as of October 2026, ahead of Israel's election on 27 October and America's on 3 November.
