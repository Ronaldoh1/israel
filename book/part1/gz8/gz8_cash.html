The Qatari Cash

In November 2018 $15 million in Qatari money was brought into Gaza through Israel, the first of six monthly payments totalling $90 million. The money went mainly to pay the salaries of officials working for the Hamas-run government. The transfer was authorized by Israel, which Netanyahu said his security cabinet and security establishment supported, and appeared to be tied to talks in which Hamas would halt the border protests in return for an easing of the blockade. Benjamin Netanyahu called it "the right step," taken to restore quiet near the border and prevent a humanitarian crisis. Inside his own government, the education minister Naftali Bennett compared it to "protection money" paid to criminals, and the opposition leader Tzipi Livni called it "submission to Hamas." [cite:gz_ds_qatar2018]

The payments continued. By 2021 about $30 million in Qatari cash was being carried into the strip in suitcases each month, through a crossing Israel controlled. Israel halted them after the war of May 2021, insisting on safeguards that none of the money would reach Hamas, and in August 2021 agreed to a new arrangement under which the UN would pay part of the Qatari grant directly to families, with Israel overseeing the list of recipients. [cite:gz_aj_qatar2021]

The policy behind it. After October 7 the payments became evidence in an argument about what Israel's policy toward Hamas had been. The New York Times, drawing on interviews with more than two dozen officials in Israel, the United States, Qatar and elsewhere, reported in December 2023 that Netanyahu had "not only tolerated" the payments but "had encouraged them," that Israeli intelligence officers had escorted the Qatari official who carried the cash into Gaza, and that Netanyahu had spoken since at least 2012 of keeping Hamas strong as a counterweight to the Palestinian Authority. Politico, among others, cited a remark he was reported to have made to Likud lawmakers in 2019, that anyone who wanted to prevent a Palestinian state should support strengthening Hamas; the remark was first reported by the Jerusalem Post from an unnamed lawmaker's paraphrase, and the reporter has since written that it concerned letting money into Gaza and has been distorted in later retellings. [cite:gz_harkov_2019] Netanyahu said the payments had been made "to avoid a civilian humanitarian collapse" in Gaza, and called the claim that he had wanted to build up Hamas "a big lie." [cite:gz_week_qatar]

The record establishes the payments, their size, their route through Israel and the government's approval of them. What they were for is where the accounts divide: a humanitarian measure and a price for quiet, in Netanyahu's account, or, in his critics', part of a strategy of keeping Gaza and the West Bank under divided Palestinian rule.
