Part Three reads the myth that gave the Gnostic movement its most lasting image. It follows the Apocryphon of John from the unknown God to Sophia's flawed son Yaldabaoth, who claims to be the only God, and the archons who rule with him, then traces the same rulers in the Hypostasis of the Archons, the Ophite diagram Origen saw and the rites for passing them after death. It then asks what the myth says about earthly authority, from texts that call bishops "dry canals" to Pagels' political reading and Plotinus's objections.
