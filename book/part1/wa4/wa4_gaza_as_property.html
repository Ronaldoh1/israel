Gaza as Property, 2024–2026

In an interview at Harvard's Kennedy School in February 2024, Kushner said that "Gaza's waterfront property, it could be very valuable", that "from Israel's perspective, I would do my best to move the people out and then clean it up", and that civilians could be moved to Egypt or to the Negev; he called a Palestinian state "a super bad idea", and later stood by his remarks. [cite:euronews_kushner_waterfront] [cite:fox_kushner_defends] On 4 February 2025, at a White House press conference with Netanyahu, Trump said the United States would "take over" Gaza and that he saw "a long-term ownership position"; asked days later whether Palestinians would have a right to return, he said, "No, they wouldn't." [cite:cbs_no_return] [cite:pbs_no_return]

A prospectus called the GREAT Trust, reported by the Washington Post on 31 August 2025, proposed an American-administered trusteeship of at least ten years, "voluntary" relocation of Gaza's two million people, $5,000 in cash and four years of rent for each who left, and resorts and "smart cities" in their place; Boston Consulting Group said the work done by its staff had not been approved and dismissed two partners. [cite:ynet_great_trust] [cite:yahoo_great_trust_egypt] At Davos in January 2026, Kushner presented a master plan for a "New Gaza" under the Board of Peace that Trump chairs. [cite:cnn_gaza_davos] [cite:npr_gaza_masterplan] Gaza, Documented follows these plans in full.

The law

Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention prohibits "individual or mass forcible transfers, as well as deportations of protected persons from occupied territory", whatever the motive. [cite:geneva_convention_art49] The plans call departures voluntary; Egypt, Jordan and other Arab governments rejected any transfer of Gaza's population. [cite:yahoo_great_trust_egypt] Whether departure from a destroyed territory with no right of return can be voluntary is the legal question the plans raise, and it has not been tested in any court.
