The Tests

Each test removes part of the documented network and recalculates the whole map. The figures below are the chance the operation runs as documented, 1991–2019, and the lowest and highest the same test gives when every weight the map relies on is varied: each of the six channels at half and at double its weight, reported ties at 30 and at 100 percent, the $1 million default at a quarter and at four times that, unstated banking years at one and at ten, the weight of cover, and the decision weights.

Money and banking are what it could not run without. Without Wexner, the money the record quantifies for 1991 to 2011 shrinks to a few million dollars; without Wexner and Black together, the operation could not have been paid for. Without any bank, the money, the payroll and the property costs have no route. These are the clearest findings, and they hold under every weighting, though their exact size does not: the ranges are wide, and the published figure sits in the middle of each.

One bank alone, or one prosecutor, was replaceable. Deutsche Bank took him on in 2013 when JPMorgan let him go; removing the US Attorney who approved the 2008 deal barely moves the deal's chance, because the push for it came from a large defence team and from inside the office.

Maxwell's removal costs little on this measure, and that is a limit, not a verdict. Her conviction covers years of conduct, but on the documented ties the money, the accounts and the properties were his. The map counts ties; it cannot count the work a person did that left no tie of its own.

The reputation work served his standing, not the running of the operation. Weighing access and reputation at half or at double moves that test by less than a point.
