Part Twenty-Nine follows the stake and the heresy trial from 1307 to 1633, through three kinds of target that popular accounts often fold into one story. It begins with the Knights Templar, a papal order arrested by the king of France, absolved in private at Chinon and dissolved at Vienne without a heresy verdict, and sets the record beside the legends that have gathered around them, from the origin of Friday the 13th to a parchment said to prove their innocence. It then turns to the mystics inside the Latin Church who taught that God is found within, Marguerite Porete, Meister Eckhart, the alumbrados of Castile and Miguel de Molinos, and sets what each taught beside the document that condemned it. It ends with Giordano Bruno, burned in 1600, and Galileo Galilei, made to abjure in 1633, and with what the Holy See has and has not said about them since.
