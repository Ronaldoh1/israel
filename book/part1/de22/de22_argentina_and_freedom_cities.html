Argentina, and the Freedom Cities

Two governments in the Americas have been named in connection with exit zones since 2023. One is Javier Milei's Argentina, whose president calls himself an anarcho-capitalist and to which Peter Thiel moved in 2026. The other is the United States, where Donald Trump promised in his 2023 campaign to charter up to ten new "freedom cities" on federal land. This section sets out what each government has actually done, keeping campaign words, advocates' plans and enacted policy apart, and then tests the book's worst-case reading against the whole of Part Twenty-Two.

Argentina: a president's philosophy and a neighbour's development

Milei's own label for his philosophy is anarcho-capitalism, a strand of libertarianism that would replace the state's services with private ones. After receiving Thiel at the Casa Rosada on 23 April 2026, he called him "an anarcho-capitalist, just as I am philosophically," said both men regard "taxes as theft," and said no specific investments were discussed. [cite:de9_bat_milei] Thiel's meetings with Milei, his move to Buenos Aires and the reports of a citizenship offer, which Milei's spokesperson denied, are set out in [scene:de9_politics_and_argentina|Part Nine]. [cite:de9_nn_nyt] [cite:de9_fortune_ba]

On exit zones specifically, the record in Argentina is thin. Milei's government has deregulated rents, property transactions and mortgages by decree, which an academic review of its urban policy describes as a "libertarian city" in an analytical sense; the same review cites no charter city, free city or special governance zone created by the government. [cite:de22_unlp_delrio] An essay on the Mises Institute's Spanish-language site argued in 2024 that Argentina needed "free cities," semi-autonomous zones with their own economic rules; it attributed no such proposal to Milei's government. [cite:de22_mises_ar] What the government did create, by Decree 524/2025 published on 31 July 2025, was a programme of citizenship by investment run by an agency of the economy ministry; as of January 2026 its rules were still unwritten and it was not operating, and Fortune reported in July 2026 that it was still not live. [cite:de22_cronista_cbi] [cite:de9_fortune_ba] Citizenship for sale is a route for individuals to join a state, not a zone exempt from one.

The nearest documented link between Milei and an exit-style project lies across the River Plate in Uruguay. On 30 August 2023, after the primaries and before he was elected, Milei made a short trip to Colonia del Sacramento, met the businessman Eduardo Bastitta and toured +Colonia, a 500-hectare development Bastitta was planning as a technology hub. [cite:de22_lanacion_colonia] [cite:de22_perfil_bastitta] Bastitta, a self-described libertarian, later served on Milei's council of advisers. [cite:de22_perfil_bastitta] In 2026 Praxis, the network-state project described in [scene:de22_seas_and_zones|the first section], signed a mostly non-binding memorandum with +Colonia. Bastitta has said the development will not have a separate legal order and compared it to Nordelta, a private gated development outside Buenos Aires; residents would choose between Uruguayan and Argentine tax and legal arrangements. [cite:de22_perfil_bastitta] [cite:de22_xataka_praxis] The tie is between a former adviser's private development and Praxis. It is not a tie between Milei's government and Praxis, and no document shows the Argentine state taking part. Nor does any document link Milei to Yarvin's or Land's texts.

The pledge: ten cities on federal land

On 3 March 2023 Trump's campaign released a video, part of its "Agenda47" series, promising "a quantum leap in the American standard of living." Among its proposals, with flying cars and "baby bonuses," was a plan to set aside a small share of federal land and charter up to ten new cities through a national contest: "These freedom cities will reopen the American frontier." [cite:de22_hill_fc] The video did not describe how the cities would be governed. After 2023, according to PolitiFact's check of the transcript archive FactBase, Trump did not mention them again; neither did the White House in his second term. [cite:de22_pf_fc]

Others filled the space. In March 2025 Wired reported that a new lobbying group, the Freedom Cities Coalition, was drafting legislation for zones where anti-ageing trials, nuclear start-ups and construction could proceed without prior approval from the Food and Drug Administration, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission or the Environmental Protection Agency. The coalition is a project of NeWay Capital, a firm involved in developing Próspera, and Próspera's chief of staff, Trey Goff, said its representatives had met the administration, which was "very receptive." [cite:de22_giz_fc] A model Freedom Cities Act drafted by Tom W. Bell, a law professor at Chapman University, would let a federal board waive large parts of the tax code, environmental and labour law; Bell called the cities "small safely-contained experiments in governance." [cite:de22_bblaw_fc] Published summaries of the draft record that it would also bar residents from voting in state elections. [cite:de22_wiki_fc] In April 2025 the American Enterprise Institute's housing centre proposed selling 850 square miles of land managed by the Bureau of Land Management, 600 of them for about twenty new cities, and Paul Dans, who had directed the Heritage Foundation's Project 2025, described freedom cities to NBC News as part of a "construction phase" after the "demolition" of the administrative state. [cite:de22_wiki_fc] By February 2026 the Frontier Foundation, another advocate, told PolitiFact that "Drafts are circulating. This promise will be fulfilled," and in June advocates were pressing for the first sites to be unveiled on 4 July 2026, the 250th anniversary of independence. [cite:de22_pf_fc] [cite:de22_inq_fc]

What happened, and the legal limits

Nothing was enacted. PolitiFact rated the promise "Stalled" on 10 February 2026: no bill in Congress mentioned freedom cities, no executive order had been issued, and the White House did not answer a request for comment. [cite:de22_pf_fc] The nearest legislative step went the other way. Senator Mike Lee put a provision in the Senate's 2025 budget bill requiring the sale of between 0.25 and 0.5 per cent of Bureau of Land Management land in eleven Western states for housing; he withdrew it on 28 June 2025 after the Senate parliamentarian ruled against it and House Republicans threatened to oppose the bill. [cite:de22_kpcw_lee] The Interior secretary, Doug Burgum, proposed selling up to 625 square miles near existing cities for housing, a different plan from new chartered cities; the Bureau's acting director said it had seen no specific freedom-city proposal. [cite:de22_bblaw_fc] As of late July 2026, the German daily taz reported, no law mentioned freedom cities; no unveiling on 4 July was reported. [cite:de22_taz_fc] Status as of October 2026: a campaign pledge, not a policy.

The limits are structural, and they sit with Congress. The Constitution's Property Clause gives Congress, not the president, the power to dispose of federal land and make rules for it; large transfers of Bureau of Land Management land need an act of Congress, and the Federal Land Policy and Management Act of 1976 ended homesteading and narrowed sales. [cite:de22_bblaw_fc] [cite:de22_inq_fc] Exempting a zone from the tax code, the Clean Water Act or federal labour law would likewise take a statute, which is why the advocates drafted bills rather than orders. Andrew Mergen of Harvard Law School told Bloomberg Law that Congress has broad authority over federal land, but "this would be at a whole other scale"; states, Bell said, would have little power over such enclaves, much as over military bases. [cite:de22_bblaw_fc] An executive acting alone could not create such a city; at most it could propose one.

On the book's red-flag scale, the freedom-city pledge is ORANGE: a public promise not delivered. The grade attaches to the outcome, not to intent. Trump has given no explanation on the record for dropping it, and the obstacle that stands in the way, Congress's control of federal land, is visible and ordinary.

The worst-case reading, tested

The book's working hypothesis, the Sophia platform's and never a finding, reads immigration enforcement and government cuts as the occasion and a sovereign executive without a veto as the destination. Exit zones bear on it in a particular way. On one reading they are an escape: people who dislike a state leave it and build elsewhere, which is what Patchwork and Thiel's 2009 essay proposed. On the other they are an enclave for the few: territory carved out of a country where a company sets the rules, residents have a weaker vote, and the surrounding state's law stops at the gate.

Documented: Próspera's charter gives its company a blocking vote over the zone's rules, defers residents' fuller powers until a population it has not approached, and gives remote "e-residents" no vote at all. [cite:de22_charter] Documented too: when Honduras's elected Congress and its Supreme Court revoked the law beneath the zone, the company sought restitution or damages through a treaty tribunal and drew support from members of the United States Congress. [cite:de22_po6] [cite:de22_crec_horsford] [cite:de22_cc_ruling] Documented: Thiel's money reaches Próspera and Praxis one step removed, through Pronomos, and people working for Próspera lobbied the Trump administration for freedom cities in the United States. [cite:de22_bbg_pronomos] [cite:de22_giz_fc] And documented: advocates' drafts would remove federal law within the zones and bar residents from state elections. [cite:de22_wiki_fc]

Probable: that "enclave for the few" describes Próspera better than "escape for the many." The zone's own rules give investors more power than residents, and its neighbours' account is that they were not told what was being built; this is a reading of documented facts, and Próspera's stated position, that it is a voluntary, job-creating community that has renounced expropriation, is also on the record. [cite:de22_guardian_cr] [cite:de22_heraldo_2020]

Not supported: that the exit projects are a path to a sovereign executive over the United States. The record runs against it. Every zone described in this part depends on a host state's law: French Polynesia let a memorandum lapse, Danish and Greenlandic law closed off Greenland, Johor shut the Network School's premises, and Honduras repealed and voided the ZEDE law, leaving Próspera operating while it pursues a pending treaty claim that now asks for restitution or, in the alternative, $1.63 billion, against the $10.775 billion first claimed. [cite:de22_rnz_fp] [cite:de22_giz_praxis] [cite:de22_newswav_ns] [cite:de22_cc_ruling] [cite:de22_po6] [cite:de22_eyn_2025] In the United States the only government step toward freedom cities was a campaign video; Congress holds the land and did not act, and the one legislative provision to sell some of it was withdrawn. [cite:de22_pf_fc] [cite:de22_kpcw_lee] In Argentina no exit zone exists. Exit, as built, has meant leaving or carving out small places, not capturing a large one. Whether the digital version of exit fared differently is the subject of [scene:de23_address_space|Part Twenty-Three].
