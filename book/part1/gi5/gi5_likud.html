The Platform and the Party, 1977–2017

The sentence most often quoted to show that Likud holds a Greater Israel programme comes from its 1977 election platform. It sits in a section headed "The Right of the Jewish People to the Land of Israel". The section opens: "The right of the Jewish people to the land of Israel is eternal and indisputable". It goes on to say that this right is linked to the right to security and peace, and that "therefore, Judea and Samaria will not be handed to any foreign administration; between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty." [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] The quotation is accurate. The text is reproduced by the Jewish Virtual Library from the Likud's own website and from Laqueur and Rubin's documentary collection The Israel-Arab Reader. [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977]

What the 1977 text says, and what it does not

Read whole, the section makes three claims. It makes a right claim: the land belongs to the Jewish people by a right described as eternal. It makes a sovereignty claim: between the Mediterranean and the Jordan only Israel will rule. And it makes a political argument: any plan that "relinquishes parts of western Eretz Israel" undermines that right and "unavoidably leads to the establishment of a 'Palestinian State'". [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] The section on settlement calls settlement "in all parts of the Land of Israel" "the focal point of the Zionist effort to redeem the country". It says the government will call on young people in Israel and abroad to settle, "while taking care not to dispossess anyone". [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] The peace section promised direct negotiations with Israel's neighbours "without pre-conditions on either side" and attendance at the Geneva conference. [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977]

The boundaries of the claim are stated in the text itself. The phrase "western Eretz Israel" and the line "between the Sea and the Jordan" place the eastern limit at the river. The Revisionist claim to the east bank of the Jordan, the land that became the Kingdom of Jordan, is not in the platform. Nor are Sinai, southern Lebanon, Syria beyond the Golan, or anything like the "Nile to the Euphrates" of later polemics. [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] [scene:gi1_versions|Part One: the three versions] [scene:gi10_myths|Part Ten: the myths] On the platform's own terms, then, the documented Likud programme of 1977 was a claim to the West Bank and Gaza, held together with Israel inside its 1949 lines. Within that area the claim was categorical.

One common description of the text goes beyond the record. Several accounts, including earlier material in this library, say that the Likud charter "still says" these words and that this has been the party's position "since its founding". The text that can be sourced is the 1977 platform. Likud's current constitution and its platforms for later elections were not found in a verifiable form for this book. This book therefore does not claim that the 1977 sentence is current party text. It sets out instead what the party's governing bodies voted for later, which is on the record. [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] [cite:gi1_wiki_greaterisrael]

The party bodies: 2002 and 2017

On 12 May 2002, during the second intifada, Likud's Central Committee, the party's main policy body, voted on rival resolutions. Benjamin Netanyahu, then out of office and preparing to challenge Prime Minister Ariel Sharon for the leadership, backed a resolution stating: "No Palestinian state will be created west of the Jordan". Sharon's alternative, endorsing his government's diplomatic and security efforts, lost a secret ballot by 59 to 41 per cent, and Netanyahu's text then passed by a show of hands. [cite:gi5_ap_likud2002] Sharon had tried to block the vote. He had said he could accept a Palestinian state under strict conditions and had once called one "inevitable", and after the defeat he said he would keep leading "according to the same ideas". The Palestinian minister Saeb Erekat called the vote "a real slap in the face" for President George W. Bush, who had backed a Palestinian state. [cite:gi5_ap_likud2002]

On 31 December 2017 the Central Committee went further. It voted unanimously to call on the party's elected leaders "to extend Israeli law and sovereignty in all the areas of liberated settlement in Judea and Samaria" and to allow unhindered construction there. [cite:gi5_toi_likud2017] The resolution did not bind Likud's members of the Knesset. It carried weight because candidates depend on the committee's roughly 3,000 members in party primaries. Netanyahu, by then prime minister, did not attend; the Times of Israel noted that he had long opposed ideological declarations by party bodies. The Knesset speaker Yuli Edelstein, the Jerusalem mayor Nir Barkat and several ministers backed it, and the keynote speaker, Gideon Sa'ar, said annexation could come "in just a few years". [cite:gi5_toi_likud2017] Hamas called the decision "the continued harming of Palestinian rights" and said it would lead to "more resistance". [cite:gi5_toi_likud2017]

Unlike the 1977 platform's "between the Sea and the Jordan", the 2017 text applied sovereignty to "the areas of liberated settlement", the settlements and the land around them. It made no claim to every part of the West Bank. That narrowing reappears in the annexation proposals of 2019 to 2026 described in Part Six. [cite:gi5_toi_likud2017] [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] [scene:gi6_plans|Part Six: the annexation plans]

The leaders in office

Likud's prime ministers have often acted differently from the party's platform and its committee. Begin, elected on the 1977 platform, applied Israeli law to the Golan and entrenched it in East Jerusalem. In the West Bank and Gaza he kept the sovereignty claim but proposed leaving it "open", and he returned Sinai. [cite:gi5_ecf_selfrule] [cite:gi1_csm_sinai1982] [scene:gi5_begin|Begin and "Judea and Samaria"] Sharon, who had planned settlement under Begin, removed every settlement in Gaza in 2005. Likud's members had rejected his disengagement plan in a party referendum on 2 May 2004, by about 65 per cent, and he carried it through the cabinet and the Knesset regardless. [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] [scene:gz3_withdrawal|Gaza, Documented: the withdrawal]

Netanyahu has stated both positions. At Bar-Ilan University on 14 June 2009 he called the West Bank "the land of our forefathers", but said Israel would accept "a demilitarised Palestinian state" alongside the Jewish state if Palestinians recognised Israel as the Jewish state and security guarantees were met. He added that "we have no intention of building new settlements or of expropriating additional land for existing settlements", and that Jerusalem "must remain the united capital of Israel". [cite:gi5_aje_barilan] In 2002 he had sponsored the resolution against any Palestinian state west of the Jordan, and his later statements rejecting a Palestinian state, through to September 2025, are set out in Part Six. [cite:gi5_ap_likud2002] [scene:gi6_votes|Part Six: the votes]

The documented pattern is a party whose founding platform and governing body claimed the land west of the Jordan in categorical terms. Its prime ministers in office sometimes held the claim in reserve, sometimes narrowed it and in two cases (Sinai in 1982 and Gaza in 2005) gave up land. Whether those reversals show that the claim was rhetoric, or that it was pursued where possible and yielded where not, is a matter of interpretation; Part Thirteen weighs both readings.
