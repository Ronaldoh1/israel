When the Institution Protects Itself

The Church

The largest documented case of the pattern is the abuse of children by Catholic clergy and its concealment by the Church's hierarchy. In August 2018 a Pennsylvania grand jury reported on six dioceses: 301 priests, more than 1,000 victims identified from the Church's own records, and, the jurors believed, thousands more. "They wanted to cover up the cover up," said the state's Attorney General, Josh Shapiro. The time limits in force when the abuse happened meant that most of the priests were dead or beyond prosecution; two were charged. [cite:ep12_ap_pa2018] In France an independent commission estimated in 2021 that about 216,000 people had been abused as minors by clergy and religious since 1950. [cite:vt12_ji_ciase2021] Inquiries in Ireland, Germany and Australia reached similar conclusions, and in 2020 the Vatican published its own report on how Theodore McCarrick rose to cardinal despite warnings. [cite:vt12_wiki_ryan] [cite:vt12_ba_mhg2018] [cite:vt12_ru_mccarrick2020] The library's volumes on the Vatican set this out in full. For this book, the point is the mechanism: abusers moved rather than reported, records kept in secret archives, and time allowed to run out.

The broadcaster

Jimmy Savile, one of the most famous entertainers in Britain, abused at least 72 people connected to the BBC over five decades, the youngest aged eight, according to the review by the former judge Dame Janet Smith in 2016. Complaints were "disbelieved, disregarded or covered up", and staff who raised them were warned it could harm their careers; she found an "atmosphere of fear" at the corporation. [cite:ep12_itv_savile2016] Many of those complaints were made while he was alive and famous; none led to a prosecution.

The FBI and the team doctor

The closest parallel to the FBI's handling of Epstein in 1996 is its handling of Larry Nassar, the USA Gymnastics doctor. The Justice Department's Inspector General found in 2021 that the FBI's Indianapolis office sat on the complaint for about eight months after it was reported in 2015, failed to document it, and gave incomplete and inaccurate accounts of its own conduct. During a fourteen-month period when the FBI knew of the allegations, at least 40 more girls and women said Nassar abused them. [cite:ep12_opb_nassar2021] The same agency, the same failure to act on an early report, in two cases two decades apart.

The town

In Rotherham, in the north of England, an independent inquiry led by Professor Alexis Jay estimated in 2014 that at least 1,400 children had been sexually exploited between 1997 and 2013. Police "regarded many child victims with contempt", and three reports that described what was happening, between 2002 and 2006, were suppressed or ignored. The council's leader resigned. [cite:ep12_c4_rotherham2014] Here the victims were not near wealth or power. The pattern held anyway, which shows that the mechanism does not depend on rich clients. It depends on institutions that decide some victims are not worth believing.

The youth organisation

When the Boy Scouts of America filed for bankruptcy in 2020, at least 82,000 people filed claims that they had been abused in scouting. [cite:ep12_wish_scouts2020] Scale on that order is only possible where reports were absorbed for decades without being acted on.
