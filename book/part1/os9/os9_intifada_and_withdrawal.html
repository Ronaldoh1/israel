The Second Intifada and the Withdrawal, 2000–2005

The Second Intifada began after the opposition leader Ariel Sharon visited the Temple Mount, the Haram al-Sharif, on 28 September 2000. Palestinians describe the visit as the trigger; Israel argued the violence was planned by the Palestinian leadership; an international commission led by George Mitchell examined both claims in 2001. [cite:ecf_second_intifada] Hamas, Islamic Jihad and Fatah's al-Aqsa Martyrs' Brigades carried out shootings and suicide bombings in Israel; in March 2002 alone 133 people were killed in attacks, including a Passover-night bombing at a hotel in Netanya, and Israel responded with Operation Defensive Shield, its largest West Bank operation since 1967. [cite:milwiki_second_intifada] [cite:jpost_defensive_shield] By January 2005 about 1,000 Israelis had been killed, about two thirds of them civilians by B'Tselem's count, and between about 3,000 and 3,350 Palestinians. [cite:milwiki_second_intifada]

The bombings were the main stated reason for the separation barrier; in July 2004 the International Court of Justice advised that the barrier was unlawful where it ran inside occupied territory. [cite:os_icj_wall] [cite:ecf_second_intifada]

Disengagement

Sharon, now prime minister, proposed leaving Gaza in 2003. From 15 to 23 August 2005 Israel evacuated all 21 Gaza settlements and four in the northern West Bank, about 8,000 settlers, without casualties. [cite:crs_disengagement_2005] Sharon's senior adviser described the plan as a way of freezing the political process. [cite:wiki_disengagement] Israel kept control of Gaza's airspace, coastline and most crossings; the UN and the International Court of Justice continued to treat Gaza as occupied, and Israel argued its occupation had ended. Hamas won the Palestinian legislative elections of January 2006 and took control of Gaza in June 2007, and the blockade followed. [cite:wiki_disengagement] Gaza, Documented follows what came after.
