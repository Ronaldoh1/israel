The Register

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The inquisitors wrote everything down, and their registers are now the main source for the people they prosecuted. [cite:vt28_mednet_gui] Jacques Fournier's register of his questioning in the Pyrenees, taken to Rome when he became pope, became the historian Le Roy Ladurie's portrait of a village in its inhabitants' own words. [cite:vt28_wiki_fournier] The trial records of Menocchio, burned in 1599, became Ginzburg's reconstruction of a miller's own view of the cosmos. [cite:iq_wiki_menocchio] The benandanti are known only because the Inquisition questioned them. [cite:iq_wiki_nightbattles]

The inner reading finds in this a strange justice. The instrument built to silence dissent is the reason the dissenters can still be heard. A miller who read too much, peasants who fought for the harvest in their sleep, the villagers of Montaillou: each was written down by a notary who meant to record their guilt, and each survives as a voice. The register meant to close the case kept it open.

The reading draws one more lesson from it. What is said honestly, even under questioning, outlasts the questioner's purpose. Menocchio abjured once, and was arrested again because he went on spreading his beliefs. [cite:iq_wiki_menocchio] The Heretics, the book before this one, found the same: the decisive offence was refusing to unsay. Here the register shows the other side of that refusal. What one will not unsay can be burned with its speaker, and still be read four hundred years later.

The record limits the reading. The registers record what the accused said under pressure, in the judges' language and through a notary's hand, and historians debate how far they can be trusted as the accused's own words. [cite:hr_ihr_moore] The reading uses them as the only voices that survive, not as clean ones.
