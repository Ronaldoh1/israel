The Ledger: Claims About Christian Zionism, Judged

This section takes the claims about Christian Zionism that a reader is most likely to meet and gives each a verdict. Four verdicts are used. Documented means the record supports the claim. Contested means named sources stand on both sides and the record does not settle it. Unsupported means no source was found for it. Myth means the record contradicts it. Each verdict rests on documents already cited in Parts One to Thirteen, and the stated position of the person or organisation concerned is given beside each critical finding. Earlier volumes of this library carried several of these claims; where they did, the correction is stated. [scene:ts4_rumours|Part Four: the rumours tested]

Origins

"Scofield created Christian Zionism." Myth. Christian belief in a Jewish return to the land was in print three centuries before his Bible: in Thomas Brightman's commentaries, first published in 1609, in Henry Finch's book of 1621, which James I treated as a libel, in Increase Mather's Boston sermons of 1669 and in the London Society, founded in 1809 to convert Jews. [cite:ts2_crome_cdamm] [cite:ts2_wiki_finch] Palmerston asked the sultan to admit Jewish settlers on 11 August 1840, Darby set out his two peoples at Geneva the same year, the Niagara Creed of 1878 declared that "Israel shall be restored to their own land," and Blackstone put the case to President Harrison on 5 March 1891. [cite:ts2_cojs_palmerston1840] [cite:ts3_darby_hopes1] [cite:ts3_ak_niagara] [cite:ts6_moorhead_jets] What Scofield added was reach: a Bible with the system printed beside the verse, about two million copies by 1945. [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] Two related claims fail with it. The system was not "substantially non-existent" in America before 1909; it had a creed, conferences and Bible institutes. [cite:ts3_bt_niagara] [cite:ts3_weber_ct1999] And neither Darby nor the 1917 notes made a political return of Jews a precondition of Christ's coming: Darby's rapture was "any-moment," and Scofield's sequence at Deuteronomy 30 puts "The return of the Lord" before "Restoration to the land." [cite:ts3_weber_ct1999] [cite:ts5_sac_deut30] The library's Road to 1948 scene on Scofield stated both claims. [scene:s05|Road to 1948: the Scofield Reference Bible]

"The secret rapture came from Margaret MacDonald's vision." Unsupported. Darby dated his central idea to 1827, three years before her utterances of 1830; her text, read in full, places believers inside the "fiery trial"; and Kenneth Strand, a reviewer outside Darby's tradition, concluded that the case "falls apart." Whether Darby had fully worked out the doctrine by the Powerscourt conference of 1831 is still argued among specialists. [cite:ts3_wiki_macdonald] [cite:ts3_strand_auss] [cite:ts3_brarchive_powerscourt]

"A land without a people for a people without a land" as a Zionist coinage. Myth; its later use and meaning are contested. Every documented use before 1901 is by a Christian writer: the Methodist Richard Watson before 1833, Alexander Keith in 1843, a Free Church review in 1844 and Shaftesbury in 1853, who wrote of "a country without a nation." The first recorded Zionist use was Israel Zangwill's in 1901, and by about 1904 he was saying the country "has already its inhabitants." [cite:ts2_wiki_landwithout] [cite:ts2_muir_meq] How widely Zionists later used it divides the historians: Diana Muir found it four times in seven American newspapers before 1948, while Anita Shapira and Boaz Neumann describe it as common among early Zionists and Rashid Khalidi calls it "widely propagated." Whether it meant the land was empty, or that its inhabitants were not counted as a nation, is argued by Muir and Himmelfarb on one side and Said and Masalha on the other. [cite:ts2_muir_meq] [cite:ts2_wiki_landwithout] [scene:ts2_slogan|Part Two: the phrase]

The man and the book

"Scofield was a convicted forger who served a prison sentence." Myth; the jailing is documented. He was held in the St. Louis jail from 8 October 1878 on forgery charges, and on 6 November 1879 the prosecution entered a nolle prosequi, dropping them for lack of evidence; there was no trial, verdict or sentence. [cite:ts4_rushing] [cite:ts4_doj_ks] The rest of the unflattering record holds: the Justice Department says he was "forced to resign" as US Attorney in 1873 over "questionable financial transactions," he deserted his wife and daughters, and he styled himself a doctor of divinity with no degree on record. The embellishments do not: he was divorced once, not twice, served no Canadian prison term on any record, and was not a decorated hero of Antietam. [cite:ts4_doj_ks] [cite:ts4_wiki_kellogg] [cite:ts4_wiki_scofield] [cite:ts4_sfiles] Scofield's own account, through his biographer, was that grace changed him in 1879, and no later charge is recorded. [cite:ts4_trumbull]

"Samuel Untermyer, or the Rothschilds, paid for the Scofield Bible." Myth. The only documented link is that both men belonged to New York's Lotos Club: Untermyer from 1894, Scofield from 3 March 1900, proposed by Horatio Fraser and seconded by John Elderkin. No letter, meeting or payment is recorded. [cite:ts4_sfiles] The story begins with Joseph Canfield's hostile biography of 1988, which named Untermyer as a likely sponsor with the words "we assume"; David Lutz in 2005 and the Washington Report on Middle East Affairs in 2015 restated it as fact without citing a document; and Michael Collins Piper's The High Priests of War (2004) was the first text to say the Rothschilds paid. [cite:ts4_sfiles] [cite:ts5_ocathail] [cite:ts5_wiki_piper] Check Your Fact rated the Rothschild claim false on 12 March 2025, and the writer Mike Rothschild found no primary source for it. [cite:ts4_cyf] [cite:ts4_mrothschild] The documented backers were Alwyn Ball, John Pirie, John Buss and Francis Fitch, named by Gaebelein in 1943 and, for Ball and Pirie, by Scofield himself, with a reported $1,000 from the oilman Lyman Stewart in 1908; Untermyer's leading Zionist office began in 1921, and in 1905 Scofield's own consulting editor called Zionism "a movement ... of unbelief." [cite:ts4_sfiles] [cite:ts5_dunlap] [cite:ts5_wiki_lstewart] [cite:ts3_wiki_gaebelein] The claim has the shape of one of the oldest antisemitic accusations, that Jewish financiers secretly steer what Christians believe. It is rejected here because the record contradicts it, and named because it travels faster than the record and was printed in this library's own earlier account. [scene:ts5_funding|Part Five: who paid]

"The 1917 edition referred to the Balfour Declaration." Unsupported. The reference work cited for the claim does not say so, and the 1917 notes examined for this volume still describe Israel as "now in the third dispersion." The declaration is dated 2 November 1917, weeks before the revision could have been printed. [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] [cite:ts1_sac_gen15] [cite:wb_bl_addms41178]

"Oxford sold tens of millions of Scofield Bibles." Unsupported. The citable figures are 1,925,000 copies published within thirty years and more than two million sold by the end of the Second World War; Christianity Today spoke in 1967 of "several million copies in print." [cite:ts5_dunlap] [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] [cite:ts5_ct_1967] The tens of millions belong to two later books in the same tradition: Hal Lindsey's The Late Great Planet Earth, an estimated 28 to 35 million, and the Left Behind novels, 50 to 80 million by the reported counts. The "65 million" for Left Behind printed in earlier library material has no source. [cite:ts7_wiki_lgpe] [cite:ts7_wrap_lindsey] [cite:ts7_cp_lahaye] [cite:ts7_sa_lahaye]

Aims and influence

"Christian Zionists want Jews to die, or to convert." The texts are documented; the claim about what organisations want is contested, and the wish for Jewish deaths is a myth as a statement of any stated aim. The theology's own texts foresee a judgment and a conversion. Darby said in 1840 that after the return "there will only be a remnant saved" and that "Two thirds" would be cut off; Scofield's 1917 notes place a "remnant" turning to Jesus "During the great tribulation" and list "National conversion" after the restoration; most restorationists sought the conversion of Jews, and Blackstone told a Zionist meeting in 1918 that God's plan was conversion. [cite:ts3_darby_hopes10] [cite:ts5_sac_rom11] [cite:ts5_sac_deut30] [cite:ts2_crome_cdamm] [cite:ts6_moorhead_jets] A forecast in a theology is not a stated wish, and the organisations state the opposite aim. CUFI has an official policy against proselytising Jews, Hagee says the support is "a debt of gratitude," David Brog says his members reject replacement theology, and CUFI's spokesman argues that no human can hasten the end. [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] [cite:ts1_jvl_cufi] [cite:ts9_j_brog2017] [cite:ts9_jta_morgenstern2010] Critics answer with documents of their own: Jewish and interfaith leaders have questioned how that stance fits Hagee's teaching that salvation is through Christ alone; his 1990s sermon calling Hitler "a hunter" led John McCain to repudiate his endorsement in 2008; Sarah Posner calls Israel "just a pawn" in the prophecy; and in 2001 Israel's Chief Ashkenazi Rabbi condemned Christian money used to "expand Christian missionary propaganda." [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] [cite:ts8_cnn_hagee] [cite:ts9_dn_posner] [cite:ts8_jta_eckstein]

"Evangelicals set American policy on Israel." Contested. Their presence is documented: Pence promised the embassy move at CUFI's summit in July 2017, Jeffress and Hagee prayed at its opening on 14 May 2018, and Trump said in August 2020 that the move was "for the evangelicals." [cite:ts12_ncr_pence2017] [cite:ts12_cnn_pastors] [cite:ts9_almon_trump2020] The decisions' own texts gave other reasons: the 1995 Act and failed waivers for Jerusalem, security and Iran for the Golan, law and the stalled peace process for the settlements statement, which Blinken reversed in 2024. [cite:ts12_toi_trump2017] [cite:gi8_ucsb_procl9852] [cite:ts12_toi_pompeo2019] [cite:wb_axios_blinken] The theology also met limits. Huckabee called his "take it all" answer "hyperbolic," Trump said he would "not allow Israel to annex the West Bank," Brazil opened a trade office instead of an embassy, and in Thomas Massie's 2026 primary, where CUFI's Action Fund bought billboards, the Associated Press judged Trump's opposition the deciding factor. [cite:gi11_aje_huckabee] [cite:gi6_ap_trump0925] [cite:ts7_pti_brazil] [cite:ts8_ji_massie] [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways] Earlier claims that Trump called the Golan prophecy, or that Pence and Pompeo called themselves Christian Zionists, were not found. [scene:ts12_officials|Part Twelve: the officials]

"The Balfour Declaration was an act of Christian Zionism." Contested. Donald Lewis and Philip Alexander argue that Balfour's and Lloyd George's Christian formation made them receptive; the War Cabinet's minutes record "useful propaganda" in Russia and America, and Lloyd George's own evidence to the Peel Commission in 1937 called the declaration a war measure. [cite:ts6_wiki_cz] [cite:ts6_wiki_balfourdecl] [cite:ts6_jn_lloydgeorge]

Churches, members and stories

"Kairos Palestine calls Christian Zionism a heresy." Unsupported. The word is not in the passages checked; the document of 2009 rejects "any theology that legitimizes the occupation" and targets "Biblical fundamentalism." [cite:ts11_transcend_kairos] [cite:ts11_jcr_kairos] The condemnation the claim has in mind belongs to the Jerusalem Declaration of 22 August 2006, which called Christian Zionist doctrines "false teaching," and in January 2026 the Heads of Churches named Christian Zionism among "damaging ideologies." [cite:ts1_wiki_jerdecl] [cite:ts11_osv_heads2026] Nor did Rome rule against "Scofield's entire prophetic system": paragraph 676 of the Catechism rejects millenarianism in general. [cite:ts11_usccb_ccc676]

"CUFI has ten million members." Documented as CUFI's claim; uncounted. The organisation announced the figure on 22 December 2020 without defining a member, and its earlier figures, from "over 400 pastors" in 2006, are its own. [cite:ts8_hfl_10m] [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi]

"Israel gave Falwell a Learjet," and "Begin telephoned Falwell to explain the Osirak strike." Unsupported. Neither was found in a contemporary report or document. What is documented is the 1979 tour on which Falwell met Begin, the Jabotinsky medal Begin presented in November 1980, and Rabbi Marc Tanenbaum's criticism in 1981 of Begin's choice of Falwell as his only Christian interlocutor. [cite:ts8_lib_falwell] [cite:ts8_wiki_jabmedal] [cite:ts8_djn_tanenbaum]

"I am Cyrus." Documented as reported; its meaning is contested. Truman's remark of November 1953 is known from later accounts, not a transcript; Paul Merkley reads it as the key to his conduct, John Judis as a later self-portrait of a president full of doubt in 1948. [cite:ts6_merkley_cyrus] [cite:rs_cw2_judis_truman_cyrus]

The pattern across the ledger runs in both directions. The claims that fail most completely are the ones that make the movement's history simpler than it was, whether by giving it one author, one patron or one secret aim; the claims that are documented are often less flattering to the movement's founders than their admirers allowed, and more ordinary than their critics supposed. What remains contested is mostly about motive and effect, which the next section takes up. [scene:ts14_record|What the record settles]
