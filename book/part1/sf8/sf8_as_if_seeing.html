As If You See Him, and the Polished Heart

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The hadith of Gabriel defines the third dimension of religion as "That you worship Allah as if you are seeing Him, for though you don't see Him, He, verily, sees you." [cite:sf_wiki_gabriel] The sentence has two halves, and the inner reading takes them as two stages. In the first, the worshipper tries to see: to pray with full attention, as if the One addressed were present before the eyes. In the second, when that seeing fails, the worshipper remembers being seen. Either way, the act is never done in absence.

The Sufi tradition explains why the seeing fails: the heart, which should be a mirror, is rusted. A tradition attributed to the Prophet holds that "the polishing of the heart is the act of invoking or remembering God (dhikr)", and Rumi wrote that through remembrance and meditation "the heart is polished until the mirror of the heart receives virginal images". [cite:sf_aa_mirror] He asked his reader whether they knew why their mirror did not shine: "Because the rust is not cleansed from its surface." [cite:sf_aa_mirror]

The image joins this shelf's other readings. Matthew's single eye fills the body with light; the Gnostic spark wakes when it knows its source; Kabbalah's sparks are gathered by intention. [cite:cn_wiki_mt622] [cite:kb_brit_luria] In the Sufi picture nothing needs to be brought in from outside. The mirror is already there; it needs only to be cleaned, and the instrument of cleaning is the simplest practice in the tradition, the repeated remembrance of a name. On this reading, ihsan is not a special vision granted to a few, but what any heart shows when its rust is removed.

The record limits the reading. The sayings about the polished heart quoted here come through a modern article that cites the tradition and Rumi's verse; the Arabic and Persian originals and their chains of transmission are matters for specialists. [cite:sf_aa_mirror] The hadith of Gabriel, in its own setting, defines a dimension of religion within the law, not apart from it. [cite:sf_wiki_gabriel]
