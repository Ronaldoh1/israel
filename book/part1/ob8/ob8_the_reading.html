The Inner Reading: Where Science Stops

The record of this book clears the ground. Physics does not need a mind to make a measurement, and the founders who thought it might have mostly withdrawn the idea. [cite:ob_sep_decoherence] [cite:ob_wiki_ccc] The theories of consciousness have been tested and none has passed whole. [cite:ob_mcgill_cogitate] The question of why there is experience at all is, on the testimony of a majority of the philosophers asked, still a genuine question. [cite:ob_wiki_hard] That is where the science stops, and where the reading begins.

The observer as the one who sees

The inner traditions read the word observer differently from the physicist. In them the observer is not a detector but the one who sees, the inner point of view that Chalmers' hard problem names and cannot explain. The Gnostics' texts, read in The Gnostics, describe rulers that act on people who do not know themselves, and a knowing, gnosis, that frees them. [cite:vt24_brit_gnosticism] In this reading the hard problem is not a gap in the science but a pointer to what the traditions call the self: the one thing in the universe that cannot be observed from outside, because it is the observing.

Observe, collapse, reignite, read honestly

The motto of this library borrows from physics, and honesty requires saying how. To observe, in the physics, is to interact; in the reading it is to attend, to look at a system, a habit or a fear with full attention. To collapse, in the physics, is the passage from many possibilities to one result; in the reading it is what happens to a story when it is examined against the record: the many versions narrow to what is documented. To reignite has no meaning in physics at all. It belongs to the reading, and to the inner traditions on this shelf: the flame carried in secret, the light that the Gnostics said was hidden in the human being. The motto is a metaphor built from physics, not a law of it.

Attention and the harvest

Monroe's loosh, read beside the Gnostic rulers, offers a last reading, and it is the most speculative in this book. If fear and despair are what is harvested, then attention withdrawn from fear is what starves the harvest. [cite:cn_wiki_monroe] The Gnostics' reading in this library put it the same way: the rulers are fed from within and weaken when attention is withdrawn. Nothing in the hospital studies or the laboratory tests that idea. What the science does say is narrower and still striking: that attention can be trained, that the brain at the edge of death is more active than was believed, and that the observer, in the sense the traditions mean, remains the one thing no theory has yet explained.
