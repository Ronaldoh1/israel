The Two Forces

Before the war, Israel's Institute for National Security Studies estimated Hamas's armed strength at 20,000 to 25,000 fighters, with 18,000 to 30,000 mostly short-range rockets and 500 to 700 kilometres of tunnels. [cite:hm_inss_arsenal] Israel had about 169,500 active troops and 465,000 reservists. [cite:hm_almon_idf] Within 48 hours of 7 October it called up 300,000 reservists, and about 360,000 in all, some 4 percent of its population; its army spokesman said it had "never mobilized so many reservists so quickly." [cite:hm_adn_360k] On the ground, then, Israel's mobilized reserve alone outnumbered Hamas's fighters by roughly fifteen to one, before counting its air force, navy, armour and intelligence services. Part Eight sets out the money behind each side. [scene:hm8_money|The power ledger]

The Ledger

Killed. Israel's claims about how many Hamas fighters it killed were large and early. In May 2024 Netanyahu said: "Fourteen thousand have been killed, combatants, and probably around 16,000 civilians." [cite:hm_pa_netanyahu14k] A year later, Israel's own military intelligence database, leaked to The Guardian, +972 Magazine and Local Call, listed 8,900 Hamas and Islamic Jihad fighters as confirmed or probably dead by May 2025, against at least 53,000 Palestinians killed in the same period: named fighters were about 17 percent of the dead. The army said the figures in the reporting were "incorrect" without saying which. [cite:hm_aj_83pct] By October 2026 Israel's army itself reportedly accepted a total death toll of about 70,000. [cite:pr_toi_idf70k]

Replaced. In January 2025 Secretary of State Antony Blinken said Hamas had recruited almost as many new fighters as it had lost, and that it could not be defeated by "a military campaign alone." [cite:hm_aj_blinken2025]

Detained. Israel detained thousands of people from Gaza, most held without charge under its unlawful-combatants law. The same intelligence database, according to The Guardian, identified only about one in four of them as fighters. [cite:hm_guard_detainees]

Rescued. Of the 251 hostages taken on 7 October, eight were rescued alive by military operations: one soldier in October 2023, two in Rafah in February 2024, four at Nuseirat in June 2024, and one from a tunnel in August 2024. [cite:hm_ynet_rescued8] The Nuseirat rescue killed at least 274 Palestinians, by the health ministry's count. [cite:hm_eun_nuseirat] Some 160 others came home alive because Hamas released them, almost all under negotiated deals: in November 2023, in early 2025 and in October 2025. [cite:hm_s24_hostages]

The ledger does not mean Hamas was weak where it mattered most: it killed about 1,200 people on 7 October, and its tunnels and its fighters' ability to vanish among two million civilians are the reasons Israel's commanders gave for the war's length. [scene:hm7_attack|October 7] It does mean that, by Israel's own internal count, the great majority of the people killed in Gaza were not named Hamas fighters, and that the hostages came home mainly through negotiation with Hamas, not through its defeat.
