From Election to Two Governments, 2006–2007

After Hamas won the election of January 2006, it formed a government in March. Israel withheld the tax revenue it collected for the Palestinian Authority, and the Quartet stopped aid to a Hamas-led government. [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] [cite:hm_wiki_2006elect] Fatah and Hamas, fighting each other in the streets of Gaza, signed the Mecca Agreement in February 2007 and formed a unity government in March. In June the fighting resumed. Between 7 and 15 June 2007 Hamas forces took Fatah's security headquarters and bases in Gaza; on 14 June President Mahmoud Abbas dissolved the unity government, and by 15 June Hamas held the whole strip. The Palestinian Centre for Human Rights counted at least 161 people killed. [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007]

What those days were is disputed. Fatah called it a Hamas coup. In April 2008 the journalist David Rose reported in Vanity Fair, from documents and on-record interviews with former Bush administration officials, that after the election the United States had worked on a plan for Abbas to dismiss the Hamas government and for Fatah forces to be armed, through Arab states, to handle the fighting that would follow; Rose's reading was that Hamas struck first. The State Department called the article "absurd". [cite:hm_dn_rose] [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] Gaza, Documented sets out the takeover in more detail. [scene:gz4_takeover|An election, a capture, a takeover]

Since then there have been two Palestinian governments: Hamas in Gaza and the Fatah-led Palestinian Authority in parts of the West Bank. In September 2007 Israel's security cabinet declared Gaza "hostile territory" and authorized cuts to fuel, electricity and goods. [cite:gz_un_hostile2007] No national Palestinian election has been held since 2006. Abbas called legislative elections for May 2021 and then postponed them, saying Israel would not allow voting in East Jerusalem; Israel's foreign ministry called the elections "an internal Palestinian issue". Hamas called the postponement "a coup against the path of national partnership and consensus". [cite:hm_aj_2021coup]
