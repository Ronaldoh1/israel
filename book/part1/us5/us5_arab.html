The Killings in Israel's Arab Communities

Israel's national homicide figures since 2019 are, in the main, a record of killings in its Arab communities: Arab citizens, about a fifth of the population, were 73 per cent of homicide victims from 2018 to October 2023. [cite:us5_aawsat_police] This section follows that record year by year, and then what the state did about it. [scene:us5_homicide|Homicide over time]

Year by year

The police count of Arab victims rose from 73 in 2018 to 94 in 2019, 110 in 2020 and 126 in 2021, fell to 109 in 2022 and reached 222 in 2023. [cite:us5_aawsat_police] The Abraham Initiatives' counts are slightly higher: 126 in 2021, then a record, and 244 in 2023, more than double 2022. In 2023 about 88 per cent were shot, nearly half the victims were men aged 18 to 30, and 16 were women. [cite:us5_toi_2021] [cite:us5_toi_2023toll] The group counted 230 in 2024. In 2025 it counted 252 Arab citizens killed in 218 incidents, the deadliest year on its record; 88 per cent were shot, and more than 72 per cent were aged 18 to 40. [cite:us5_yahoo_herzog] Twenty-three of them were women, also a record, and 12 were killed in police operations. Israel Police disputed the total, saying it counted offenders whom officers had killed while they were on their way to carry out a killing. [cite:us5_maariv_2025] With about 2.1 million Arab citizens, 252 deaths is about 12 per 100,000 by this book's arithmetic. That is above the highest American national rate in the CCJ series, 10.2 in 1980, and about three times the American rate of 2025. [cite:us5_taub_homicide] [cite:us5_ccj_trends]

President Isaac Herzog called the 2025 figures "shocking numbers - an unacceptable situation". [cite:us5_yahoo_herzog] Police Commissioner Daniel Levi declared "a state of national emergency" early in 2026. [cite:us5_cnn_killingaday] The killing continued at a high rate. The Abraham Initiatives counted 83 deaths by 6 April 2026, 30 per cent more than in the same period of 2025, and 149 by 2 July. By 13 October 2026 the count was 184, 156 of them shot, against 200 at the same point in 2025. As of October 2026, then, the year was running somewhat below 2025 but far above any year before 2023. [cite:us5_toi_oct2026]

What the record says about the state's part

The crisis is documented as a failure of policing and public services. The figures describe what the state did and failed to do, and say nothing about the character of the communities where the killings happened. The clearest measure is the share of killings the police solve. By the Abraham Initiatives' count the clearance rate for Arab-community homicides was 19 per cent in 2021, 21 per cent in 2022, 10.5 per cent in 2023 and about 10 per cent in 2025. [cite:us5_toi_2023toll] [cite:us5_toi_shinbet] Knesset and Eilaf data cited by CNN put it at about 15 per cent, compared with 65 per cent for killings of Jewish Israelis. [cite:us5_cnn_killingaday] State Comptroller Matanyahu Englman called the rise "a resounding failure of Israeli governments". [cite:us5_toi_2023toll]

Policy changed with each government. In 2021 the Bennett–Lapid government launched a five-year NIS 30 billion plan to close gaps between Arab and Jewish localities, along with NIS 2.4 billion for policing in Arab towns. The count fell in 2022. [cite:us5_toi_2023toll] [cite:us5_aawsat_police] Under the coalition agreement of 25 November 2022, Itamar Ben-Gvir received an expanded National Security Ministry with authority over the police. [cite:hm_jta_coalition2022] The Abraham Initiatives' report for 2023 said both plans had been "hollowed out", and it blamed the minister. Ben-Gvir had urged that the development plan be cancelled. [cite:us5_toi_2023toll] Funding for the "Stop the Bleeding" programme was cut after he took office, and in the following year the police official in charge of crime in Arab communities was dismissed. [cite:us5_cnn_killingaday] One finding points to police presence as a lever: in the two months after 7 October 2023, when more police were deployed in Arab towns, homicides fell by half compared with the two months before. [cite:us5_toi_2023toll] Ben-Gvir's own defence pointed elsewhere: "There is 20% less murder in the Jewish sector." [cite:us5_cnn_killingaday]

In December 2025 the Prime Minister's Office planned to move about $70 million from an Arab economic development programme to the police. [cite:us5_cnn_killingaday] On 15 July 2026 the government approved a role for the Shin Bet, the domestic security service, against the smuggling and trade of illegal weapons in Arab communities. The money, NIS 497 million, came out of the 2021 development plan: NIS 345 million for the Shin Bet and NIS 132.4 million for a new police unit. The proposal came from Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Social Equality Minister May Golan and Ben-Gvir. The Abraham Initiatives objected that "Crime is not terror". The Association for Civil Rights in Israel said crime among Jewish Israelis is handled by the police, and the Mossawa Center said the money was being taken from youth, training, transport and housing programmes. [cite:us5_toi_shinbet]

Neither country's national average describes the risk any given resident faces. America's record-low rate in 2025 sits on top of a racial gap of roughly eight to one. Israel's low national rate before 2023 hid a gap that grew from four to one to thirteen to one. In 2025, a homicide rate inside Israel's Arab communities above any American national rate on record was matched by a police force that solved about one Arab-community killing in ten. [cite:us5_ccj_trends] [cite:us5_taub_homicide] [cite:us5_toi_shinbet] Both governments face elections with these figures before them: Israel votes on 27 October and the United States on 3 November 2026.
