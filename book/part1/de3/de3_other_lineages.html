Exit, the Austrians, the Machine, and the Disputes

Three other currents run into the Dark Enlightenment texts, and each is claimed by the texts in their own words. The first is the libertarian idea of exit: that freedom is secured less by voting than by the ability to leave. The second is the Austrian school's long quarrel with democracy, carried by Hans-Hermann Hoppe and the institutions around him. The third, quite different in temper, is the cybernetic and accelerationist strand that Nick Land brought with him from 1990s academic philosophy. This section takes each in turn, then sets out where scholars and the ancestors themselves dispute the lineage, and closes by testing the book's worst-case reading against what this part has found.

Exit: from an economist's vocabulary to a political programme

The words "exit" and "voice" come from the economist Albert Hirschman's 1970 book Exit, Voice, and Loyalty, which described the two ways people respond to a failing organisation: they complain, or they leave. The critic Shuja Haider observed that Land read Peter Thiel's call to escape politics through exactly these terms. [cite:de3_haider] In Land's 2012 serial, "Voice-vs-Exit" becomes the defining choice of the age, and libertarians "are opting for voiceless flight." [cite:de3_land_de] His summary of Moldbug's ideal state is three words: "No voice, free exit." [cite:de3_land_de]

The documented source of the exit current is a 2009 essay by Thiel. On 13 April 2009, in the online forum Cato Unbound, he wrote: "I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible." [cite:de3_cato_thiel] He linked the decline of what he called capitalist democracy to the growth of welfare beneficiaries and the extension of the vote to women in 1920, and proposed three escape routes from politics: cyberspace, outer space and the ocean, where he backed the building of floating communities called seasteads; the libertarian's task, he wrote, was an "escape from politics in all its forms." [cite:de3_cato_thiel] On 1 May 2009 he posted a follow-up, "Your Suffrage Isn't in Danger. Your Other Rights Are," writing that "Voting is not under siege in America, but many other rights are." [cite:de3_cato_thiel] Land opened his 2012 serial with the 2009 discussion and Thiel's sentence; that quotation is the only appearance Thiel makes in the text. [cite:de3_land_de] Thiel's own later thinking is treated in [scene:de10_girard_and_the_katechon|Part Ten].

The seasteads had an institution behind them. The Seasteading Institute was founded on 15 April 2008 by the software engineer Wayne Gramlich and Patri Friedman, and Thiel gave $500,000 in seed money, as Wired reported on 18 May 2008. [cite:de3_wiki_tsi] Friedman supplied the line Land quotes as the creed of the current: "we think that free exit is so important that we've called it the only Universal Human Right." [cite:de3_land_de] Two figures circulate for Thiel's total giving. A 2011 report gave $1.25 million, with the institute insisting that its most generous funder "doesn't dictate" its plans; Business Insider in 2018 gave $1.7 million, reported that Thiel had left the board in 2011, and quoted him calling seasteads "not quite feasible." [cite:de3_yahoo_tsi] [cite:de3_bi_tsi] The two totals come from different dates and neither is itemised in a public filing. The institute's most concrete step, a memorandum signed with a French Polynesian minister on 13 January 2017, was declared by that government to have lapsed at the end of 2017. [cite:de3_wiki_tsi]

The Austrian institutions and the "odd marriage"

The Austrian current reached the texts through people and institutions as well as books. Hoppe was a fellow of the Mises Institute, founded in 1982 by Lew Rockwell, Burton Blumert and Rothbard, and in 2006 he founded the Property and Freedom Society, which holds annual conferences in Bodrum, Turkey. [cite:de3_wiki_hoppe] His book contains a passage that has followed him since, on how a libertarian covenant community should treat its opponents: "There can be no tolerance toward democrats and communists in a libertarian social order," continuing that they must be physically separated and expelled. [cite:de3_wiki_hoppe] That is an explicit call to exclude political opponents from a community, in the author's own words.

Land devoted a whole instalment of his serial, "Odd Marriages" (15 June 2012), to the alliance between this libertarian current and the nostalgic politics of the American South. He listed his evidence as "semi-random stripped-down data-points": the Mises Institute's founding in Auburn, Alabama; Ron Paul newsletters of the 1980s containing remarks "of a decidedly Derbyshirean hue", after the columnist John Derbyshire, whose 2012 race column is discussed in Part Four; Rothbard's writing in defence of "HBD", short for human biodiversity, a label for hereditarian claims about racial difference; and the libertarian Tom Palmer's charge that Rockwell and Hoppe had "Opened the Gates of Hell" to racists and cranks. [cite:de3_land_de] Land did not condemn the marriage. He concluded that "liberty has no future in the Anglophone world outside the prospect of secession." [cite:de3_land_de] The race material in that instalment is examined in [scene:de4_the_hard_passages|Part Four].

The cybernetic strand

The third current has nothing to do with Austrian economics. Land arrived at the Dark Enlightenment from continental philosophy, cybernetics and cyberpunk fiction, by way of a research group at the University of Warwick in the 1990s, and from a theory of capitalism as a self-accelerating machine that escapes human control. [cite:de3_haider] That strand, accelerationism, and its split into left and right versions are the subject of [scene:de4_ccru_acceleration|the next part]. It matters here for one reason: it means the Dark Enlightenment has two parents who do not agree. Moldbug's line runs from libertarianism to monarchy and asks who should own the state. Land's runs from a philosophy of runaway technological change, and in his serial treats Moldbug's corporate state as one vehicle among several for escaping democracy. [cite:de3_land_de]

Where the lineage is disputed

The most direct dispute comes from the most important ancestor. Asked in 2025 by Ava Kofman of The New Yorker about the claim that his book had midwifed Yarvin's politics, Hoppe replied: "I do not follow Yarvin's writings closely – too much rambling for my taste." [cite:de3_hoppe_reply] He said he had met Yarvin once, at a gathering to which Thiel had invited him, and that Yarvin had cited him on his blog. He set out his own ideal as "thousands upon thousands of Liechtensteins," small territories under a monarch or "member of the natural elite" from which subunits may secede, and he separated it from what he called the "Silicon valley crowd" model of running government like a business, arguing that states are founded on violence and should be dismantled rather than made efficient. [cite:de3_hoppe_reply] Kofman's sources called the influence direct and heavy; Hoppe accepts that he was cited and rejects the programme built on the citation. [cite:de3_wiki_dtgtf] [cite:de3_hoppe_reply] Both can be true: a book can open a door without approving of the house built beyond it.

The libertarian inheritance is disputed from the other side too. Moldbug himself declared it over, and Land's own case that libertarianism and the Confederate-nostalgic right had married was offered as a provocation that libertarian critics such as Palmer had already denounced. [cite:de3_land_de] The accelerationist inheritance is contested by its own founders, as the next part shows: Land's former colleague Ray Brassier said Land had gone from "Politics is dead" to "completely old-fashioned, standard reactionary stuff." [cite:de4_wiki_land] And the claim of a Technocracy lineage, set out in the previous section, rests on a family tie and a resemblance, not on any text. [cite:de3_cbc_leo]

The worst-case reading, tested

The book's working hypothesis, stated in the preface as the Sophia platform's and never as a finding, is that immigration enforcement and government cuts serve as the occasion and a sovereign executive without a veto as the destination. This part asked whether the inheritance of the texts supports it.

Documented: the texts descend, by their own account, from writers who preferred concentrated, owned rule to democratic rule, and Moldbug's line travelled openly from libertarianism to monarchy. [cite:de3_princeton_carlyle] [cite:de3_land_de] Documented too is a sharper strand of exclusion in the ancestry: Hoppe's "no tolerance" passage and Carlyle's defence of hierarchy, which Yarvin extended to a line on slavery in his own voice. [cite:de3_wiki_hoppe] [cite:de3_mm] Documented, finally, is Thiel's 2009 statement that freedom and democracy are incompatible, and his money behind the seasteading project. [cite:de3_cato_thiel] [cite:de3_wiki_tsi]

Probable: that the Hoppe-to-Moldbug step supplied the ownership argument on which the later case for a chief-executive state rests; Yarvin's own titles and Land's quotations make this the best reading, though Hoppe disowns the result. [cite:de3_hoppe_reply]

Not supported: that any of these lineages carries a plan for the American executive of 2025. The dominant inherited idea in this part is exit, which points away from capturing a national state, toward leaving it; Thiel's 2009 programme was an "escape from politics," and Hoppe's ideal is a scatter of small principalities from which subunits may secede. [cite:de3_cato_thiel] [cite:de3_hoppe_reply] The Technocracy line, often drawn to Musk and from Musk to the cuts of 2025, is a family tie and a resemblance; Musk's own stated case in February 2025 was for elected control over officials, and no document connects Technocracy to the Dark Enlightenment texts. [cite:de3_dcpd_musk] [cite:de3_land_de] On this part's material the record runs against the destination half of the hypothesis: the ancestors were mostly trying to get out of the state, not to seize it. Whether later readers turned an exit doctrine into a capture doctrine is a question for the parts on the men and the machinery, graded in [scene:de40_worst_case_scorecard|the final scorecard].
