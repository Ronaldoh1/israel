The Secret Cosmos: Sophia, the Blind Creator and the Way Back

Most of the Nag Hammadi texts teach through myth rather than sayings. They tell where the true God is, how a divine power fell, how a lower and ignorant ruler made the visible world, how a spark of the higher light came to be trapped in human beings, and how that spark is to be awakened and brought home. Scholars group most of these myths under two schools described in Part Twenty-Four: the Sethians, named for their claim to descend spiritually from Adam's son Seth, and the Valentinians. [scene:vt24_what_they_taught|Part Twenty-Four: What They Taught] This section goes through the central myth text by text, then the texts that describe the soul's ascent, then those that turn the myth against the bishops of their day.

The Apocryphon of John (Codex II, 1; III, 1; IV, 1)

What it is. The Apocryphon (Secret Book) of John is the fullest statement of the Sethian myth and the most heavily copied text in the find. Three copies were in the jar, and a fourth is in the Berlin Codex, a separate manuscript bought in Cairo in 1896 that also holds the Gospel of Mary. The copies come in two recensions: a long one in Codices II and IV, and a short one in Codex III and the Berlin Codex. All four are fourth-century Coptic translations of a Greek work written in the second century, before about 180. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] The Gospel of Mary is treated in the next part. [scene:vt26_gospel_of_mary|Part Twenty-Six: The Gospel of Mary]

Outline. After the crucifixion the apostle John is grieving. The Saviour appears to him in shifting forms, calms his fear and begins a revelation of how the world came to be and how it will end. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] The revelation runs in a fixed order, set out in the figure below. It begins with the unknowable first principle, the Monad, of which the text says: "The Monad is a monarchy with nothing above it." [cite:vt25_ecw_apjohn] From the Monad's thought comes Barbelo, the first emanation, and from her a series of aeons, eternal beings that together make up the divine fullness, the Pleroma. The last of these aeons, Sophia (Wisdom), wants to bring forth a likeness of herself without the consent of the Spirit and without her partner. The result is a misshapen being with a lion's face and a serpent's body, whom she hides in a cloud and names Yaldabaoth. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] Yaldabaoth has stolen some of his mother's power. He makes rulers, the archons, and a world of his own, and then he boasts: "I am a jealous God, and there is no other God beside me." [cite:vt25_ecw_apjohn]

The rest of the myth is the recovery of that stolen light. Sophia repents. A voice and an image of the true Human shine down on the waters, and the archons try to copy the image, which produces Adam, who lies motionless. The higher powers trick Yaldabaoth into breathing his mother's power into Adam, and the light passes out of the creator into the human being. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] The archons then imprison Adam in a body and a false paradise. The Saviour says he himself caused Adam to eat from the tree of knowledge, which the archons had hidden. Eve is drawn from Adam, and through her Adam recognises his own nature. Yaldabaoth binds humanity with a "counterfeit spirit" that keeps people ignorant. Salvation comes when Christ, speaking as Pronoia (Providence), descends into this prison. "I am the remembrance of the pleroma," he says, and he wakes those who sleep and seals them in light with five seals. [cite:vt25_ecw_apjohn] [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn] The text ends with a curse on anyone who would "exchange these things for a gift or for food or for drink". [cite:vt25_ecw_apjohn] The teaching may be given away, but it must not be sold.

What the institution said. Irenaeus does not name the Apocryphon of John. But in Against Heresies I.29, written around 180, he summarises a myth that closely matches its first half, which he attributes to "Barbeliotes". It has Barbelo, Ennoia, the Self-Begotten, Sophia "also called Prunicus", and a first ruler who says, "I am a jealous God, and besides me there is no one." Irenaeus answers from Exodus 20:5 and Isaiah 45 and calls the teaching a falsehood. [cite:vt25_iren_129] This is the strongest evidence that a version of the myth was in circulation before 180, and the text was still being copied in Egypt in the fourth century and again in the fifth (the Berlin Codex). [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn]

What scholars dispute, and a caution. The creator's boast echoes Isaiah 45:5 and Exodus 20:5, so the myth is a reading of the Hebrew Bible's God as an ignorant lower power. [cite:vt25_iren_129] That reading belongs to second-century Christian writers who were reinterpreting scripture they shared with Jews; it is a claim made inside a Christian argument, and this book does not repeat it as a claim about Judaism or about Jews. Modern antisemitic uses of the "demiurge" idea exist, and they have no basis in the history recorded here. Scholars also dispute whether the Sethian myth began among non-Christian Jewish or Platonist thinkers and was Christianised later. The long recension inserts material from a book attributed to Zoroaster, and the Christian frame can be lifted away from the core cosmology. [cite:vt25_wiki_apjohn]

Retellings of Genesis: the Hypostasis of the Archons and On the Origin of the World

The Hypostasis (Reality) of the Archons (Codex II, 4) is a Sethian retelling of Genesis 1 to 6, usually dated to the late second or third century. The rulers shape Adam but cannot make him stand. A spiritual woman gives him life, and when the rulers try to seize her she escapes into a tree and leaves only her shadow, the fleshly Eve. The serpent becomes the instructor who urges eating from the tree of knowledge, and the text treats that act as a gain rather than a fall. Norea, Seth's sister, burns Noah's ark when she is refused passage, and the angel Eleleth rescues her from the rulers and foretells a "true Man" who will overthrow them. [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch] Its neighbour in the codex, On the Origin of the World (Codex II, 5; opening lines also in XIII, 2), covers much of the same ground. It opens by denying that chaos came first, describes Pistis Sophia rebuking the arrogant ruler, and ends with the defeat of darkness and the return of the perfect to the light. Both texts tell of Sabaoth, the creator's son, who repents and is enthroned in the seventh heaven. It may have been written in Alexandria in the late third century. [cite:vt25_wiki_origin] [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch]

The voice of the divine feminine: Trimorphic Protennoia and Thunder, Perfect Mind

The Trimorphic Protennoia (Codex XIII, 1), a Sethian text, is spoken in the first person by Barbelo, the divine First Thought. She descends three times: first as Voice, then as Speech, then as Word, which takes human form. [cite:vt25_wiki_protennoia] The Thunder, Perfect Mind (Codex VI, 2) is a poem in a female divine voice made of paradoxes: "For I am the first and the last"; "I am the whore and the holy one"; "I am the wife and the virgin." [cite:vt25_pbs_thunder] It names no Sophia, no archons and no Christ, and scholars such as George MacRae and Hal Taussig have argued that it should not be classed as Gnostic at all. [cite:vt25_wiki_thunder]

Sethian scripture and the ascent texts

Several texts describe the Sethians' sacred history or an inner ascent through the heavens. The Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit, also called the Coptic Gospel of the Egyptians (Codex III, 2; IV, 2), traces Father, Mother and Son out of the invisible Spirit and makes Seth the father of an incorruptible race that is saved through baptism. [cite:vt25_wiki_holybook] In the Apocalypse of Adam (Codex V, 5), Adam tells Seth how knowledge was lost and how an Illuminator will come, while thirteen kingdoms give false accounts of where the Illuminator came from. It contains no clear Christian content, which leads some scholars to place it before or outside Christianity. [cite:vt25_wiki_apocadam]

Four treatises describe a visionary ascent. In Zostrianos (Codex VIII, 1), the longest tractate in the find, the seer leaves his body on a luminous cloud and rises through the aeons, receiving a baptism at each stage. [cite:vt25_wiki_zost] Allogenes (Codex XI, 3) describes contemplation rising through Barbelo toward an Unknowable God beyond being and mind, who can be approached only through a learned ignorance. [cite:vt25_wiki_allogenes] Marsanes (Codex X) ranks thirteen "seals" and links the soul's shape to the vowels and consonants, and the Three Steles of Seth (Codex VII, 5) is a liturgy of hymns to Barbelo for ascent and descent. [cite:vt25_wiki_marsanes] [cite:vt25_wiki_3steles] The Apocalypse of Paul (Codex V, 2) takes Paul up through the heavens, past a toll collector and an old man enthroned in the seventh heaven, to the tenth. The First Apocalypse of James (Codex V, 3) has Jesus teach James the answers he must give to the toll collectors who will question his soul after death. [cite:vt25_wiki_apocpaul] [cite:vt25_wiki_1apjas]

What the institution, and a philosopher, said. These ascent texts drew criticism from outside the church as well as inside it. The pagan philosopher Porphyry records that Christians in his teacher Plotinus's circle in Rome in the 260s circulated revelations under the names of Zoroaster, Zostrianos, Nicotheus, Allogenes and Messos. Plotinus wrote a treatise against them, now Ennead II.9, "Against the Gnostics", arguing that those who claimed sole possession of truth were the ones ignorant of it. [cite:vt25_bmcr_plotinus] [cite:vt25_ecw_allogenes] [cite:vt25_wiki_allogenes] Around 375 the bishop Epiphanius of Salamis reported in his Panarion that the Sethians and the Archontics "make use of books called 'Allogenes'". [cite:vt25_ecw_allogenes] So the first recorded attacks on the Sethian ascent books came from a pagan Platonist school and from a Christian heresy writer.

The Valentinian system: the Tripartite Tractate

The Tripartite Tractate (Codex I, 5) is the most systematic Valentinian work in the find, dated to the third or fourth century in its present form. In place of Sophia, the Logos is the power whose overreach causes the fall. Humanity is divided into three kinds: spiritual people, who will be saved completely; material people, who will perish; and "psychic" people in between, who can be saved if they choose the good. [cite:vt25_wiki_tripartite] This middle class matters for the history in this book. It gave Valentinian teachers a place for ordinary church members as people who could be saved, which helps explain why many Valentinians stayed inside the churches rather than founding separate ones. [scene:vt24_how_they_lived|Part Twenty-Four: How They Lived]

Texts that answer the bishops

Some of the texts in the jar argue back against the church that was condemning their schools. In the Apocalypse of Peter (Codex VII, 3), written around 200, Peter sees the living Jesus "glad and laughing" above the cross while only his fleshly body is nailed. The Saviour warns that some who call themselves "bishop" and "deacons", as if their authority came from God, are "dry canals". [cite:vt25_wiki_gapocpeter] The Second Treatise of the Great Seth (Codex VII, 2) also has Jesus die only in appearance. It calls the patriarchs, prophets and Moses a "laughingstock" of the lower rulers and mocks those who preach "the doctrine of a dead man". [cite:vt25_wiki_2seth] The Testimony of Truth (Codex IX, 3) turns in several directions at once. It rejects the Law, martyrdom, water baptism, marriage and childbearing, and it also condemns the Valentinians, the followers of Basilides and the Simonians as heretics. [cite:vt25_wiki_testtruth] These texts show that the condemnations ran in both directions. Their authors were rival Christians making their own claims to authority, not passive victims of an established church.

Interpretation, not finding: Sophia's fall and return as a map of ascent

The Sophia platform reads the myth in the Apocryphon of John as a map of consciousness rather than a cosmology. In this reading, Sophia's grasping without her partner is the mind acting apart from its source. Yaldabaoth is the self-made ego that believes it is the only god. The archons are the habits and compulsions that keep awareness asleep, and the five seals and the ascent past the toll collectors are stages of awakening. Carl Jung, whose institute bought Codex I, read Gnostic myth in a similar psychological way in the twentieth century, and the esoteric "solve et coagula" reading (dissolving the false self and recombining it in light) belongs to the same modern lens. These are readings brought to the texts by later interpreters. The ancient sources show that Sethian and Valentinian readers understood these myths as accounts of real divine beings and a real heavenly ascent, sometimes acted out through baptism and ritual.
