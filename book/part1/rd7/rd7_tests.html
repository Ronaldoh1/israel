The Tests

The map comes with a set of prepared questions, each of which removes part of the documented network and reads the result. They are listed below from the lowest chance to the highest, as in the map's Tests panel, and anyone can run them there, change them, or remove any other combination of nodes. The figures are the chance of a founding on time, in 1948, and the chance of a founding ever, against about 93 percent for the full record.

Seven observations follow from the table, and each is a statement about the record rather than about what anyone intended. Rows marked as often denied take a part of the record that accounts of the founding commonly deny or play down; the map's panel gives, for each, the claim usually made beside the record.

The movement and its armed groups were the two things nothing else replaced. Remove the organized movement and no one is left to pursue the state; remove the armed groups and the arms channel is empty, with nothing outside it able to fill it. Every other removal leaves something to rebuild with.

Two sources of money mattered more than any single leader. Edmond de Rothschild's colonies and the American appeals were the two largest sums, and they came at opposite ends of the period: his from 1882, when almost no one else was funding settlement, theirs in 1946 to 1948. Removing either makes the founding unlikely; removing both makes it impossible on this record. The founder of the political movement, by contrast, could be removed with little effect, because the institutions he started carried on without him.

Britain built the framework, and the framework was the Mandate. Without the Balfour Declaration, or without the British state that issued it and held the Mandate, immigration and settlement from 1920 to 1948 go at the Ottoman pace, and about 26 years of that work are lost. The stricter reading, under which the Declaration could not have been issued without its named addressee, Walter Rothschild, produces the same result because it removes the same document; the record shows that it was addressed to him and that he worked on its draft, not that it would have been withheld otherwise.

The United States counted far more than the Soviet Union, and the difference was its bloc. In 1947 the two powers voted the same way. Remove the American government and its bloc follows it only at its measured independence; the vote becomes uncertain, American recognition is gone, and the founding is delayed. Remove Stalin, or the Soviet bloc whole, and the vote still clears two-thirds in most outcomes. The map now weighs the Czechoslovak arms by what arrived before 15 May 1948, about 14,700 rifles and 1,655 machine guns for a force of about 30,000, close to half the rifles in hand at the founding; on their own they cost about ten points, and with the other arms from abroad, much of it bought in breach of embargoes, they bring the chance down to about two in three. Their full weight fell in the war after the founding, which the next section explains. [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel]

The men who decided mattered less than the decisions. Remove Truman and Henry Wallace decides in his place, a man on record as a supporter; the founding still happens on time. Keep Roosevelt alive and bound by his April 1945 pledge to Ibn Saud, and the American vote and recognition become uncertain, and the founding is delayed. The decisions were contested, and the record shows who pushed and who resisted. But in most of the tests someone else would have taken them, and the map can say who.

Who sat in the White House mattered through his own position, not through his party. Roosevelt's heart failure was diagnosed in March 1944, and in July his surgeon Frank Lahey wrote that he would not survive another term; the public was not told, and he ran and won. [cite:rd7_history_fdr_health] [cite:rd7_wiki_lahey] Had the diagnosis been public and the Republican Thomas Dewey won instead, little changes: Dewey had declared for a Jewish commonwealth in October 1944, after meeting Abba Hillel Silver, and in October 1946 asked for several hundred thousand immigrants, more than Truman did; the 1944 Republican platform had condemned Roosevelt for not pressing Britain. [cite:rd7_jta_dewey_1944] [cite:rd7_jta_dewey_1946] [cite:rd7_jvl_gop_1944] A Democrat with no recorded position delays the founding by about five years. Only a president bound by Roosevelt's own pledge to Ibn Saud lowers the chance materially, and even then to about 85 percent. American recognition on 14 May, removed on its own, costs only two points on the founding; its weight came afterwards, in arms, loans and membership of the United Nations.

The Holy Land did its work in the choice of the land, more than through organized Christian support. Remove the Christian Zionists the map holds, from Darby and Scofield to Blackstone's Memorial of 1891 and the American Christian Palestine committees, and the chance barely moves. But every other homeland the movement weighed, without either the Christian or the Jewish attachment to the land, scores between 0 and 2 percent on the same formula. The religious motives of Balfour and Lloyd George are recorded but not counted as a separate push, so the first figure is a floor.
