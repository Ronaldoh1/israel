Part Six established that instant runoff produces a winner with a majority of the ballots still counting, not of all ballots cast: Maine's 2018 winner finished with about 49 per cent of all ballots, and New York's 2021 primary ended with 140,202 inactive ballots. [cite:dt6_bp_me2_2018] [cite:dt6_nyc_2021] [cite:dt6_burnett_kogan] Two states and eleven cities use the method in 2026, eighteen states ban it, and voters rejected most statewide proposals to adopt it in 2024, while Alaska votes on repeal on 3 November 2026. [cite:dt6_fv_2026use] [cite:dt6_bp_bans2026] [cite:dt6_bp_2024results] [cite:dt6_bp_ak2026] The research on complexity and turnout is mixed, Alaska's 2022 count eliminated the Condorcet winner, and the reviewers most sympathetic to the method conclude that it changes less on its own than in combination with multi-seat districts, which the Fair Representation Act would require. [cite:dt6_mcdaniel_sf] [cite:dt6_na_review] [cite:dt6_clelland_ak] [cite:dt6_fra_text] Part Seven turns to the other fixes, from public financing and disclosure to redistricting commissions and open primaries.
