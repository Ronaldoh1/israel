What the Record Settles

The Muslim Brotherhood, the movement Hamas calls its parent, was founded in Egypt in 1928 and had branches in Gaza in the 1940s, before the State of Israel existed. [cite:hm_tamimi_2007] Israel licensed the Brotherhood's charity network in Gaza in 1979 and largely left it alone while it fought the PLO; some of the officials responsible later called that a mistake. [cite:hm_wsj_spawn] Hamas was founded in December 1987, in the first weeks of the uprising against twenty years of occupation, and its 1988 charter combined a claim to all of Palestine with antisemitic conspiracy theory. [cite:hm_avalon_charter]

From 1994 Hamas carried out suicide bombings against Israeli civilians, and from 2001 it fired thousands of rockets at Israeli towns; Human Rights Watch called the bombings crimes against humanity and the rockets war crimes. [cite:hm_wiki_afula] [cite:hm_hrw_erased] [cite:hm_hrw_rockets2009] It won the 2006 election, took Gaza by force in 2007, and ruled it with arbitrary detention and torture. [cite:hm_wiki_2006elect] [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] [cite:hm_hrw_2019] Israel restricted movement between Gaza and the West Bank from 1991 and blockaded Gaza from 2007, and from 2018 let Qatari cash into the strip, some of which, by the Shin Bet's account, reached Hamas's military build-up. [cite:hm_aj_separation] [cite:gz_un_hostile2007] [cite:hm_fdd_shinbet]

On 7 October 2023 Hamas-led fighters killed about 1,200 people in Israel, most of them civilians, and took 251 hostages; the war that followed killed more than 74,000 people in Gaza by the health ministry's count. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] [scene:hm7_attack|October 7] [scene:pr1_lists|The count] The two sides were never close to equal: Israel's economy was roughly 190 times the size of Gaza's before the war, and its military budget in 2024 was more than a hundred times Hamas's highest estimated military spending.
