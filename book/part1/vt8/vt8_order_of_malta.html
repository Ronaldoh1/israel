The Order of Malta: a Second Catholic Sovereign

Two Catholic bodies in Rome are treated by other states as sovereign. One is the Holy See. The other is the Sovereign Military Hospitaller Order of St John of Jerusalem, of Rhodes and of Malta, usually called the Order of Malta, which has no territory, about 13,500 members, and diplomatic relations with more than a hundred countries. Two questions are often asked about it: whether it is connected to the Vatican, and why it exists at all. The documents answer the first plainly: it is connected in two different ways at once, as a religious order that depends on the Holy See and as a sovereign body that exchanges ambassadors with it. The answer to the second lies in its history: a hospital that became a military order, ruled two islands, lost them, and kept the recognition that ruling them had brought.

A hospital in Jerusalem, recognised by a pope

The Order's own account begins in the second half of the eleventh century, when merchants from the Italian maritime republic of Amalfi obtained permission from the caliph of Egypt to build a church, convent and hospital dedicated to St John the Baptist in Jerusalem, to care for pilgrims. Under its first master, Blessed Gerard, the community became a lay religious order. Pope Paschal II's bull of 15 February 1113 recognised it, placed it under the protection of the Church and gave it the right to elect its superiors without outside interference. [cite:vt8_om_history] Popular accounts date the foundation to 1048 or to 1099; the hospital's exact start is not fixed by a single document, but the papal recognition of 1113 is. That bull is the first documented tie between the Order and the papacy, and it placed the Order under the Holy See and outside the control of local bishops. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom]

The Order took on armed defence of pilgrims and the sick in the crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem, and later the defence of the faith. [cite:vt8_om_history] After the crusader states fell it ruled Rhodes from 1310 until 1523, when it left the island under a negotiated surrender to Suleiman the Magnificent's besieging army. In 1530 the Emperor Charles V and his mother Joanna granted it Malta, with Pope Clement VII's approval, in return for a symbolic tribute of a falcon. It held Malta until June 1798, when Napoleon's forces took the island and Grand Master Ferdinand von Hompesch surrendered. After moving through Messina, Catania and Ferrara, the Order settled in Rome in 1834. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom]

Sovereignty without territory

The Order has been without land for more than two centuries. Italy recognises its two seats in Rome, the Palazzo Malta on Via Condotti and the Villa of the Priory on the Aventine Hill, as extraterritorial, and Italy's Supreme Court of Cassation held in 1974 that the Order should be treated in law as a foreign state. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom] Legal scholars disagree about what this amounts to. Some describe the Order as a subject of international law without territory; the British jurist Ian Brownlie argued that its legal capacities "must be limited" because it lacks the territory and population of a state. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom]

Its reach is nonetheless real. The Order states that it has diplomatic relations with 118 countries and with the European Union, cooperation agreements with more than 50 states, and accredited missions in more than 100 countries (its page as modified on 29 September 2026). [cite:vt8_om_diplomacy] An independently maintained list counts 114 members of the United Nations plus the Holy See, with Andorra, on 16 June 2025, the latest addition; the difference reflects how relations and recognitions are counted and should be stated with its source. The Order exchanges ambassadors with the State of Palestine and with the EU. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom_rel] On 24 August 1994 the UN General Assembly, by resolution 48/265 adopted without a vote, invited the Order to take part in its sessions as an observer. [cite:vt8_un_48_265] The Holy See itself, by comparison, had diplomatic relations with 184 states, the EU and the Order of Malta as of 9 January 2026. [cite:vt5_pv_relations2026]

The Order issues its own passports, coins and stamps. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom] The Grand Master, its head, is often described as a cardinal. He is not one: a royal Italian decree of 1929 gives him in Italy "the honors due to the Cardinals", which is a matter of precedence, not of rank in the Church. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom]

How it is tied to the Holy See

The tie runs through religious law, not through the Vatican City State. The Holy See said on 16 October 2012 that among orders of knighthood it "recognizes and protects only the Sovereign Military Order of Malta" and the Equestrian Order of the Holy Sepulchre, apart from its own pontifical orders. [cite:vt8_rv_equestrian2012] A Tribunal of Cardinals ruled in 1953 that the Order's sovereignty is "functional", serving its own purposes. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom] The two bodies have had formal diplomatic relations since February 1930. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom_rel] The Order is therefore at once a sovereign partner of the Holy See and a religious order under it. The limit of the first role was tested in 2016 and 2017.

In December 2016 Grand Master Matthew Festing removed the Grand Chancellor, Albrecht von Boeselager, after condoms had been distributed by other aid groups in a project in Myanmar run jointly with the Order's relief agency. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom] On 22 December 2016 the Holy See announced that Francis had set up a group to investigate the dismissal. On 10 January 2017 the Order called the dismissal "an internal act of governance" and said the papal inquiry was "legally irrelevant" given its sovereignty. On 24 January 2017 Francis received Festing and asked him to resign, and the resignation was accepted the next day. [cite:vt8_cna_festing] Boeselager was reinstated, and on 2 February 2017 Francis named Archbishop Giovanni Angelo Becciu as his special delegate to the Order. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom] (Becciu was convicted at first instance in a separate Vatican financial trial on 16 December 2023; on 17 March 2026 the Vatican Court of Appeal found a relative nullity and ordered the hearings renewed before the appeal court. Vatican News and ANSA reported that the first-instance judgment keeps effect for now, while AP reported that the indictment was nullified; the 2023 sentence is not final. As of October 2026 the next hearing was set for 29 October; Becciu denies wrongdoing and is presumed innocent. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_ap_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_us_oct2026] [scene:vt10_london_trial|Part Ten: the London trial])

The reform that followed was imposed by papal decree. On 3 September 2022 Francis promulgated a new Constitutional Charter and Code for the Order with immediate effect, revoked its high offices, dissolved its Sovereign Council, appointed a provisional council and convened an extraordinary General Chapter for 25 January 2023. The decree quoted the 1953 judgment that the Order is "a religious Order, approved by the Holy See", and stated: "being a religious Order, it depends, in its various articulations, on the Holy See." [cite:vt8_va_smom2022] The Order announced the same day that the new texts had entered into force and that the provisional council had taken its oath. [cite:vt8_om_const2022] On 3 May 2023 the Chapter elected Fra' John Dunlap as Grand Master for a ten-year term, renewable once; he is the 81st head of the Order counted from Gerard. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom] [cite:vt8_om_history]

What it does: Malteser International

The Order's stated reason for its diplomatic network is humanitarian: it describes its relations with states as "humanitarian diplomacy" serving its medical and relief work. [cite:vt8_om_diplomacy] The largest part of that work abroad is Malteser International, its relief agency. In 2024 the agency reported 152 projects in 36 countries, 7.2 million people supported, 3.2 million of them with emergency relief, 1,202 staff, revenue of €105.8 million and programme spending of about €74.5 million. In the Middle East and North Africa it spent €30.7 million on 18 projects in six countries. It reported 200 tonnes of food delivered into Gaza, including six convoys to the north, preparation of a container clinic in Gaza with the Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem, support for seven mobile medical units and eleven health centres in Lebanon, and named the Holy Family Hospital in Bethlehem as a focus of its work. [cite:vt8_mi_ar2024] The Order counts about 13,500 members and roughly 95,000 to 100,000 volunteers worldwide. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom]

The answer to the question

The Order of Malta is connected to the Vatican, but not as a hidden arm of it. The documents show two open ties: a religious tie, under which the Order "depends … on the Holy See" and a pope can remove its Grand Master and rewrite its constitution, as Francis did in 2017 and 2022; and a diplomatic tie, under which the two exchange ambassadors as sovereign equals. [cite:vt8_va_smom2022] [cite:vt8_cna_festing] [cite:vt8_wiki_smom_rel] It exists because a pilgrims' hospital of the eleventh century became a military order that governed two islands, and the recognition it gained as a ruler outlived the loss of its land; Italian courts, the UN General Assembly and more than a hundred states have continued to treat it as sovereign for the purposes of its medical and relief work. [cite:vt8_wiki_smom] [cite:vt8_un_48_265] [cite:vt8_om_diplomacy] When the Order claimed in January 2017 that its sovereignty put its internal acts beyond papal review, the claim did not hold. [cite:vt8_cna_festing] Its sovereignty is real in relation to states and limited in relation to the pope.
