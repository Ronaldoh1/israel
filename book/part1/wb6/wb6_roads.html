Checkpoints, Gates and Roads

925 obstacles. Inside the West Bank, movement is shaped by a second system. At the end of 2025 the UN's humanitarian office counted 925 obstacles that permanently or at times restrict the movement of 3.4 million Palestinians: 89 checkpoints staffed around the clock, 218 partial checkpoints, 232 road gates, 114 stretches of fencing or trenches, 167 earth mounds and 105 roadblocks. That was 43 percent more than the average of the previous twenty years. More than 120 new road gates were installed in 2025 alone, and 159 of the gates are frequently closed, cutting a village off from the main road at a soldier's decision. At least 459 of the obstacles block or slow access from Palestinian towns to main roads, 121 of them on Road 60, the West Bank's main north–south highway. [cite:wb_ocha_move2026]

The roads. Some roads were built across Palestinian land and then closed to Palestinians. Route 443, which links Jerusalem with the Tel Aviv area through the West Bank, was widened in the 1980s on thousands of dunams expropriated from Palestinian villages, with the army telling the court it would serve the local population. After shootings on the road in 2002 Israel barred Palestinians from using it, by car or on foot. In December 2009 the Supreme Court ordered the total ban lifted; the army's new arrangements, two more checkpoints among them, still kept Palestinians from using it as their main route to Ramallah, and more than a hundred shops along it have closed. [cite:wb_btselem_443]

The islands. Put together with the Oslo map, these measures produce the geography the Palestinians of the West Bank live in: 165 separate enclaves of Areas A and B, surrounded by Area C, [cite:wb_wiki_oslo2areas] joined by roads that can be closed at any of 925 points, [cite:wb_ocha_move2026] and cut off from 9 percent of their own land by a barrier. [cite:wb_ocha_barrier2022] Israel's stated reason for each obstacle is the security of Israelis on the roads and in the settlements, and the UN's counts do not record the reasons given for each. What the counts record is the result: a population that can move between its own towns only on terms set by another state's army.
