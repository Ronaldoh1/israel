Taxes, Debt and the Budget

How much a state takes in taxes, from whom, and how much it borrows are choices written into law, and the two countries have made different ones. This section compares total tax revenue as a share of the economy, the mix of taxes, who carries the burden, and the debt each government owes, over the longest periods the sources allow. The OECD totals used here count taxes at every level of government in both countries, which matters for the United States, where income, sales and property taxes are levied by states and localities as well as by Washington. The totals are therefore comparable; figures for the federal level alone, such as the Budget Office's, are not.

How much each state taxes

By the OECD's Revenue Statistics, all taxes in Israel, including social security contributions, came to 34.0 per cent of GDP in 2000, the highest level in the series since then, and 29.1 per cent in 2009, the lowest. They were 32.8 per cent in 2022 and 29.8 per cent in 2023, against an OECD average of 33.9 per cent. [cite:us11_oecd_rev_il] Israel's Finance Ministry chief economist reported in 2022 that the tax burden had fallen by 12 percentage points of GDP from its 1986 peak, when the state was paying for the stabilisation plan described in the previous section. [cite:us11_globes_taxburden] [scene:us11_turn|The turn] The American total, on the same OECD definition, was 23.6 per cent of GDP in 1965 and 27.7 per cent in 2022, the latest year in the published series, and has moved within a band of about 23 to 28 per cent throughout. [cite:us11_dbn_rev_us] Israel has therefore taxed a larger share of its economy than the United States, but by a narrowing margin: about 5 percentage points of GDP in 2022, against a 1986 Israeli peak of about 42 per cent implied by the ministry's figures, well above any American level in the series (this book's arithmetic). [cite:us11_globes_taxburden] [cite:us11_dbn_rev_us]

Which taxes

The mix differs more than the total. In 2022 Israel raised 23 per cent of its tax revenue from VAT and other taxes on goods and services, against an OECD average of 21 per cent, and 12 per cent from property taxes, against 5 per cent. Social security contributions brought in 15 per cent, against 25 per cent across the OECD, and personal income tax 22 per cent, against 24 per cent. [cite:us11_oecd_rev_il] Israel's standard VAT rate rose from 17 to 18 per cent on 1 January 2025, a measure presented as part of the effort to curb the wartime deficit; one estimate put the cost to a family at NIS 1,000–2,000 a year. [cite:us11_herzog_vat] [cite:us11_jns_vat] The United States has no national VAT; its consumption taxes are state and local sales taxes. Israel's top income tax rate in 2026 was 47 per cent above ILS 560,280 a year, with a 3 per cent surtax above ILS 721,560 bringing the maximum to 50 per cent, and from 2025 a further 2 per cent surtax on capital income above that threshold. [cite:us11_pwc_il_pit] The top American federal rate is 37 per cent, before state income taxes. [cite:us11_tpc_toprate]

Who pays

A heavy reliance on VAT matters for who pays, because a consumption tax takes a larger share of income from households that spend most of what they earn. Israel's income tax is narrow at the bottom: in 2021 about half of the people with income liable in principle to income tax earned below the threshold and paid none. In the Finance Ministry's figures the direct tax rate on gross income was about 40 per cent for the top tenth of earners, 30 per cent for the second tenth and 10 per cent for the bottom tenth. [cite:us11_globes_taxburden] The United States runs part of its support for low earners through the income tax itself: the Congressional Budget Office finds that the average federal income tax rate in 2022 ran from about minus 10 per cent for the lowest fifth of households, meaning that credits paid out exceeded tax owed, to 17 per cent for the highest. Across all federal taxes the average rate for all households was 20.6 per cent, close to its 1979–2022 average of 20.9 per cent. The top fifth paid 70 per cent of federal taxes in 2022, against 55 per cent in 1979, and the top 1 per cent paid 27 per cent, against 14 per cent. That rise in the top's share of taxes came alongside a rise in its share of income: the top 1 per cent's share of pre-tax income doubled from 9 to 18 per cent over the same years. [cite:us11_cbo_dist2022] The CBO figures cover federal taxes only; state and local taxes are not included.

Debt over time

The two governments' debts have moved in opposite directions over forty years. Israel's public debt stood at about 163 per cent of GDP in the mid-1980s. [cite:us11_jvl_melnick] The World Bank series for central government debt shows 132 per cent in 1990 and 88 per cent in 1999. [cite:us11_wb_cgdebt] On the IMF's measure of general government gross debt, Israel owed 77.5 per cent of GDP in 2000, 69.4 per cent in 2010, 63.2 per cent in 2015 and 61.9 per cent in 2023. [cite:us11_imf_debt] The war then reversed the trend. The Bank of Israel records gross public debt rising from 61.3 per cent of GDP in 2023 to 67.6 per cent in 2024 and 68.5 per cent in 2025, with deficits of 6.7 per cent of GDP in 2024 and 4.7 per cent in 2025. [cite:gi16_boi_2025] The IMF projected 68.8 per cent for 2026 and 71.9 per cent for 2030. [cite:us11_imf_debt]

American federal debt held by the public, the measure the Budget Office uses, was 26.1 per cent of GDP in 1980, 42.0 per cent in 1990 and 49.2 per cent in 1995, fell to 33.0 per cent in 2001, and was 36.8 per cent in 2007. [cite:us11_omb_debt] It then rose to 99 per cent of GDP at the end of 2025; the World Bank's broader central-government series shows the steepest jumps in 2008–09 and in 2020, from 100 to 124.5 per cent of GDP in a single year. [cite:us11_wb_cgdebt] [cite:us11_cbo_outlook2026] CBO projected in February 2026 a deficit of $1.9 trillion, 5.8 per cent of GDP, for fiscal 2026, debt passing the 1946 record of 106 per cent of GDP in 2030 and reaching 120 per cent in 2036, and net interest costs of $1.0 trillion in 2026, 3.3 per cent of GDP. [cite:us11_cbo_outlook2026] The two countries' figures here use different definitions (general government gross debt for Israel, federal debt held by the public for the United States) and are shown for their trends, not to rank the levels.

What the budget pays for

The largest single change in Israel's budget over the period is defence. Melnick and Mealem put defence spending at more than 20 per cent of GDP in the mid-1980s and under 10 per cent by 2008; the Bank of Israel records 4.2 per cent in 2022, before rising to 8.4 per cent in 2024 and 7.8 per cent in 2025. [cite:us11_jvl_melnick] [cite:gi16_boi_2025] The fall in defence spending from the 1980s to 2022 coincided with Israel's falling tax burden and debt; the wars since 2023 have reversed part of it. The cost of the wars to each treasury is set out in detail elsewhere in the library, and defence budgets over time in Part Nineteen of this volume. [scene:gi16_costs|The bill: costs] [scene:us19_spending|Part Nineteen: defence spending] The American aid of $3.8 billion a year is about half a per cent of Israel's GDP and is, by 2028, to be spent entirely on American weapons. [cite:gi16_inss_aid] [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] [scene:gi16_aid|The aid] For the United States the rising item is interest: CBO projects net interest of $1.0 trillion in 2026, 3.3 per cent of GDP, rising to $2.1 trillion, 4.6 per cent, in 2036. [cite:us11_cbo_outlook2026]
