Part Three followed Gaza from uprising to withdrawal. The first intifada began in Jabaliya on 9 December 1987; in its first year 142 Palestinians were killed in the strip, and over six years, by B'Tselem's count, between 1,087 and 1,204 Palestinians and 179 Israelis were killed in both territories, while hundreds of Palestinians were killed by Palestinians as alleged collaborators. [cite:gz_wiki_intifada1] Hamas, founded the day after it began, set out in 1988 a charter rejecting any settlement. [cite:gz_wiki_hamas] The Oslo agreements brought a Palestinian Authority to Gaza in 1994 while Israel kept the settlements, the crossings and the permits, and the second intifada ended the interim period in violence. [cite:gz_wiki_gazajericho] [cite:gz_carnegie_labor] [cite:gz_wiki_intifada2] In 2005 Israel removed all 21 settlements and its army, and kept control of the strip's borders, airspace, sea and registry. [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement]

What is settled is the sequence and the numbers as the named organizations counted them. What is contested is whether Israel helped the Islamist movement grow in its early years, what the disengagement was for, and above all whether the occupation ended in 2005. What the record leaves open is the question the next part answers in detail: what control from outside meant for the people inside, once Hamas took power in the strip and Israel and Egypt closed it.
