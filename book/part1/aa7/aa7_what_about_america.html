What About America?

The case for

The case for the aid, as its advocates make it, has four parts. Israel is a democratic ally in a hostile region. The missile defences built with American money, Iron Dome, David's Sling and Arrow, protect civilians and are now partly made in the United States. Intelligence and technology flow both ways. And the military financing is spent largely on American weapons, in American factories. [cite:aa_crs_2023] [cite:ow8_rtx_camden] [cite:aa_mil_f15]

The case against

The case against has its own four parts. Israel is now a wealthy country; Secretary of State Rubio said in November 2026 that it was rich enough to buy its own weapons. [cite:dv4_toi_rubio_nov2026] The aid covers about 16 percent of Israel's defence budget, a share Israel could replace. [cite:aa_crs_2023] The cost to Americans goes beyond the aid: between October 2023 and September 2025 American operations in the region cost an estimated $9.65 to $12.07 billion more. [cite:aa_hartung_2025] And the laws meant to condition the aid were not applied when two agencies said they should be. [cite:aa_pp_620i]

The public and the president

An ABC News, Washington Post and Ipsos poll in October 2026 found that 58 percent of Americans wanted the United States to reduce or end military support for Israel. [cite:aa_abc_trump] On 3 November 2026, the last day of voting in the midterm elections, President Trump said on Fox News that "by the end of this year, we won't be paying Israel." He said Israel had "become a very successful country" and did not need the money, that Netanyahu had raised the idea, and that Republicans should not "suffer the slings" of Democratic attacks over it. [cite:aa_abc_trump] Part Eight follows what happened next.
