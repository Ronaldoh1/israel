Part Three set out both sides of a counterfactual that no record can settle. The case that Israel made Hezbollah rests on documented facts: the organisation took shape during the 1982 invasion, named the occupation as its first enemy, and was called the occupation's product by Ehud Barak, in words reported since 2006 though their original source is unconfirmed, while Yitzhak Rabin warned in 1985 of the Shia resistance it was producing and the leading American scholar, Augustus Richard Norton, said it was "created by the Israeli occupation of Lebanon and by Iran". [cite:hz3_wiki_hezbollah] [cite:hz3_statesman_barak] [cite:hz3_djn_rabin85] [cite:hz3_npr_norton] The case that it would have formed anyway rests on facts too: Musa al-Sadr's movement and Amal's armed wing predated 1982, Hezbollah's founders came from a clerical network trained in Najaf, and Iran's revolutionary state sent an ambassador and Revolutionary Guards to build it, which an Israeli researcher argues was decided in Tehran before the invasion and which Naim Qassem has said the invasion did not cause. [cite:hz3_wiki_amal] [cite:hz3_wiki_dawa] [cite:hz3_itic_shapira] The documented answer is that Hezbollah had more than one parent; which one was decisive is an argument, labelled here as such.
