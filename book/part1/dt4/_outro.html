Part Four established that the rules on campaign money were made by the courts: Buckley split contributions from spending in 1976, Citizens United removed the ban on corporate and union independent spending by five votes to four while upholding disclosure eight to one, and SpeechNow created the super PAC; in June 2026 NRSC v. FEC, by six votes to three, lifted the caps on parties' spending coordinated with their nominees. [cite:dt4_wiki_buckley] [cite:dt4_wiki_cu] [cite:dt4_volokh_speechnow] [cite:dt7_skadden_nrsc] Outside spending reached a record $4.5 billion in 2024, more than $1 billion of it from undisclosed sources, with nine-figure donors on both sides and, in 2026 so far, $197.7 million in dark money flowing into the four party-leadership super PACs. [cite:dt4_os_2024record] [cite:dt4_issueone_2026] What that money buys is disputed: top fundraisers usually win, but the measured effect of super PACs is modest, the affluent's policy edge is real but contested in size, and the heaviest spenders have lost high-profile races.
