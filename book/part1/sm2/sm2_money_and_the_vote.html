Money and the Vote

In 1948 Abraham Feinberg and Ed Kaufmann raised the $100,000 that Truman's campaign needed for its whistle-stop tour, and in the historian John Judis's account Truman believed that money made his election possible. [cite:mondoweiss_judis_truman] It is one historian's account of one decision. It is specific, dated and sourced, and it is the only instance of its kind in the record of that era. [cite:mondoweiss_judis_truman] It was also small: about a tenth of a million dollars beside a $100 million Export-Import Bank loan of 1949 and the tens of millions raised for arms. [cite:jvl_truman_marshall_dejure] [cite:israelaffairs_italy] Truman's own pro-recognition record came before the money, and in May 1949 he threatened Israel with a "revision" of policy that he never enforced. [cite:jvl_truman_note_may1949]

The money today is of a different order. Pro-Israel contributions ran at about $15 million a cycle in 2018; in 2024 AIPAC's political action committee and its super PAC, the United Democracy Project, spent between $95 million and $127 million, depending on the count. [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] The method has a history. In 1982 Representative Paul Findley of Illinois lost his seat and in 1984 Senator Charles Percy lost his, in races where activists associated with AIPAC backed the challenger. [cite:pm_wiki_aipac] In 2024 the super PAC spent almost $9.9 million against Jamaal Bowman and more than $5.2 million against Cori Bush, and both lost. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] In 2026 it helped defeat Thomas Massie alongside the president. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways]

The same filings record defeats. In 2026 the super PAC lost races in Michigan, California and Illinois despite record sums. [cite:pm_prospect_illinois] [cite:pm_kqed_wahab] Critics argue that the main effect of the money is in the races it never has to enter, among members who decline to criticise Israel after seeing what happened to Bowman, Bush and Massie. Defenders answer that members who support Israel do so because they and their voters believe in the alliance, and that the money follows positions rather than creating them. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways] Disclosure records show who gave and where it went; they do not by themselves show why a member voted as they did.

What changed is the function of the money. In 1948 it was a gift to a candidate who had already decided. Today it is a standing capacity to enter a primary, and its warning reaches members who never face it.
