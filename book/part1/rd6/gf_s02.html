Charity for an Army: Budgets, Defense, and Britain's Bill

It is sometimes said that the pre-state army was funded secretly, by unknown means, and that Britain ran Palestine at little cost. On both counts the record is more specific. The movement's operating budgets were reported at the time, its defense financing ran through named channels, and Britain itself put a figure on what holding the country cost.

The budgets can be followed from 1939. That year, after the Zionist Congress, the US consul in Jerusalem reported that the Jewish Agency's budget had been set at £720,000, about $74 million today. Of that, £100,000 was for refugee settlement and £80,000 for "the Jewish security service (defense)." [cite:frus_1939_ja_budget] Defense, in other words, was a line in the budget from at least that date.

The American share grew fast. In 1944 the Jewish National Fund spent $10.8 million and the Jewish Agency and Keren Hayesod $14.9 million of money raised in the United States, a combined $25.7 million. The United Palestine Appeal's budget for 1945 was $35.3 million. [cite:djn_1945_upa] In 1946 the Agency said it had received $12 million from the United States the previous year, and it sought a budget of $56 million, about $0.93 billion today. [cite:djn_1946_budget]

Defense money had its own channels. In 1938 the Jewish National Council created Kofer HaYishuv, a levy on cigarettes, restaurant bills and luxuries, to fund central security and expand the Haganah. [cite:w_kofer] Ten years later the link between charity and defense was stated openly in the United States. In June 1948 a community campaign advertisement in Detroit told donors that "the United Jewish Appeal is the official American agency for aid to Haganah," and that the only channel for such aid was the local Allied Jewish Campaign. It also warned that collecting money for dissident militias was fraud. [cite:djn_1948_haganah] Charity and defense ran through the same pipe, and the campaign said so.

The other side of the ledger was Britain's. The Mandate's civil budget rose from £P1.9 million in 1922–23 to £P3.2 million in 1934–35. [cite:horowitz_1937] The garrison cost £1.5 million in 1923–24. [cite:league_report_garrison] By the British government's own end-of-Mandate statement, the military forces in Palestine cost the British taxpayer about £100 million in 1945–47, about $6.7 billion today, and Parliament debated keeping some 80,000 to 100,000 soldiers there. [cite:hmg_termination_1948] [cite:hansard_1947_08] About 750 British military and police personnel were killed over the period. [cite:nam_palestine]

The picture that results is substantial but not complete. The operating budgets, the defense levy, the charity channel and Britain's cost are all documented. No audited total of the Haganah's budget is publicly available, and this book does not supply one. What can be said is that the pre-state defense effort was not financed by unknown means. It was financed in part through a published budget line, in part through a domestic levy, and in part through American campaigns that described themselves as the official channel of aid. Britain, for its part, was paying heavily, by its own account, to hold the territory in the Mandate's final years.
