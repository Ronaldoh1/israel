What Washington Cannot See

The second half of the one-way claim is about visibility. The phrase that prompted this volume put it plainly: Israel can see into American systems, but the United States cannot see what Israel does in Gaza or the West Bank. The record supports a version of that claim, but a narrower and more specific one than the slogan. What the United States cannot see is mostly not the territory as such, over which American drones have flown since 2023. [cite:ow4_d1_drones] It is how Israel uses what the United States gives it, and whether American law on that use is being kept.

Raw intelligence, without verification. Under the memorandum agreed in principle in March 2009, as published by The Guardian in 2013, the NSA sends Israel's Unit 8200 raw collection, including unevaluated and unminimised transcripts that may contain the communications of Americans. Israel undertakes to destroy communications of American officials "upon recognition," and the NSA undertakes to review a sample of what it sends; the memorandum states that it "is not intended to create any legally enforceable rights." [cite:ow4_alternet_guardian] The NSA said in 2013 that it "cannot... use these relationships to circumvent US legal restrictions." [cite:ow4_alternet_guardian] The terms of this and other American intelligence-sharing agreements have never been officially released; what is known comes from leaked documents, and freedom-of-information requests for the current terms of even the oldest such arrangement, with Britain, have produced nothing newer than a 1955 text. [cite:ow4_aclu] [cite:ow4_js_ukusa] The record does not document a reciprocal raw feed from Israel to the NSA on comparable terms; the memorandum is examined in [scene:ow4_memorandum|Part Four].

Gaza: sharing more, seeing less of the use. Before October 2023 the United States relied largely on Israel for intelligence about Gaza. After the Hamas attack, a White House memorandum expanded sharing to include raw intelligence such as live drone feeds, and the United States widened its own collection; the Pentagon said in November 2023 that it was flying unarmed drones over Gaza to support efforts to recover hostages. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] [cite:ow4_d1_drones] American officials told The Wall Street Journal in April 2024 that the United States does not share intelligence intended specifically for ground or air strikes, that Israel certifies its own compliance with the rules on use, sometimes orally, and that once American intelligence is combined with Israel's it is hard to know how it is used. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] Reuters reported in December 2025 that in the second half of 2024 the United States briefly cut off a live drone feed and restricted Israel's use of some intelligence on high-value targets, over civilian deaths and the treatment of prisoners, and resumed after assurances; the Israeli army said, "The strategic intelligence cooperation continued throughout the war." [cite:ow3_reu_withheld] The intelligence side of this is set out in [scene:ow4_gaza|Part Four].

Weapons, without a check on use. When President Biden required recipients of American weapons to give written assurances that they would use them lawfully, the resulting report of 10 May 2024 said that "Israel has not shared complete information to verify whether U.S. defense articles were specifically used" in incidents of concern, and that the lack of American personnel on the ground made it "difficult to assess or reach conclusive findings on individual incidents," though it was "reasonable to assess" that American weapons had been used in instances inconsistent with international humanitarian law. [cite:ow4_axios_nsm20] The Leahy laws bar American aid to foreign security units credibly implicated in gross violations of human rights, but for Israel alone, according to Charles Blaha, a former State Department official who worked on them, decisions on ineligibility are made at the level of the Deputy Secretary of State, through a special forum, the Israel Leahy Vetting Forum; Israel may take up to three months to answer American queries, and in more than four years the forum did not identify a single ineligible Israeli unit. [cite:ow11_js_blaha] A classified State Department inspector general's report, described by The Washington Post in October 2025, found a backlog of "many hundreds" of potential violations by Israeli units in Gaza that would take "multiple years" to review; the department did not comment. [cite:ow11_wapo_oig] The full mechanism is set out in [scene:ow8_leahy|Part Eight] and [scene:ow11_exceptions|Part Eleven].

No independent eyes. The public's view is narrower still. Since October 2023 Israel, which controls entry, has not allowed foreign journalists into Gaza except on visits escorted by its army. The Foreign Press Association petitioned Israel's Supreme Court; on 8 January 2024 the court rejected the petition on the state's security grounds, and the association called the ban "unprecedented." [cite:pr_mm_fpa2024] As of mid-October 2026, three years into the war and with a ceasefire in place, the ban remained. [cite:pr_wlrn_ban_full] Its course through the courts is told in [scene:pr8_gate|The Gate] and [scene:gz12_access|the Gaza volume]. The stated Israeli reason is the danger to reporters and to troops. The effect, for American oversight, is that the government's own finding on its weapons was limited by the absence of American personnel on the ground, and the press could not fill the gap independently. [cite:ow4_axios_nsm20]

The governments' positions. The American government's position, stated in each of these documents, is that sharing serves American interests in counterterrorism and in the release of hostages, that it does not supply intelligence for strikes, and that Israel's assurances are credible. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] [cite:ow4_axios_nsm20] Israel's army has said that its intelligence cooperation with the United States "continued throughout the war," and its government has told its own courts that independent press access would endanger journalists and soldiers. [cite:ow3_reu_withheld] [cite:pr_mm_fpa2024] The asymmetry this section documents does not depend on deciding who is right. It is structural: at each step after an item leaves American hands, verification passes to the recipient.
