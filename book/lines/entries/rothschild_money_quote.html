No document shows that Mayer Amschel Rothschild, who died in 1812, ever said it. The earliest versions traced so far are anonymous: a "maxim of the money lenders of the Old World" quoted at a United States congressional hearing in 1908, and a 1913 Treasury text that pinned it on "the House of Rothschild" rather than on any person. Attributions to Mayer Amschel by name appear only in the 1930s, more than a century after his death, with the wording and the date changing from one telling to the next [cite:rs_ch01_popik_quote]. The line still circulates widely; in 2024 a former British prime minister's memoir carried it, and her publisher agreed to remove it after it was shown to be false [cite:rs_ch01_jc_truss].

The family's real record is documented and does not need the invented line: the loans to states, the Suez advance of 1875 and Edmond's colonies are all set out in The Road to 1948. The quotation turns that record into a claim of hidden rule over nations, which is the shape of an old anti-Jewish story about Jewish money, not a finding.
