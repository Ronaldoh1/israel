Agent, Asset or Broker

The intelligence question is usually asked as if it had two answers: either Epstein was a spy, or the idea is a conspiracy theory. The record supports a third answer more precisely than either, and it helps to name the distinction. A spy is employed and directed by an agency. An asset is someone an agency, or several, cultivates for what they know and whom they can reach, often in exchange for favours, without employing them. The journalist Craig Unger put it this way about Epstein: "I would think he's an asset, not a spy." [cite:ep7_euronews_intel2026]

Set against the documents, the evidence fits that description better than it fits a spy. Epstein sought contact with officials of several states at once: an FSB-connected Russian official, a Russian ambassador, a former Israeli prime minister, a Qatari former prime minister, a Gulf ports chief, envoys and diplomats. He traded introductions, information and favours. And the network map shows the same thing from another angle: his money sources and his foreign contacts do not connect to each other except through him, with two exceptions. One is a banker, Paul Morris, who followed Epstein's account from JPMorgan to Deutsche Bank and then joined Bank of America. [cite:ep3_ta_morris2026] The other is the building on East 66th Street, which Wexner had sold to a company linked to Epstein's brother and in which Barak later stayed. [cite:ep7_aj_apt66_2026] Both routes run through people who worked for or around Epstein himself. Nothing documented connects his backers to Russian or Gulf officials at all. That is the position of a broker who sells access in every direction, and someone in that position is valuable to intelligence services without being employed by any of them.

What would change the finding

The finding is limited by what has been released, and the record says where the missing pieces are. Four documents would change it. The unredacted DEA memo would name the fourteen people investigated alongside Epstein and might show where his money went. The full bank wire records would show who paid him and whom he paid. The deliberations behind the 2008 agreement would show whether anyone above the US Attorney's office was consulted, and why. And the files the Justice Department has still not published would show whether the informant's claims were ever checked. Each is held by an American government office or a bank, and each has been asked for.

The answer the record can give in 2026 is this: Epstein was close to intelligence-adjacent people in several countries and useful to many of them, and the documented protection he received came from American institutions, prosecutors, banks and a Justice Department that is still withholding records, not from a foreign service. Whether any agency used him is an open question with named documents that would answer it. Part Eight turns to the institutions whose protection is documented.
