2006–2007: An Election, a Capture, a Takeover

Within two years of the withdrawal, Gaza had a different government and a different relationship with the world around it. This section sets out the three events that produced the blockade: an election that Hamas won, a raid that took an Israeli soldier into Gaza, and a short war between Palestinian factions that ended with Hamas alone in control of the strip.

The election. On 25 January 2006 Hamas won the election to the Palestinian Legislative Council, in a vote that international observers judged free and fair, and on 29 March it formed a government. [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] The response was immediate. Israel withheld the tax revenue it collected on the Palestinian Authority's behalf, the Quartet of the United States, the European Union, Russia and the UN stopped aid to a Hamas-led government, and Egypt and Israel largely closed Gaza's crossings. [cite:gz_wiki_blockade] [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007]

The capture. On 25 June 2006 militants from Gaza crossed into Israel through a tunnel near Kerem Shalom, attacked an army post, killed two soldiers and took a third, Gilad Shalit, back into the strip. Hamas held him for more than five years; he was freed on 18 October 2011 in exchange for 1,027 Palestinian prisoners. [cite:gz_wiki_shalit]

The takeover. Fatah and Hamas signed the Mecca Agreement on 8 February 2007 to halt the fighting between them and formed a unity government in March, but in June fighting broke out again. Between 7 and 15 June 2007 Hamas forces took the strip's security headquarters and Fatah's bases; on 14 June President Mahmoud Abbas dissolved the unity government and declared a state of emergency, and by 15 June Hamas controlled the whole strip. The Palestinian Centre for Human Rights counted at least 161 people killed. [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007]

A contested account. What the fighting was is disputed. Fatah and its supporters called it a Hamas coup. In April 2008 the journalist David Rose, writing in Vanity Fair, reported documents suggesting that the United States had worked with the Palestinian Authority and Israel on a plan to oust Hamas by force, which Hamas had pre-empted; David Wurmser, a former adviser in the Bush administration, was quoted calling the events "an attempted coup by Fatah that was pre-empted." [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] The record shows the outcome without settling the intentions: from June 2007 Hamas governed Gaza, and the Palestinian Authority, under a new prime minister appointed by Abbas, governed the parts of the West Bank under its control.
