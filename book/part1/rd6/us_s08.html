Fifty-One Years From a Bank to a State: The Whole Sequence, Step by Step

In 1897 a congress at Basel set a goal; in 1948 a state was declared. A common account treats Israel's creation as a sudden response to the Holocaust and to a postwar decision at the United Nations. Laid end to end, the record shows something else: a sequence built over five decades and across two continents, each step resting on the one before. The movement did not start cold and turn hot in 1947.

The first layer was money and land. In 1897 the Basel Congress set the goal. In 1899 the Jewish Colonial Trust was founded, a bank chartered in London and funded by small shareholders. In 1901 the Jewish National Fund began buying land that was never to be resold. In 1902 came the Anglo-Palestine Bank. Between 1912 and 1918 an American machine took shape: Hadassah; Brandeis, with access to President Wilson; and the Zionist Organization of America, at 147,000 members. In 1917 came the Balfour Declaration, Britain's pledge, addressed to Lord Rothschild. In 1920 came Keren Hayesod, the movement's central fund.

The second layer was political and monetary. In 1921 came the Weizmann–Einstein tour, and a $5 million tour that met President Harding. In 1922 Congress endorsed Balfour through the Lodge–Fish resolution. In 1927 a Palestine pound managed from London was introduced. In 1929 non-Zionist American money joined an enlarged Jewish Agency. In 1932, and again in 1941 and 1942, Christian committees were organized from the Zionist political office. In 1933 the Haavara transfer agreement was concluded.

The third layer was mobilization. In 1942 the Biltmore program committed the movement to a commonwealth. In 1943 AZEC built its lobbying machine. In 1944 the movement's aims entered both party platforms. In 1945 the secret Sonneborn meeting created an arms network. In 1948 Golda Meyerson's tour raised about $50 million, Czech arms were bought, and banknotes were printed in New York.

Beneath all of this lay older foundations: the British and Ottoman layers of administration, a Christian restorationist lobby dating from 1839, and the Rothschild colonies dating from 1882.

Seen whole, the sequence changes what 1948 means. The familiar version stops at the Holocaust. The documents go further back. The Holocaust transformed the urgency, the politics and the moral case for a state. But the record shows that it did not create the apparatus. The bank, the land fund, the American fundraising and lobbying machine, the congressional endorsement and the arms channels had been assembled over the previous half-century. When the moment came, the instruments already existed. The state was not improvised in two years.
