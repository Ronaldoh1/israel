The Leaders

Israel killed most of the men who led Hamas into the war. Its political leader, Ismail Haniyeh, was killed in Tehran in July 2024; Yahya Sinwar, its leader in Gaza, was killed by Israeli troops in Gaza in October 2024; Israel said it had killed the military commander Mohammed Deif in July 2024, his deputy Marwan Issa in March 2024, and Sinwar's brother Mohammad in May 2025. Khalil al-Hayya, based in Qatar, became the most prominent of those left. [cite:gz_almon_hamasleaders]

The Ceasefire and the Weapons

The ceasefire of October 2025 rested on a twenty-point American plan: Hamas's tunnels and weapons to be dismantled under independent monitoring, amnesty for members who gave up their arms, Gaza run by a Palestinian committee under a Board of Peace chaired by President Trump, and Israeli troops to withdraw in stages as an international force took their place. [cite:gz_gn_20points] Hamas agreed to release all the hostages and to hand Gaza's administration to independent technocrats, without saying whether it would disarm. [cite:gz_almon_hamas_plan] The last hostage's body was recovered in January 2026. A National Committee for the Administration of Gaza was formed that month; Hamas rejected a disarmament plan in April 2026 and announced in July that its civil administration would step down. [cite:hm_wiki_peaceplan]

On 30 July 2026 the Board of Peace announced an agreement on disarmament. Hamas's weapons were to be inventoried and held only by a Palestinian National Committee, never handed to Israel; heavy weapons, workshops, depots and tunnels were to go first; and Israeli troops were to withdraw stage by stage as each step was verified. Hamas's negotiator Ghazi Hamad said it would take no step if Israel did not withdraw. [cite:hm_aj_disarm2026] An Israeli diplomatic source said Israel required full disarmament before any withdrawal: "There will be no Israeli withdrawal from the Yellow Line." [cite:hm_jp_noWithdraw] In September the army said Hamas was taking weapons back out of its depots and struck several of them; Netanyahu said Israel held 60 percent of the Strip. [cite:hm_jp_reclaim]
