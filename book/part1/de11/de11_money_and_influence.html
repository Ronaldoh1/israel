Money and Political Influence

Until 2024 Musk's political money was modest for a man of his wealth. In the 2024 election he became the largest individual donor in the country, and in 2026 he spent more again. This section is a ledger: what he gave, to whom, when, and on what documentary basis, followed by the influence that is documented in the same period, appointments, contracts and regulatory relief, and the break with President Trump that interrupted it. The ledger reports sequence. Where money precedes an office, a contract or a policy, the record shows the order of events; it does not, on its own, show that one caused the other, and no document shows an exchange.

2024: the largest donor

Musk endorsed Trump in July 2024 and put most of his money into a super PAC of his own, America PAC. Year-end filings with the Federal Election Commission showed that he spent more than $290 million on the election, about a quarter of a billion of it through America PAC, including $11.2 million on the last day of 2024 earmarked for "petition incentives." [cite:de11_cnn_290m] He also gave millions to other Republican outside groups and $10,000 cheques to dozens of state Republican Party committees. [cite:de11_cnn_290m] The research group OpenSecrets put his 2024 giving at more than $291 million, at the top of the national list. [cite:de7_os_donors2024]

The most contested part of the spending was a daily $1 million giveaway to registered voters in swing states who signed an America PAC petition. Launching it in Harrisburg on 19 October 2024, Musk said the money would go "randomly to people who have signed the petition every day," and the next day posted that signers had "a daily chance of winning $1M!" [cite:de11_ap_sweeps] Philadelphia's district attorney, Larry Krasner, sued to stop it as an illegal lottery. In court on 4 November, America PAC's lawyer said: "The $1 million recipients are not chosen by chance." The PAC's director testified that recipients were vetted in advance and signed non-disclosure agreements, and conceded that "randomly" was not "the word I would have selected." [cite:de11_ap_sweeps] Judge Angelo Foglietta let the giveaway continue through election day. [cite:de11_ap_sweeps] A separate class action by a voter in federal court in Texas, in which Musk argued that the recipients were "selected to earn" the money, survived his motion to dismiss on 20 August 2025. [cite:de11_audit_mcaferty] The giveaways cost more than $50 million in all. [cite:de11_cnn_290m]

From donor to office

Trump won on 5 November 2024 and announced that Musk, with Vivek Ramaswamy, would lead a "Department of Government Efficiency." In January 2025 it was created by executive order, and Musk was designated a special government employee, a status limited to 130 days' service in a year. His formal title was not head of DOGE: in a court filing on 17 February 2025 the White House described him as a "Senior Advisor to the President" who "can only advise the President and communicate the President's directives," and named no role for him in DOGE's own chain of command. [cite:de11_audit_fisher] He was nonetheless its public face, and in March 2025 a federal judge, in a preliminary ruling, found that he was likely acting as its de facto leader. He left government at the end of May 2025, when his 130 days ran out. [cite:de11_wiki_musk] What the office was in law, who staffed it, what it reached and what it cut are the subjects of Book Three, beginning with [scene:de17_money_to_office|Part Seventeen]; this section records only the sequence. More than $290 million of campaign money preceded an advisory office with wide reach into federal systems. No document records any agreement between Musk and Trump connecting the two.

Wisconsin, April 2025

While still in government, Musk spent heavily on a state judicial election. In the race for a seat on the Wisconsin Supreme Court, which decided control of a court expected to rule on abortion, labour law, voting rules and possibly congressional maps, he and groups he backed spent more than $20 million supporting the conservative candidate, Brad Schimel, about one dollar in every five spent in the race. He paid people to sign a petition against "activist judges" and, in Green Bay, handed $1 million cheques to voters. [cite:de11_npr_wisc] On 1 April 2025 the liberal candidate, Susan Crawford, won by ten points. [cite:de11_npr_wisc]

The break, the party and the return

The partnership ended in public. Musk criticised the Republicans' tax and spending bill in late May 2025, and on 5 June the dispute escalated. Trump wrote that "the easiest way" to save billions would be to "terminate Elon's governmental subsidies and contracts"; Musk claimed that Trump would have lost the election without him and alleged, without offering evidence, that Trump was implicated in the unreleased Epstein files. [cite:de11_npr_feud] He later deleted the Epstein post, and on 11 June wrote that some of his posts about Trump "went too far." [cite:de11_audit_pbs_regret] On 5 July he announced on X that he had formed an "America Party" to "give you back your freedom," carrying out a threat he had made if "this insane spending bill passes." No filing for it was confirmed with the Election Commission, and Trump called the idea "ridiculous." [cite:de11_ap_party]

The party did not become the vehicle for his money. In May 2025 Musk had said he intended to spend less on politics: "I think I've done enough." [cite:de11_ap_2026] By the autumn of 2026 he had reconciled with Trump, who presented him with the National Medal of Science on 8 October 2026, and he had become the largest known donor of the midterm elections, giving to his own America PAC and to super PACs aligned with the Republican leadership. [cite:de11_ap_2026] Filings and the PAC's own statements put his giving since January 2025 at about $366 million by the end of September 2026, more than $276 million of it between July and September, and a pre-election filing on 22 October added $76.8 million more; known giving in the cycle passed $440 million. [cite:de11_ibt_fec] [cite:de11_ap_2026]

Documented influence, and its limits

The influence that is documented in the same years falls into three kinds. The first is office: the special-government-employee role as senior adviser and public face of the cuts, from January to May 2025. The White House told a court that the role gave him no greater authority than other senior advisers; a federal judge, in a preliminary ruling, found that he likely led DOGE in practice. [cite:de11_audit_fisher] [cite:de11_wiki_musk] The second is contracts and regulation favourable to his companies. In April 2025, while he was in government, SpaceX won about $5.92 billion of national-security launch work, in a procurement that began before 2025 and in which it was already the leading provider. [cite:de11_ussf_nssl] In August 2025, after the break, a launch-licensing order benefited SpaceX more than any other firm. [cite:de11_reuters_spaceeo] In April 2026, after the reconciliation began, SpaceX was among twelve firms given Golden Dome interceptor agreements. [cite:de11_bd_sbi] The third is regulatory exposure. A minority staff memo for Senator Richard Blumenthal, the senior Democrat on the Senate's investigations subcommittee, counted 65 federal actions involving Musk's companies that appeared to be active when Trump took office, and estimated potential liability of at least $2.37 billion for 40 of them, among them $1.19 billion over Tesla's claims for Autopilot and Full Self-Driving; it noted that the Justice Department had dismissed one SpaceX case in February 2025. [cite:de11_psi_memo] The memo is a partisan staff estimate of potential, not assessed, penalties, and the claim that DOGE was run to make those cases disappear is the sponsor's characterisation; the agencies' own records are examined in [scene:de21_conflicts_and_contracts|Part Twenty-One].

Two things in the record run against the simplest reading of this sequence. The first is the break itself: on 5 June 2025 the president publicly threatened the largest donor's contracts, which is hard to square with a government that donor had bought. [cite:de11_npr_feud] The second is the order of the largest regulatory benefit to SpaceX, which came after the break rather than during his time in office. [cite:de11_reuters_spaceeo] What the record does establish is scale and access: in two election cycles one man gave more than $700 million to one side of American politics, held a government role in between, and controls companies whose largest customer is the same government. How that compares with Peter Thiel's far smaller and earlier giving is set out in [scene:de9_money_and_influence|Part Nine], and the network-wide money map in [scene:de14_network_map|Part Fourteen].
