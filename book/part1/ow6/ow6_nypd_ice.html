New York's Precinct in Israel, and ICE's Tools

Two further ties go beyond the occasional training trip. One is permanent: the New York Police Department keeps an officer inside an Israeli police headquarters. The other is commercial: Immigration and Customs Enforcement has bought Israeli-made technology for getting into telephones. Neither rests on an act of Congress, and both are documented mainly through procurement records and reporting.

The Kfar Saba office. The NYPD began stationing an officer in Israel in 2007 under its International Liaison Program, which places senior intelligence-bureau officers in cities abroad. [cite:ow6_cs_nypd2026] In September 2012 Israeli and American outlets reported that the department had opened a branch at the Israel Police's headquarters in Kfar Saba, the Sharon District command, chosen over the US embassy in Tel Aviv; its purpose, as reported, was daily cooperation with the Israel Police. [cite:ow6_toi_nypd2012] The officer chosen to open it was Detective Charlie Ben-Naim (also spelled Benaim), an NYPD veteran of Israeli origin and a former officer in the Israel Defense Forces, who in 2026 was still described as the department's officer in Israel, one of more than a dozen in the liaison programme. [cite:ow6_toi_nypd2012] [cite:ow6_cs_nypd2026] Accounts differ on whether he has held the post continuously since 2007 or since 2012; the public record does not settle it.

The office is not paid for entirely by the city. City & State reported in August 2026 that the liaison programme is partly funded by the New York City Police Foundation, a charity supported by private donors, among them the Tisch family; two trustees of the foundation, Andrew and Alexander Tisch, are the uncle and cousin of the police commissioner, Jessica Tisch. [cite:ow6_cs_nypd2026] Private funding means that the office's budget does not pass through the City Council. The same report found that the NYPD contracts with Terrogence Global, an Israeli-founded web-intelligence firm, and that department officials travelled to Israel for counterterrorism and antisemitism training as recently as September 2025. [cite:ow6_cs_nypd2026]

The 2026 position. As of August 2026, according to an NYPD spokesperson quoted by City & State, Mayor Zohran Mamdani had not directed the department to stop cooperating with the Israeli police or to stop attending related conferences; the NYPD said no trips to Israel were planned that year, and City Hall said no official had travelled to Israel on city business under Mamdani, while no rule barred travel to any country. [cite:ow6_cs_nypd2026] At an unrelated press conference, Mamdani said of the commissioner generally: "I would not have someone serving our city if I was not confident in what they were doing." [cite:ow6_cs_nypd2026] The department's stated rationale for the office, as for its other overseas posts, is counterterrorism: learning quickly from attacks abroad. The public record does not include any report of what information the office has sent to New York, or received from it, and no oversight body has published one.

ICE and Israeli phone tools. ICE's link to Israel runs through purchases, not exchanges. In March 2017 it bought phone-extraction technology from Cellebrite, an Israeli-founded company, for $2.2 million, and on 24 June 2019 it gave notice of its intent to award Cellebrite a contract worth up to $30–35 million for "universal forensic extraction devices", licences and training; the contracting officer declined to say how ICE would use them. [cite:ow6_ipt_cellebrite] In September 2024, under the Biden administration, ICE signed a $2 million contract with Paragon Solutions, an Israeli-founded company whose "Graphite" spyware can reach encrypted messaging applications. The contract was paused for review under Executive Order 14093, which limits the government's use of commercial spyware; in September 2025 the Guardian reported that procurement records showed the pause lifted, with ICE as the contracting agency. By then Paragon was owned by an American firm. [cite:ow6_wiki_paragon] [cite:ow6_guardian_paragon] John Scott-Railton of the Citizen Lab said such tools "were designed for dictatorships, not democracies"; neither ICE nor Paragon commented. [cite:ow6_guardian_paragon] Part Ten follows these companies, and others, in detail.

The direction of travel. This part's question is whether these ties run one way. Here the answer depends on the tie. The DHS research programmes fund joint ventures in which American companies take part. [cite:ow6_lii_8606] [cite:ow6_bird_hls2021] The police exchanges carry American officers to Israel to learn, by their sponsors' own account, Israeli methods; Israeli police are not described in the sources as coming to learn American ones. [cite:ow6_reason_cops] [cite:ow6_cops_leep] The NYPD's office sits inside an Israeli headquarters; this book found no report of an Israeli police office inside an American one. [cite:ow6_cs_nypd2026] And ICE's purchases send American public money to companies founded in Israel. [cite:ow6_ipt_cellebrite] [cite:ow6_wiki_paragon] None of this is concealed, but very little of it is reported to an elected body: the memorandum is non-binding, the trips are private, the New York office is partly privately funded, and the uses of the phone tools are undisclosed.
