The Open Secret

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

Every secret in this book was published. The three Masonic degrees were printed by Samuel Prichard in 1730, only thirteen years after the Grand Lodge was formed. [cite:od_wiki_pgle] William Morgan disappeared in 1826 while planning to print them again, a century after they had first appeared. [cite:od_wiki_antimasonic] The Bavarian government seized the Illuminati's papers and published them in 1787. [cite:vt30_wiki_illuminati] The Templars' secret receptions were described in the confessions taken from them, and the Chinon record has been in print since 2004. [cite:vt29_barber_cup2] [cite:vt29_wiki_chinon] The Rosicrucians announced their secret brotherhood in print to begin with. [cite:vt30_wiki_rosic]

The inner reading takes this as the lesson. A secret that survives being printed was never information. The words of a ritual can be read in any library; what the ritual is for, the change in the person who goes through it, cannot be printed, because it does not exist until it happens. The mysteries on this shelf said the same: the Kabbalists restricted their teaching by age and preparation not because the words were hidden but because the reader had to be ready for them. [cite:kb_ou_age40]

Read this way, the myth of the hidden masters is the open secret turned inside out. It says that the secret is power over others, held by a few and hidden from the many. The allegories the orders left say the opposite: that the work is on oneself, that its tools are ordinary, and that the only thing that cannot be handed over is the doing of it. The fear that powerful institutions had of the orders, and the fear the myth spread about them, both looked outward. The allegories look in.

The record limits the reading. Some of these secrets were guarded for ordinary reasons, ritual surprise, fraternal privacy or political danger, and the Illuminati in particular kept secrets for plainly political ends. [cite:vt30_wiki_illuminati] The reading does not claim that every order understood its secret as inward. It claims only that the secrets which survived their own publication are the ones that point in.
