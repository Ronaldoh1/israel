"Hamas Is an Asset"

The argument that the split served Israel rests on statements by Israeli leaders and on documented policy. Four items carry most of the weight.

Smotrich, 2015. In a 2015 interview Bezalel Smotrich, later finance minister and the minister in charge of settlement administration, [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis] said: "The Palestinian Authority is a burden, and Hamas is an asset." He explained that the Authority harmed Israel in international forums, while Hamas, designated a terrorist organization, would never be recognized, given standing at the International Criminal Court, or able to table a Security Council resolution. [cite:wb_wiki_smotrich]

The Qatari money. From 2018 Israel allowed Qatar to bring cash into Gaza, much of it for the salaries of the Hamas government's officials. Netanyahu called it "the right step" to restore quiet and avert a humanitarian crisis; Naftali Bennett compared it to "protection money." [cite:gz_ds_qatar2018] The New York Times reported in December 2023, from more than two dozen officials, that Netanyahu had "not only tolerated" the payments but encouraged them. Netanyahu has said the payments were to avoid a humanitarian collapse and called the idea that he wanted to build up Hamas "a big lie." [cite:gz_week_qatar] Gaza, Documented sets out the money in full. [scene:gz8_cash|The Qatari cash]

The 2019 remark. A line widely attributed to Netanyahu, that anyone who wanted to prevent a Palestinian state should support strengthening Hamas, was first reported by the Jerusalem Post from an unnamed Knesset member paraphrasing his remarks to his party's lawmakers in March 2019. The reporter has said it concerned letting money and aid into Gaza and has been "rewritten and twisted" online. [cite:gz_week_qatar] [cite:gz_harkov_2019] It is a paraphrase, not a recorded quotation.

"Fatahstan." After 7 October 2023 Netanyahu rejected an American request that the Palestinian Authority take over Gaza, saying: "I will not replace Hamastan with Fatahstan." [cite:wb_jns_fatahstan] In 2024 Smotrich withheld the Authority's tax revenues, about 70 percent of its budget. Israeli security officials, speaking anonymously, warned that it was weakening the Authority and could lead to violence; Smotrich's office answered that the Authority "promotes terror, pays for terror and acts against Israel in the international arena." [cite:wb_ynet_smotrich2024] His 2017 "Decisive Plan" had proposed filling the West Bank with settlements and leaving Palestinians, in the journalist Ron Ben-Yishai's summary, "a life of subjugation under Israeli rule, emigration, or a shahid [martyr] death." [cite:wb_wiki_smotrich]

What the Record Shows, and What It Cannot

The record shows that the split was made by the two rival factions, Fatah and Hamas, in the fighting of June 2007 and in a decade and a half of failed reconciliation, and that President Abbas postponed the 2021 elections. [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] [cite:wb_medialine_agreements] [cite:wb_gpb_elections2021] It also shows that, once it existed, senior Israeli figures described it as useful, that Israeli governments let money reach Hamas while withholding money from the Authority, and that settlement approvals reached records in 2023–2025, the years of the Gaza war. [cite:wb_wiki_smotrich] [cite:gz_week_qatar] [cite:wb_ynet_smotrich2024] [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis] Whether that amounted to a deliberate strategy of division is a question about intent. Some officials' own words support it; Netanyahu denies it, and the documents that would settle it, if they exist, have not been published. The volume leaves it there: the statements and the outcomes are documented; the strategy is argued.
