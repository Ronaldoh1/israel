The Rules Others Follow: The Nuclear Treaty, The Hague and the United Nations

A sovereign state is bound by the treaties it signs and answerable in the forums whose jurisdiction it accepts. Part One measured the three cities against the tests of sovereignty; this section measures the fourth sovereign against the rules most states have accepted, and records, as of November 2026, where Israel stands with each and what Israel says in reply.

The nuclear treaty. Israel has never signed the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons. It neither confirms nor denies having nuclear weapons, a policy of deliberate ambiguity. Its reactor at Dimona was built with French help in the 1950s and reached criticality in 1962; in 1986 a technician there, Mordechai Vanunu, revealed the programme's extent, and was abducted by Mossad agents and imprisoned. Public estimates of its arsenal range from 80 to 400 warheads. [cite:dv5_newarab_nuclear] The United States accepted the ambiguity early: under President Johnson, American inspection visits to Dimona between 1964 and 1969 were, according to documents declassified in 2020, staged and managed, while the conventional arms relationship deepened. [cite:nsarchive_duplicity_1964_65] [cite:state_frus1964_68v18] In June 2025 and again from February 2026 Israel struck Iran's nuclear programme; the second campaign, begun jointly with the United States on 28 February 2026, killed Iran's Supreme Leader. [cite:wiki_iran_war_2026]

The International Court of Justice. Two proceedings concern Israel. The first is the advisory opinion of 19 July 2024, described in the previous section, which found the continued occupation unlawful and obliged other states not to assist it. [cite:icj_press_19jul24] The second is a contentious case. South Africa brought proceedings under the Genocide Convention, and the court issued three orders of provisional measures to protect Palestinians in Gaza, which remain binding. Israel filed its counter-memorial in March 2026, objecting to the court's jurisdiction; by an order of 21 May 2026 the court set South Africa's reply for 22 November 2027 and Israel's rejoinder for 22 May 2029. [cite:dv5_gov_za_icj_2026] A final judgment is therefore years away. Israel denies the charge of genocide and argues that it has acted in self-defence against Hamas; South Africa's position is that "self-defence is not a defence to genocide." [cite:dv5_gov_za_icj_2026]

The International Criminal Court. Israel is not a party to the Rome Statute and rejects the court's jurisdiction. [cite:israel_icc_position] On 21 November 2024 the court's Pre-Trial Chamber issued arrest warrants for Prime Minister Netanyahu and former Defence Minister Gallant. [cite:icc_warrant_issued_nov2024] On 24 April 2025 the Appeals Chamber sent Israel's challenge to jurisdiction back to the Pre-Trial Chamber for reconsideration but held that the warrants "remain valid and unaffected by the jurisdictional review." [cite:dv5_thesun_icc_2025] The United States, also not a party, responded by sanctioning the court's prosecutor in February 2025 and later several of its judges. [cite:verfassungsblog_icc] Netanyahu travelled to Washington and New York in 2026 while the warrant stood, to states that do not recognize the court's authority over him. [scene:nhh_s03|The prime minister with an ICC warrant]

The United Nations. At the Security Council, the one UN body that can bind, Israel has been shielded by more than fifty American vetoes; the exception, Resolution 2334 of December 2016, declared the settlements to have "no legal validity," and Israel's government denounced it. [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes] [cite:deccan_unsc_2334] Elsewhere the trend has run against Israel's position. Spain, Ireland and Norway recognized the State of Palestine in May 2024, and on 21 and 22 September 2025 Britain, Canada, Australia, France, Portugal, Belgium and others followed, bringing recognition to 157 of 193 UN members, among them four of the five permanent members of the Security Council; the United States is the exception. [cite:wiki_recognition_palestine] [cite:aljazeera_157_list] On 21 February 2024 the Knesset voted 99 to 9 to back Netanyahu's declaration against any "unilateral" recognition, and on 11 September 2025 he said that "there will be no Palestinian state." [cite:toi_knesset_99] [cite:globalsecurity_two_state]

Israel's case. Israel's governments and their supporters answer the record on its own terms. They argue that the UN system singles Israel out, pointing to the Human Rights Council's permanent agenda item on Israel and to the volume of General Assembly resolutions; that the courts in The Hague lack jurisdiction over a state that has not accepted it; that a state surrounded by enemies cannot accept limits its enemies do not observe; and that a Palestinian state would, in present conditions, endanger Israel. [cite:unwatch_item7] [cite:unwatch_unga2024] [cite:toi_knesset_99] Those arguments are part of the record too, and many governments, above all the American one, have accepted some of them.

What the record shows. Israel stands outside, or in open dispute with, most of the international rules that bind other states on its central questions: nuclear weapons, the territories, the conduct of the war and Palestinian statehood. It has been able to do so at little practical cost because one government has used its own power, at the Security Council and in sanctioning the International Criminal Court, to keep those rules from being enforced. [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes] [cite:verfassungsblog_icc] That is the subject of this part's final section.
