Part Thirteen asks why Israel has made Iran its principal target, and keeps two kinds of answer apart. The first is what Israel's governments have said they want: no nuclear weapon in a hostile state's hands, the doctrine Menachem Begin declared after bombing Iraq's reactor in 1981; an end to Iran's arming of Hezbollah, Hamas and the Houthis; and, by 2026, conditions for Iranians to overthrow their government. The second is why, which is a matter of argument: military supremacy, ideology, the collapse of the old alliance, domestic politics and American strategy are each set out with the case against them. The part then reads the two documents most often cited as proof of a long-laid plan, the 1996 paper "A Clean Break" and Oded Yinon's 1982 essay, for what they actually say about Iran, considers what oil and the Strait of Hormuz put at stake, and draws up a ledger of the two states' populations, economies, budgets, forces, missiles and bombs, with the American aid and forces on Israel's side.
