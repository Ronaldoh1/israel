Part Thirty-Six has laid the record of Book Two against the thesis Book Two tests and found it supported in substance and corrected in detail. The inner traditions taught what the thesis says: a kingdom inside, an ascent of the soul, no rule beyond the Saviour's, and no tithe documented for any school. [cite:vt24_gthom] [cite:vt26_mattison_gmary] [cite:vt24_apjohn] The institution that prevailed did build mediation from the second century, a church tithe from 585, the inquisitorial search from 1184 and, from 1231, papal law that sent condemned heretics to the stake; under that system the Cathars of Montségur were burned, and Giordano Bruno was handed by a church tribunal to the city authorities who burned him, as Arius's books had been ordered burned by an emperor after 325. [cite:vt21_vc3_66] [cite:vt22_canons] [cite:vt27_hessayon] In the wider form tested in Parts Thirty-Two to Thirty-Five the thesis is again supported in substance and corrected on its actors. Emperors and local bishops closed the mystery cults, from Theodosius's laws of 391–392 to Justinian's general at Philae; friars and bishops, outside the Inquisition's jurisdiction, burned the Maya codices at Maní in 1562 and the Andean shrines and religious quipus; Spanish conquistadors, a viceroy and an archbishop dismantled the sun cults of Cusco and Tenochtitlan; and the mother goddesses were pressed into Marian forms.
