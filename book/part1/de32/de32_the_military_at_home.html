The Military at Home

The armed forces are the largest force any president commands, and American law has kept them out of ordinary policing for almost 150 years. In 2025 and 2026 that line was tested more often than at any time since the civil-rights era: National Guard troops were sent into several cities, Marines into one, military land was turned into a border zone and a detention camp, and the president twice said he might invoke the one law that lets troops enforce the law. This section sets out the legal routes, what was done along each, and what the courts said.

The rules

The Posse Comitatus Act, first passed on 18 June 1878 and now 18 U.S.C. 1385, makes it a crime to use any part of the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force or Space Force "as a posse comitatus or otherwise to execute the laws," except where the Constitution or an Act of Congress expressly allows it. [cite:de32_govinfo_1385] A posse comitatus is the old common-law body of citizens a sheriff could summon; the Act stops the military being used as one. The main exception is the Insurrection Act of 1807, which lets a president deploy troops to enforce the law when unrest makes ordinary courts and police unable to; the Brennan Center counts 30 uses, the last in Los Angeles in 1992. [cite:de32_aj_insurrection] The National Guard sits between the two worlds. Under Title 32 of the US Code a governor commands it, with federal money, and Posse Comitatus does not apply; under Title 10 it is federalised and the Act does. A separate statute, 10 U.S.C. 12406, lets a president federalise the Guard to repel invasion or suppress rebellion, or when he is "unable with the regular forces" to execute the laws. As Joseph Nunn of the Brennan Center put it, the limit on Title 32 is that "there needs to be a cooperative governor." [cite:de32_csm_guard] [cite:de32_lawdork_il]

Four cities

In June 2025, without the consent of California's governor, who sued, about 4,000 California Guard members were federalised under section 12406 and 700 Marines deployed to Los Angeles during protests against immigration raids. [cite:de32_fox_breyer] The wider context is in [scene:us3_speech|the library's chapter on speech]. After a trial, Judge Charles Breyer entered judgment for California on its Posse Comitatus claims on 2 September 2025, warning against "creating a national police force with the President as its chief" and barring uses such as riot control, arrests and traffic stops in the state. [cite:de32_breyer_judgment] [cite:de32_fox_breyer] In Oregon, Judge Karin Immergut, appointed in the president's first term, permanently barred a Guard deployment to Portland, finding that "the President's determination was simply untethered to the facts." [cite:de32_nbc_immergut]

Illinois went to the Supreme Court. On 23 December 2025, ruling on an emergency stay application rather than the merits, the Court refused 6–3 to let the deployment proceed. Its unsigned order read "regular forces" in section 12406 as the regular military, so a president must first be unable to execute the laws with the military, and found the government had "failed to identify a source of authority" letting the military execute the laws in Illinois. Justice Alito, dissenting on other grounds, wrote that the country has "traditionally been wary of using soldiers as domestic police." [cite:de32_lawdork_il] The deployments in Los Angeles, Chicago and Portland ended on 31 December 2025. [cite:us3_audit_lawdork_il] The practical effect is that section 12406 now offers little against an objecting governor, leaving the Insurrection Act as the remaining federal route. [cite:de32_votebeat_polls]

Washington, DC, is different because it is not a state. Judge Jia Cobb ruled on 20 November 2025 that using the DC Guard for crime control and bringing in troops from other states was unlawful; on 17 December the D.C. Circuit stayed her order, citing the president's "unique power within the District." [cite:de32_nbcw_dc] In July 2026 the DC deployment was extended to 2029 with about 5,000 personnel. [cite:us3_audit_dc_guard]

Standing forces, the Insurrection Act and the words at Quantico

Memorandums from the National Guard Bureau in October 2025 told every state to form a quick reaction force, often 500 troops each and more than 23,000 in all, trained in crowd control with batons, shields, Tasers and pepper spray, a quarter of them deployable within eight hours, and operational by 1 January 2026. [cite:de32_mt_qrf] About 1,700 Guard members in 20 states led by Republican governors were authorised to do clerical, transport and processing work at ICE facilities; Vermont's Republican governor, Phil Scott, refused. [cite:de32_csm_guard]

On 15 January 2026, during the Minneapolis protests, the president wrote that unless state officials stopped "professional agitators and insurrectionists," "I will institute the INSURRECTION ACT." [cite:de32_aj_insurrection] About 1,500 soldiers of the 11th Airborne Division were put on orders to deploy if it was invoked; the president then said there was no reason to use it "right now," adding, "If I needed it, I'd use it." [cite:de32_ap_11th] Asked in an interview published on 1 October 2026 about the midterm elections, he said "I'm not committing to anything" and, of the Act, "I've never used it. I could use it." [cite:de32_hill_time] [cite:de32_meaww_time]

The language around the force changed too. On 21 February 2025 the chairman of the Joint Chiefs, the chief of naval operations, the Air Force vice chief and the top lawyers of the Army and Air Force were removed; the defence secretary said the lawyers were not "well-suited" to advise on lawful orders. [cite:de32_mt_jags] An executive order of 5 September 2025 authorised "Department of War" as a secondary title for the Department of Defense; its legal name is unchanged unless Congress acts. [cite:de32_fr_14347] On 30 September 2025, addressing hundreds of senior officers at Quantico, the president said "We should use some of these dangerous cities as training grounds for our military" and "We're under invasion from within," and called it "a war from within." [cite:de32_ap_quantico] [cite:de32_abc_quantico] Words are not orders, and no order to treat a city as a training ground is on the record.

The border and the bases

On 18 April 2025 a strip of about 180 miles along the New Mexico border became a "National Defense Area," military land where migrants could be charged with trespass. A magistrate judge dismissed charges against those arrested there because the complaints did not show they "knew he/she was entering" a military zone. [cite:de32_aj_nda] Military bases also became detention sites. Camp East Montana at Fort Bliss, Texas, holds up to 5,000 people in soft-sided structures, as many as 72 to a pod; Human Rights Watch and the ACLU, from interviews with 80 people, reported beatings and medical neglect in July 2026. [cite:de32_hrw_bliss] Guantánamo Bay received its first migrants on 4 February 2025 after a memorandum of 29 January; about 1,100 troops were moved there, and 832 people passed through in the year to May 2026, when fewer than 2 percent of about 400 beds were occupied. [cite:de32_wiki_gtmo]

History cuts both ways. In September 1957 President Eisenhower federalised the Arkansas Guard and sent 1,000 troops of the 101st Airborne to protect Black students entering Central High School in Little Rock, against a governor who had used the Guard to keep them out. [cite:de32_nara_10730] In June 2020, in the same president's first term, Defense Secretary Mark Esper said, "I do not support invoking the Insurrection Act." [cite:de32_mil_esper] Troops at home have enforced civil rights, and their own leaders have argued against using them.

If it were connected

The Guard's quick reaction forces joined to an Insurrection Act order: possible. The forces exist and are trained for crowd control, and the order would be a single presidential proclamation; nothing documents a plan to issue it, and courts would review it. [cite:de32_mt_qrf] Guard units joined to ICE's operations: documented, under Title 32 in consenting states, in support roles. [cite:de32_csm_guard] Military land joined to the detention system: documented at Fort Bliss and Guantánamo. [cite:de32_hrw_bliss] [cite:de32_wiki_gtmo] Troops joined to elections: not supported. Federal law bars troops or armed men at polling places, and the White House chief of staff called the idea "categorically false, will not happen." [cite:de32_votebeat_polls] What stands in the way of the wider joins is the Supreme Court's reading of section 12406, the Posse Comitatus Act as applied in California, and the refusal of governors; the Insurrection Act is the one switch that would bypass most of them, and it had not been used as of 28 October 2026.
