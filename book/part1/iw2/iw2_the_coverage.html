The Coverage of the War

The Intercept analysed more than 1,000 articles in the New York Times, the Washington Post and the Los Angeles Times over the first seven weeks of the war. Words such as "slaughter", "massacre" and "horrific" were used almost exclusively for Israelis killed by Palestinians; the Times called Israeli deaths a "massacre" 53 times and Palestinian deaths once; antisemitism was mentioned far more often than Islamophobia, 549 times to 79. [cite:intercept_study] In April 2024 The Intercept published a leaked internal Times memo advising reporters to restrict the use of terms such as "genocide" and "slaughter"; the paper said such guidance is meant to ensure accuracy. [cite:intercept_memo] DAWN's analysis reached similar conclusions. [cite:dawn_biases_gaza_coverage]

The criticism runs the other way as well. On 17 October 2023 the Times and other outlets reported Palestinian claims that an Israeli strike had killed hundreds at al-Ahli hospital in Gaza; on 23 October the Times published an editors' note saying its coverage "relied too heavily on claims by Hamas" and left "an incorrect impression". [cite:npr_nyt_note] Human Rights Watch later found an Israeli strike unlikely, and American intelligence estimated a lower death toll. [cite:wiki_alahli] The Jerusalem Post's editor called the Times's coverage "by far" the worst in American media, and a 2026 shareholder suit cited a study counting 72 corrections between October 2023 and June 2024, 48 of them concerning Israel. [cite:foxnews_jpost_editor_nyt] [cite:foxnews_shareholder_lawsuit_nyt]

Both sets of findings are documented. They are not of the same kind: one is a measured pattern across thousands of articles, the other a set of specific errors and charges. A reader can hold both: the papers' language weighed Israeli deaths more heavily, and the papers also corrected at least one major story in Israel's favour.
