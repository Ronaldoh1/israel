The War of 1967 and the Occupation

Israel struck first on 5 June 1967, after Egypt closed the Straits of Tiran, and in six days took Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan. Whether its survival was at stake is disputed by Israeli historians. [cite:wiki_six_day_war] [cite:segev_1967] On 22 November the Security Council adopted Resolution 242, stressing "the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war" and calling for withdrawal "from territories occupied in the recent conflict," a phrase read two ways. [cite:unsc_242] [cite:wb_wiki_242]

Only Sinai was returned. The war of 1973 began the era of large-scale American aid; the Likud's victory in 1977 turned settlement into policy; and the same government signed peace with Egypt in 1979, gave back the Sinai and removed its settlements there, while the autonomy plan for the Palestinians was never carried out. [cite:os_wiki_yomkippur] [cite:mas_settlements_history] [cite:wiki_camp_david_accords]

The rest stayed under military rule. Settlers in the West Bank, outside East Jerusalem, went from about 110,000 at Oslo to about 500,000; with East Jerusalem the total is about 737,000. [cite:peacenow_settlement_population] The Security Council declared the settlements unlawful in 2016, and on 19 July 2024 the International Court of Justice found the continued occupation unlawful and obliged other states not to assist it.
