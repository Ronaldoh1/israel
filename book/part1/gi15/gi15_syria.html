Syria: Strikes, Sweida and the Southern Zone

Israel's only documented territorial programme in Syria concerns the Golan Heights, annexed in law in 1981 and treated in Part Eight. [scene:gi8_golan|The Golan] Everything else Israel has done in Syria since 2013 has been military: an air campaign against Iranian and Hezbollah targets during the civil war, a second and heavier campaign after Bashar al-Assad fell on 8 December 2024, and occupation of the UN buffer zone and the Syrian side of Mount Hermon. Part Nine sets out the ground taken, Israel's statements that it will stay, and the fortified line known as Sufa 53. [scene:gi9_syria|Part Nine: the Syrian buffer zone] This section counts what those operations cost in lives and says who did the counting. It also separates the deaths Israel caused from the far larger number caused by Syrians fighting one another, which are often run together. [cite:hz10s_cnn_480] [cite:gi15_olj_sweida]

The campaign between the wars, 2013–2024

For years Israel neither confirmed nor denied its strikes in Syria. The scale became public in January 2019. Netanyahu told his cabinet on 13 January that the army had "attacked hundreds of times Iranian and Hezbollah targets". The outgoing chief of staff, Gadi Eisenkot, said about 2,000 bombs had been dropped on Iranian targets in 2018 alone, and that "we carried out thousands of attacks without taking responsibility". [cite:hz10s_jc_netanyahu] [cite:hz10s_toi_eisenkot] The stated aim was to stop Iran building a military presence in Syria and moving arms to Hezbollah. Iran's position was that its forces were in Syria at the Syrian government's invitation. [cite:hz10s_rferl_advisers] The Hezbollah volume tells that campaign in full, including Israel's medical aid to Syrian civilians and its arming of rebel groups on the Golan frontier. [scene:hz10_syria|The Hezbollah Architecture: Syria]

The best-known single strike attributed to Israel killed Iranian officers, not Syrians. On 1 April 2024 a strike that Iran blamed on Israel destroyed Iran's consulate building in Damascus and killed General Mohammad Reza Zahedi, his deputy and five other Iranian officers. Israel did not confirm or comment, and Iran answered on 13–14 April with its first direct attack on Israel. [cite:ir8_ap_consulate] [cite:ir14_cbs_damascus] [cite:ir8_gs_tp1] Strikes of this kind killed Syrian soldiers, Iranian personnel, Hezbollah fighters and, in some cases, civilians. No consolidated public count of all those killed by Israeli strikes in Syria between 2013 and 2024 was found. The monitoring group Airwars, which grades each allegation of civilian harm against the number and quality of local reports, tracks Israeli strikes in Syria from January 2013. Its tracker gives a headline estimate of 60 to 108 civilians alleged killed. It has graded only a small number of incidents so far, so the figure should be read as provisional. Airwars records the Israeli military's own assessment as zero civilian deaths, for the one incident it addressed. [cite:gi15_airwars_syria] [cite:gi15a_airwars] On every count available, the campaign's reported civilian toll was small beside the deaths of the civil war around it; what sets it apart is its length and the years in which Israel would not acknowledge it.

After Assad, December 2024 to 2025

When Assad fell, Israel struck on a far larger scale. Over 9 and 10 December 2024, by its own count, the military carried out about 480 strikes, hitting most of Syria's strategic weapons stockpiles, and its navy struck two naval facilities where 15 vessels were docked. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar said the aim was to keep the weapons from falling "into the hands of extremists". [cite:hz10s_cnn_480] Those strikes hit depots, airfields and ships rather than towns, and no civilian toll for them was reported in the sources reviewed. The ground operations that followed were counted by the Syrian Observatory for Human Rights (SOHR), a British-based monitor with sources inside Syria. In its review of 2025 it recorded 460 Israeli ground violations in southern Syria, mainly in the Quneitra and Daraa provinces, and more than 90 ground operations in November alone. It did not attribute a death toll to them. [cite:gi15_shafaq_sohr2025] Israel's stated reason for the presence was security: "We will not allow any hostile force to establish itself on our border," Netanyahu said on the first day. Syria's government and the UN called the presence a violation of the 1974 disengagement agreement. [cite:gi9_wiki_syriainv] [cite:hz10s_un_guterres]

Sweida, July 2025

The deadliest violence in southern Syria in 2025 was not, for the most part, Israel's doing. In mid-July 2025 fighting in Sweida province drew in Druze militias, Sunni Bedouin fighters and government forces. By SOHR's count, released on 22 July after a ceasefire, 1,311 people had been killed. They were 833 Druze (533 fighters and 300 civilians, 196 of the civilians "summarily executed" by members of the defence and interior ministries), 423 members of the government forces and 35 Sunni Bedouin, including three civilians executed by Druze fighters. [cite:gi15_olj_sweida] Israel intervened from the air. On 16 July it bombed the defence ministry in Damascus and an area near the presidential palace, and struck government positions in Sweida. Netanyahu said Israel was "acting to save our Druze brothers" and was committed to keeping south-western Syria demilitarised. Syria's foreign ministry condemned the strikes "in the harshest terms". [cite:hz10s_npr_sweida] SOHR also reported 15 government soldiers killed in Israeli strikes; it gives no separate total for all deaths caused by Israel that week. [cite:gi15_olj_sweida]

The figures show the order of responsibility. On SOHR's count for the whole of 2025, 990 civilians died in Sweida province. The same monitor documented by name 2,691 civilians killed in 63 massacres across Syria between March and November 2025, and attributed the field executions among them to factions affiliated with the transitional government's forces, not to Israel. [cite:gi15_shafaq_sohr2025] Israel's stated reason for striking was protection of the Druze. Syria's government saw an attack on its sovereignty and its capital. Both statements are on the record. The record also shows that the strikes on Damascus came five months after Netanyahu's demand, on 23 February 2025, that the three southern provinces be demilitarised. [cite:gi9_fdd_demil] Whether the protection of the Druze served a wider aim of a weak, divided south is an argument. Supporters of the strikes point to the documented executions of Druze civilians. Critics point to the demilitarisation demand and the continued presence of Israeli troops in the buffer zone. [cite:gi15_olj_sweida] [cite:gi9_carnegie_ring]

Displacement and status, October 2026

No figure for Syrians displaced by Israeli operations in the south was found in the sources reviewed. That is a gap in the record, not a finding that no one moved. The Sufa 53 works resumed on 4 October 2026, and Israeli forces still held the buffer zone and the Hermon summit as of October 2026. Syria's demand was a return to the lines of 8 December 2024, and talks through a US-supervised channel continued without a signed agreement. [cite:hz10s_eb_sufa] [cite:hz10s_eb_amman] [cite:hz10s_jpost_sharaa90] The documented record in Syria outside the Golan is a record of strikes, raids and a buffer zone that Israel's leaders have said they will hold until another arrangement ensures Israel's security. [cite:gi9_wiki_syriainv] No Israeli law, platform or coalition agreement found claims sovereignty over it. [scene:gi9_syria|Part Nine: the Syrian buffer zone] Whether the zone becomes a second Golan is a question the record leaves open.
