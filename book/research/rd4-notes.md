## 1. The Arab Revolt, 1936–1939 (`arab_revolt_1936_1939`)
**Sources agree on:**
- the Arab Higher Committee was formed on 25 April 1936 under al-Husseini
- the general strike lasted about six months and ended in October 1936, after an appeal by the Arab rulers
- the second phase began after Lewis Andrews was killed (26 September 1937)
- Britain outlawed the committee and deported leaders to the Seychelles
- about 20,000 British troops were deployed
- between 108 and 122 Arabs were hanged
- the revolt was crushed by summer 1939
- Arab dead were about 5,000
- the Husseini–Nashashibi split and the peace bands were real

**Sources disagree on:**
- *Death toll.* Khalidi gives 5,032 Arab dead (3,832 killed by the British, 1,200 in internal strife) and 14,760 wounded. British official figures give over 2,000 killed in combat, 108 hanged and 961 killed by "gangs". Popular accounts range from 5,000 to 8,000. Jewish dead are given as 415 (Yehudai; JVL), nearly 400 (Strickert) or "hundreds". British dead are given as about 100 (Yehudai) or about 200 (Strickert). **The simulation's scene s07a5 gives 2,652 Jews and 618 British, citing USHMM. I could not open the USHMM page, and no source I read comes near these numbers. Log this in SOURCE-CONFLICTS.md and re-check the USHMM page before the figure is printed.**
- *The 15 April 1936 attack.* Most sources give two Jews killed; JVL gives three.
- *The strike's start.* Middle East Eye gives 19 April in Nablus; Wikipedia's lead and body give different dates.
- *Where al-Husseini fled.* Unpacked says Lebanon; JVL says Syria.
- *Framing.* Middle East Eye presents a national uprising crushed by brutal repression. Unpacked stresses rebel violence against Palestinians. Hughes concludes that collaboration was "too partial to damn the Arab revolt."

**Not verified:**
- Exact detention totals (12,622 appears on Wikipedia only)
- The number of Jaffa houses demolished in 1936
- The peak British troop strength (25,000–50,000 appears on Wikipedia only)
- The date the Nashashibis withdrew from the committee
- Who was deported to the Seychelles, by name

## 2. Peel, Woodhead, St James's (`peel_woodhead_stjames_1936_1939`)
**Agree:**
- Peel was appointed in August 1936 with six members and reported in July 1937.
- Woodhead reported on 9 November 1938 with three plans.
- Britain accepted that partition was impracticable.
- The London conference of 1939 failed, with Arab and Jewish delegations meeting the British separately.

**Disagree:**
- The Jewish state's share of the country: 17% or about 20%.
- The transfer numbers: 225,000 Arabs and 1,250 Jews, or about 200,000 and 1,000.
- Whether the Zionist side "accepted" Peel. JVL says it accepted the principle and rejected the borders. The 1949 UN working paper says both sides rejected it.

**Not verified:**
- The St James's conference dates (7 February–17 March 1939) and the Woodhead plan populations. These are on Wikipedia only and are kept out of the prose.
- The Bludan Conference (September 1937): no source fetched.

## 3. The 1939 White Paper (`white_paper_1939`)
**Agree:**
- The text, from the COJS reproduction of Cmd 6019 and the US consul's quotation of the ten-year clause.
- The reception in the Commons (Amery, Churchill, Morrison).
- The Jewish Agency's legal case.
- The Permanent Mandates Commission's finding: unanimous on interpretation, and four to three, "a majority of one", on conformity with the Mandate.
- War prevented the League Council from acting.

**Disagree:** How Arabs received it. The US consul reported "very obvious relief", but the Arab Higher Committee rejected it. Morris's reading of al-Husseini's motive comes through Wikipedia only and is attributed.

**Not verified:**
- The Commons division of 268 to 179 and the 110 Conservative abstentions (Wikipedia only)
- Ben-Gurion's exact wording ("as if there were no White Paper")
- Use of the quota by 1944: 51,000 of 75,000, then 1,500 a month (Wikipedia only)
- The dates and zones of the February 1940 Land Transfer Regulations (Wikipedia only; its own citation for them is unreliable)
- The July 1940 acceptance of the White Paper by Jamal al-Husseini and Musa al-Alami (Wikipedia only)

## 4. Labour and Palestine, 1944–45 (`labour_britain_1944_1945`)
**Agree:** The December 1944 Labour conference resolution and the record of Labour leaders against the White Paper are documented in a State Department memorandum of 30 July 1945 (FRUS).

**Not verified:**
- The full text of the 1944 resolution from Labour's own records
- Bevin's appointment date and early statements
- Churchill's cabinet committee on partition (1943–44)
