Madagascar: A Plan Made by Others, 1937–1942

Madagascar belongs in this account because it is so often named as an alternative homeland. On the record it never was one.

The idea of sending Europe's Jews to Madagascar came from antisemites, first Paul de Lagarde in 1878. No Jewish organization is documented as seeking it. [cite:a_wp_madagascar]

In 1937 Poland, working with France, sent a commission to the French colony. Its head, Mieczysław Lepecki, estimated 5,000 to 7,000 families could settle there; its Jewish members estimated about 500 families or fewer, because of the climate and the lack of infrastructure. [cite:a_wp_madagascar]

In 1940 Nazi Germany planned to deport about a million Jews a year for four years to the island, to be run by the SS as a police state, expecting many to die. Ian Kershaw calls it genocide by another method. It was shelved after the Battle of Britain left the sea lanes in British hands, and formally in February 1942. [cite:a_wp_madagascar]

The likely path. Not an alternative homeland: a plan by others to remove Jews from Europe. In its 1940 form it was a plan for mass death.

The cost. Had the Nazi plan been carried out, the record's own estimate is that many of the deportees would have died.
