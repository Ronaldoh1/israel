Part Sixteen sets out what the rest of the world has said and done since October 2023, and why the support Israel receives has largely held. It begins at the United Nations: the Security Council's votes and the American vetoes, the General Assembly's resolutions, the laws that barred the UN's refugee agency, the findings of the UN's own commission and the end of the peacekeeping mission in Lebanon. It then turns to the courts: the genocide case at the International Court of Justice, the Court's advisory opinions on the occupation and on aid, the arrest warrants of the International Criminal Court and the American sanctions on that court, and the cases brought in British and Dutch courts over parts for the F-35. The third section follows what states did on their own account, from recognising Palestine and restricting arms to sanctioning ministers and settlers, cutting trade or, in the other direction, joining the Abraham Accords and recognising Israel's claim to the Golan. The last section sets out the documented reasons support persists, in law, money, industry, intelligence and politics, and the evidence that it has begun to erode, and keeps the arguments about motive apart from the record.
