Middletown to Yale

James David Vance was born James Donald Bowman on 2 August 1984 in Middletown, Ohio, a steel town between Cincinnati and Dayton. [cite:de12_britannica] His parents divorced when he was small, and he was raised mostly by his maternal grandparents, who had moved to Middletown from eastern Kentucky. [cite:de12_britannica] In 1990 his mother's third husband adopted him and he became James David Hamel; in April 2013 he took the surname Vance, his mother's maiden name, and became the man the public knows. [cite:de12_wiki_vance] This part follows his life on the same terms as the book's other biographies: dated facts carried by independent records first, his own account second and flagged as his, and the lives of his relatives left as their own.

That last rule matters more for Vance than for anyone else in this book, because almost everything the public knows about his childhood household comes from one source: his own memoir, Hillbilly Elegy, published by Harper in June 2016. [cite:de12_wiki_vance] The book's portrait of his grandmother and grandfather, of a mother's long addiction, of instability and of the culture of the Kentucky hills is his telling of his family's story. The household details are not independently documented in the public record in the way his schooling, his service and his career are. They are not examined here, and nothing in this part treats a relative's life as part of Vance's record.

The Marines and Ohio State

Vance graduated from Middletown High School in 2003 and enlisted in the United States Marine Corps the same year. [cite:de12_britannica] He served from 2003 to 2007 as a combat correspondent, a military journalist, with the 2nd Marine Aircraft Wing, and spent six months of 2005 in Iraq in a non-combat role, writing articles and taking photographs. [cite:de12_wiki_vance] He left the Corps as a corporal in 2007. [cite:de12_wiki_vance]

He entered Ohio State University in 2007 and finished a bachelor's degree in political science and philosophy in 2009, with highest honours. [cite:de12_britannica] [cite:de12_wiki_vance] The speed is documented; it is the first sign of the ambition that runs through every later stage of the record.

Yale Law School, 2010–2013

In 2010 Vance entered Yale Law School, the most selective law school in the United States and a standard route into the country's legal, political and financial elite. He took his law degree in May 2013 and was admitted to the Kentucky bar later that year. [cite:de12_wiki_vance] At Yale he met Usha Chilukuri, whom he later married; her career and record are her own and are not part of this book's subject. [cite:de12_wiki_vance]

The single event at Yale that matters most for this book is one he described himself, seven years later. In 2011 Peter Thiel, the co-founder of PayPal and Palantir, whose life is told in [scene:de9_formation|Part Nine], gave a talk at Yale Law School. In an essay for the Catholic literary magazine The Lamp in 2020, Vance called it "the most significant moment of my time" at the school. [cite:de12_forbesau] He wrote that Thiel was "possibly the smartest person" he had ever met, and that Thiel's open Christian faith "defied the social template I had constructed," a template in which, as he put it, dumb people were Christians and smart ones atheists. [cite:de12_forbesau] The substance of the talk, as Vance recalled it, was Thiel's argument that technological progress had stalled and that America's elites had become a competition for a shrinking number of prestigious positions, two trends Thiel "saw … as connected." [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] Vance has said that the talk turned him away from a career in large-firm law and toward technology and investment. [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] [cite:de12_forbesau]

This account is Vance's own, written in 2020 when he was already Thiel's friend and investee and a year before Thiel funded his Senate race. It is the strongest available evidence of how the relationship began, because it is the only first-hand account of it, and it should be read with that standing: a sincere description by the person whose career it shaped, not an independent record of what happened in the room. What it does establish is the order of events. The documented relationship between the two men began with an idea, the stagnation thesis, in 2011, five years before any employment and ten years before any campaign money. [scene:de10_girard_and_the_katechon|Part Ten] sets out the ideas Thiel was carrying at the time.

Law, and a book

After Yale he clerked for a year for Judge David Bunning of the federal district court for the Eastern District of Kentucky, then practised corporate law at Sidley Austin, one of the country's largest law firms, and worked for a time for Senator John Cornyn of Texas; the exact dates of the clerkship and the firm are not consistent across published biographies and are given here as a span. [cite:de12_wiki_vance] [cite:de12_britannica]

Hillbilly Elegy appeared in June 2016, in the middle of Donald Trump's first campaign for the presidency, and became a bestseller because it seemed to explain the voters who were carrying him. [cite:de12_pbs_ap] Its jacket carried a blurb from Thiel, who, as Vance later wrote, would "become a good friend." [cite:de12_fortune_mithril] The book made Vance a public figure before he had held any public role, and it gave him two things that the rest of his career drew on: an income from royalties, which still appeared on his financial disclosures as Vice President in 2026, and a public identity as an interpreter of the white working class of Appalachia and the industrial Midwest. [cite:de12_ians_2026] The same year he told the television interviewer Charlie Rose that he was "a Never Trump guy," a position he would reverse within five years; [cite:de12_axios_trump2016] that reversal is told in [scene:de12_senate_and_the_ticket|the third section] and graded in [scene:de12_claims_vs_record|the last].

By the summer of 2016, then, the record shows a man of thirty-one with a working-class Ohio childhood, a Marine Corps enlistment, two elite degrees and a bestseller, and a friendship with one of the richest and most politically ambitious investors in Silicon Valley that had begun with a lecture. Nothing in this stage of the record connects him to the texts this book examines. His first documented contact with Curtis Yarvin came the year before the memoir appeared, and it came, like his first job in finance, through Thiel. [scene:de12_the_thiel_years|The next section] follows those years.
