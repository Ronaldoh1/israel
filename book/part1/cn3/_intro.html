Part Three covers 312 to 435, the decades in which the bishops in Irenaeus's line gained the backing of the Roman state. It follows the money first: the tax exemptions, courts, buildings, land and imperially funded Bibles that Constantine gave the catholic clergy, and the edict that handed dissenters' meeting houses to them. It then follows the law, from the emperor's creed at Nicaea and his order to burn Arius's books to the edict of 380 that made agreement with the bishops of Rome and Alexandria the legal test of the faith.
