Human Rights Were Born the Same Week

It is sometimes assumed that the international human-rights system examined the Palestinian displacement and passed judgment on it in 1948. The chronology tells a different story. The machinery of modern human rights was being written in the very days the refugee population was being counted, and the parts of it that might have adjudicated what happened did not yet exist.

Before 1948 there was no universal human-rights treaty system. The League of Nations' Permanent Mandates Commission reviewed Britain's administration of Palestine, but there was no treaty of individual rights and no court to enforce one. The International Committee of the Red Cross worked under the laws of war, the Geneva Conventions of 1929. Its delegate Jacques de Reynier entered Deir Yassin on 11 April 1948 and reported what he found. That was documentation, not judgment.

Then, in a single week of December 1948, the UN General Assembly adopted three instruments. On 9 December it adopted the Genocide Convention. [cite:w_genocideconv] On 10 December it adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, by 48 votes to 0 with 8 abstentions. Article 13(2) of the Declaration reads:

Everyone has the right to leave any country, including his own, and to return to his country. [cite:un_udhr]

On 11 December came Resolution 194, on the return of refugees and compensation for those not returning. The modern rights order was thus written in the same days the Palestinian refugee population was being counted. The UN Conciliation Commission later estimated that population at about 711,000.

The text of Article 13(2) is not in dispute. Its application is. Palestinians cite it alongside Resolution 194. Israel has argued that neither creates a binding right of return for descendants. [cite:un_udhr]

What the international system did provide, it provided as relief. The General Assembly created UNRWA in December 1949, and it began work in May 1950. UNRWA provides schools, health care and relief to registered Palestine refugees. Its mandate is relief and works. It was not given the task of resettlement, of legal protection, or of securing return. [cite:unrwa_who]

The next instrument set Palestinians apart. The 1951 Refugee Convention, in Article 1D, excluded people already receiving protection or assistance from another UN agency. Because Palestinian refugees had UNRWA, they were left outside the protection regime that covers other refugee populations, and outside the durable-solutions mandate of the UNHCR. [cite:w_refconv] The very fact that they were receiving relief had the effect of placing them in a category of their own.

And there was no court. The International Criminal Court did not exist in 1948. Its Rome Statute was adopted in 1998 and entered into force in July 2002, without retroactive reach. Palestine acceded in 2015, which is why the Court's jurisdiction begins in that era rather than earlier. [cite:w_romestatute]

The sum is plain. The displacement of 1948 was documented by the Red Cross, counted by a UN commission, addressed in a General Assembly resolution, and relieved by a dedicated agency. It was debated. It was never adjudicated, because the institutions that might have done so were either not yet built or not given the task.
