How a Viral Claim Works

A viral claim usually carries one real, checkable detail. The detail earns the reader's trust, and the reader then accepts the conclusion along with it. "Egypt built a wall on the Gaza border" is true; "so the problem is Muslims" does not follow. The fix is to pull the two apart: check the fact, then ask whether the fact proves the point. Every claim in this volume is handled that way.

Where the lines come from

Some of the most repeated lines were written for a purpose. In 2009 The Israel Project, an American advocacy group, commissioned the pollster Frank Luntz to test which language would win American support. His Global Language Dictionary, marked not for distribution, was leaked to Newsweek and is now public. [cite:luntz_pdf] [cite:archive_luntz] [cite:wiki_tip] Its advice includes conceding that Israel is not without fault, repeating the phrase "Iran-backed Hamas", and, whatever the question, bridging back to a positive message in "the language of America": democracy, freedom, security and peace. Its own polling found Americans hostile to settlements, even among Israel's supporters, so it advised steering the conversation toward peace. [cite:luntz_pdf]

In June 2024 the New York Times, citing Israeli officials and documents, reported that Israel's Ministry of Diaspora Affairs had allocated about $2 million in October 2023 for a campaign run by STOIC, a Tel Aviv political marketing firm, using fake accounts and fake news sites aimed at American lawmakers. Meta removed 510 Facebook and 32 Instagram accounts that posed as Jewish students, Black Americans and concerned citizens; OpenAI also reported the operation. The ministry denied involvement, and both companies said the campaign gained little traction. [cite:opb_stoic] [cite:toi_stoic] [cite:cyberscoop_stoic] The Information War follows these campaigns in full.

A line written by a campaign is not false for that reason. Many talking points are sincerely held, and some are accurate. What a campaign shows is that a phrase heard from thousands of people in the same words was not worked out by each of them. That is a reason to check it, not a reason to dismiss it.
