Israel's own government has paid for online campaigns aimed at Americans, and its filings say so. A September 26, 2025 filing under the Foreign Agents Registration Act shows Israel's Foreign Ministry funding the "Esther Project," up to $900,000 from June to November 2025, through a firm called Bridges Partners, to recruit 14–18 US influencers posting 25–30 times a month [cite:jpost_esther] [cite:wiki_esther_project]. TIME reported group chats in which influencers were coached on wording and paid up to $4,250 per post; their names are still undisclosed [cite:mee_esther_backfire] [cite:rs_paid_influencers]. Earlier, per the New York Times, Israel's Diaspora Affairs Ministry paid about $2 million to a firm called STOIC to run fake accounts posing as Americans; Meta removed 510 Facebook and 32 Instagram accounts. The ministry denied involvement [cite:toi_stoic].

That does not mean every pro-Israel post is paid. Many people sincerely hold these views, and the companies said STOIC gained little traction [cite:cyberscoop_stoic]. Influence campaigns also run the other way: an Iran-linked network used fake profiles to stir tensions inside Israel [cite:calcalist_iran_social_media_network].
