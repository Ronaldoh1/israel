Part Eight goes front by front through the territories and neighbours named in arguments about Greater Israel, from the war of 1967 to the eve of 7 October 2023, and asks of each the same question: what did Israel's governments claim, legislate or do there, and what did they give up. It begins with the Golan Heights, the one front where a claim beyond the 1949 lines was written into law, and follows the Security Council's unanimous answer, the offers to withdraw, and the American and Colombian recognitions. It turns to Sinai, the largest territory Israel has returned, and to the treaty that left Gaza aside; to Jordan, where a ratified treaty sits beside the "Jordan is Palestine" current of the Israeli right; and to Lebanon, invaded in 1978 and 1982 and partly occupied until 2000. It ends by testing the claims made about Iraq, Iran and Saudi Arabia, three countries that appear in Greater-Israel writing but not, on the record reviewed here, in any Israeli government's territorial claim. The years since 2024 are the subject of Part Nine.
