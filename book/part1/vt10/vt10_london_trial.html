Sloane Avenue: the London Building, the Trial and the Retrial

The largest criminal case the Vatican's courts have heard concerned a former Harrods warehouse at 60 Sloane Avenue in Chelsea, bought in stages by the Secretariat of State, the pope's chief office, with money from its own reserves. [cite:vt10_rns_sale2022] The case produced the first criminal conviction of a cardinal by a Holy See court, on 16 December 2023, and then, on 17 March 2026, an appeal ruling that found the prosecution's founding acts defective and ordered the hearings to be held again. [cite:vt10_ap_verdict2023] [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] As of October 2026 the renewed proceedings had not concluded. Every defendant whose case is being reheard is presumed innocent, and each has maintained his or her innocence throughout. [cite:vt10_ap_mistrial2026] How the Vatican's courts are built, and why a 2021 change let them try cardinals, is set out in [scene:vt4_citizens_courts_prisons|Part Four].

The investment

In 2013 and 2014 the Secretariat of State paid about $200.5 million for shares in Athena Capital Commodities, a fund linked to the financier Raffaele Mincione; the Vatican tribunal later described the sum as about a third of the funds the Secretariat then had available. [cite:vt10_vn_verdict2023] The money went in part into the company that owned the London building and in part into other investments. [cite:vt10_vn_verdict2023] The investment was first made through accounts the Secretariat held at Swiss banks, including Credit Suisse. [cite:vt10_ncreg_rescript2022] Later the Secretariat sought to take the building over directly, using a second broker, Gianluigi Torzi; prosecutors alleged that the Holy See was then extorted into paying him 15 million euros to give up control, which his defence called the negotiated exit from a binding contract. [cite:vt10_ap_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_ap_verdict2023] In all, the Secretariat put about 350 million euros into the building, plus fees and commissions. [cite:vt10_rns_sale2022]

The Vatican Gendarmerie searched offices of the Secretariat of State and of the Financial Information Authority on 1 October 2019. [cite:vt5_cwr_egmont2020] On 24 September 2020 the Holy See announced that the pope had accepted the resignation of Cardinal Giovanni Angelo Becciu, who had been the Secretariat's second-ranking official, the Substitute, from 2011 to 2018, as prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints "and from the rights connected with the Cardinalate." [cite:vt10_vn_becciu2020] [cite:vt10_wiki_becciu] On 1 July 2022 the Vatican announced it had sold the building to Bain Capital for 186 million pounds (about 215 million euros). Prosecutors put total losses above 200 million euros; the Vatican said the losses had been absorbed by the Secretariat's reserves and did not touch Peter's Pence, the collection described in [scene:vt9_peters_pence|Part Nine]. [cite:vt10_rns_sale2022]

The first trial and the verdict of 16 December 2023

Becciu was indicted on 3 July 2021, with Pope Francis's approval, and the trial of ten people and four companies opened on 27 July 2021 before the Tribunal of Vatican City State, presided over by Giuseppe Pignatone, and ran to 86 hearings. [cite:vt10_wiki_becciu] [cite:vt10_vn_verdict2023] On 16 December 2023 the tribunal convicted Becciu on three counts of embezzlement and sentenced him to five years and six months, a fine of 8,000 euros and permanent disqualification from public office. The three counts were the Athena payment; payments of more than 570,000 euros to a company linked to Cecilia Marogna, made on grounds the court found untrue, that the money would help free a kidnapped nun; and 125,000 euros sent to the Caritas of the diocese of Ozieri in Sardinia but meant for a cooperative, SPES, chaired by Becciu's brother. The court acquitted him of the other charges, including the charge that the building had been overvalued. [cite:vt10_vn_verdict2023] Mincione received five years and six months, Torzi six years, the former financial manager Enrico Crasso and the Secretariat official Fabrizio Tirabassi seven years each, the lawyer Nicola Squillace a suspended term, and Marogna three years and nine months; the former heads of the Financial Information Authority, René Brülhart and Tommaso Di Ruzza, were fined for failing to report a suspicious transaction, and Becciu's former secretary, Mauro Carlino, was acquitted. [cite:vt10_vn_verdict2023] The tribunal ordered 166 million euros confiscated and 200 million euros paid in civil damages to Vatican offices; prosecutors had asked for more than 400 million. [cite:vt10_ap_verdict2023]

The positions on each side were stated on the day. The Promoter of Justice, Alessandro Diddi, said the result "showed we were correct." Becciu's lawyer, Fabio Viglione, said his client respected the sentence and would appeal. [cite:vt10_ap_verdict2023] Becciu said the 125,000 euros were for a bakery that employed young people at risk and had never left diocesan accounts, and he has maintained that Francis authorised payments to free the nun and called the case "a machination" against him; prosecutors produced letters in which Francis denied authorising the Marogna engagement or the London investment. [cite:vt10_ap_verdict2023] [cite:vt10_avv_nullita2026] [cite:vt10_wiki_becciu] Defence lawyers argued that the Vatican's old procedural rules had given prosecutors "enormous leeway to withhold evidence." [cite:vt10_ap_verdict2023]

The appeal and the ruling of 17 March 2026

The appeal opened on 22 September 2025 before the Vatican Court of Appeal, presided over by Archbishop Alejandro Arellano Cedillo, Dean of the Roman Rota. [cite:vt10_s24_appeal2025] The defence asked for messages exchanged among the public-relations consultant Francesca Chaouqui, Genoveffa Ciferri and Monsignor Alberto Perlasca, a former Secretariat official who became the prosecution's main witness; the messages had been withheld at first instance. [cite:vt10_s24_appeal2025] [cite:vt10_avv_nullita2026] In January 2026 the Court of Cassation upheld the dismissal of the prosecutor's own appeal for a procedural error, and Diddi stepped aside from the case. [cite:vt10_ap_mistrial2026]

On 17 March 2026 the Court of Appeal issued a 16-page ruling. It held that the prosecutors' failure to give the defence all of their evidence vitiated the summons to trial, and that one of four rescripts, decrees by which Pope Francis had widened the prosecutors' powers during the investigation, had not been published in time and so may have undermined some investigative acts. [cite:vt10_ap_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] The court affirmed that papal rescripts are a legitimate exercise of the pope's authority and rejected the defence's arguments based on the European Convention on Human Rights, which the Vatican has not incorporated. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] It declared a "relative nullity", declined to annul the whole first-instance trial, kept the acquittals of defendants against whom no valid appeal lay, and ordered key hearings, including witness evidence, to be held again before the appeal court itself. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_avv_nullita2026] It ordered the Promoter of Justice to deposit the complete investigative file by 30 April 2026. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026]

Reports differ on what the ruling did to the 2023 convictions. Vatican News, ANSA and EWTN reported that the first-instance judgment kept legal effect for the time being; AP reported that the errors "nullified the original indictment" and required a new trial. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_ansa_becciu2026] [cite:vt10_cwr_retrial2026] [cite:vt10_ap_mistrial2026] Read together, the sources show that the 2023 sentences cannot become final until the renewed hearings end and that the appeal court will decide afresh. Becciu said, through his lawyers, that "my complete innocence will be recognized soon"; Tirabassi's lawyers said it was the first time in Vatican history that a court had found a papal rescript invalid. [cite:vt10_ansa_becciu2026] [cite:vt10_ap_mistrial2026] The Holy See issued no comment beyond its news report of the ruling. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026]

Status as of October 2026

The defence said that the Promoter of Justice's office had not deposited the complete file by the 30 April deadline. [cite:vt10_adige_atti2026] The court had fixed 22 June 2026 for a hearing to set the calendar; by 19 September 2026 the court had fixed 21 September for defence briefs and 29 October 2026 for the next hearing, and Viglione said the defence was "at peace," confident of Becciu's innocence. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_us_oct2026] No later ruling was found as of 22 October 2026. Becciu and the other defendants in the renewed proceedings are presumed innocent until a final judgment.
