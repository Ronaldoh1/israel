The Route That Outlived Him

The most durable part of any trafficking operation is not a person. It is a route: the way young women were found, brought in and kept quiet. For Epstein, one of the documented routes ran through the modelling industry. Jean-Luc Brunel's agency, MC2, took Epstein's money: its former bookkeeper testified in 2010 that he invested $1 million, and Brunel himself acknowledged a letter of credit from him. Brunel denied trafficking. [cite:ep10_tfl_brunel] He was charged in France with the rape of minors and died in custody in 2022, before trial. [cite:ep2_aj_brunel2022]

The industry that route ran through is still being examined. Allegations against Gérald Marie, the former head of Elite's European business, covering the 1980s and 1990s, were shelved in France in 2023 because they were too old to prosecute; that decision is under appeal. In March 2026 fifteen women asked Paris prosecutors to examine the links between Marie, Brunel and Epstein. Marie's lawyer said he was "confident about any investigation". [cite:ep10_local_marie2026] After the Paris prosecutor, Laure Beccuau, appealed for victims in February 2026, about twenty came forward, about ten of them unknown to investigators before, and her office has gone back to Epstein's computers, phone records and address books. As of May 2026 no one had been charged. [cite:ep10_aj_france2026]

Whether the route still carries anyone today is not shown. What is shown is that the people who used it were never all identified, that the time limits that protected some of them in France still stand, and that new victims are still coming forward.
