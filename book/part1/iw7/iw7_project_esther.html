Project Esther

In October 2024 the Heritage Foundation, publisher of Project 2025, released "Project Esther: A National Strategy to Combat Antisemitism", drafted by Victoria Coates, Robert Greenway and Daniel Flesch and overseen by Coates, a former deputy national security adviser. [cite:iw_heritage_esther] [cite:iw_wiki_esther] It describes the pro-Palestinian movement in America as a "Hamas Support Network", "effectively a terrorist support network", naming American Muslims for Palestine, Students for Justice in Palestine and Jewish Voice for Peace among others, and sets out nineteen "desired effects", among them denying campus access to non-citizen supporters, refusing protest permits and waging "lawfare", with the aim of dismantling the network within two years. [cite:iw_aje_esther] [cite:iw_wiki_esther]

Who objected

Several Jewish organisations declined to associate with the plan, saying its silence on right-wing antisemitism made it partisan; an open letter signed by about three dozen former leaders of Jewish groups, including a former national chair of the Anti-Defamation League, criticised it. [cite:iw_wiki_esther] The plan named a Jewish organisation, Jewish Voice for Peace, as part of the network. [cite:iw_aje_esther]

What happened next

In May 2025 the New York Times found that the second Trump administration had called for or acted on more than half of the plan's proposals; Heritage said there were "clear parallels" but that it did not know whether the White House had used the plan as a guide. [cite:iw_wiki_esther] Parts Eight and Nine follow what was done. In July 2026 Mahmoud Khalil sued Heritage under the Ku Klux Klan Act, alleging a conspiracy with pro-Israel groups and administration officials to deny Palestinian advocates their constitutional rights. [cite:iw_wiki_esther]

The correspondence is documented; causation is not. The Times found parallels, not instructions, and the same policies had other advocates. What the record shows is a published plan and a government that, within months, did much of what it proposed.
