What Followed

Ask of each later conflict and agreement whether it would exist had the state not been founded as it was, and the answer is nearly always no, with a few honest exceptions.

Directly downstream

The refugee question exists because of 1948. The occupation exists because of 1967. Hamas was founded in December 1987, twenty years into the occupation of Gaza, from an Islamist association Israeli authorities had registered in 1979. [cite:npr_hamashistory] [cite:toi_hamasblogs] Hezbollah emerged after the invasion of Lebanon in 1982, and a former Israeli prime minister, Ehud Barak, said Israel's presence there created it. [cite:hezbollah_founding] [cite:terrorism_info_hezbollah_birth] The Oslo Accords were a plan to divide the land taken in 1967; during their years the settler population of the West Bank, outside East Jerusalem, kept growing, from about 110,000 at the signing, and the plan failed. [cite:oslo_1_text] [cite:peacenow_settlement_population] Every armed movement in this story formed just after an event it was reacting to, and the founding is the first of those events. [cite:npr_hamashistory]

Partly downstream

The Abraham Accords of 2020 would not exist without the state, by definition; but the documented drivers of them were fear of Iran and concrete American rewards: recognition of Morocco's claim to Western Sahara, Sudan's removal from the terrorism list, an arms package for the Emirates. [cite:brit_abraham] [cite:w_abraham] The enmity with Iran grew from a former alliance, after a revolution whose roots run through a CIA and MI6 coup in 1953 that had nothing to do with Israel. [cite:w_1953_coup]

Not downstream

The borders of the Arab states were drawn by Britain and France between 1916 and 1922, and revolts against that order in Iraq and Syria came before Israel existed. [cite:w_sykes_picot] [cite:w_iraq_revolt_1920] [cite:w_great_syrian_revolt] The region would have had wars without the founding. It would not have had these.
