How the Split Happened

In January 2006 Hamas won the Palestinian legislative election, judged free and fair by international observers, and formed a government in March. Donors imposed sanctions, Israel withheld the tax revenues it collects for the Palestinian Authority, and the Quartet stopped aid to a Hamas-led government. In February 2007 Fatah and Hamas signed the Mecca Agreement and formed a unity government; in June 2007 fighting between them ended with Hamas in control of Gaza, and President Mahmoud Abbas dissolved the government. From then on there were two Palestinian administrations: Fatah's in Ramallah, Hamas's in Gaza. [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] Gaza, Documented sets out the takeover and the blockade that followed. [scene:gz4_takeover|The takeover]

The record assigns the failures to both factions. The Media Line, reviewing the agreements, describes the 2007 seizure of Gaza as a Hamas coup, and says the later agreements foundered on mutual accusations. [cite:wb_medialine_agreements]

No Elections Since 2006

The legislature elected in 2006 has not been renewed. Elections were finally called for May and July 2021. On 29 April 2021, two days before campaigning was to begin, Abbas postponed them indefinitely, saying "We will not move to hold elections without Jerusalem," because Israel would not commit to allowing Palestinians in East Jerusalem to vote. Critics, including Palestinian officials, said the real reason was that Fatah had split into rival lists and Hamas was likely to win the most seats. The United States and Israel, wary of Hamas's participation, did not press for the vote. [cite:wb_gpb_elections2021]

Security Coordination

Under the Oslo arrangements the Palestinian Authority's security forces share information with Israel and act to prevent attacks on Israelis, soldiers and civilians alike. Abbas has called the coordination "sacred." It is deeply unpopular among Palestinians, many of whom see it as collaboration with the occupation. After an Israeli raid on Jenin camp killed ten people in January 2023, Abbas announced its suspension, and then quietly assured US officials that intelligence-sharing would continue. [cite:wb_fp_coordination] The result, in the West Bank, is an authority that polices its own population in coordination with the army that occupies it, while holding no election and, as Part Twelve shows, depending on revenues that Israel collects and can withhold.
