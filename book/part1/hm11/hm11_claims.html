Claims Made in Hamas's Name

Throughout the war, Israel justified strikes, raids and policies by naming Hamas: a hospital was a command centre, aid staff were members, the casualty count was Hamas propaganda. Some of these claims were later borne out in part, some were not, and in at least one major case the Hamas-run authorities' own claim proved wrong. The record is set out in both directions.

Al-Shifa hospital, November 2023. Israel said Hamas ran a command centre beneath Gaza's largest hospital, and the United States backed the assessment; Israel released video of a tunnel shaft and of hostages brought into the hospital on 7 October. A Washington Post analysis of the material Israel released found that it "falls short of showing that Hamas had been using the hospital as a command and control center": Israel exposed a tunnel, but none of the five buildings it named appeared connected to it, and the underground rooms showed no immediate sign of military use. [cite:hm_es_wapo_shifa]

UNRWA, January–August 2024. Israel accused 12, then 19, staff of the UN relief agency of taking part in 7 October, and several donor states suspended funding. The UN's internal investigators found that nine "may have been involved," that the evidence was insufficient for nine others and that there was none for one; they could not independently authenticate most of what Israel supplied. [cite:hm_un_oios]

The death toll. Israeli officials dismissed the count from Gaza's "Hamas-run" health ministry for most of the war; in January 2026 a senior military official, unnamed, said the army estimated about 70,000 dead, though the army said that did not reflect official data. [cite:pr_toi_idf70k] [cite:hm_jw_idf70k] PRESS sets out the counting. [scene:pr1_lists|The count]

Journalists. Israel said some journalists it killed were members of armed groups; PRESS sets out each case, the evidence offered and the press-freedom groups' findings, including their own removals of some names. [scene:pr7_law|PRESS: "Journalists are not terrorists"]

Al-Ahli hospital, October 2023: a claim against Israel that failed. Gaza's health ministry said an Israeli strike on the al-Ahli hospital killed 471 people. Human Rights Watch found the blast "resulted from an apparent rocket-propelled munition, such as those commonly used by Palestinian armed groups," and called the reported toll unusually high for the damage. [cite:hm_mm_hrw_ahli]

The pattern the table shows is not that every claim about Hamas was false. It is that the word "Hamas" was used to justify actions at a scale the evidence, when it came, often did not support, while the same scrutiny also caught false claims made against Israel.
