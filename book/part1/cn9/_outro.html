The canon of scripture was fixed by named people over fourteen centuries, and the authority to fix it was, from Irenaeus onward, an authority over readers. [cite:cn_iren_3_3] That authority drew imperial money and law in the fourth century, territory and forged titles in the eighth, the rivalries of Roman families from the eleventh, the confiscated lands of heretics in the thirteenth, a bank's loan in the sixteenth and a list of banned books that lasted until 1966. [cite:vt21_hist_x7] [cite:cn_wiki_donation] [cite:cn_wiki_benedict9] [cite:vt28_fordham_lat4c3] [cite:cn_brit_albert] [cite:cn_va_index1966] The families who stood beside the throne held rank more than rule, and the money that moved through the institution came, in the documented cases, from donors, rulers, borrowers and banks. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt11_felisini_ch4] What the list left out survived elsewhere: in a jar in Egypt, in Ethiopia's 81 books, in teachers who said the kingdom was within.
