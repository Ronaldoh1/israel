Abroad and at Home

The release of the files in January 2026 had consequences, and they fell almost entirely outside the United States. In Britain, Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor and Peter Mandelson were arrested in February on suspicion of misconduct in public office and released without charge. [cite:ep4_aj_arrests2026] In October a High Court judge quashed the warrants used to search Andrew's former homes, after the police accepted they were unlawful; Thames Valley Police say their investigation continues. [cite:ep10_malaymail_andrew2026] In Norway, the former prime minister Thorbjørn Jagland, the diplomat Mona Juul and her husband Terje Rød-Larsen were charged with corruption offences. In France, prosecutors opened an investigation into the former culture minister Jack Lang, and in Paris they opened two framework investigations into Epstein, one into trafficking and one into financial crime. Officials resigned in Slovakia, Dubai and elsewhere. [cite:ep4_aj_arrests2026] [cite:ep10_local_marie2026]

In the United States, the same release produced resignations from corporate, academic and legal posts, congressional interviews and civil settlements. It produced no arrest and no criminal charge. [cite:ep4_aj_arrests2026]

The book has to be fair about why. Most of the foreign cases are not about sex crimes. They are about officials accused of passing state information to Epstein or taking money from him, offences in which the released emails are themselves the evidence. In the United States, most of the people named appear as social and business contacts, which is not a crime. That explains part of the difference. It does not explain why the American cases that could be built from the same files, against the people his staff scheduled, his banks served and his pilots flew, have not been.
