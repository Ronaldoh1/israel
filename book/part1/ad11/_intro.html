Part Eleven follows the money that leaves a household through the public side of the ledger: taxes, fees and the benefits a family loses as it earns more. It begins with who pays what share of all taxes, federal, state and local together, setting the progressive federal system beside the regressive state and local one, the cap on the payroll tax, the gap between taxes on wages and on investment gains, the disputed claim that the richest pay lower rates than working families, and the distribution of the 2025 budget law. It then turns to fees: prices hidden until checkout and the federal rule that now requires them to be shown, utility shutoffs counted nationally for the first time, and towns that fund themselves from the fines their courts impose. It ends with the benefits cliff, the point at which a raise can leave a low-income family worse off, with worked examples, the measured size of the problem and the evidence that the safety net still pays most people to work.
