The record holds two real episodes, and neither amounts to the movement siding with Nazism. On 25 August 1933 the Zionist Federation of Germany and the Anglo-Palestine Bank, acting for the Jewish Agency, signed the Haavara ("transfer") Agreement with the Reich Economics Ministry, letting emigrating German Jews move part of their assets to Palestine as German export goods [cite:wiki_haavara_agreement]. About 60,000 German Jews reached Palestine through it between 1933 and 1939, but it also undercut the worldwide anti-Nazi boycott. Jabotinsky, Rabbi Stephen Wise and Abba Hillel Silver opposed it, and the 1935 Zionist Congress voted by a large majority to continue it [cite:jvl_haavara]. It was both a rescue and a commercial accommodation.

Separately, a document dated 11 January 1941 records an offer by the Lehi underground, a militant fringe group, to fight on Germany's side in exchange for support for a Jewish state; no German reply is recorded [cite:marxists_ankara_document]. The mainstream Zionist Executive denounced Lehi and the Irgun as "traitors" [cite:daat_moyne].
