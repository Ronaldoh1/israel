The Salesman, 1982–1993

In 1982 Moshe Arens, then Israel's ambassador in Washington, made the 32-year-old Netanyahu his deputy chief of mission. [cite:ny_wiki_bibi] It was the year of Israel's invasion of Lebanon, and the embassy's work was explaining it to Americans. From 1 September 1984 to 1 March 1988 he was Israel's ambassador to the United Nations. [cite:ny_wiki_bibi]

The New York years made him a television figure. He was a regular guest on American news programmes, and during the Gulf War of 1991, when Iraqi missiles struck Israel, he became Israel's main English-language spokesman on CNN and other networks. [cite:ny_wiki_bibi] A study of his relations with the press, published by Nieman Reports, traces a consistent method across four decades: cultivate the American and international media, and treat the domestic press as an opponent. [cite:niemanreports_netanyahu_vs_israeli_media]

The subject he chose

His subject in those years was terrorism, and he made it a field of his own: the conferences of the Jonathan Institute, the edited volume Terrorism: How the West Can Win in 1986, and the argument, as a reviewer of that book summarised it, that terrorism was a tool of hostile states rather than a reaction to grievance. [cite:ir5_enc_bio] [cite:ir5_nyrb_1986] The Iran Architecture on this shelf follows the second subject he took up after 1992, the warning that Iran was a few years from a bomb, through three decades of dated predictions.

Into politics

He came home in 1988, joined the Likud and entered the Knesset that year, and on 24 March 1993 won the leadership of the party against Benny Begin and David Levy. [cite:ny_wiki_bibi] He was 43, and the party he led was in opposition to the government of Yitzhak Rabin, which that year signed the first Oslo agreement with the Palestine Liberation Organization.
