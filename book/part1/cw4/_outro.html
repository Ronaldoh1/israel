By the end of 1962 Israel was armed through channels that had not existed in March 1957, and almost none of them rested on a public commitment. Each was run by a few officials, each was disputed inside the government that ran it, and each left a record thinner than the thing it built.

The legal layer was the thinnest. The French agreement of 1957 is known in outline, not in text: a 24-megawatt reactor with systems built for three times that power, a reprocessing plant covered by protocols that were never written down, and a signature that, in Peres's and Goldschmidt's accounts, was backdated by a day because the Bourgès-Maunoury government had fallen, though the signing date is given several ways. [cite:rs_cw4_fas_israel] [cite:rs_cw4_goldschmidt] [cite:rs_cw4_peres_memoir] De Gaulle's compromise of 1960 set it on new terms, Israel's pledge of no weapons and no reprocessing against France's agreement not to insist on inspection. [cite:rs_cw4_jvl_nuclear] Ben-Gurion then refused inspection by the International Atomic Energy Agency "until all reactors are treated as equals." [cite:rs_cw4_ebb510] The pact with Turkey was a secret agreement between prime ministers; Iran's recognition of Israel was de facto, and spoken of in public only in 1960; Bonn withheld diplomatic relations. [cite:rs_cw4_schonmann_2024] [cite:rs_cw4_frus58v12_d273] [cite:rs_cw4_kas_borchard] What did take public legal form was American: the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961 and the Agency for International Development, created inside the State Department by Executive Order 10973. [cite:rs_cw4_eo10973] At the United Nations, Resolution 138 asked Israel for "appropriate reparation" for Eichmann's seizure, and Resolution 171 called its raid of March 1962 "a flagrant violation." [cite:rs_cw4_unsc138] [cite:rs_cw4_unsc171]

The administrative layer shows who could act. Israel's armament ran through the Prime Minister: Peres for France and Germany, Manes Pratt at Dimona, the Mossad, under Harel and directly under Ben-Gurion, for the Trident, SAVAK's training, Eichmann and Morocco. [cite:rs_cw4_alpher_brookings] [cite:rs_cw4_isa_eichmann] [cite:rs_cw4_wiki_yachin] The cabinet was not bypassed. It adopted the periphery on 20 July 1958 and divided six to five over Hausner's Lavon opinion and over immunity for Eichmann's defence witnesses. [cite:rs_cw4_schonmann_2024] [cite:rs_cw4_djn_1960_10] [cite:rs_cw4_isa_eichmann] Nor could the Prime Minister always carry it. In the Lavon quarrel, at bottom a fight over which forum could find the facts, a committee of ministers cleared Lavon, Ben-Gurion resigned in protest on 31 January 1961, and the election that followed cost Mapai five seats. [cite:rs_cw4_cie_bg_resigns] [cite:rs_cw4_idi_1961] Inside Israel the same years produced Wadi Salib, where the Etzioni commission, as its report is summarized, found no deliberate discrimination by state institutions but discrimination at lower levels of implementation. [cite:rs_cw4_hamichlol_wadisalib]

The institutional and financial layer is where the record is most divided. West Germany's "Aktion Geschäftsfreund" sent 629.4 million marks to 1965, booked through a Frankfurt development bank as aid to unnamed developing countries, alongside military financing put at 340 million marks by German records and 500 million by one historian. [cite:rs_cw4_barjoseph] American aid ran in the tens of millions a year, $93.4 million in 1962, of which $13.2 million was military and all of that loans. [cite:rs_cw4_jvl_aid_table] Iran sold the crude that made the Eilat pipeline work. [cite:rs_cw4_frus58v12_d273] Morocco was paid, by reported terms, $500,000 down and then a sum for each emigrant. [cite:rs_cw4_aish_egoz] Who paid for Dimona is the open question at the center of this layer. Eisenhower said "we do not know where they have obtained the funds"; the estimates ran from the embassy's $17.8 million in foreign exchange to $100–200 million; Peres put a known half at about $40 million, from donors in one version and from the government in another; and the case that German money filled the gap is a probability on indirect evidence for Bar-Joseph, who concedes it cannot be asserted unequivocally, and unproven on the documents. [cite:rs_cw4_ebb510] [cite:rs_cw4_natinterest]

The Palestinian layer was a politics without a channel to any of these decisions. Fatah's founders in Kuwait, whose movement became public in Beirut in October 1959, chose to act independently of the Arab governments; Al-Ard, inside Israel and under military government, chose the licensing law, the companies registry and the courts, and was refused a newspaper licence. [cite:rs_cw4_palquest_fatah] [cite:rs_cw4_jiryis_pij] In the documents where the refugees' future was discussed, at the Waldorf in May 1961 and in Feldman's talks of August 1962, they did not speak: Ben-Gurion called them the Arab states' "best weapon at hand," Kennedy cited America's 70 percent of UNRWA's costs, and the Johnson plan did not win Israel's consent in August 1962. [cite:rs_cw4_jvl_memcon] [cite:rs_cw4_jvl_feldman_aug19]

The regional layer turned in 1958. The United Arab Republic, the fall of Iraq's monarchy and the American landing in Lebanon remade the region, and the loss of Iraq brought Turkey toward Israel. The Trident that followed was, in Alpher's assessment, lopsided, and Israel valued it chiefly as proof that it was not isolated. [cite:rs_cw4_liel_bmlv] [cite:rs_cw4_apn_review] [cite:foreignaffairs_trident_legacy] Egypt broke with Iran after the Shah spoke of recognition in July 1960, for reasons the sources weigh differently. [cite:rs_cw4_iranica_akhavi] On the armistice lines, the Mixed Armistice Commission condemned Israel's attack on Tawafiq and the Security Council called its attack of March 1962 a flagrant violation, while Egypt's move into Sinai in February 1960 showed how little warning Israel might have. [cite:rs_cw4_unyb1960] [cite:rs_cw4_unsc171] [cite:rs_cw4_wiki_rotem]

The American layer moved furthest. In August 1958 Washington refused Israel arms and a written guarantee and Dulles approved "extreme caution" in encouraging Ben-Gurion's periphery plan. [cite:rs_cw4_schonmann_2017] By December 1960 it had judged that weapons were "at least one major purpose" at Dimona. [cite:rs_cw4_ebb510] By 1962 it had accepted Israeli assurances and two visits on Israeli terms, and had offered the Hawk, which it told the world was "purely defensive" while renewing to Nasser its assurances about the reactor. [cite:rs_cw4_frus61_d53] [cite:rs_cw4_frus62_d87] [cite:rs_cw4_kramer_hawks]

What was settled, then, was the existence of the channels and Israel's place at their center. What was contested was what they were for. Israel's strongest case was Ben-Gurion's: a small state forced out of Sinai, refused American arms and a written guarantee, facing a neighbour he expected to do "what Hitler did," would not leave its survival to another government's word. [cite:rs_cw4_jvl_memcon] The strongest case of its critics was put by its own allies: Eisenhower's advisers found the plant "not for peaceful uses," cabled about a "lack of candor," and Kennedy warned that the United Arab Republic would follow Israel into the field; inside Israel, according to Aronson, Leibowitz and Livne's committee was the only public voice of dissent. [cite:rs_cw4_ebb510] [cite:rs_cw4_jvl_memcon] [cite:rs_cw4_aronson_ch6] What the record leaves open lies at the reactor's heart: whether a separation plant existed in 1961 that two visitors were kept from seeing, who paid for Dimona, and what Ben-Gurion meant by "for the time being." Those questions passed to Kennedy's confrontation of 1963, told in [scene:s11b|the 1963 letters].
