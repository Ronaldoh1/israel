What Was Agreed, and What Each Side Said It Meant

The war described in [scene:hz7_war|Part Seven] ended, formally, at four o'clock on the morning of 27 November 2024. The text that the United States and France announced the day before was titled "Announcement of a Cessation of Hostilities and Related Commitments on Enhanced Security Arrangements and Toward the Implementation of UNSCR 1701." It was short: thirteen numbered points, which the Times of Israel published in full the same afternoon. [cite:hz8_toi_text]

The core obligations ran in both directions. Lebanon's government was to prevent Hezbollah and every other armed group in Lebanon from carrying out operations against Israel. Israel was to carry out no offensive military operations against Lebanese targets, civilian or military, by land, air or sea. Both sides committed to steps toward the full implementation of Security Council Resolution 1701, the text that had ended the 2006 war. And point four preserved for both the "inherent right of self-defense, consistent with international law." [cite:hz8_toi_text]

The rest of the text dealt with who could carry weapons, and where. Only the official forces of the Lebanese state, which the text listed as the army, the Internal Security Forces, General Security, State Security, customs and municipal police, were to be armed in the "Southern Litani Area," the strip between the Israeli border and the Litani river. The Lebanese government was to control all sales, supply and production of arms in the country and to dismantle unauthorized facilities and confiscate unauthorized weapons, "starting in the Southern Litani Area." The United States and France would work to bring the Lebanese army's strength in the south to 10,000 soldiers. Israel was to withdraw its forces in phases to the south of the Blue Line while the army deployed, on a coordinated plan that "should not exceed 60 days." [cite:hz8_toi_text]

Enforcement was given to a body the text called the Mechanism: a reformulated version of an existing tripartite committee, hosted by UNIFIL, chaired by the United States and including France. Violations were to be reported to it and to UNIFIL, without prejudice to either government's right to go to the Security Council directly. [cite:hz8_toi_text]

The side letter

What the published text did not contain was the point Israel had pressed hardest. At the end of October 2024, Israel's Channel 12 reported that Israel was seeking a side letter in which Washington would commit to giving Israel "freedom of action to respond to any violation of the ceasefire deal by Hezbollah"; Axios reported the same demand as "active enforcement." [cite:hz8_toi_sideletter] When the deal was announced, Channel 12 reported that such a letter existed and affirmed Israel's right to act against threats from Lebanon and to keep flying reconnaissance over it. The Times of Israel said it had not seen the letter. [cite:hz8_toi_text] Hours after the truce began, Israel's Channel 13 broadcast what it said was the letter's content, set out in [scene:hz7_war|Part Seven]; neither government has published it. [cite:hz7_olj_sideletter] What is on the official record is how the two leaders described the arrangement.

On 26 November, Benjamin Netanyahu told Israelis: "With the United States' full understanding, we maintain full freedom of military action." If Hezbollah tried to rearm, rebuild near the border, launch a rocket, dig a tunnel or bring in "a truck carrying rockets," he said, "we will attack." He gave three reasons for agreeing to a ceasefire at all: "to focus on the Iranian threat," "to give our forces a breather and replenish stocks," and "to separate the fronts and isolate Hamas." [cite:hz8_jvl_netanyahu] President Joe Biden said the same evening that the arrangement was "designed to be a permanent cessation of hostilities," that Israel "retains the right to self-defense consistent with international law," and that Hezbollah's infrastructure in the south "will not be allowed to be rebuilt." [cite:hz8_biden_dcpd]

Hezbollah's new secretary-general, Naim Qassem, gave his account on 29 November. He called the outcome a "divine victory," larger than that of 2006, and said coordination between "the resistance" and the Lebanese army "will be at a high level" in carrying out the agreement. [cite:hz8_aj_qassem] The two readings were not compatible. Israel read the deal as permission to keep striking anything it judged a threat; Hezbollah read it as an obligation on Israel to stop and leave, with the south handed to the army.

The deadlines

The sixty days ran out on 26 January 2025, and Israel had not withdrawn. Residents of border villages tried to return that day; Lebanese health officials said Israeli fire killed at least 22 people, among them six women and a soldier, and wounded 124. The Israeli army said its troops had fired warning shots to "remove threats" from suspects approaching them, and blamed Hezbollah for organizing the marches. The same day the White House announced that the arrangement "will continue to be in effect until February 18, 2025." [cite:hz8_nbc_jan26]

On 17 February, the eve of the new deadline, the Israeli army said it would stay past it at five posts inside Lebanon, which The National listed as Jabal Blat, Labbouneh, and the hills of Al Azziyeh, Al Awidah and El Hamames. "We need to remain at those points at the moment to defend Israeli citizens," said the military spokesman, Lt Col Nadav Shoshani, describing the aim as eventually handing over to the Lebanese army. President Joseph Aoun rejected reports that Lebanon had agreed to any extension. Qassem said: "Israel must fully withdraw on February 18, it has no excuse." A Hezbollah-allied MP called the decision a "new violation of the ceasefire agreement." [cite:hz8_national_five]

The text itself said nothing about Israel keeping positions after the withdrawal, and Israel's stated reason was a security judgment rather than a clause. The text also said nothing that allowed Hezbollah to keep weapons anywhere in Lebanon, a point that would matter later: the obligation to bring all arms under the state ran, in the agreement's words, to the whole country, "starting" in the south. Each side's subsequent conduct, set out in the next section, followed its own reading.
