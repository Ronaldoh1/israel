Thirty-Four Days

On the morning of 12 July 2006 Hezbollah fighters crossed the border into Israel and attacked an army patrol. They killed three soldiers in the ambush and took two reservists, Ehud Goldwasser and Eldad Regev, back into Lebanon. An Israeli tank sent after them struck a large mine; five more soldiers died in the failed rescue attempt. Eight Israeli soldiers were killed that day and two were captured. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Accounts that give three dead count only the ambush; the figure of eight includes the pursuit. [cite:hz5_wiki_war]

The raid was not a new tactic. In October 2000 Hezbollah had captured three Israeli soldiers in the Shebaa Farms area and traded their bodies, with a captured Israeli businessman, for prisoners in 2004. [scene:hz4_withdrawal|The 2000 capture and the 2004 exchange] Hezbollah's stated aim in 2006 was the same: to hold soldiers and swap them for four Lebanese held in Israel. Its secretary-general, Hassan Nasrallah, said the only way to get them back was "indirect negotiations and a swap." [cite:hz5_wiki_war]

Israel's answer. The Israeli cabinet met the same day and said it would "respond aggressively and harshly to those who carried out, and are responsible for, today's action." Its communiqué held the "Lebanese Government responsible for the action that originated on its soil," while also saying that "Israel is not fighting Lebanon but the terrorist element there." Israel's case rested in part on UN Security Council Resolution 1559 of 2004, which called for militias in Lebanon to disarm; the Lebanese state, Israel argued, had failed to carry it out. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] The chief of staff, Lieutenant General Dan Halutz, was reported as saying that if the soldiers were not returned, "we will turn Lebanon's clock back 20 years." [cite:hz5_wiki_war] The state commission Israel later appointed found that Prime Minister Ehud Olmert's government chose an immediate, intensive strike without a detailed, approved military plan. [cite:hz5_mjl_winograd] [scene:hz5_doctrine|The Winograd findings]

The air and sea campaign. Within days Israel destroyed the runways of Beirut's international airport, imposed an air and naval blockade on Lebanon, and bombed bridges over the Litani and Zahrani rivers. A strike on the Jiyeh power station released fuel oil into the sea in what became the largest oil spill recorded in the Mediterranean. By the war's end, damage included about 640 kilometres of roads and 73 bridges, around 15,000 homes destroyed and about 130,000 damaged; Lebanon's Council for Development and Reconstruction put damage to civilian infrastructure at close to $2.5 billion. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] The Israeli Air Force flew 11,897 combat missions, more than in the 1973 war. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Human Rights Watch counted "some 7,000 bomb and missile strikes" on Lebanon, plus naval and artillery fire. [cite:hz5_rw_why] Beirut's southern suburbs, the Dahieh, where Hezbollah had its headquarters, were struck repeatedly and large parts of them were flattened. [cite:hz5_wiki_dahiya]

The rockets. Hezbollah fired between 3,970 and 4,228 rockets into Israel over the 34 days, more than 100 a day, most of them unguided short-range Katyushas. An estimated 23 percent landed in towns and built-up areas; the rest fell in open ground. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] They reached Haifa, Israel's third city. Nasrallah said Hezbollah had first aimed only at military sites and turned to Israeli cities in response to Israel's bombing of Lebanese civilian areas. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Human Rights Watch's Peter Bouckaert said Hezbollah was "directly targeting civilians," and the organisation found that civilian places appeared about four times as often as military ones in Hezbollah's own wartime communiqués. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Between 300,000 and 500,000 Israelis left the north during the war; in Lebanon about one million people were displaced. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] [cite:hz5_rw_why] The next section sets out the deaths on both sides and how they were counted. [scene:hz5_toll|The toll]

The ground war. Israeli ground forces fought Hezbollah in the border villages from July, at first in limited operations. On 9 August the security cabinet approved a wider ground offensive, and it was launched in the last days before the ceasefire. [cite:hz5_jvl_winograd] More than 30 Israeli soldiers were killed in that final push. [cite:hz5_ap_winograd] The commission found that this offensive did not achieve its aim of limiting rocket fire. [cite:hz5_jvl_winograd] Before the ceasefire took effect at 08:00 on 14 August Israel was still seizing ground and bombing while Hezbollah kept up what it called "defensive operations." [cite:hz5_wiki_1701]

The United States. The record documents three American roles. The first was diplomatic. As Lebanon's government and many states called for an immediate ceasefire, the United States, with Britain, held out for a different sequence. John Bolton, then US ambassador to the UN, later confirmed that the US and UK, with support from several Arab leaders, delayed the ceasefire process. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] A Security Council resolution came only on 11 August, the 31st day. [cite:hz5_un_1701] The second was material. The first publicly reported transfer from the US war-reserve stockpile held in Israel, known as WRSA-I, took place during this war, when the Defense Department under President George W. Bush transferred precision-guided munitions to Israel. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa] The third was intelligence. An internal National Security Agency account, published by The Intercept in 2019, records that Israel's signals intelligence unit relied heavily on the NSA during the war and pressed it for geolocation data on Hezbollah figures; the NSA liaison called that request "particularly problematic" because US law barred the agency from supplying intelligence for targeted killing, and Israel's unit commander sought an exemption. [cite:ow4_int2019] [scene:ow4_memorandum|ONE-WAY MIRROR: the intelligence relationship] The text the Council finally adopted, negotiated between the United States and France, tied the end of the fighting to keeping armed Hezbollah personnel out of the area south of the Litani. [cite:hz5_un_1701] [cite:hz5_wiki_1701]

Two things about the start of the war are not in dispute in the sources used here. Hezbollah's cross-border raid, which killed eight soldiers and took two, began it. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] And Israel's response was aimed from the first day at the Lebanese state's infrastructure as well as at Hezbollah, on the stated ground that the state was responsible for what came from its territory. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Whether that response was proportionate is the question the rest of this part examines through the casualty record, the war's own inquiry, and the doctrine Israeli commanders drew from it.
