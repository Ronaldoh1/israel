From Escape to Kingmaker, and on to Buenos Aires

Thiel's public politics run on two tracks that are often treated as contradictory. One is exit: leaving the state behind, by sea, by second passport or by moving country. The other is voice at full volume: speaking at a party convention, sitting on a presidential transition, and spending tens of millions of dollars to put his former employees into the Senate. This section follows both, from 2009 to October 2026. What he thinks, and how his reading of René Girard and the Christian idea of the katechon bears on it, is the subject of [scene:de10_education_of_a_libertarian|Part Ten]; here the record is what he said in public, what he paid for and where he went.

Exit, 2008–2011

On 13 April 2009, in an essay for the Cato Institute's online forum Cato Unbound, Thiel wrote: "I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible." He linked the decline of what he called capitalist democracy to the growth of welfare beneficiaries and to the extension of the vote to women, and named three frontiers for "escape from politics in all its forms": cyberspace, outer space and the open sea. [cite:de3_cato_thiel] On 1 May 2009 he added a follow-up on the suffrage passage: "While I don't think any class of people should be disenfranchised, I have little hope that voting will make things better." [cite:de1_audit_cato_followup] The essay, and how Nick Land used it to open The Dark Enlightenment, are discussed in [scene:de3_other_lineages|Part Three].

The sea was the frontier he paid for. He gave $500,000 in seed money to the Seasteading Institute, founded in April 2008 to develop floating, self-governing communities. [cite:de3_wiki_tsi] His total giving was reported as $1.25 million in 2011, when the institute said he did not "dictate" its plans, and as $1.7 million by Business Insider in 2018, which also reported that he had left its board in 2011 and later called seasteads "not quite feasible." [cite:de3_yahoo_tsi] [cite:de3_bi_tsi] Both figures are reported; they are cumulative totals at different dates and are not added together. The same year, 2011, he became a New Zealand citizen, as told in [scene:de9_gawker_citizenship_and_tax|the previous section]. Where the exit projects went next is in [scene:de22_seas_and_zones|Part Twenty-Two].

Voice, 2016

On 21 July 2016 Thiel addressed the Republican National Convention in Cleveland. "I am proud to be gay. I am proud to be a Republican. But most of all I am proud to be an American," he said, and he dismissed the argument over transgender bathroom access as one of the "fake culture wars" that "distract us from our economic decline." [cite:de9_fortune_rnc] In October, in the campaign's final weeks, he pledged $1.25 million to help elect Donald Trump. On 11 November, after the election, Trump named him to the executive committee of the presidential transition, a body of more than a dozen members chaired by the vice-president-elect, Mike Pence. [cite:de9_cnn_transition] Defending his choice, he said: "Most Americans don't live by the [Washington] Beltway or San Francisco Bay." [cite:de9_cnn_transition] Curtis Yarvin claimed in private emails that year that he had watched the election at Thiel's house and had been "coaching" him; that is Yarvin's account, discussed in [scene:de5_the_public_years|Part Five], and Thiel has not confirmed it. [cite:de5_pein_baffler]

The Senate bets, 2021–2022

Thiel stayed out of the 2020 presidential race while giving to congressional Republicans, including more than $2 million to a committee backing Kris Kobach's Senate primary in Kansas. [cite:de9_bp_thiel] [cite:de9_mj_2025] In 2021 he introduced J.D. Vance, who had worked at his fund Mithril Capital, to Trump. [cite:de9_fortune_net24] On 7 February 2022 he announced that he would leave the board of Meta, after seventeen years, to support candidates in that year's elections. [cite:de9_wiki_thiel]

He backed two of his own former employees for the Senate. Blake Masters had been president of the Thiel Foundation from 2015 and chief operating officer of Thiel Capital from 2018, and had co-written Thiel's 2014 book on start-ups, Zero to One. [cite:de9_12news_masters] According to FEC records reported by CNBC, Thiel gave $15 million to the super PAC supporting Vance in Ohio's Republican primary, and $15 million to the one supporting Masters in Arizona's primary, plus $2.5 million in October, about $32 million across the midterms. [cite:de9_cnbc_2022] Other tallies differ: Ballotpedia, citing Forbes, gives $10 million to Protect Ohio Values, at least $20 million to Saving Arizona PAC and $35 million across sixteen federal races. [cite:de9_bp_thiel] [cite:de9_audit_bp_2022] Vance won his seat; Masters lost to Senator Mark Kelly. [cite:de9_cnbc_2022] The two campaigns, run on the same model, are compared in [scene:de15_vance_and_masters|Part Fifteen].

Stepping back, 2023–2024

In April 2023 Reuters, citing two people close to him, reported that Thiel did not plan to give to candidates in 2024, unhappy with the Republican Party's focus on cultural issues, though he might still support former employees. [cite:de9_reuters_2023] In November 2023 Barton Gellman of The Atlantic reported that Thiel wanted it published that he "wouldn't be giving money to any politician, including Donald Trump, in the next presidential campaign"; the words are Gellman's paraphrase. [cite:de9_pw_atlantic] He told Gellman that voting for Trump in 2016 "was like a not very articulate scream for help," and said he had turned down a $10 million request from Trump's campaign. [cite:de9_forbesau_vance] At the Aspen Ideas Festival in June 2024 he said: "If you hold a gun to my head, I'll vote for Trump," and "I'm not going to give any money to his super PAC." [cite:de9_nbc_aspen] After Trump chose Vance as his running mate in July 2024, Vance urged him, in an interview with the Financial Times, to "get off the sidelines." [cite:de9_forbesau_vance] A single magazine report that Thiel lobbied for Vance's selection is examined, as contested, in [scene:de12_senate_and_the_ticket|Part Twelve].

No gift from Thiel to Trump's 2024 campaign or its super PACs is on the record. Ballotpedia, citing FEC records, nonetheless puts his giving in the 2024 cycle at "over $1.7 million to candidates for federal office and political parties," without itemising it, while Bloomberg and Mother Jones describe him as having sat that cycle out. [cite:de9_bp_thiel] [cite:de9_bgov_cfg] [cite:de9_mj_2025] Because the cycle began in January 2023, before either statement, the figures cannot be tested against his words without dated line items; the conflict is recorded in [scene:de9_claims_vs_record|the claims against the record].

Re-engagement and departure, 2025–2026

In February 2025 Thiel gave $852,200 to Grow the Majority, the fundraising committee of the House speaker, Mike Johnson, and in February 2026 $1.5 million to Club for Growth Action, a conservative political action committee; Bloomberg described him as "ramping up his engagement with federal politics again." [cite:de9_mj_2025] [cite:de9_bgov_cfg] On 29 December 2025 he gave $3 million to the California Business Roundtable to fight a proposed one-time 5 percent tax on the wealth of California's billionaires. [cite:de9_fortune_cbrt] He had moved his voter registration to Florida in March 2024, and two days after that donation his investment firm opened an office in Miami. [cite:de9_bl_miami]

Argentina, 2024–2026

Thiel first visited Argentina's presidential palace, the Casa Rosada, in May 2024, accompanied by the businessman Alec Oxenford, later Argentina's ambassador in Washington. On 23 April 2026 President Javier Milei received him there with the foreign minister, Pablo Quirno, and two associates from Thiel Capital and Founders Fund. Milei called it "a wonderful meeting," described Thiel as "an anarcho-capitalist, just as I am philosophically," said both men regard "taxes as theft," and said no specific investments were discussed. [cite:de9_bat_milei] Several outlets reported that Thiel had bought a mansion in the Palermo Chico district of Buenos Aires for about $12 million. [cite:de9_bat_milei]

On 28 May 2026 the New York Times reported that the government was considering offering him permanent residence or citizenship; a spokesperson for Milei denied it. [cite:de9_nn_nyt] In July Fortune reported that he had moved to Buenos Aires with his family earlier that summer, and that Argentina's planned citizenship-by-investment scheme was not yet in operation. [cite:de9_fortune_ba] In late August a committee hearing in the Chamber of Deputies examined his presence and his ties to the government; he was invited and did not appear. Testimony noted that Palantir had registered with Argentina's industrial-property office in 2022 and had shown interest in digitising the archives of the 1994 AMIA bombing, but no Palantir contract with Argentina was reported. [cite:de9_bat_congress] As of October 2026 no grant of Argentine residence or citizenship to Thiel is documented. Milei's own programme and the exit zones around it are in [scene:de22_argentina_and_freedom_cities|Part Twenty-Two].

In September and October 2025, between these visits, Thiel gave four private, off-the-record lectures on the Antichrist at the Commonwealth Club in San Francisco, organised by the Acts 17 Collective and drawing on Girard. [cite:de9_fortune_antichrist] Their content, reported from recordings, belongs to [scene:de10_girard_and_the_katechon|Part Ten].

Read together, the two tracks are less contradictory than they look. In 2009 Thiel wrote that the libertarian's task was to escape politics; from 2016 he was inside it, as a speaker, a transition official and the largest backer of two Senate candidates. In 2026 he was again moving his life beyond the reach of a government that proposed to tax him, this time to a country whose president shares his description of taxation. The record shows one man using both levers, exit and voice, as circumstances changed. It does not show a plan to combine them, and his own explanations, where he has given them, are recorded beside each step.
