# Running the Network: Operations, the DOJ Decision Ledger, the Surveillance Question and the Family Files
### Research file v1.0 for *The Epstein Architecture* · status as of 30 October 2026

Ron's brief: *"Map the architecture for running the network itself… look at it like a narcos cartel. It takes more than one guy. If the main guy gets taken down, someone else steps up."* This file applies the protocol's institution lens to the operation: who held which job, whether each person was ever questioned, and what happened to the structure after 2019. **One rule for this file.** A person who was a minor during the events is never named, even if they later spoke publicly. Several recruiters in the Palm Beach police file were themselves 14–17.

## Part B · The cartel test: was the network dismantled, or did someone step up?

The protocol's pattern step: name the mechanism, not the actor. Require two or more precedents. Test the claim on three separate layers.

**The mechanism: a delegated operation behind an immunity shield.** The operation had distinct functions held by distinct people. A single agreement (2008) legally protected the four named staff plus an open class of unnamed "potential co-conspirators". Precedents for this shape: modelling-agency pipelines with documented abuse (Brunel and MC2; Gérald Marie, whose 2023 French case was closed as time-barred) and the Belgian Dutroux case. More are to be added.

| Layer | What happened after 2019 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | 1 convicted (Maxwell). 3 dead (Epstein, Brunel, Siad). 4 immunised staff, never charged. Unnamed co-conspirators never identified. DOJ's July 2025 memo: no basis to investigate uncharged third parties. | **Never dismantled. This is documented.** The human infrastructure was not prosecuted. |
| **Money** | The estate ($577M, revised to $634.8M) is run by the two men who ran his finances. It has paid out hundreds of millions in victim settlements. Its largest remaining asset is the Valar stake (about $170M). | **Continued under the same hands as an estate.** No document shows it funding new activity. |
| **Mechanism** | Paris: two probes, about 20 victims heard, **no one questioned as of May**. New Mexico: active. The modelling-scout route was still producing complaints in 2026. | **Not established either way.** Open in France and New Mexico. |

**What the book can say.** "The network was never dismantled" is documented. "The network is still operating" is not established. The book states what evidence would establish it:
- a post-2019 payment or transaction;
- a named recruiter still active;
- a property still in use;
- a sworn account of post-2019 abuse.

Those are the leads to chase. Ron's instinct that "it hasn't been shut down" becomes a precise, checkable claim. It is not dropped and it is not inflated.

## Part D · The surveillance question: "If they wanted to, they could spy on them. Why haven't they?"

**What Snowden documented (2013):**
- bulk collection of domestic phone records (Section 215; ended by the USA FREEDOM Act in 2015);
- PRISM and upstream collection under Section 702, aimed at foreign targets, with "incidental" collection of Americans;
- raw intelligence shared with Israel's Unit 8200 under a memorandum.

The capability is real and documented (epx_s06, epx_s08).

**Why capability isn't the bottleneck.**
- **What the law requires:** to listen to an American's calls for a crime, the government needs a predicated investigation and a judge's warrant on probable cause (Title III), not intelligence tools.
- **What it already has:** the evidence. The 2019 safe, the devices and, by the FBI's own count, terabytes of seized material. Nobody needs to spy. They need to open a case.
- **Where the gate is:** the July 2025 memo states DOJ found **no predicate to investigate uncharged third parties**. The decision point is *predication*, not surveillance.
- **The book's answer to "why haven't they?":** the agencies that collect everything on ordinary people are not barred from this case by law. They chose not to open it, and that choice is documented.

**Why a speeding ticket feels heavier.** Trend data are cited as such:
- White-collar prosecutions: nearly 11,000 in 1995, fewer than 4,000 in 2025 (TRAC, as reported).
- FBI white-collar agents: about 1,700 in 2001, about 600 by 2008.

Low-level enforcement is automated (cameras, fines) and needs no investigator to decide anything. Elite cases need someone to open a file. **That is the asymmetry: enforcement that runs on its own at the bottom, and enforcement that depends on someone's decision at the top.**

**Guardrail.** The book argues for predicated investigation and transparency, not for surveilling people without a warrant. Warrantless spying on "people of interest" is the same power the book criticises when it is aimed at everyone else.
