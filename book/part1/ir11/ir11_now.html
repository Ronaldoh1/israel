From Ceasefire to Blockade, April–October 2026

The war did not end; it changed shape. After forty days of bombing came a ceasefire, a failed round of talks and an American naval blockade; then a signed memorandum that promised a final settlement within sixty days; then the memorandum's collapse in July and a second blockade that, by October, had cut Iran's oil exports to almost nothing. This section follows those turns, gives each government's stated position at each one, and sets the stated aims of February against what had happened by October 2026. Because the war was not formally over when this was written, every status below is given as of the date stated.

The April ceasefire and the first blockade

A two-week ceasefire mediated by Pakistan began on 7–8 April and was later extended. [cite:ow7_wiki_ceasefire2026] [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026] Talks between American and Iranian officials over the following weekend broke down, and on 12 April Trump announced that the US Navy would block all ships entering or leaving the Strait of Hormuz; Central Command narrowed the order to vessels travelling to and from Iranian ports, effective 13 April. Iran was by then letting only a limited number of vetted ships through: 17 crossed on 11 April, against about 130 a day before the war. Brent crude, which had topped $119 a barrel in March, went back above $103. [cite:ir11_aj_blockade] The truce held between the United States and Iran, but Israel said it did not cover Lebanon, where its war against Hezbollah went on until a separate ten-day truce from 16 April. [cite:hz8b_reu_apr8] [cite:hz8b_aj_yellow] [scene:hz8_war2026|The 2026 war in Lebanon]

The nuclear material that had been the war's first stated target was still unaccounted for. On 29 April the IAEA's director general, Rafael Grossi, said Iran's near weapons-grade uranium, thought to be buried at sites the United States had bombed, "is accessible if there's a wish to go there"; his inspectors had not been to those sites in ten months. [cite:ir11_bbg_grossi]

The Islamabad Memorandum

On 17 June the American authorities released the text of a memorandum of understanding between the United States and Iran, negotiated through Pakistan. It declared an "immediate and permanent" end to military operations on all fronts, Lebanon included. The United States would begin lifting its blockade at once and end it within thirty days; Iran would restore commercial shipping through Hormuz within thirty days, allowing time to clear mines. Iran reaffirmed that it would not acquire nuclear weapons; its enriched stockpile would be diluted on site under IAEA supervision; enrichment itself was left to later talks. The United States would grant waivers for Iranian oil exports on signing, free frozen Iranian funds, lift sanctions on a timetable to be set later, and join regional partners in a reconstruction plan of at least $300 billion. A final agreement was due within sixty days, to be ratified by a binding Security Council resolution. [cite:ir11_sole_mou] A ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah followed on 19 June, brokered, a US official said, with Iran's help under the interim deal. [cite:hz8b_olj_jun19]

The memorandum's nuclear clauses were read differently in each capital. Grossi said on 24 June that his inspectors would visit the enrichment sites: "This is going to happen." Iran's deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, said such issues would be decided "only within the framework of a final agreement," after sanctions ended. Iran said it was not enriching uranium, and the IAEA said the same, but without access to the bombed sites the agency could not verify the stockpile. [cite:ir11_ap_grossi]

The collapse, 7–8 July

The memorandum lasted three weeks. Iran told ships to use its own "safe route" close to its coast, marking part of Omani waters as a restricted zone. On 7 July three tankers were hit by projectiles off the Omani coast; Central Command blamed Iran, and local television reported that the ships had ignored warnings from Iranian forces to change course. The US Treasury reimposed oil sanctions that day. Early on 8 July American forces struck more than 80 targets in Iran, including air defences, coastal radars, anti-ship missiles and more than 60 Revolutionary Guard boats, and the Guards fired back at targets in Bahrain and Kuwait. Trump, at a NATO summit in Ankara, said the memorandum was "over." [cite:ir11_aj_jul8] [cite:ir11a_aj_jul8]

Each side said the other broke it first. The American account was that Iran had attacked commercial shipping. Iran's foreign ministry called the American strikes a "flagrant violation of Paragraph 1" of the memorandum, and the parliament speaker, Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf, Iran's negotiator, cited the reimposed oil sanctions and what he called the violation of "Iranian adjustments in the Strait." [cite:ir11_aj_jul8] The memorandum required Iran to ensure "unimpeded" transit and the United States to grant oil waivers; the record shows each government acting against one of those clauses within days of the other, and it does not settle which act came first in intent. [cite:ir11_sole_mou]

The second blockade and the war at sea

The American blockade of Iran's ports was reinstated on 14 July. [cite:hz12a_cbs_oct] Its effect on Iran's oil trade was close to total. The tanker-tracking firm Kpler counted Iranian loadings of about 740,000 barrels a day in July and about 260,000 in August, against 1.7 million in August 2025; TankerTrackers.com put the fall at 100 per cent against January and February. Mohsen Rezaei, the new secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, warned that "economic warfare will be met by a maritime exclusion zone." [cite:ir11_oilprice_exports] By 13 October Central Command said four vessels had been disabled and 135 turned back since the blockade took effect, while Iran was attacking ships in the strait; no direct exchange of fire between American and Iranian forces was reported in early October. [cite:hz12a_cbs_oct]

The bomb question after two wars

Two wars fought in the name of stopping an Iranian bomb left the world knowing less about Iran's nuclear material than before them. On 7 September Grossi told the IAEA Board that the agency had had no access to the facilities hit in June 2025 and had "lost continuity of knowledge in relation to the previously declared inventories" of enriched uranium, and he urged Iran to meet its obligations under its safeguards agreement. [cite:ir11_iaea_sep7] He called it a proliferation concern that "needs to be rectified with the utmost urgency"; Iran's foreign ministry spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, blamed the American and Israeli attacks for the lack of access. [cite:ir11_aj_iaea_sep8] On 9 September the Board voted 23 to 3, with eight abstentions, to report Iran to the Security Council for "continued non-compliance," the first such referral in twenty years; Russia, China and Niger voted against, and Iran's representative, Reza Najafi, called it "completely counterproductive." [cite:ir11_am_referral] Iran's parliament had debated leaving the Non-Proliferation Treaty since March, but as of the end of September no formal notice of withdrawal had been reported, and searches for this book found none by mid-October. [cite:ir4_aca_npt2026] [cite:ir4_k24_npt] Iran remained, as of October 2026, a party to the treaty that Israel has never joined. [scene:ir4_dimona|Israel's undeclared arsenal]

Oil and prices

The closure of Hormuz, through which much of the world's oil and gas passes, was the war's widest economic effect. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] The 32 members of the International Energy Agency released 400 million barrels from reserves. [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026] In its October outlook the US Energy Information Administration assumed that Middle East oil flows would stay constrained through the end of 2026, forecast Brent at $105 a barrel in the fourth quarter, and reported that American gasoline had averaged $4.35 a gallon in September, against about $3 before 28 February. [cite:ir11_eia_oct] [cite:hz12_almon_ipsos]

Where it stands, October 2026

In late September Iran offered to reopen the strait within a week if the United States lifted its blockade, released frozen Iranian assets and waived sanctions on Iranian oil. Trump rejected it, saying Iran wanted a deal because it was "losing so badly." Iran received an American counter-proposal at the start of October, and President Pezeshkian said, "We will make every effort to bring the agreement to fruition." [cite:ir11_egt_proposal] On 8 October Trump wrote that the United States "will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections" of 3 November, and that his team was "having productive discussions" with Iran; Araghchi said a formal reply would follow within days. [cite:ir11_ap_midterms] Trump had said in September that the conflict "is going to end immediately after the elections" and had told Time that more bombing after the vote was "possible"; Al Jazeera reported that a new deployment brought American forces in the region to about 20,000, with at least three carrier groups, a lower figure than the more than 50,000 the New York Times had reported in March. [cite:ir11_aj_midterms] [cite:ir13_nyt_50k] Central Command put the American cost at $43.6 billion as of 3 September. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] As of 19 October 2026 no agreement had ended the war, the American blockade and Iran's restrictions on shipping through the strait remained in place, and the latest reporting reviewed showed no direct exchange of fire between American and Iranian forces. [cite:hz12a_cbs_oct] [cite:ir11_eia_oct]
