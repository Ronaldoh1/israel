Suriname: Approved, Then Withdrawn, 1947–1948

The last territorial plan came after the war, from the heirs of the territorialists, and for a year it had the approval of a European government.

In April 1947 the Freeland League, the successor to the territorialists, reached a preliminary agreement in Paramaribo; on 27 June 1947 Suriname's Legislative Council voted to accept a plan to settle 30,000 homeless European Jews in an undeveloped area, and the Netherlands government approved it on 3 July. [cite:a_jta_sur47]

The terms were disputed: an American Zionist reported in April 1948 that only about 200 skilled workers had been agreed as a first group, on 75-year leases rather than ownership; the League called this "an absolute distortion", citing the Dutch delegate at the UN and the governor's own letter. [cite:a_jta_sur48a]

On 14 August 1948, three months after Israel's founding, the governor suspended the talks "in the light of the present international situation". [cite:a_jta_sur48b]

The likely path. At most 30,000 settlers in an autonomous community under Dutch rule, on leased land. Approved by a government, then withdrawn once Israel existed.

The cost. Peaceful on the record; who lived in the area it targeted is not documented here and would have had to be established.
