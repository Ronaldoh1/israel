Part Thirteen asks for whom the promise has been kept. It sets the documented record of four groups side by side on mobility, income, wealth, homeownership, education and health, and follows each gap to the policies and conditions the evidence ties it to, together with the disputes among scholars over how much each cause explains. It begins with Black Americans, where the federal record of the 1930s and 1940s is most fully documented and most argued over; turns to immigrants and Latino Americans, whose children's climb is some of the strongest evidence for the taught version, and to the taxes undocumented workers pay into programmes they cannot use; and ends with Asian Americans, whose high average conceals the widest spread of any group, and Native Americans, whose record runs through allotment, trust land and the Cobell settlement.
