Part Nine followed the files. For a decade the record was built by victims' lawyers, one Miami Herald reporter and judges asked to unseal it. The 2019 arrest found labelled discs in a safe, and the death in custody, ruled a suicide amid documented failures by the jail, ended the trial that would have tested the evidence in public. Congress then ordered the release by 427 votes to 1, in a law with no way to enforce it. The department blacked out hundreds of pages, exposed the names of dozens of victims, pulled a photograph of the president and put it back, and still holds about half the records. The Attorney General who ran the release announced a client list that did not exist, would not turn to face the survivors behind her, and when questioned at length did so under no oath. A court has now ordered more, and the department is appealing.

Part Ten asks the question that follows: with Epstein dead and his partner in prison, did the network he brokered shut down, or did the structure that served it simply carry on?
