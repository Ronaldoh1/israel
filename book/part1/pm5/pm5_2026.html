2026: More Money, Different Names, Mixed Results

The 2026 cycle brought the largest sums yet and the most losses. By June the super PAC had raised about $104 million for the cycle, more than in the whole of 2024. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] It also changed how it spent. Politico reported that more than 40 percent of its primary spending in 2026 went through pop-up and pass-through committees, groups with names unconnected to Israel that received its money and spent it under their own. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] And for the first time it faced an organized opponent: a super PAC formed specifically to counter it. This section follows the year's main races in the order they were held, then sets the whole cycle out in a single ledger. Because the cycle was still running when this volume was written, every figure carries the date of the filing it comes from.

New Jersey, February. The year began with a result that cut both ways. In a special Democratic primary for New Jersey's 11th District, the super PAC spent more than $2.3 million against Tom Malinowski, with advertisements saying he had "voted with Trump and the Republicans to fund ICE." Malinowski lost by 889 votes in an eleven-candidate field to Analilia Mejia, who has said she believes Israel committed genocide in Gaza. [cite:pm_njmonitor_malinowski] The super PAC had helped defeat a candidate it opposed and, in doing so, helped elect one far more critical of Israel. AIPAC's spokesperson answered the result with a promise: "Our six million members will be very active this election cycle." [cite:pm_njmonitor_malinowski]

Illinois, March. On 17 March four open House seats in and around Chicago held their primaries. AIPAC-linked committees spent more than $21 million across the four, most of it through newly formed committees with names such as Affordable Chicago Now and Elect Chicago Women rather than in AIPAC's own name; in the 7th District the super PAC spent about $5 million directly for Melissa Conyears-Ervin. [cite:pm_prospect_illinois] [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] Its candidates won two of the four seats, Donna Miller in the 2nd and Melissa Bean in the 8th, and lost two: Conyears-Ervin in the 7th and Laura Fine in the 9th, where Daniel Biss won. [cite:pm_prospect_illinois] In all, about $92 million of outside money was spent on the four House races and the state's Senate primary. [cite:pm_prospect_illinois]

Kentucky, May. The defeat of Thomas Massie, told in the previous section, was the super PAC's largest success of the spring, achieved alongside the president. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways]

Maryland, June. In Maryland's 5th District the super PAC spent over $1 million for Adrian Boafo, who won the primary on 23 June. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026]

Michigan, August. The largest single campaign in the super PAC's history was fought in the Democratic primary for an open Senate seat in Michigan. It spent more than $30.6 million supporting Representative Haley Stevens and opposing Abdul El-Sayed. [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] El-Sayed won on 4 August. AIPAC responded that its members "remain determined to ensure that voters reject Dr. El-Sayed and his radical anti-Israel agenda in November." [cite:pm_itt_elsayed] The race was the clearest single test of whether money on this scale could carry a statewide Democratic primary, and it could not.

California, August. Two weeks later, in a special election runoff for California's 14th District, the super PAC spent $2.4 million for Melissa Hernandez and gave more than $1.5 million to another committee, BOLD America, which spent over $2 million helping her. Aisha Wahab, a progressive state senator, won by 53 percent to 47. [cite:pm_kqed_wahab] [cite:pm_aljaz_wahab]

The opposition. The cycle also produced the first super PAC organized against AIPAC's. American Priorities filed with the Federal Election Commission on 12 February 2026, pledged more than $10 million, and raised $3.85 million in its first quarter. It spent about $1 million for Nida Allam in North Carolina's 4th District, where she lost to Valerie Foushee by less than a percentage point; about $2 million for Adam Hamawy in New Jersey's 12th, who won; more than $400,000 for Chris Rabb in Pennsylvania's 3rd, who won; and $2 million for three New York candidates in June, two of whom, according to the reference summary used here, defeated AIPAC-endorsed incumbents, Representatives Dan Goldman and Adriano Espaillat. [cite:pm_wiki_ampri] Its sums were a small fraction of the super PAC's, and in Illinois it chose not to spend at all.

The ledger, read plainly. In the nine races in the table the super PAC's side won five and lost four; measured by money, its largest bet of the cycle, more than $30 million in Michigan, was a loss. That is a much weaker record than AIPAC's claims for 2024, and it is consistent with the claim, made by the super PAC's opponents, that by 2026 the money had itself become an issue in Democratic primaries. It does not show that the spending had no effect: several of the results were close, and the filings cannot show what the margins would have been without it. Nor is the cycle over. The general elections of November 2026, the primaries not covered here and the final filings will change the record, and the conclusion of this volume returns to it with the latest figures available.
