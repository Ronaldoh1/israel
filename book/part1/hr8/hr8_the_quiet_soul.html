The Quiet Soul

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The Quietists taught a stillness before God. Molinos taught that Christian perfection comes through contemplation, with divine help; Madame Guyon taught "an extreme passivity and indifference of the soul", in which a person became an agent of God. [cite:hr_brit_molinos] [cite:hr_brit_guyon] Bishop Bossuet, her chief opponent, did not deny that such states existed; he argued that they were rarely achievable and doubted she had experienced them. [cite:hr_brit_guyon]

The inner reading takes Quietism as the practical end of every inner path on this shelf. If the self is emptied, as Porete taught, and the self that fills all space withdraws, as Luria's God did, then what remains is a quiet in which another can act. [cite:hr_wiki_porete] [cite:kb_brit_luria] The worship "as if you are seeing Him" of the hadith of Gabriel, and the Sufi polishing of the heart, are other names for the same quiet. [cite:sf_wiki_gabriel] [cite:sf_aa_mirror]

Bossuet's objection belongs beside the reading, because it is a fair one. A teaching of passivity can be misused: a person can call their own inertia, or their own will, the action of God. Bossuet's doubt that the states were often reached is the same caution the Talmud expressed in the story of the four who entered the orchard, of whom only one came out unharmed. [cite:hr_brit_guyon] [cite:kb_mjl_danger] The reading does not set the warning aside. It notes only that the Church's answer to the risk was a prison cell for Molinos and the Bastille for Guyon, and that this is the answer this whole shelf has documented: when someone claims that the soul can rest in God without the office, the office locks the door.

The record limits the reading. Quietism was condemned for specific propositions within a specific theological debate, and its teachers and opponents were all Catholics arguing about the life of prayer. The universal reading is the reader's.
