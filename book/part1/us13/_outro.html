Part Thirteen established that the two systems set their wage floors on opposite principles: America's federal minimum has stood at $7.25 since 24 July 2009 and by 2023 had lost 42 per cent of its 1968 buying power, leaving thirty states to set higher floors, while Israel's, pegged by law to 47.5 per cent of the average wage, rose from NIS 2,348.88 a month in 1997 to NIS 6,443.85 in April 2026, though it was held at NIS 5,300 from 2017 to 2022. [cite:us13_dol_minwage] [cite:us13_epi_history] [cite:us13_bp_states2026] [cite:us13_nii_minwage] [cite:us13_kz_minwagelaw] Israel works more hours a year than the United States, though the gap has narrowed since 2000; American law guarantees no paid vacation and Israel's only twelve days; and both countries' gender pay gaps are well above the OECD average, Israel's on a monthly measure that is not directly comparable. [cite:us13_wiki_hours] [cite:us13_dol_vacation] [cite:us13_toi_vacation] [cite:us13_oecd_lmf15] [cite:us13_jpost_shoresh] Organised labour shrank in both: the Histadrut lost most of its members when the 1995 health law cut the link with Clalit, while American membership fell from 20.1 per cent in 1983 to 10.0 per cent in 2025, 5.9 per cent in the private sector. [cite:us13_shalev_labor] [cite:us13_wiki_histadrut] [cite:us13_bls_union2025] West Bank Palestinians working in Israel and the settlements fell from about 172,000 before October 2023 to about 51,000 at the end of 2025, as Israel turned to recruited foreign workers, while the United States relies on about 8.3 million unauthorised workers.
