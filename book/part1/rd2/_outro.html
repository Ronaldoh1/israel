By the end of 1922 the architecture of the Mandate stood complete. A letter written in 1917, about land Britain did not yet hold, had been carried through San Remo into the League of Nations Mandate, approved on July 24, 1922, and endorsed by the United States in Public Resolution 67-73. [cite:mandate_text] [cite:wiki_lodge_fish] A High Commissioner governed from Jerusalem. The administration that would soon issue Palestinian passports, currency and stamps had begun its work.

The Zionist movement had built durable instruments of its own: Keren Hayesod, a permanent fund; an organized American fundraising campaign; and the Haganah, a defense organization. [cite:keren_hayesod_wiki] The Palestinian national movement had organized too, through Muslim–Christian Associations, national congresses and an Arab Executive that Britain never recognized. [cite:wiki_palestine_arab_congress]

The record of these years also held the objections: Faisal's dropped condition, the shelved King–Crane report, the unpublished Palin inquiry, the House of Lords vote of 60 to 29. And Britain had already narrowed its own promise. It capped immigration by the country's absorptive capacity, and it separated Transjordan, roughly three-quarters of the Mandate's land. [cite:wiki_churchill_wp_1922] The contradiction at the heart of the Mandate was now built into the machinery that governed it.
