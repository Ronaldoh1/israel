Speech, Press, Protest and Civil Society

Elections show who governs. Whether people can find out what the government is doing, say what they think of it and organise against it depends on another set of laws: on the press, on boycotts, on protest and on the groups that make up civil society. Both systems narrowed some of these freedoms between 2023 and 2026, by different means. Israel did so mainly through legislation and wartime controls; the United States mainly through executive action, which courts partly reversed.

The press

Reporters Without Borders (RSF) ranks 180 countries each year; its method changed in 2022, so only recent years are compared here. In the 2026 index Israel fell from 112th to 116th, its score dropping from 51.06 to 46.46, and the United States fell seven places to 64th, with a score of 62.61. [cite:us3_rsf_il] [cite:us3_wboc_rsf] RSF described the United States as "slipping due to growing political pressure". [cite:us3_rsf_regions] Freedom House gave Israel 3 of 4 for free media and recorded the arrest of a journalist, Israel Frey, over a social-media post. [cite:us3_fh_il2026]

Israel's media rules have three features the American system lacks. First, a military censor: reporting on a range of security subjects needs prior approval. [cite:us3_rsf_il] Second, a law passed in April 2024 that lets the government shut foreign broadcasters it deems a threat to state security; Al Jazeera's local operations were closed in May 2024, and in December 2025 the law was extended to 2027, usable outside wartime and with less judicial oversight. [cite:us3_fh_il2025] [cite:pr_aj_ajlaw] Third, foreign journalists have been barred from entering Gaza independently since October 2023, a ban the Supreme Court upheld in January 2024 and that was still in force in October 2026. [cite:pr_mm_fpa2024] [cite:pr_wlrn_ban_full] RSF counted more than 220 Palestinian journalists killed by the Israeli army in Gaza since 7 October 2023, at least 70 of them because of their work; the Committee to Protect Journalists counted 189 Palestinian journalists killed in Gaza as of 24 September 2026. Israel says that some of those killed were Hamas members; NPR reported that it often provides no evidence for this. [cite:us3_rsf_regions] [cite:gz_cpj_2026] [cite:gz_wlrn_ban] The Gaza volume covers the access ban and the journalists killed. [scene:gz12_access|Gaza, Documented: access]

Ownership and government pressure affect both. RSF found most Israeli outlets owned by "large corporations or magnates who are difficult to investigate", noted that the right-wing Channel 14 had become the second most watched channel by 2023, and reported that politicians have significant influence over appointments to the broadcast regulators. In 2024 the communications minister proposed privatising or even closing the public radio service and called for a boycott of the daily Haaretz; in November 2024 the government halted state advertising in it. [cite:us3_rsf_il] [cite:us3_fh_il2025] In the United States, Freedom House recorded in 2025 that the chairman of the Federal Communications Commission threatened the licences of broadcasters he objected to, that some media companies settled lawsuits brought by the president or made concessions, that the Associated Press was barred from presidential events for refusing the administration's renaming of the Gulf of Mexico, that Congress defunded the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, which then closed, and that physical assaults on journalists rose to 188. [cite:us3_fh_us2026] After Skydance's merger with Paramount, approved with commitments on "viewpoint diversity" at CBS, Paramount bought The Free Press in October 2025 and made Bari Weiss editor-in-chief of CBS News. [cite:gz_aj_weiss] [scene:gz12_coverage|Gaza, Documented: coverage in the US]

Boycotts and campus speech

Both countries legislate against boycotts of Israel, in different ways. Israel's Boycott Law of 2011 makes it a civil wrong to call publicly for an economic, cultural or academic boycott of Israel, its institutions or "an area in its control", which includes the settlements, and allows the state to deny boycott supporters tenders and benefits. On 15 April 2015 a nine-justice panel of the Supreme Court upheld the core of the law by 5 to 4, struck down unanimously the clause allowing damages without proof of harm, and held that such calls "do not fall under the classic purposes of free speech". [cite:us3_versa_avneri] A 2017 law bars entry to foreigners who publicly support a boycott of Israel or the settlements. [cite:us3_fh_il2025]

American federal law since 1977 bars American companies from complying with boycotts imposed by foreign governments, a measure aimed at the Arab League boycott of Israel; it regulates commercial compliance, not individuals' speech. [cite:ow11_bis_oac] Thirty-eight states had by 2024 adopted laws or orders against boycotts of Israel, some requiring state contractors to certify they do not boycott, others requiring public funds to divest from companies that do. Courts split: federal judges blocked the laws in Kansas and Arizona, which were then amended, and found Texas's unconstitutional in 2019, while the Eighth Circuit upheld Arkansas's law in 2022 and the Supreme Court declined to hear the case. No federal anti-boycott law against individuals has passed. [cite:pm_wiki_antibds] [cite:pm_aclu_waldrip] [scene:pm8_laws|Follow the Money: state anti-boycott laws]

On campuses, a 2019 executive order directed agencies enforcing civil-rights law to consider the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance's definition of antisemitism, while saying they "shall not diminish or infringe upon" First Amendment rights; a January 2025 order added steps toward monitoring and removing foreign students and staff. [cite:ow11_eo13899] [cite:ow11_eo14188] Freedom House reported that the government revoked hundreds of visas after social-media vetting, some over nonviolent speech about the Israel–Hamas war, and that a federal court ruled in September 2025 that the administration had unfairly targeted non-citizens for their speech; the government said it would appeal. [cite:us3_fh_us2026] [scene:ow11_boycott|The One-Way Mirror: boycott and speech] In Israel, Freedom House recorded the arrest, twice, of a Tel Aviv University lecturer over posts calling for "rebellion" against the government. [cite:us3_fh_il2026] The Israel Democracy Institute found in 2025 that 53 per cent of Jewish and 70 per cent of Arab respondents preferred to keep their political views to themselves among strangers, a share that has risen since 2016–17. [cite:us3_idi_2025]

Protest

Protest is common and mostly peaceful in both countries. In Israel, Freedom House found demonstrations widely permitted and detained protesters generally released by the courts, but recorded that police sometimes used excessive force and that the authorities disallowed some protests, mostly by Arab citizens calling for a ceasefire. [cite:us3_fh_il2025] In the United States, the "No Kings" demonstrations drew an estimated five million people in June 2025; there were clashes with police and federal agents at protests against immigration raids; the National Guard was deployed in Los Angeles without the governor's consent, and plans for Chicago and Portland were dropped in late December 2025 after court rulings; some states added restrictions on protest. [cite:us3_fh_us2026] [cite:us3a_fh_us2026] Policing is covered in Part Four. [scene:us4_policing|Part Four: policing]

NGOs and foreign funding

Israel has legislated specifically on foreign-government money to its non-governmental organisations (NGOs). A 2012 law requires NGOs to report foreign-government support every quarter, and a 2016 law requires NGOs that receive most of their funding from foreign governments to say so publicly; Freedom House notes it falls mainly on left-leaning groups critical of policy toward Palestinians. [cite:us3_fh_il2025] A further bill, sponsored by Likud MK Ariel Kallner, would tax foreign-government donations to NGOs that receive no Israeli state funding at 80 per cent and let courts disregard petitions from groups "primarily financed by a foreign political entity". The ministerial committee backed it on 16 February 2025; the Association for Civil Rights in Israel called it "a direct assault on Israel's democracy" and argued that it targets funding from allied governments while leaving untouched right-wing groups that rely on private donations, and Kallner said it would "block improper foreign interference". [cite:us3_toi_ngotax] [cite:us3_tml_ngotax] In May 2025 it was still in committee. [cite:us3_jpost_ngotax] This book could not confirm whether it advanced further before the October 2026 election. Freedom House gives Israel 2 of 4 for NGO freedom. [cite:us3_fh_il2026]

The United States has no comparable law on foreign funding of domestic NGOs; FARA covers agents of foreign principals, not charities that receive grants. [cite:ow2_doj_faq] Pressure on American NGOs in 2025 came through the executive: federal funding freezes and grant terminations forced many groups to suspend work, an order designating "antifa" a domestic terrorist organisation was followed by instructions to investigate NGOs with foreign ties, and officials signalled interest in investigating the Open Society Foundations and the Ford Foundation. [cite:us3_fh_us2026]

The comparison does not resolve into a single ranking. Israel's restrictions are written into statute and apply even when courts trim them; America's in 2025 came mainly by executive action, against a constitutional text that courts used to block some of it. Both fell in RSF's 2026 index, and V-Dem rates both lower than before 2023. [cite:us3_rsf_il] [cite:us3_wboc_rsf] [cite:us3_vdem2026]
