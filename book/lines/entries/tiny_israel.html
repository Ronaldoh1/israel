The underdog picture fits only the opening weeks of the 1948 war; after that, the balance reversed. From the Arab armies' invasion on May 15 until the first truce in June 1948, Israeli forces are described as initially inferior in numbers and armament. A contemporaneous US government assessment dated July 1, 1948 put Israeli forces at roughly 90,000, about 35,000 of them used for active operations, and found the Arab armies stronger in artillery and tanks at that point. [cite:frus1948_military_situation]

After the truce, new enlistments and arms, including Czech shipments, let Israel seize the initiative; one historical anthology concludes that for most of the war "the Jews were superior in personnel, equipment, logistics and organization." [cite:jvl_israel_studies_1948] By the later stages, Israel's army outnumbered the combined Arab armies by roughly 2 to 1. Many of the popular "outnumbered" figures trace back to John Glubb, commander of Jordan's Arab Legion, who historians note had reason to exaggerate Israeli numbers.
