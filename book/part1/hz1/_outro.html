Part One set out what came before Hezbollah. Lebanon's 1943 settlement divided power by religious community on a 1932 census and left the Shia of the south and the Bekaa on the political and economic margins. [cite:hz1_wiki_pact] [cite:hz1_mrg_shia] Musa al-Sadr organised them in the Movement of the Deprived in 1974 and armed them in Amal in 1975, years before Iran's revolution and Israel's occupation; he disappeared in Libya on 31 August 1978, and no court has established what happened to him. [cite:hz1_brit_amal] [cite:hz1_olj_sadr] The Cairo Agreement of 1969 and the PLO's expulsion from Jordan made the south a base for attacks on Israel, including the killing of Israeli children at Kiryat Shmona and Ma'alot, and a target of Israeli reprisals that the Security Council condemned. [cite:hz1_cairo_text] [cite:hz1_wiki_1974] [cite:hz1_unscr262] Israel's 1978 invasion killed, by different estimates, between 1,100 and about 2,000 people in Lebanon; the Security Council ordered it out and created UNIFIL, and Israel handed its positions to Saad Haddad's militia, which it armed and paid. [cite:hz1_wiki_litani] [cite:hz1_unifil_bg] [cite:hz1_csm_haddad] In 1979 Iran's new constitution committed it to the continuation of its revolution abroad.
