Part Ten turns to the people who are still reporting, and what it costs them to keep going. A journalist in Gaza needs electricity to charge a camera, an internet connection to file, fuel to reach a story, and a roof to work under; for most of the war they had little of any of it. This part sets out the conditions recorded by press-freedom groups, before and after the ceasefire; the money: the price of fuel and data, and the freelancers working without contracts; and what the organizations that support them have done, including taking Israel to court.
