## cw4_french_nuclear_deal_1955_1960
- **Agree:** the 1957 package was a reactor plus a reprocessing plant; Peres negotiated it; Bourgès-Maunoury signed after his government fell; construction was secret; de Gaulle forced a review in 1960 and a compromise followed.
- **Disagree:** the signing date (3 or 30 October in the Burr–Cohen book; 1 October backdated to 30 September in the CIE and Peres accounts). Whether the 1960 compromise changed anything in practice (FAS: plutonium plant halted; JVL: little changed).
- **Weak or single-source:** Goldschmidt's recollections (a 1993 interview; Cohen says he often claimed not to remember); The National Interest's "three contracts" and 385 tonnes of uranium; the Nuclear Weapon Archive (1997, unsigned) for the 7 November 1956 meeting and the November 1960 date of the compromise.
- **Not verified:** the June 1960 Ben-Gurion–de Gaulle meeting date; Saint-Gobain's role; the EL-102 designation.

## cw4_dimona_discovery_1958_1960
- **Agree (documents):** the AMF report and Despatch 75 (2 August 1960) are the earliest declassified references; Gomberg's December debriefing; the 2 December JAEIC report; SNIE 100-8-60 of 8 December.
- **Disagree:** whether U-2 photography identified Dimona in 1958 (FAS, JVL, Nuclear Weapon Archive) or whether the photographs were not telling and the government did not know until 1960 (Burr and Cohen, and the 1961 post-mortem).
- **Inconsistent within one source:** the "textile plant" remark is dated September 1960 and February 1960 in the same briefing book.
- **Not verified:** Lundahl's and Brugioni's role; the Daily Express and New York Times stories of mid-December 1960 (the Nuclear Weapon Archive says the NYT made it public on 16 December; not used as a claim).

## cw4_dimona_december_1960
- **Agree:** the sequence of 7–31 December 1960 and the officials' words, all from documents.
- **Disagree:** the Knesset statement's date (21 vs 22 December 1960). Whether the Israeli account was true (Israel's statements vs US doubts at the time and Cohen and Burr's later judgment).
- **Cost figures differ and must stay attributed:** Herter $40–80 million (8 December); Eisenhower $100–200 million (19 December); embassy $17.8 million in foreign exchange plus IL29.2 million (28 December); scientists in May 1961 about $15 million plus $20 million.
- **Not verified:** the full Hebrew text of the Knesset statement; an MK's 2000 citation of it as 20 December (secondhand, not used).

## cw4_kennedy_dimona_visit_1961
- **Agree:** the January memoranda, the April invitation, the scientists' names and findings.
- **Disagree:** dates of the visit (18 May per FRUS editors; arrival 17 and Dimona 20 May per the National Security Archive; 17–22 May per the Wilson Center summary). What the visit saw (the scientists' report vs Cohen and Burr's staging argument).
- **Reported only:** Meir's objection (National Security Archive, "reportedly").

## cw4_kennedy_ben_gurion_1961_1962
- **Agree:** the official memorandum's quotations; the second visit of 26 September 1962.
- **Disagree:** the draft memorandum vs the official text vs Israeli minutes on Dimona's purpose; seven vs eight US visits in the 1960s.
- **Note for the Hawk section:** Kennedy called the Hawk "a defensive weapon" in May 1961; s11d calls the 1962 Hawk sale the first US sale of "offensive" weapons. Writers must not repeat s11d's word as fact.

## cw4_periphery_doctrine_1957_1958
- **Agree:** Ben-Gurion's July 1958 decision and letter; Turkey, Iran and Ethiopia as the core partners; the Trident as an intelligence framework from autumn 1958.
- **Disagree:** where and how the August 1958 Turkish agreement was made (Schonmann: a secret formal agreement, no place given; Liel and Asa-El: a secret visit to Ankara); whether it raised relations to embassy level (Asa-El yes; Liel: only in 1992). Washington's reception (Alpher's "lukewarm blessing" vs the Policy Planning Staff's and Dulles's caution).
- **Translation:** the "dam" sentence exists in two renderings (Schonmann's English; Liel's German).
- **Not verified:** Reuven Shiloah's role as the doctrine's author; Sudan's and the Kurds' place in 1958 (Wikipedia only).

## cw4_iran_israel_1957_1960
- **Agree:** SAVAK's 1957 approach; the Eilat pipeline's dependence on Iranian oil; the July 1960 statement and Nasser's break.
- **Disagree:** 1949 vs 1950 for Iran's de facto recognition; Israel vs the Gulf as the real cause of the break.
- **Open:** FRUS 1958–60 vol. XII doc. 273 carries a file number read as 8–959 (August 1959), but its editors' note describes the Shah's statement of 24 July, which Iranica dates to July 1960. The telegram's year is unresolved; writers should date Bakhtiar's talk "around 27 July" and say the year is uncertain.
- **Reported only:** the 1958 Ahvaz radio station (The New Arab).

## cw4_middle_east_1958
- **Agree:** the dates of the UAR, the Iraqi coup and the landing; one US combat death; withdrawal by 25 October.
- **Disagree:** peak US troop strength (almost 15,000 vs about or at most 14,000). The cause of Lebanon's crisis (domestic, per SNIE 36.4–58 and Riedel, vs Nasser's subversion as Chamoun charged).
- **Not verified:** the exact date of the UAR's proclamation and plebiscite; Resolution 128's text; Chehab's election day.

## cw4_overflights_crisis_1958
- **Agree (documents):** the 1–10 August sequence.
- **Disagree:** Ben-Gurion's motive (the Soviet note and cabinet pressure vs British politics and the marginal value of more troops).
- **Not verified:** the US oil airlift's volume; the text of Israel's reply to the Soviet note.

## cw4_germany_arms_money_1957_1962
- **Agree:** the 14 March 1960 meeting; a large German loan from December 1961 under "Aktion Geschäftsfreund"; secret arms aid; relations only in May 1965.
- **Disagree:** whether the loan financed Dimona (Bar-Joseph, Die Welt vs missing records and Peres's denial); 340 vs 500 million marks of military aid; Peres's two accounts of the $40 million.
- **Not verified:** the commonly cited December 1957 Strauss–Peres meeting at Rosenheim (Bar-Joseph gives Bonn, 3 July 1957); the 1959 Israeli arms sales to Germany and the cabinet crisis they caused; Strauss's 1961 Paris claim beyond The National Interest's report.
