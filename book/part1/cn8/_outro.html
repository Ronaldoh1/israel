From 1559 to 1966 the Roman Index told Catholics which books they could not read, with the force of church law. [cite:cn_wiki_paul4] [cite:cn_va_index1966] It began with a ban on Protestant books and on Italian and German translations of the Bible, issued under a pope whose nephew-cardinal directed his government and was strangled in 1561, then declared unjustly condemned in 1567. [cite:cn_wiki_ccarafa] It ended with about four thousand titles, from Copernicus and Bruno to Kant, Hugo and Sartre, and with a notification that kept its "moral" force while ending its legal one. [cite:cn_fordham_index] [cite:cn_wiki_indexlist] [cite:cn_va_index1966] Part Nine steps back from the chronology to set out the pattern that repeats across all eighteen centuries, and the families whose place in it is most often misunderstood.
