Lebanon and Hezbollah

The Cairo Agreement of 1969 and the PLO's expulsion from Jordan made southern Lebanon a base for attacks on Israel and a target of Israeli reprisals. [cite:hz1_cairo_text] Israel invaded Lebanon on 6 June 1982 and drove the PLO to Tunis. In September, Phalangist militiamen killed hundreds, by some counts thousands, at Sabra and Shatila, camps Israeli troops had surrounded; Israel's own inquiry found Sharon personally responsible. The occupation of the south lasted until 2000. [cite:wiki_1982_lebanon_war] [cite:wiki_sabra_shatila_massacre] [cite:jvl_kahan_commission]

Hezbollah took shape during the invasion and named the occupation as its first enemy. In the same year Iran's Revolutionary Guards set up a base in the Bekaa, and an American court later found the organisation "formed under the auspices of the government of Iran." [cite:hz3_wiki_hezbollah] [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks] [cite:hz2_anderson] Ehud Barak called it the occupation's product, in words reported since 2006 whose original source is unconfirmed. [cite:hz3_statesman_barak]

The record also shows what came first. Musa al-Sadr organised Lebanon's Shia in 1974 and armed them in Amal in 1975, years before Iran's revolution and Israel's occupation, after a settlement of 1943 that had left them on the political margins. [cite:hz1_brit_amal] [cite:hz1_mrg_shia] Hezbollah has two documented roots, and the occupation is one of them. The Hezbollah Architecture tells this in full.
