Part Two established that the founders' record contains real territorial claims, each narrower and more specific than the slogan later attached to them. Herzl noted in 1898 a plan to ask the sultan for land from the Egyptian frontier to the Euphrates, but never made the request, and the movement he founded never adopted it; calling the note a fabrication, as earlier accounts in this library did, is an error. [cite:gi1_pipes_calumny] The Zionist Organization's formal claim at Paris on 3 February 1919 ran from south of Sidon, over Hermon and to a line west of the Hejaz Railway, justified mainly by water; the powers gave far less, leaving Hermon and most of the Golan to the French mandate and separating Transjordan in 1922. [cite:gi2_bu_zo1919] [cite:gi2_wiki_paulet] [cite:gi2_wiki_tjmemo] "Both banks of the Jordan" was the programme of a named minority, the Revisionists, from 1925, carried by Betar's song and the Irgun's emblem and opposed by the mainstream. [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] [cite:gi1_wiki_eastbank] [cite:gi2_wiki_irgun] In 1937 the mainstream accepted partition in principle while rejecting Peel's small state, and Ben-Gurion wrote privately that a partial state would be "the beginning"; the one sentence in that letter about expelling Arabs is disputed and is given here in both readings.
