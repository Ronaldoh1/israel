The mechanism, in plain terms

The familiar statement is that Israel was created in 1948. As a statement about the declaration, it is accurate. As a statement about when the state's machinery came into being, it misleads. By the time the world saw the state declared, its working parts had been running for decades.

A simple comparison makes the point. Imagine a restaurant holding its grand opening. The opening is real, and it is the first moment most people notice the place. But the kitchen was built in 1899. The land was bought in 1901. The health permit was granted in 1922. The marketing team had been working the neighbourhood since the 1940s. Everything that made the opening possible happened before the sign went up, and most of it happened out of public view.

Each part of that comparison corresponds to a documented institution. The kitchen is the financial base: the Jewish Colonial Trust and the Anglo-Palestine bank, established between 1899 and 1902. The land is the Jewish National Fund's permanent holdings, begun in 1901 and held in perpetuity. The permit is the Mandate's recognition of a "Jewish agency" in its Article 4, in 1922, which gave the movement's agency legal recognition. The marketing team is the American political machine of the 1940s, the American Zionist Emergency Council, which mapped Congress district by district. Each was in place before May 1948, and each had been running for years by the time the state was declared on 14 May. The declaration was the first line most people were shown. It was not the first line of the program.

That is the sense in which the state was initialized before it was declared. The phrase is a way of describing the documented sequence, not an additional claim about it: the institutions and their dates are those set out in [scene:us_s08|the whole play, step by step]. What the comparison adds is a way of seeing why 1948 can look like a beginning when the record shows it as the end of a fifty-year build.
