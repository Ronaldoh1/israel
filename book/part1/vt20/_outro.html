Part Twenty has set out what the earliest record shows. Yeshua was a Galilean Jew with a common name, born around 6 to 4 BCE and crucified under Pontius Pilate between about 30 and 36 CE, an execution confirmed by Tacitus and a family confirmed by Josephus's notice of "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ". [cite:vt20_wiki_chronology] [cite:vt20_tacitus_ann15] [cite:vt20_josephus_ant20] His message centred on a coming kingdom of God, and whether Luke has him place it "within" or "among" his hearers is a live translation dispute; the Gospel of Thomas, of disputed date, says plainly that it is "inside of you". [cite:vt20_esv_mark1] [cite:vt20_ramelli_luke17] [cite:vt20_thomas_lambdin] Paul, the earliest Christian writer, grounded his gospel in a revelation rather than acquaintance, yet spent fifteen days with Cephas, met James, and took part in the Jerusalem meeting of about 48 to 50 that opened the movement to Gentiles, the only council of his lifetime. [cite:vt20_nrsv_gal12] [cite:vt20_wiki_jerusalem] The first communities met in houses, named women in recognised roles, and gave voluntarily, "not as an exaction", with no tithe system; church tithe laws appear only in 567 and 585. [cite:vt20_esv_rom16] [cite:vt20_esv_2cor9] [cite:vt20_justin_67] [cite:vt20_cathen_tithes] Offices formed early all the same: the Didache had communities appoint bishops and deacons, and by Ignatius's time a single bishop controlled baptism and the common meal in some cities, years before any emperor favoured the church.
