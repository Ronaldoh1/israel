Part Four sets out the two nuclear programmes at the centre of this volume, side by side and in the same register. The first is Iran's: a programme that began with American help under the Shah, was revived after the revolution, was exposed in 2002 as larger than Iran had declared, was found by the International Atomic Energy Agency to have included coordinated work relevant to a weapon until the end of 2003, was capped by the 2015 nuclear deal, and by 2025 had produced a stockpile of uranium enriched to 60 percent that no other state without nuclear weapons possesses. The second is Israel's: a reactor built at Dimona with French help in the late 1950s, an arsenal that independent researchers estimate at about 90 warheads, a policy of neither confirming nor denying it, and a treaty Israel has never joined. The part records what each government has said about its own programme and about the other's, and what the inspectors and intelligence agencies found. It is the baseline against which the predictions examined in Parts Five and Six are tested.
