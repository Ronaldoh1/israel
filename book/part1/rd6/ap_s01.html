The Absentees' Property Law: From Flight to State Title, 1948–1953

The war of 1948 left hundreds of Palestinian localities empty of their inhabitants. [cite:rs_rd6_plands_villages] Flight alone did not decide who would own the land and houses left behind. That was settled afterward, on paper, by a sequence of regulations and statutes passed between July 1948 and 1953. Together they moved the property of the refugees first into the custody of the state, then into its ownership and that of the Jewish National Fund, and finally into retroactive legal validation. The same chain reached people who had never left the country at all.

It is widely believed that the Palestinian Arabs who stayed inside Israel in 1948 kept their homes and land as full citizens. But tens of thousands who had moved from one village to another inside the lines Israel held were classed in law as "absentees," and their property was taken on the same terms as that of refugees across the border. They were registered as citizens and could vote, and still could not reclaim what they had owned.

The first link came during the fighting. A Custodian of Abandoned Property was appointed on 15 July 1948. [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] The occupation of empty houses that followed, and the cabinet decision of June 1948 against return during the war, are told in [scene:rd6_return_barred|how return was barred]. In December 1948 the government issued Emergency Regulations on absentees' property, which transferred control to a new Custodian of Absentee Property. [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] Even the date of this step is uncertain. The historian Michael Fischbach, working from Israeli sources, gives 2 December 1948; an American document he cites refers to an "Israeli ordinance of December 12, 1948." [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] The sources consulted here do not resolve which is correct.

The regulations were temporary in form. The international body charged with a settlement objected to them while they were still temporary. In April 1949 the United Nations Conciliation Commission for Palestine proposed, as a preliminary measure, "suspension of the application of the Absentee law until the final peace settlement." Arab delegations argued that the law treated nationals of Palestine as enemy aliens. Israel did not suspend it. [cite:un_ccp_absentee_1949] The talks in which that request was made are told in [scene:lau_s01|the Lausanne conference].

Instead the regime was made permanent. On 14 March 1950 the Knesset passed the Absentees' Property Law, an expanded version of the 1948 regulations, and it remained the main law governing refugee property for decades. [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] The law, numbered 5710-1950, is plain in its terms. All absentees' property is vested in the Custodian. Every right an absentee had passes automatically to the Custodian. Anyone holding absentee property must hand it over. And the fact that an absentee's identity is unknown does not prevent property from being vested. [cite:un_absentees_property_law_text] [cite:wiki_present_absentee] Anyone found occupying such property, including its original owner, was in breach of the law.

Everything turned on the definition of an "absentee." The law reached anyone who, during the war, had left his usual place of residence for areas outside Israeli control, or for anywhere else. [cite:wiki_present_absentee] The second clause was the decisive one. It captured not only the refugees beyond the armistice lines but also Arabs who had moved between villages inside Israel and had then been registered as citizens, issued identity numbers, and allowed to vote and to stand for the Knesset. [cite:wiki_present_absentee] In law they were absent. In fact they were citizens living inside the state. They came to be called "present absentees."

Their numbers were large and remained so. By 2001 the present absentees were estimated at about 200,000 people, nearly 17 percent of Israel's Arab citizens, and half the populations of the two largest Arab towns in Israel are internally displaced. [cite:wiki_present_absentee] [cite:wiki_land_day] [cite:cornell_bilsky_absentee_law] The law did not act alone on them. Military rule over Arab citizens restricted their movement and, combined with the absentee laws, prevented the internally displaced from physically returning to reclaim their homes. [cite:wiki_present_absentee] [cite:wiki_land_day] That regime is described in [scene:rd6_military_government|the military government].

One interpretive reading, used elsewhere in this library, borrows a term from programming for what the law did: the word "absentee" kept its everyday meaning while its working definition was swapped to include people who were present. It is a reading of the mechanism, not a finding about anyone's intent.

Custody was not yet ownership. The next steps moved the land on. Fischbach records that in early 1949 Ben-Gurion wanted to sell a significant amount of refugee land to the Jewish National Fund, and that the Fund wanted legal title transferred to it. His book tabulates a sale in January 1949 and a further purchase in 1950 known as the "second million" dunams. [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] The exact acreage and prices of these transactions are not reproduced here, because they could not be confirmed from the material consulted.

In 1950 a Development Authority was established. A 2016 study in the Journal of Israeli History describes it as both a major allocator of land and the body that set "the rules of the game." The same study finds that the ban on selling "national lands" was strictly kept in rural areas but not in urban ones. [cite:rs_rd6_jih_devauthority] According to an analysis published by the organization BADIL, the 1950 law allowed the Custodian to transfer absentee land to the Development Authority, which could in turn pass it to the Jewish National Fund. BADIL states that nearly 2.4 million dunams were transferred in this way, "more than trebling the JNF's holdings as of 1941," and that by 1951 the government, together with the Fund, held 92 percent of the land within the state. [cite:rs_rd6_badil_landregime]

The last link closed the chain. The Land Acquisition (Validation of Acts and Compensation) Law of 1953 retroactively validated land seizures made during and after the war on grounds of security and development. The Finance Minister said its purpose was to "instill legality in some acts undertaken during and following the war." [cite:rs_rd6_badil_landregime] Read plainly, the minister's words concede that some of the earlier seizures had needed legality supplied after the fact. Adalah, in its database of laws it classes as discriminatory, states that 1.2 to 1.3 million dunams were expropriated from Arab owners under the 1953 law, from 349 towns and villages, and that Arab ownership has fallen to 3 to 3.5 percent of the land, compared with 48 percent in 1948. [cite:rs_rd6_adalah_landacq] These are Adalah's figures. Over a longer span, Salman Abu-Sitta estimates that more than 1,000 square kilometres were expropriated from Arab citizens between 1948 and 2003. [cite:wiki_present_absentee] [cite:wiki_land_day]

Who gained and who bore the cost follows from the chain. The property of the refugees and the present absentees passed first to a state custodian, then to a development body, and from there to the state's own land holdings and to the Jewish National Fund. The owners, inside and outside the country, lost title. The law offered them no route back to it: occupying one's own former house was itself a breach.

The two sides of the argument were set out at the time. The Israeli government's stated grounds, written into the 1953 law, were security and development, and in its own account it was regularizing acts taken in war. Israel declined the Conciliation Commission's request to suspend the regime pending a peace settlement. [cite:rs_rd6_badil_landregime] [cite:un_ccp_absentee_1949] The Arab delegations' objection, made in 1949, was that the law treated nationals of Palestine as enemy aliens, and the Commission's own proposal implied that permanent measures should wait for a final settlement. [cite:un_ccp_absentee_1949] The present absentees are the hardest case for any reading of the law as a wartime measure against the enemy's side, because they never left the state's territory and became its citizens.

According to the sources used here, the Absentees' Property Law remains in force and has been applied in East Jerusalem. [cite:wiki_present_absentee]

The record does not settle the totals. The 2.4 million dunams and the 92 percent figure come from BADIL, and the 1.2 to 1.3 million dunams, the 349 localities and the fall from 48 percent to 3 to 3.5 percent come from Adalah. Both are advocacy organizations, and the scholarly sources consulted here, Fischbach and the 2016 study, describe the same legal chain without confirming those totals in the material available. The acreage of the Jewish National Fund's purchases of 1949 and 1950 is unconfirmed, and so is the exact date of the December 1948 regulations. What the record establishes beyond dispute is the mechanism: the text of the 1950 law, the Conciliation Commission's request to suspend it, the definition that turned residents into absentees, and the 1953 law that validated what had been taken.
