Even the definition most governments use says the opposite. The IHRA working definition, which the State Department has used in some form since 2010, states in its own text that "criticism of Israel similar to that leveled against any other country cannot be regarded as antisemitic" [cite:state_defining_antisemitism] [cite:ihra_text_safeguard]. Its lead drafter, Kenneth Stern, has said it was never intended to chill speech, and in a December 2019 Guardian op-ed warned that it was being weaponized against critics of Israel [cite:stern_guardian_2019]. The ACLU has warned that equating criticism of Israel's government with antisemitism chills speech [cite:politifact_aaa_2024].

Antisemitism, meaning hatred of Jews, is still real: the ADL counted 9,354 US incidents in 2024, the most since it began counting in 1979. Some speech that calls itself criticism of Israel does cross that line. The test the definition itself sets: is the criticism aimed at a government's actions, and would it be fair if aimed at any other country? Or is it aimed at Jews as a people, holding them responsible for a state?
