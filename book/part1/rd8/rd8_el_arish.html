El Arish: Sovereignty Without Water, 1902–1903

Herzl's first attempt to secure land through Britain looked not to Africa but to the edge of Palestine itself: the northern Sinai, between Egypt and the Ottoman frontier.

In 1902 Herzl met Joseph Chamberlain, and northern Sinai, including the Pelusian Plain, was designated for an autonomous Jewish settlement sponsored by the British government. The idea had been proposed by Davis Trietsch. [cite:a_jvl_elarish]

From 11 February to 25 March 1903 an expedition led by Leopold Kessler, with an agronomist, an architect, the physician of the Palestine colonies, a water engineer and a representative of the Egyptian government, toured the area. Its joint report found that everything depended on water, and recommended diverting some of the Nile. [cite:a_wp_kessler]

Egypt refused, calculating that the plan needed five times the water the engineer had estimated. Lord Cromer, Britain's consul-general in Egypt, upheld the refusal even after Herzl narrowed the scheme to the El Arish area and gave up any claim to Nile water. [cite:a_wp_kessler] [cite:a_jvl_elarish]

The likely path. Refused before it began. Had Egypt agreed, a small autonomous settlement on irrigated land at the edge of Egypt, dependent on Cairo for its water.

The cost. No settlement, so no conflict on the record; its whole future depended on a neighbour controlling its water.
