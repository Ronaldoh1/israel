The West Bank and East Jerusalem: Two Legal Systems, Three Statuses

Israel's own measures of how its system serves people cover its citizens. Most international indices do the same: Freedom House scored Israel 73 out of 100 in its 2026 report, and said the score did not reflect actions in the West Bank or Gaza, which it rated separately at 22 and 2, both "Not Free". [cite:us3_fh_il2026] This part is about the people those scores leave out. The Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics estimated 3.43 million Palestinians in the West Bank and 2.13 million in Gaza at the end of 2025, about 5.56 million in all. [cite:us9_pcbs_2025] Israel's own population is about ten million, on World Bank figures for 2025. [cite:ir13_fred_ilpc] The two counts come from different statistical offices and may overlap in East Jerusalem, but on either reading roughly a third of the people in the territory Israel controls between the Jordan River and the sea are not its citizens. They fall into three statuses: Israeli citizens (including settlers), East Jerusalem residents, and West Bank and Gaza Palestinians with no Israeli status at all.

Two systems of law on one territory

Since 1967 the West Bank has been held under military rule. Palestinians there are governed by military orders issued since 1967 and by earlier Jordanian law, and are tried in military courts for offences ranging from traffic violations to theft. Israeli citizens living in the same territory are governed by Israeli law and tried in Israeli civilian courts. The Association for Civil Rights in Israel, which calls the arrangement "institutionalized discrimination", found in 2014 that an Israeli suspect had to be brought before a judge within 24 hours and a Palestinian within 96. [cite:wb_acri_twosys] An internal army document reported in 2011 showed that 99.74 per cent of military-court trials of Palestinians in 2010 ended in conviction. [cite:wb_972_conviction] Part Four and the West Bank volume set out the military courts, administrative detention and children in custody in detail. [scene:us4_detention|Part Four: detention and military courts] [scene:wb7_courts|The West Bank, Documented: the courts]

The legal device that carries Israeli law to Israeli citizens in the West Bank is a set of emergency regulations dating from after the 1967 war. They extend Israeli law to settlers personally, without annexing the land, and they are also the legal basis for holding Palestinian prisoners in jails inside Israel. They must be renewed by the Knesset every five years. [cite:us9_jta_regs2022] On 6 June 2022 the renewal failed, 58 to 52, when two left-wing members of Naftali Bennett's coalition voted against because they opposed the dual system, and Benjamin Netanyahu instructed Likud and his right-wing allies, most of whom backed the regulations, to vote against; JTA reported that his aim was to force a crisis in which he would replace Bennett. [cite:us9_jta_regs2022] [cite:us9a_jta] The next Knesset passed it on 24 January 2023, 39 to 12, for five more years, to 15 February 2028. [cite:us9_jp_regs2023] Justice Minister Yariv Levin, introducing the bill at its first reading on 10 January 2023, said, as quoted by Haaretz, "we have restored our belief that we have the right to the whole of the Land of Israel". [cite:us9_tna_regs2023]

The gap between the two systems widened after 2022. Under the coalition agreement of 1 December 2022, the Religious Zionism party received a minister in the Defence Ministry with authority over the Civil Administration, the body that rules on Palestinian building permits and movement, and in 2023 planning powers passed to that minister, Bezalel Smotrich. [cite:gi6_wiki_37gov] [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis] On 30 March 2026 the Knesset passed, 62 to 48, a law making hanging the default sentence for West Bank Palestinians convicted in military courts of nationalistic killings, while civilian courts trying Israelis may choose between death and life imprisonment. [cite:us4_ap_deathlaw] Petitions against it were pending before the High Court, and an army order applying it was signed on 17 May 2026; Part Four records its status as of October 2026. [cite:us4_wiki_deathlaw] [scene:us4_prisons|Part Four: prisons and the death penalty]

The settler population over time

The population living under the Israeli side of the dual system has grown in every decade. Peace Now's series, which excludes East Jerusalem, counts 1,500 settlers in 1972, 12,500 in 1980, 116,300 when the Oslo process began in 1993, 198,300 in 2000 and 311,100 in 2010. [cite:wb_peacenow_pop] In 2025 the figure was 529,704, with about 246,000 more in East Jerusalem. [cite:wb_wiki_popstats] Under the Oslo II agreement of 1995, Area C, where Israel keeps full civil and security control, was to shrink by stages; it still covered about 63 per cent of the West Bank in 2013, and from 2009 to 2018 only 98 of 4,422 Palestinian building-permit requests there were granted, about 2 per cent. [cite:wb_wiki_oslo2areas] [cite:wb_ocha_areac] [scene:wb5_areac|The West Bank, Documented: living in Area C]

East Jerusalem: residents, not citizens

East Jerusalem is the exception in law. Israel applied its law to the area in June 1967, and the Palestinians living there are permanent residents of Israel, not citizens. [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex] [cite:us9_wiki_jlm2024] [scene:wb3_jerusalem|The West Bank, Documented: Jerusalem] About 362,000 Palestinians lived in East Jerusalem in 2024. As residents they may vote in municipal elections but not for the Knesset. [cite:us9_aje_ejvote] Most have boycotted the municipal vote since 1967; in the February 2024 Jerusalem election no Palestinian ran for mayor, and the one list headed by an Arab candidate won 1.07 per cent and no seats. [cite:us9_aje_ejvote] [cite:us9_wiki_jlm2024] Residents are covered by Israel's National Health Insurance Law, which applies to citizens and permanent residents alike. [cite:us1_cmwf_israel]

Residency, unlike citizenship, can be revoked by the Interior Ministry. HaMoked, an Israeli rights group that calls the practice a "quiet deportation", compiled the ministry's own figures: 14,808 revocations from 1967 to 2022. The numbers were low for most of the first three decades, rose sharply after 1995, and peaked at 4,577 in 2008 alone; they then fell to double digits a year after 2016. [cite:us9_hamoked_revoc] Citizenship can be applied for, but few have received it. Reuters reported in March 2017 that about 15,000 East Jerusalem Palestinians had applied since 2003 and fewer than 6,000 had reportedly been approved; by figures first published by the Times of Israel, of more than 4,000 applications made from 2014 to September 2016, 84 had been approved, 161 rejected and the rest were pending, a slowdown the Interior Ministry attributed to its workload. Applying, Reuters wrote, still carried the stigma of implied acceptance of Israeli control. [cite:us9_reuters_ejcit] [cite:us9a_reuters] These counts cover a different period from the Reuters 2015 and Haaretz 2022 estimates given in Part Six. [scene:us6_citizenship|Part Six: citizenship]

Who votes, and for whom

The Knesset elected on 27 October 2026 will make law for the whole territory. Settlers vote in it as Israeli citizens; their Palestinian neighbours do not. An AP report from Mas'ha in 2019 described the arrangement in one place: the settlers of Elkana could vote, and the villagers of Mas'ha, on the other side of a wall and a gate, could not. [cite:us9_ap_masha] Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza vote, when votes are held, for the Palestinian Authority. The last legislative election was in 2006, the Legislative Council has not met since 2007, and President Mahmoud Abbas, whose term should have ended in 2009, rules by decree. [cite:us9_kt_postpone] Legislative elections had been set for 28 November 2026. On 10 October 2026 Abbas postponed them, together with the presidential vote, to 11 September 2027, citing "obstacles imposed by Israeli measures"; the Authority said Israel had refused its election officials access to East Jerusalem and Gaza. Israel's foreign minister, Gideon Saar, accused Abbas of "fooling the international community". [cite:us9_kt_postpone] [cite:us9_nat_elect] Both accounts are on the record: the Authority's own failure to hold elections for twenty years, and Israel's control of where Palestinian ballot boxes may go.

What the courts and Israel say

The International Court of Justice, in its advisory opinion of 19 July 2024, found Israel's continued presence in the occupied territory unlawful and found systemic discrimination in its legislation and measures there; eleven of fifteen judges agreed with all its findings. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu answered that "the Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land". [cite:wb_livelaw_icj2024] B'Tselem in 2021, Human Rights Watch in 2021 and Amnesty International in 2022 each concluded that the arrangement amounted to apartheid; Israel rejects the finding. [cite:wb_oj_apartheid] The Levy Report, commissioned by Netanyahu in 2012 but never formally adopted, argued that Israel's presence is not an occupation and that the settlements are legal. [cite:wb_wiki_levy] The West Bank volume sets these rulings and answers side by side.
