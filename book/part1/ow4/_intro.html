Part Four turns to signals intelligence, the one strand of the relationship whose working rules are known from the agencies' own papers. It sets out the 2009 memorandum under which the National Security Agency sends Israel's Unit 8200 raw intercepts, Americans' communications included, and the few, unenforceable limits it places on their use. It then reads the NSA's own documents for what each side gets, how the NSA judged the balance, and how the same agency ranked Israel as an intelligence threat, alongside the American monitoring of Israel's prime minister. Finally it tests the volume's central claim: that American data flows to Israel while the United States has no comparable view of Israel's surveillance of Gaza and the West Bank, much of it reported to have been stored on an American company's cloud.
