Part Three has shown that the CIA–Israel relationship rests on no published agreement and no act of Congress, but on a channel that began with a meeting of directors in 1951 and was held for two decades by one officer whose control of it the CIA's own historian called "an administrative anomaly." [cite:ow3_tablet] [cite:ow3_robarge] It produced documented returns for the United States, from Khrushchev's speech to Soviet radar and missile equipment, a shared attack on Natanz, and a working channel for the hostage negotiations of 2023–24. [cite:ow3_meq_siskind] [cite:ow3_npr_sanger] [cite:ow3_axios2023] It also produced the CIA's own record of concern, in NUMEC, the 1979 report and the Pollard assessment, none of which interrupted the liaison, and the NUMEC question remains officially unresolved. [cite:ow3_nsa_numec] [cite:ow3_jta1982] [cite:ow3_nsa_pollard] What the record does not show is any American means of inspecting how Israel's services use what they receive: after October 2023 that rested on Israeli assurances, sometimes oral, and on the blunt option of cutting a feed. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] [cite:ow3_reu_withheld] Part Four turns to the signals relationship between the National Security Agency and Unit 8200, where the flow of raw data, including Americans' communications, is documented in the agencies' own papers.
