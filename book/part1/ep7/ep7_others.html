Russia, the Gulf and the American Agencies

A book that examined only one country's links would be making a choice before the evidence did. The same questions, asked of every state that appears in the record, give a different picture: Epstein sought access to several governments at once, and the strongest documented contact with an intelligence service runs to Russia.

Russia

The released files show Epstein cultivating Sergei Belyakov, a graduate of the academy of Russia's security service, the FSB, a former deputy economy minister and from 2014 the chairman of the St Petersburg International Economic Forum. In 2015 Epstein asked Belyakov for information about a Russian woman he said was trying to blackmail a prominent American businessman, and Belyakov sent a background description; in a draft apparently meant for the woman, Epstein wrote that he had consulted "some friends in the FSB". He introduced Belyakov to Peter Thiel and to Thomas Pritzker. He met Vitaly Churkin, Russia's ambassador to the United Nations, and arranged an internship for Churkin's son. Vladimir Putin's name appears more than a thousand times in the files, and Epstein tried to reach him through intermediaries, among them the former Norwegian prime minister Thorbjørn Jagland; nothing released shows that the two men met. The Kremlin dismissed the allegations. [cite:ep7_euronews_intel2026] None of this shows that Epstein worked for Russia. It shows a man who used an FSB-connected official as a source and wanted Putin's ear, which is more documented contact with a security service than the record holds for any other country.

The Gulf

The files show him acting as a go-between in the Gulf. In December 2018 he arranged a meeting in London between a former prime minister of Qatar, Hamad bin Jassim, and Ehud Barak, at a time when Qatar was under a blockade by its neighbours. Before President Trump's first foreign trip in 2017, a figure apparently from Kuwait asked Epstein how to deal with the new president. He encouraged the Dubai ports chief Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem to accept an invitation to the inauguration. [cite:ep7_reason_qatar2026] [cite:ep7_reason_mideast2025] These are the activities of a broker selling access between governments and money, and they put Barak, again, at the centre of the deals he brokered.

The American agencies

The American side of the record is, in one respect, the most concrete. In 2010 the Drug Enforcement Administration's organised-crime task force opened an investigation in New York, code-named Chain Reaction, into Epstein and fourteen alleged co-conspirators, looking at possible drug trafficking and at "illegitimate wire transfers" tied to drug or prostitution activity in the Virgin Islands and New York. A 69-page memo of May 2015 still described the case as pending. No charges were ever brought, and the names of the fourteen are blacked out in the released copy. [cite:ep7_cbs_dea2026] Senator Ron Wyden asked for the unredacted memo, and in March 2026 said the Deputy Attorney General, Todd Blanche, had intervened to stop the DEA from providing it; the Justice Department has not answered that account publicly. [cite:ep7_wyden_blanche2026] The book records this as what it is: a federal investigation of Epstein's money and his associates that ran for at least five years, ended without charges, and is still being withheld in part from the Senate.

No released document shows the CIA or any other American intelligence agency directing or protecting Epstein. The remark attributed to Acosta, set out earlier in this part, is the only account that suggests it, and he denies making it.
