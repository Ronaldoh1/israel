Empire, Bank and Superpower

On 2 November 1917 Britain's foreign secretary, Arthur Balfour, addressed his declaration in favour of "a national home for the Jewish people" to Lionel Walter Rothschild. Palestine's Muslim and Christian Arabs, about nine-tenths of the population, appear in it only as "existing non-Jewish communities", promised civil and religious rights and nothing political. [cite:balfour_letter] Britain then ruled the country for thirty years under a League of Nations mandate that required it to facilitate Jewish immigration and settlement. [cite:wb_wiki_balfour]

The money came first from one man. Edmond de Rothschild of the Paris house funded the early colonies from 1882, when almost no one else did, and was reported in 1934 to have invested over sixty million dollars in Palestine. [cite:f_jta_baron] The land company he founded, PICA, became the largest Jewish landowner in the country. [cite:wiki_yehoshua_hankin] From 1947 the United States carried the rest: the partition vote, recognition eleven minutes after the declaration, and every year of aid that followed. [cite:trumanlibrary_recognition]

What each power was worth

The network map of the Road to 1948 puts the chance of a state in 1948, on the full record, at about 93 percent, and recounts it with any part removed. Without the Rothschild family it falls to 34 percent; without Britain's backing, 36; without the United States, 24; without both the Rothschilds and the United States, 5. Without the banks, land companies and funds that raised the money and bought the land, and without the organized movement itself, it falls to nothing.

The finding concerns named institutions and named people: a British cabinet, one family's bank and land company, an American government. It is a finding about institutions and decisions, not about Jews as a people.
