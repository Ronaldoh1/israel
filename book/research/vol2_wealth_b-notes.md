# Volume II, Wealth B (Walter Rothschild, the Balfour addressee, modern context): research notes

Verified 2026-10-17. Every claim in `vol2_wealth_b.json` comes from a page fetched or seen in search results in this session. WebFetch returns a model-written summary of each page, not the raw text, so quoted phrases are short and marked approximate where needed. Check any quotation that will be printed against the original before publication.

**Limits of this pass.** Two primary pages were blocked by the fetch tool: law.cornell.edu (26 U.S.C. §1014) and the IRS newsroom release IR-2025-103. OpenJurist's statute text and KPMG's summary of the IRS release were used in their place. ## 1. Walter Rothschild (`wb_walter`)
**Agree:**
- Life dates: 1868–1937.
- Peerage inherited in 1915.
- MP for Aylesbury from 1899 to January 1910.
- At the bank from 1889 to 1908.
- Tring museum opened in 1892 and was bequeathed to the British Museum (now the NHM at Tring).
- He gave the original letter to the British Library in 1924 (Add MS 41178 A, a primary catalogue record).
- Bird collection: about 280,000 birds sold to the AMNH, with blackmail as the reason, revealed by Miriam Rothschild in *Dear Lord Rothschild* (1983).

**Disagree:**
- Sale year: 1931 (Mental Floss, Ripley's) or 1932 (Wikipedia).
- Party label: Liberal Unionist (Wikipedia) or Conservative (JVL).
- EZF office: Al Jazeera and Wineman call Walter president of the (English) Zionist Federation. A Wikipedia-derived page says Weizmann became its president in 1917. The Rothschild Archive names no office. Marked `disputed`.
- Natty's death date: 31 March 1915 (Wikipedia). The Rothschild Archive's wording ("five days after an operation on 31 March") could mean early April.

**Not verified:**
- Walter's estate at death in 1937.
- Natty's estate in 1915. No figure was found.
- The $225,000 price comes only from popular outlets (Mental Floss, Ripley's). No AMNH page or scholarly source was reached.
- "Money troubles" beyond the blackmail story.

## 2. Why the letter went to Rothschild (`wb_addressee`)
**Agree:**
- Addressed to Lord Rothschild at 148 Piccadilly, for transmission to the Zionist Federation.
- He was the "unofficial leader" of British Jewry (Rothschild Archive) and inherited that standing in 1915 (Jacob Rothschild, JC 2017).
- Balfour asked Rothschild and Weizmann for a formula on 19 June 1917 (Wikipedia, citing Friedman).
- The Zionist draft ("accepts the principle that Palestine should be reconstituted…") was sent on 18 July. TNA holds CAB 21/58 (222), dated 18 July 1917.
- The changes from that draft to the final text are given in the JSON.

**Disagree:**
- The draft's date: Wikipedia's draft table says 12 July, while its prose says it was sent on 18 July. Both can be true: written on the 12th, sent on the 18th.
- The TNA image title "Balfour to Rothschild" for the 18 July item looks like a catalogue shorthand. The item is Rothschild's letter to Balfour, so confirm before citing it as a Balfour letter.
- The NLI blog says Weizmann presented the Zionist draft. Wikipedia says Rothschild sent it.
- How Weizmann met Walter: through Dorothy (Dunner) or through Rozsika (Jacob Rothschild).

**Not verified:**
- That Weizmann or the Zionists explicitly asked for the letter to be addressed to Rothschild. No source states it. The explanations found are Sokolow's "no address" quip (via Jacob Rothschild), the seniority argument in Jewish News, and Cecil Roth's "incongruous".
- Schneer's or Stein's own explanation of the addressee was not reached.
- That Weizmann was "not wealthy". No source states it.
- The full 46-word text of the 18 July draft. Only partial wording was confirmed.

## 3. The Board of Deputies, May–June 1917 (`wb_board_1917`)
**Agree:**
- The Alexander–Montefiore statement appeared in The Times of 24 May 1917, p. 5 (Wikipedia, citing the Times; also Grobman).
- The Board's censure on 17 June 1917 passed 56–51, and Alexander resigned (Wikipedia, citing Kadish/ODNB; Wineman agrees).

**Disagree:**
- Wineman dates the letter 25 May and names Lucien Wolf and Alexander as signatories. Dunner says Wolf wrote it.
- Walter's reply: "the next day" (Dunner) or the Times of 28 May (Wikipedia's source list).

**Not verified:**
- A dated Weizmann reply in The Times.
- The full text of Walter's reply. One short phrase came via Dunner.

## 4. Montagu (`wb_montagu`)
**Agree:**
- The memorandum is dated 23 August 1917, catalogued at TNA as CAB 21/58 (218).
- The JVL prints its text. Its key phrases ("mischievous political creed", "world's Ghetto") match Grobman's account.

**Disagree:**
- The title wording: TNA has "Anti-Semitism in the Present Government"; the JVL has "The Anti-Semitism of the Present Government".

**Not verified:**
- The JVL transcription against the TNA original. Only the TNA image record was reached, not its text.

## 5. The quid pro quo claim (`wb_quidproquo`)
**Agree:** No source reached documents a Rothschild payment or loan in exchange for the declaration. Schneer (2010) and Bauer and Fox (2018) frame the finance angle as British officials' antisemitic overestimate of "Jewish power", not as a transaction. The Rothschild Archive notes only a general British hope, from 1916, that Jews would help finance the war.

**Disagree:** Lloyd George's memoirs (via Al Jazeera) list attracting Jewish financial resources as one motive. That is a stated motive, not evidence of a deal.

**Verdict:** `not_established`.

**Not verified:** Any war-loan figures involving N M Rothschild in 1916–17. Not searched in depth: the record surfaced no link to the declaration.

## 6. Step-up in basis (`wb_stepup`)
**Agree:** The text of §1014(a)(1), the effect of the step-up, and "buy, borrow, die" (McCaffery, via ProPublica).

**Not verified:** law.cornell.edu and IRS pages were blocked. No JCT or Treasury cost estimate was gathered.

## 7. ProPublica (`wb_propublica`)
**Agree:** $401bn wealth growth, $13.6bn tax, a 3.4% "true tax rate", 2014–2018, 25 richest. The authors and the date (8 June 2021) were confirmed on ProPublica's own page.

**Caveat:** The 14% median-household figure is an income-based rate and does not compare like for like.

## 8. Estate tax 2026 (`wb_estate_tax`)
**Agree:** $15,000,000 per person for 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, 9 Oct 2025). Under OBBBA it is permanent and indexed for inflation after 2026 (KPMG; Morgan Lewis).

**Not verified:** The IRS page itself (blocked).

## 9. Rothschild & Co, 2023 (`wb_rothschild_co_2023`)
**Agree:**
- Announced 6 February 2023 at €48 cum-distributions, with a 19% premium.
- Reuters valued the bank at about €3.6bn.
- Squeeze-out at €38.60, with delisting on 11 October 2023 (AMF notice, primary).

**Disagree:**
- Family stake: Reuters gives Concordia 38% and the family about 55%. The company presentation gives 54.5% of capital for the enlarged concert. The two figures are consistent but measure different things.

**Not verified:** The total cost of the buyout.

## 10. Albania (`wb_albania`)
**Agree:**
- February 2024 protected-areas law amendment; Kushner's March 2024 sketches; December 2024 strategic-investor status (AFP).
- The Shehu land sale and SPAK's freeze of the proceeds. The files make no allegation against Kushner or the investors (Reuters).
- Protests from 30 May 2026; Rama's defence of the project.

**Disagree:**
- Affinity Partners' role: the press says Affinity; Sazan says Kushner invested personally.
- The protected area's name: Vjosa-Narta, or Pishë-Poro-Nartë after the October 2022 merger.
- Sale price: €122m (AFP) or about €110m (Reuters). The court order is reported at more than $127m (CBS via The Deep Dive).
- Project value: about €1.4bn for Sazan, or about $4bn for the whole project.
- Scope of the SPAK probe: land titles and funds (Al Jazeera, OCCRP, Reuters), or the 2024 law (The New Region).

**Not verified:**
- Any reporting later than 11 August 2026. The "as of" status in the JSON is that date, from an aggregator citing Reuters and CBS.
- Whether construction has resumed.
- Whether any of the 20 suspects have been arrested or charged.
- Flamingo-specific data. Flamingos appear only as habitat description.
