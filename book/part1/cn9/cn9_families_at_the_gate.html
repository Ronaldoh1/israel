The Families at the Gate: Rank, Money and the Black Nobility

No account of who held power around the canon is complete without the Roman families, and none is more often distorted. The record shows families that bought the papacy, fought over it, filled the College of Cardinals with their sons and nephews and drew land and income from the office for centuries. It does not show any family, or any network of families, secretly controlling the institution. The difference matters, because the documented story is more interesting than the legend, and it explains how such families lasted so long.

The name usually given to them is modern. "Black nobility", nobiltà nera, describes the Roman aristocratic families who sided with the pope after the Italian army entered Rome on 20 September 1870 and kept their palace doors closed in mourning while Pius IX called himself a prisoner in the Vatican. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob] The families themselves were old. The standard list of those still in existence names the Colonna, Massimo, Orsini, Ruspoli, Pallavicini, Theodoli, Sacchetti, Borghese, Odescalchi and Boncompagni-Ludovisi; the Savelli, Caetani, Aldobrandini and Conti are counted among the lines now extinct. [cite:vt7_wiki_blacknob] Several of these names have appeared in this book as popes or the kin of popes.

What these families held in modern times was rank, not rule. Court offices passed by inheritance: the marshal of the conclave was a Chigi, the hereditary quartermaster of the Apostolic Palace a Sacchetti, and the highest lay honour, Prince Assistant to the Papal Throne, belonged to the heads of the Colonna and the Orsini. [cite:vt7_wiki_hered] [cite:vt7_laj_pa] The duties were ceremonial: standing beside the throne at great liturgies, doing the honours at the reception of sovereigns and ambassadors, taking precedence immediately after the cardinals. Nothing in the office gave its holder a vote, a signature or a budget. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] In January 1958 Pius XII removed Filippo Orsini from it after a public scandal, and the family had no recourse; ten years later, on 28 March 1968, Paul VI's motu proprio Pontificalis Domus declared that no office in the papal household was to be regarded as hereditary. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt7_pd_latin]

The money came from elsewhere, and the clearest case shows how money and rank traded. The Torlonia were not an old baronial line. Giovanni Torlonia administered Vatican finances in the late eighteenth century and was rewarded with papal titles, and the family's bank became the Roman partner in the first loan of the Rothschild house to the Papal States in the early 1830s, made, in the words of the historian Daniela Felisini, "in partnership with James de Rothschild" and regarded at the time as one of the most controversial operations of the period because of its terms. [cite:vt7_wiki_torlonia] [cite:vt11_felisini_ch4] In 1823 Domenico Orsini married Maria Luisa Torlonia, joining the oldest name in Roman papal history to new banking money. [cite:vt7_wiki_orsini] No document found links that marriage to the loans that followed. [scene:vt7_orsini_record|The Orsini record]

Read together, these documents suggest a structure, and this book offers it as an interpretation of the record rather than a finding. The old families held what might be called an authorisation chain: the right, conferred by the papacy and recognised by it, to stand beside the throne, to bear ancient titles and to lend their names. They did not hold the money chain. That belonged to bankers and administrators who held capital and credit. Each needed what the other had. The papacy rewarded its bankers with titles, as Pius VI and Pius VII rewarded Giovanni Torlonia; new money married into old names, as in 1823; and the old names survived, through each collapse of the systems that had supported them, by renting their legitimacy to the next generation of capital. [cite:vt7_wiki_torlonia] [cite:vt7_wiki_orsini] Seen this way, the popular picture of a hidden family at the top of the world's money is upside down. The documented exchange ran the other way: capital buying the appearance of ancient legitimacy.

The guardrail that runs through this book applies here with special force. Stories of who "really" controls the money of Rome have for more than a century attached themselves to the Rothschild name and, through it, to Jews as a people. The record supports nothing of the kind. It shows a commercial loan by a named banking house to a sovereign borrower on terms contemporaries disputed, and a Roman partner bank rewarded by the papacy. [cite:vt11_felisini_ch4] That is a finding about two banks and a state. What the loans did and did not do is examined in [scene:vt11_papal_loans|The Vatican Architecture, Part Eleven]. For the canon, the point is narrower. By the time the Roman families became the "black nobility", the decisions about scripture had long since passed to councils, congregations and the pope himself. The families had stood at the gate for centuries. They had rarely held the key.
