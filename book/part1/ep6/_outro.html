Part Six followed the people who made a convicted sex offender respectable again: a man paid to bury search results, a firm that wrote the strategy, publicists who offered to steer reporters, editors who did not publish, scientists and universities that took the money, and friends who coached him. Almost none of them broke a law. That is the point. The machine that protected Epstein socially was made of ordinary professional services, sold or given to a man everyone could have looked up.

Part Seven turns to the question that has drawn more attention than any other, and that the record can answer only in part: whether any intelligence service was involved.
