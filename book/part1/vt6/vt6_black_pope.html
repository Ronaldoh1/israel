The "Black Pope": a Nickname and an Office

Much writing about hidden power in Rome starts from a phrase: the "Black Pope". In that writing the head of the Jesuits is the real ruler and the pope in white is his front. A milder version describes "White Pope and Black Pope offices", with the Jesuit general as the layer that implements the Vatican's decisions; the institutional role it implies does not survive a check against the record. This section sets out where the nickname comes from, what the office it refers to actually controls, who holds it as of October 2026, and what the record shows when the two "popes" have disagreed.

Where the nickname comes from

"Black Pope" is not a title in any document of the Society of Jesus or the Holy See. It is an informal name for the Superior General of the Jesuits, most likely taken from the plain black cassock of the order against the white of the pope, and it may also reflect the fear of Jesuit influence in Protestant Europe in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. [cite:vt6_wiki_supgen] The Catholic apologetics organisation Catholic Answers describes it as a Roman joke from the period when the Jesuits were the most prominent missionary order, treating their head as a second pope, and calls conspiracy readings of it a "garbled understanding of a Catholic in-joke." [cite:vt6_ca_blackpope] That is the Catholic side's own account of the name; the documentary point it rests on, that the Superior General has no office in the government of the Holy See, is tested below against the constitution of the Curia and against the record.

The conspiratorial reading has its own documented history. The Southern Poverty Law Center traced one prominent modern version, Eric Jon Phelps's "Vatican Assassins", to centuries of Protestant anti-Jesuit writing and to the nativist Know Nothings and American Protective Association, whose texts Phelps reissued. In his version the Jesuits rule the world "directly or through their Jewish and Masonic proxies"; the SPLC classed the site as a hate site. [cite:vt6_splc_phelps] The two prejudices are joined in that sentence. A theory that begins as a story about Catholics ends by naming Jews as the hidden instruments. This volume treats neither as evidence.

What the office controls

The Superior General governs one religious order. He is elected by the General Congregation, a gathering of Jesuit delegates that the order calls "the highest authority in the Society of Jesus" and that meets when the generalship falls vacant through death or resignation, or when circumstances require. [cite:vt6_wiki_gc] He is elected for life, as popes are, though the last three generals before the present one, Pedro Arrupe, Peter-Hans Kolvenbach and Adolfo Nicolás, resigned. He holds "governing power over all the members of the Society," which he normally exercises through the provincial superiors he appoints. [cite:vt6_wiki_supgen] His authority comes from the vows of Jesuits and ends with them: it reaches about a religious order and its works, such as schools and universities (examined in [scene:vt8_jesuits|Part Eight]), and no further.

The order's own law places it under the pope, not beside him. The founding Formula of the Institute defines the Society's purpose as serving "the Church, his spouse, under the Roman Pontiff." The professed members take a fourth vow of special obedience to the pope, whose "entire purport," the 35th General Congregation restated in 2008, "was and is with regard to missions": through it "the whole body of the Society puts itself at the disposition of the ministry of the Successor of Peter." [cite:vt6_gc35_d4] The Superior General holds no post in the Secretariat of State and heads no dicastery. The constitution of 2022 lists the Curia's offices in full, and the generalship of a religious order is not among them; the office holders as of October 2026 are set out in [scene:vt6_curia_2022|the previous section]. When the two "popes" disagreed

If the Superior General controlled the pope, the record of their conflicts would show it. It shows the reverse. On 21 July 1773 Clement XIV suppressed the Society throughout the world by the brief Dominus ac Redemptor: "We abolish and suppress the oft-mentioned Society." [cite:vt6_wiki_suppression] The Superior General of the day, Lorenzo Ricci, was imprisoned in the papal fortress of Castel Sant'Angelo and died there on 24 November 1775, having protested that the Society had given no grounds for its suppression. [cite:vt6_wiki_ricci] The order existed again only because a later pope, Pius VII, restored it in 1814. [cite:vt6_wiki_suppression]

The same pattern recurred in living memory. After Pedro Arrupe suffered a stroke in 1981, the Jesuits expected Vincent O'Keefe, the vicar general Arrupe had named, to lead them until a General Congregation could meet. In October 1981 John Paul II intervened and appointed his own delegate, Paolo Dezza, to govern the order in the interim; the Jesuits elected a new general, Peter-Hans Kolvenbach, only at the 33rd General Congregation in 1983. [cite:vt6_wiki_dezza] In both episodes the decision ran from the pope to the Jesuit general and was enforced against him.

Who holds it, October 2026

The present Superior General is Arturo Sosa Abascal, a Venezuelan Jesuit elected the 31st general by the 36th General Congregation on 14 October 2016, in succession to Adolfo Nicolás, who had resigned. [cite:vt6_je_sosa] He is the first Latin American in the post. As of October 2026 he remained in office: he made a visitation of a United States province from 26 January to 12 February 2026, and a Ghanaian newspaper reported on 12 October 2026 his visit to Ghana from 12 to 15 October, in the tenth anniversary year of his election. [cite:vt6_slu_sosa2026] [cite:vt6_gt_sosa2026] No convocation of a 37th General Congregation, which would be needed to elect a successor, was found in the sources checked for this volume; the reference list of congregations ends with the 36th in 2016. [cite:vt6_wiki_gc] His public role is visible and documented: visitations, letters to the order, meetings with provinces. Nothing in that record places him in the chain of decision of the Holy See.

The Society's own position, stated by its General Congregation, is that its distinctive bond is one of obedience to the pope for mission. [cite:vt6_gc35_d4] The conspiratorial position is that this obedience is a cover. That claim cannot be tested by any document, because it treats every document of obedience as part of the cover. The record that can be tested, suppression, imprisonment, restoration by papal act and papal intervention in the order's government, runs one way. The nickname survives because two men in Rome wear different colours. The authority runs from the pope to the general and not the other way. The coloured-pope scheme also has a third colour added on the internet, the "Grey Pope"; the next section takes it on its own terms.
