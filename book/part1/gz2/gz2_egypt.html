Nineteen Years Under Egypt, 1949–1967

The Cold War volume of this library follows the border war between Israel and Egypt in detail, and much of it was fought in and around Gaza. This section gathers what that volume established about the strip under Egyptian rule, with links to the full accounts, so that the story of Gaza can be read in one place. The pattern it shows is of a territory whose people belonged to no state, policed by one army and raided by another, and treated by both as a security problem before anything else.

A frontier of raids. Refugees crossing the armistice line to reach their old fields and homes, and armed infiltrators among them, made the strip's edge violent from the start, and Israel answered with reprisal raids, a policy argued over inside the Israeli government itself. [cite:rs_cw2_caplan_asia] In late August 1953, according to the historians Walid Khalidi and Neil Caplan, a unit led by the 25-year-old Ariel Sharon attacked the al-Burj refugee camp in the strip. [cite:rs_cw2_jps_qibya] On the night of 28 February 1955 Israeli forces crossed into the strip and attacked an Egyptian army camp in what Israel's Defense Ministry archive records as Operation Black Arrow; the archive counts thirty-eight Egyptian soldiers and eight Israelis killed, while Egypt's letter to the Security Council gave 37 soldiers and 2 civilians killed. [cite:rs_cw2_mod_black_arrow] [cite:rs_cw2_un_s3365] The library's [scene:cw2_gaza_raid|account of the Gaza raid] sets out its causes and its consequences.

Gaza against its own rulers. One of those consequences was a revolt in the strip against Egypt itself. On 1 March 1955 a mass demonstration in Gaza chanted against relocation and against "US agents"; Egyptian police fired on it and killed a textile worker, Hosni Belal. The protesters' main demand was the cancellation of a plan to resettle refugees in the Sinai, and the protests were organized jointly by communists and the Muslim Brotherhood. [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza] The plan was real: UNRWA had been working with Egypt on Sinai schemes since 1950, and in June 1953 it had reserved $30 million of its $200 million reintegration fund for a scheme to resettle 60,000 Gaza refugees there. [cite:rs_cw1_newlines_sinai]

The fida'i units. In September 1955 Egypt formed Palestinian fida'i units, Battalion 141, under Lieutenant Colonel Mustafa Hafez, based in Gaza, and their raids into Israel continued until Israeli intelligence assassinated Hafez in July 1956. [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza] In mid-August 1956 mines and attacks near the strip killed four Israeli citizens, and the next day nine Egyptians were killed when their car was ambushed. [cite:rs_cw2_unyb1956] By the eve of the Suez war, Ben-Gurion told the Knesset that Egypt "still sits in the Gaza Strip." [cite:rs_cw3_knesset_15oct56]

Occupation and killings, 1956. Israeli forces entered the strip on 2 November 1956, in the first days of the Sinai campaign, and governed some 300,000 people, about three-quarters of them refugees of 1948. [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza] Within ten days, at Khan Yunis on 3 November and at Rafah on 12 November, Israeli troops killed large numbers of men; the counts are disputed and set out in [scene:cw3_gaza_killings|the library's account of Khan Yunis and Rafah]. Benny Morris records that "some 275 civilians and POWs were murdered" at Khan Yunis according to the Palestinians and UNRWA, and that at Rafah Israel said 48 were killed and UNRWA 111. [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza] Israel denied that either event was a massacre. [cite:rs_cw3_time_sacco] Ben-Gurion hoped to keep the strip, telling the Knesset in January 1957 that "this Strip was never Egyptian," but under international, mainly American, pressure Israel withdrew completely on 8 March 1957. [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza] A UN Emergency Force stayed on the Egyptian side of the line until Nasser ordered it out in May 1967. [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza]

The quiet decade. After 1957 the strip was quieter. Egypt issued a Basic Law for it in 1955, in force from 1958, with four seats on its Legislative Council reserved for refugees; it continued to issue identity documents but not citizenship, and to restrict movement in and out. [cite:gz_palquest_refugees] [cite:gz_wiki_history] The Palestine Liberation Organization, founded in 1964, named the Gaza Strip in its charter as territory over which it claimed no regional sovereignty, a clause the library's account of [scene:cw6_summits_plo|the PLO's founding] sets in context. [cite:rs_cw6_charter_1964] In May 1967 Egypt's chief of staff asked the UN force to withdraw from its observation posts on the border; the UN Secretary-General, U Thant, replied that any withdrawal would have to be of all its troops from Gaza and Sinai, and the force's departure became one of the steps toward war. [cite:rs_cw6_frus19_d6]
