Prisons, Pleas and the Death Penalty

The fairest way to compare how many people two states lock up is to use one compiler for both. The World Prison Brief, kept by the Institute for Crime and Justice Policy Research at Birkbeck, University of London, counts everyone held in prisons and jails, including people awaiting trial, and divides by the national population. On that basis the United States held 1,833,700 people at the end of 2023, 542 per 100,000 residents, and Israel held 19,756 people on 31 December 2023, 217 per 100,000 on an estimated population of 9.12 million. [cite:us4_wpb_us] [cite:us4_wpb_il] The American rate was about two and a half times Israel's. That single comparison, used in the simulation's chapter 40, hides two very different histories, and the Israeli figure means something different from the American one.

The American rise and partial fall

In 1980 the United States held 503,586 people, 220 per 100,000, roughly where it had been for four decades (201 in 1940, 161 in 1970). The number then rose every year for nearly three decades: 457 per 100,000 in 1990, 683 in 2000 and a peak of 755 in 2008, when 2.3 million people were behind bars. It has fallen since, to 642 in 2018, sharply to 505 in 2020, the first pandemic year, and back up to 541 in 2022 and 542 in 2023. [cite:us4_wpb_us] [cite:us4a_wpb_us] The present rate is still about two and a half times that of 1980. About a quarter of those held (25.5 per cent in 2023) had not been convicted and were awaiting trial. [cite:us4_wpb_us]

Israel: a series with two breaks

Israel's series begins at 138 per 100,000 in 1972 and stayed in a band of about 140–190 for three decades: 189 in 1990, 146 in 1995, 158 in 2000. It then jumped, to 208 in 2004 and 302 in 2006, and stayed near 300 until 2008. [cite:us4_wpb_il] [cite:us4a_wpb_il] The jump reflects a change in who counted as an Israel Prison Service inmate rather than a crime wave. Palestinians held for security offences had been kept in army-run camps; under a national policy of "relieving the army of all its prisons", the military handed facilities such as Ketziot and Megiddo to the Prison Service, and Ofer, the last of them, was transferred on 3 October 2006. [cite:us4_jpost_ketziot] [cite:us4_wiki_ofer] From then on Israel's total combines two populations held under different law: Israeli citizens and residents convicted or accused of ordinary crimes, and Palestinians from the occupied territories held as "security" prisoners. The total fell back to 153 per 100,000 in 2021 and rose again to 217 by the end of 2023, after the arrests that followed the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023. [cite:us4_wpb_il] By December 2023 the Prison Service told the Knesset that it held about 19,372 people against a legal maximum of 14,500, that about 3,000 slept on mattresses on the floor, and that it was "very close to using up all the places for prisoners". [cite:us4_toi_jails2023] The World Prison Brief puts occupancy at 136 per cent. [cite:us4_wpb_il]

Because of this mix, Israel's headline rate cannot be read as a measure of how often Israel imprisons its own citizens. The simulation's estimate that the ordinary-crime rate is about 108–122 per 100,000 is its own back-of-envelope calculation, and this book does not repeat it; the Prison Service has not published a criminal-only series that could be checked. What can be said is that by October 2026, according to HaMoked's monthly figures from the Prison Service, 9,265 Palestinians were held in the "security" category, including those held as "unlawful combatants", a figure equal to almost half of the whole prison population at the end of 2023. [cite:us4_hamoked_charts]

How cases end: the plea bargain

In both countries the trial is the exception. Pew Research Center's analysis of federal data found that of 71,954 defendants in American federal criminal cases in fiscal year 2022, 89.5 per cent pleaded guilty, 8.2 per cent had their cases dismissed, 1.9 per cent were convicted at trial and 290, or 0.4 per cent, were acquitted. [cite:us4_pew_plea2023] Federal cases are a small share of American prosecutions, most of which are in state courts, and no national state-court series was found for this book. In Israel a study by the Supreme Court's research centre and Haifa University, reported in May 2012, found that more than 85 per cent of criminal cases filed in the courts ended in a plea bargain (76.5 per cent in the magistrates' courts and 85.7 per cent in the district courts) and that 0.3 per cent ended in full acquittal. [cite:us4_ynet_plea2012] The Hebrew University sociologist Josh Guetzkow, studying 2,012 Israeli cases decided in 2010–2011, concluded that plea bargaining in that period "was marked by the miscarriage of justice": defendants who pleaded guilty were, on his estimate, less likely to have been convicted at trial than similar defendants who went to trial. [cite:us4_osu_guetzkow] Both figures are snapshots; neither country publishes a long, comparable series on how cases end. Military courts in the West Bank, where the conviction rate in 2010 was 99.74 per cent, are a separate system, taken up in the next section. [cite:wb_972_conviction]

The death penalty

The two systems differ most sharply on execution. The United States resumed executions in 1977 after the Supreme Court allowed new death-penalty laws. Executions peaked at 98 in 1999, then fell to 85 in 2000, 46 in 2010 and 17 in 2020, before rising again to 25 in 2024 and 47 in 2025, the highest in fifteen years, in 11 states; Florida alone carried out 19. [cite:us4_dpic_facts] [cite:us4_dpic_2025] By 14 May 2026 there had been 12 more. New death sentences, which peaked at 325 in 1986, numbered 23 in 2025. On 1 October 2025, 2,024 people were on death row. Twenty-seven states have the death penalty and 23 do not; four of the 27 (California, Ohio, Oregon and Pennsylvania) have moratoriums on executions. Since 1973, 202 people sentenced to death have been exonerated. [cite:us4_dpic_facts] Public support, measured by Gallup, stood at 52 per cent in 2025, the lowest in fifty years. [cite:us4_dpic_2025]

Israel kept the death penalty on its books for genocide, wartime espionage and certain terror offences and has not executed anyone since it hanged Adolf Eichmann in 1962. [cite:us4_ap_deathlaw] [cite:us4a_ap_deathlaw] On 30 March 2026 the Knesset voted 62–48 for a law, pressed by National Security Minister Ben-Gvir and supported in person by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, that makes hanging the default sentence for West Bank Palestinians convicted in military courts of killings defined as rejecting Israel's existence; military judges may impose life imprisonment only in "special circumstances". In civilian courts, which try Israeli citizens, judges may choose between death and life, and legal experts quoted by the Associated Press said this effectively excludes Jewish citizens. The law requires no unanimous verdict, allows no clemency and does not apply to people already in prison. Ben-Gvir said "whoever takes a life, the State of Israel will take their life". The Association for Civil Rights in Israel petitioned the High Court, calling the law "discriminatory by design", and the foreign ministers of Australia, Britain, France, Germany and Italy called it "de facto discriminatory". [cite:us4_ap_deathlaw] The court asked the government on 31 March 2026 to respond to requests for an interim order; the commander of Central Command signed an order applying the law in the West Bank on 17 May 2026, and the law requires a sentence to be carried out within 90 days. As of the latest reporting checked for this book, no ruling on the petitions and no death sentence under the law had been reported; this should be rechecked after Israel's election on 27 October 2026. [cite:us4_wiki_deathlaw]

Conditions inside

Since October 2023 the record on conditions in Israeli custody has been dominated by deaths of Palestinian detainees. Physicians for Human Rights Israel counted at least 94 Palestinians who died in Israeli custody between 7 October 2023 and August 2025, 68 from Gaza and 26 from the West Bank, 29 of them at the army's Sde Teiman camp, against fewer than 30 in the previous ten years; post-mortems it reviewed showed bruising consistent with beatings, broken ribs and untreated infections. The Prison Service said it operates "in accordance with the law and under the supervision of official oversight bodies", and the army said detainees receive three meals a day and a medical check on arrival. [cite:us4_cbs_custody] Ben-Gvir had said he had cut prison conditions to the legal minimum and that prisons would no longer be "summer camps"; in September 2025 the Supreme Court ordered the state to provide more and better food to Palestinian inmates. [cite:us4_cbs_custody] The American record on conditions, from solitary confinement to deaths in local jails, is large and is not measured here with a national series; that is a gap in this part. What the two series in this section do show is that the United States imprisons a far larger share of its own population than it did in 1980 and executes again at a rising pace, while Israel's prisons have, since 2006 and above all since 2023, come to hold a large population of people from territory it controls whose residents are not its citizens, many of them under military rather than civilian law.
