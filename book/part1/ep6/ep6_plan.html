The Plan, in Writing

The rehabilitation began online. In December 2010, a year and a half after his release, Epstein complained in an email that his Google results were "not good". The man handling the problem was Al Seckel, an eccentric collector whose wife is Ghislaine Maxwell's sister. Over the following months Seckel sent Epstein screenshots of his progress: a Daily Beast article removed from the first page of results; a Huffington Post story pushed from seventh place to eighth and then to about tenth; damaging phrases gone from Google's autocomplete suggestions; a photograph and caption on Epstein's Wikipedia page replaced. "Bots did the sweep," he wrote. [cite:ep6_guardian_reputation] Other emails describe a team in the Philippines building links to websites and "pseudo sites" that pushed his work in science and sport to the top. [cite:ep6_gma_seo2026] The work, Seckel warned, needed constant maintenance, and his bills followed: about 20,000, then another 10,000, then another 10,000. Epstein objected that nobody had mentioned a monthly fee. [cite:ep6_guardian_reputation] Seckel died in France in 2015.

The strategy itself was written down by professionals. In June 2011 the London firm Osborne & Partners sent Epstein a memo titled "Issues of Reputation". It advised him to keep out of the tabloids, to rebuild his profile as a supporter of science and philanthropy, to host an annual event, to court business and financial journalists, and, as "an urgent priority", to clean up his search results. It also advised him to keep away from Prince Andrew and Sarah Ferguson. [cite:ep6_guardian_reputation]

Publicists offered to shape the coverage directly. In March 2011 R. Couri Hay offered to influence a Newsweek story about Epstein's return to New York, writing that he was "subtly guiding" the reporter; Hay later told the New York Times that he had declined to work for Epstein. In July 2011 Epstein asked the publicist Peggy Siegal to persuade Arianna Huffington to have the Huffington Post investigate Virginia Giuffre, one of his accusers. Siegal says she never passed the request on, and the Huffington Post says it never reached the newsroom. [cite:ep6_guardian_reputation]

Every step of that plan was carried out by someone, and the rest of this part follows them in turn. None of it required anyone to deny the conviction. It required only that the conviction be made hard to find, and that something more attractive be put in front of it.
