The Withdrawal and the Shebaa Farms

By the late 1990s the security zone had become a political cost inside Israel. In 1997, 73 soldiers were killed in a helicopter accident, and four mothers whose sons were serving there started a movement, Four Mothers, that demanded only one thing: withdrawal. It drew in bereaved parents, former soldiers and a former army commander in Lebanon, and made open criticism of the army's presence there acceptable. [cite:hz4_csm_mothers] Ehud Barak pledged in 1999 to withdraw and, as prime minister, promised to "bring the boys home" by July 2000. [cite:hz4_olj_sla] [cite:hz4_csm_mothers] [cite:hz4_merip_2000] After his pledge, desertions from the SLA became common. [cite:hz4_olj_sla]

The counts of what the zone cost Israel depend on the window. CRS, citing Reuters, gave roughly 250 Israeli soldiers killed between 1985 and April 2000; Israel Hayom, marking the twentieth anniversary, gave 414 for Israel's presence in south Lebanon without stating the start date. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] [cite:hz4_ih_20yrs] The SLA said it had lost 621 fighters and 200 civilians from its founding to March 2000. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] The public record does not show a comparable official count of Lebanese civilians and Hezbollah fighters killed in the zone over the fifteen years; Part Nine returns to the counting. [scene:hz9_ledger|The ledger]

Forty-eight hours in May. The withdrawal came weeks ahead of schedule and all at once. Word of the first pullbacks spread on 22 May 2000; Lebanese civilians and Hezbollah fighters moved into villages faster than the Israeli army and the SLA could leave them. [cite:hz4_merip_2000] Barak ordered the withdrawal on 23 May and it was complete the next day. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] What had been planned as a gradual, UN-supervised handover was, in MERIP's words, "crammed into a chaotic 48 hours." [cite:hz4_merip_2000] At Khiam, villagers broke open the cells. [cite:hz4_merip_2000]

The end of the SLA. The militia Israel had paid for fifteen years abandoned its posts and dissolved rather than fight alone. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] About 1,500 members crossed into Israel, with dependants making 6,000 to 7,000 people in all, mostly mid- and high-ranking Christian officers; Israel had expected only a few hundred. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] The Lebanese army held 1,488 who stayed. A Beirut military tribunal convicted 77 of them on 5 June with sentences of one month to five years, far lighter than the death and life sentences passed in absentia on senior officers. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] About 7,500 former members and their families still lived in Israel when L'Orient-Le Jour reviewed the militia's history; a 2011 Lebanese law meant to let them return never received its implementing decrees, largely because of Hezbollah's opposition. [cite:hz4_olj_sla]

Who won. In Lebanon, 25 May is marked as the day the south was liberated, and Hezbollah claimed the withdrawal as the resistance's achievement, while saying it was not yet complete. [cite:hz4_lbc_arms] [cite:hz4_merip_2000] Israel presented it as a unilateral decision taken by an elected government and certified by the UN as full compliance with Security Council Resolution 425 of 1978. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] Both accounts hold as far as they go: an Israeli government decided to leave, and the Israeli movement that pressed it to do so cited soldiers' deaths in the zone, including those caused by Hezbollah's attacks, as its reason.

The Blue Line. Because Lebanon and Israel had no agreed border, the UN drew a line of withdrawal. In a report of 22 May 2000 the Secretary-General used, for the eastern sector, the old boundary between the areas of UNIFIL, the peacekeepers in Lebanon, and UNDOF, the observers on the Golan. The Security Council endorsed the report on 23 May, and on 16 June 2000 the Secretary-General reported that Israel had withdrawn in compliance with Resolution 425. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] The line was drawn "without prejudice" to any future border agreement between Lebanon and Syria. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa]

The Shebaa Farms. That line left in Israeli hands a strip of about 25 square kilometres on the western slope of Mount Hermon, around fourteen farmsteads, known as the Shebaa Farms. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] Israel had taken it from Syria in 1967. The UN treated it as Syrian territory on the Golan, governed by Resolutions 242 and 338, not by 425. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] Lebanon claimed it, citing title deeds, a reported 1964 joint commission and a 1966 map; Syria's foreign minister told the Secretary-General on 16 May 2000 that Syria supported the claim, but the two governments never filed an agreed border to settle it. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] Israel's position was that the farms are part of the Syrian Golan and that the UN's determination bound it. The United States endorsed the UN's withdrawal requirements and took no position on who owns the farms. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa]

The dispute mattered because of Taif. Hezbollah's weapons had been tolerated as "resistance" to occupation, a status the agreement's text does not mention. [cite:hz4_wiki_taif] [cite:hz4a_olj_taif] If the occupation was over, so was the exemption. Nasrallah said in May 2000 that the withdrawal would not be complete until the Shebaa Farms were freed and the Lebanese detainees held in Israel were released. [cite:hz4_merip_2000] Some analysts argued that Syria kept the issue open to keep pressure on Israel over the Golan, which Syria denied. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] Whether the farms were a real grievance, a pretext or both is an argument, and both sides of it are on the record. What the record shows is that a 25-square-kilometre strip gave an armed movement a stated reason to stay armed after the occupation that created it had ended.

7 October 2000. Less than five months after the withdrawal, Hezbollah ambushed an Israeli patrol on the border near the Shebaa Farms and took three soldiers: Adi Avitan, Benyamin Avraham and Omar Sawaid. [cite:hz4_wiki_raid2000] [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] Israeli military intelligence later assessed that they were killed during the attack or immediately after it. Hezbollah also seized Elchanan Tenenbaum, an Israeli businessman and reserve colonel, after luring him abroad. On 29 January 2004 Israel released 435 prisoners, among them about 400 Palestinians and 30 Lebanese and other Arab prisoners, returned the remains of 59 Lebanese and handed over maps of its minefields in the south; it received Tenenbaum alive and the remains of the three soldiers. [cite:hz4_wiki_raid2000] The capture set the pattern for the next one: in July 2006 another cross-border raid to take soldiers for exchange started a war. [scene:hz5_war|The 2006 war]
