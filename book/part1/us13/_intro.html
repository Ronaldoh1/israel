Part Thirteen compares the two labour markets as each state has shaped them by law. It sets America's federal minimum wage, a fixed dollar sum that Congress last raised in 2009, beside Israel's, which since 1987 has been pegged to the average wage, and follows both from 1980 to 2026, with the state minimums that now set most American floors. It compares the hours worked in each country on the OECD's series, the paid leave each law guarantees, and the gap between men's and women's pay, stating where the two countries' measures differ. It then traces the fall of organised labour in both countries: the Histadrut, which in 1980 was employer, bank owner and health insurer as well as union, and America's firm-by-firm unions, whose membership halved from 1983. It ends with the workers neither system counts as its own: Palestinians on Israeli work permits, whose numbers collapsed after October 2023, the foreign workers recruited to replace them, and the millions of unauthorised workers in the United States.
