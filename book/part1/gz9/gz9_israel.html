Inside the Two Governments

The coalition. Israel's decisions on Gaza are taken by Benjamin Netanyahu's government, which depends for its majority on parties of the religious right. Their leaders have stated their positions openly. When the January 2025 ceasefire was put to the cabinet, the national security minister, Itamar Ben-Gvir, said: "If this irresponsible agreement is approved and implemented, the Jewish Power Party will not be part of the government and will leave it." He argued that aid and utilities to Gaza should be "completely stopped" to force the release of the hostages, and he called on the finance minister, Bezalel Smotrich, to quit with him. [cite:gz_aj_bengvir] Smotrich, who has long advocated what he calls the "voluntary emigration" of Gazans, told his party later that month that he was developing an "operational plan" to move them out of the territory, and threatened to leave the government if that policy was not carried out after the ceasefire's first phase. [cite:gz_jc_smotrich] The ceasefire collapsed on 18 March 2025, as Part Seven describes. [scene:gz7_war|The course of the war]

The record here is of the ministers' public statements and threats. Whether those pressures, rather than the government's stated reasons, decided particular turns of the war is a question of motive the record does not settle; the government's stated reason for resuming the war in March 2025 was Hamas's refusal to release more hostages on Israel's terms. [cite:gz_wiki_ceasefire2025]

Hamas's leadership. The men who led Hamas on October 7 have mostly been killed. Ismail Haniyeh, the head of its political bureau, was killed in Tehran in July 2024, an act Israel later acknowledged. Yahya Sinwar, its leader in Gaza and then its overall leader, was killed by the Israeli army in Gaza in October 2024. Israel says it killed Mohammed Deif, the commander of the armed wing, in a strike near Khan Younis in July 2024, his deputy Marwan Issa in March 2024, and Yahya Sinwar's brother Mohammad, who succeeded to the military command, in May 2025. Khalil al-Hayya, based in Qatar and a member of a five-man leadership council, is seen as its most influential figure abroad. [cite:gz_almon_hamasleaders]
