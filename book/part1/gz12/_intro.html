Part Eleven asks how the war reached American audiences, and why so much of what earlier parts record was seen by so few of them. It starts with access: who was allowed into Gaza to report, under what rules, and how many of the journalists who were there were killed. It then follows the coverage inside American newsrooms, through their own internal rules, independent measurements of what they published, and the ownership of one network, and ends with what Americans came to think. Where the record documents a cause, the part names it; where the cause is a question of motive, it gives the evidence and the outlets' own answers.
