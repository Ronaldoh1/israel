The Peace Plans

The Madrid conference of 1991 brought Israel face to face with its neighbours and the Palestinians. [cite:os_wiki_madrid] The Oslo Accords followed: mutual recognition and an interim Palestinian Authority in 1993, the West Bank divided into Areas A, B and C in 1995, and every final-status question left for later. Rabin was assassinated by an Israeli opponent. [cite:oslo_1_text] [cite:oslo_2_text] [cite:wiki_yigal_amir]

The summit at Camp David failed in July 2000. Clinton blamed Arafat; his own adviser described shared errors and no written offer. [cite:modeldiplomat_camp_david] [cite:independent_malley_barak] The Second Intifada that followed killed about 1,000 Israelis and over 3,000 Palestinians and produced the separation barrier; Israel left Gaza's settlements in 2005 while keeping its borders. [cite:milwiki_second_intifada] [cite:os_icj_wall] [cite:crs_disengagement_2005]

Every plan of these years was a plan to divide the land taken in 1967, and during them the settler population of the West Bank grew more than fourfold. [cite:peacenow_settlement_population] Recognition of Palestine reached 157 states in 2025, while the Knesset voted against unilateral recognition and support for two states fell to about a third on each side. [cite:aljazeera_157_list] [cite:toi_knesset_99] [cite:globalsecurity_two_state] From 1967 to Oslo tells this in full.
