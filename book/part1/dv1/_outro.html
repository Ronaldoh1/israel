Part One has fixed the terms. Of the three cities, only Vatican City is a sovereign state, and only since 1929; its foreign relations belong to the Holy See, a church authority recognized by 184 states. [cite:dv1_wiki_vatican] [cite:dv1_dfat_holysee] The City of London is a local authority with privileges that Parliament preserves and could remove, down to its own police force. [cite:dv1_wiki_city] [cite:dv1_police_act_1996] The District of Columbia is governed in the last resort by Congress, which reviews its laws and approves its budget. [cite:dv1_conan_dc] [cite:dv1_crs_dc_homerule] Israel, the fourth, is a state under the Montevideo test in its own right, and under that convention its existence does not depend on any other state's recognition. [cite:dv1_montevideo]

What is settled, then, is that the power the three names stand for lies mostly outside the three cities themselves: in the London market and the British state, in the Holy See, and in the federal government of the United States. What is contested is whether each of those powers acted as a parent, and in which years. What the record leaves open, so far, is whether any of them coordinated with the others. Each part that follows applies the same five-step test, beginning with [scene:dv2_square_mile|the Square Mile].
