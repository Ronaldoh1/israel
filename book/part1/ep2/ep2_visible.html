In Plain Sight, 1998–2005

The years around the turn of the century were the most visible in the whole record, and the least examined. Epstein had his island and his bank. He was photographed with royalty, his own pilots logged who flew with him, and he was already the subject of an FBI complaint. Nothing about him was hidden in any meaningful sense. The material that later took a decade of litigation and an act of Congress to bring out was, in these years, simply not being looked at.

Virginia Giuffre

In 2000 a sixteen-year-old named Virginia Roberts had a summer job at the spa at Mar-a-Lago, the Palm Beach club where her father worked in maintenance. There she met Ghislaine Maxwell, who, by Giuffre's account, offered her work as a travelling masseuse for Epstein; she said the grooming began almost at once. [cite:ep2_ap_giuffre2025] She had been abused as a child, she said later, and had lived on the streets as a fourteen-year-old runaway, and Maxwell and Epstein exploited that history. [cite:ep2_ap_giuffre2025] In a 2001 photograph taken at Maxwell's house in London, Prince Andrew stands with his arm around her, then seventeen, with Maxwell behind them. It became the most reproduced image of the case. [cite:nbc_andrew]

Under her married name, Virginia Giuffre became the most prominent of the survivors who spoke publicly, and the litigation she brought produced much of the documentary record this book rests on. She settled with Epstein in 2009; documents unsealed in 2022 showed she received $500,000. She sued Maxwell for defamation in 2016, and the case settled in 2017. In 2022 she settled her lawsuit against Prince Andrew, for a sum reported at $16 million. He denied her allegations, said he had no recollection of meeting her and questioned the photograph. [cite:ep2_ap_giuffre2025] In her deposition she said: "My whole life revolved around just pleasing these men." [cite:ep2_ap_giuffre2025] In a 2016 deposition, unsealed in January 2024, she said of Donald Trump, whose club it was: "I don't think Donald Trump participated in anything"; she said she had never seen him take part, though she had heard he had been at Epstein's house. The book records that because the standard runs in both directions. [cite:ep2_bi_giuffre_trump2024]

Virginia Giuffre died at her home in Western Australia on 25 April 2025, aged forty-one; her publicist said she died by suicide. [cite:ep2_ap_giuffre2025] Her memoir, Nobody's Girl, was published that October. Her sworn allegations are examined name by name in Parts Four and Five, at the level the record allows.

The record that was built anyway

It is worth naming precisely who produced what the public now knows. The depositions, the exhibits, the bank discovery and the eleven-year victims'-rights case that ended with Judge Marra's ruling were assembled by people who had been harmed and by the private lawyers who took their cases. [cite:marra] [cite:garcia] Through these years and after, the record was built mostly by civil litigation, not by investigation. That is an observation about who does accountability work in this system when the institutions assigned to it stop short, and it is a thread the rest of the book follows.
