The Land Since 1948

When the war of 1948 ended, the armistice agreements of 1949 left Israel within lines that became its de facto border, the Green Line; Iraq, alone among the Arab states that fought, never signed one. [cite:wiki_oslo_areas] [cite:govil_aliyah] Jordan held the West Bank and East Jerusalem until 1967. [cite:wiki_oslo_areas]

In June 1967 Israel took Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan in six days. [cite:wiki_six_day_war] It gave Sinai back to Egypt under the peace of 1979. [cite:wiki_camp_david_accords] It annexed East Jerusalem and declared it part of its capital in a Basic Law of 1980, measures the Security Council declared null and void. [cite:unsc478_text] The Oslo II agreement of 1995 divided the West Bank into three areas: Area A under Palestinian civil and security rule, about 18 percent; Area B under Palestinian civil rule with joint security, about 22 percent; and Area C under full Israeli control, about 61 percent. [cite:wiki_oslo_areas] [cite:anera_areas_abc] In 2005 Israel left Gaza's settlements while keeping control of its borders. [cite:crs_disengagement_2005]

On the ground, settlement went on through every one of these changes. Settlers in the West Bank, outside East Jerusalem, went from about 110,000 at Oslo to about 500,000; with East Jerusalem the total is about 737,000. [cite:peacenow_settlement_population] On 19 July 2024 the International Court of Justice found the continued occupation unlawful. [cite:icj_press_19jul24]
