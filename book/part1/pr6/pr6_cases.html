Hamza Dahdouh and Mustafa Thuraya, 7 January 2024

Hamza Dahdouh, 27, Wael Dahdouh's eldest son, and Mustafa Thuraya, a videographer who flew a camera drone, were driving back from filming the aftermath of a strike near Khan Younis when an Israeli missile hit their car. Both were killed, with their driver; two freelancers were badly wounded. The next day the army said it had struck "a terrorist who operated an aircraft that posed a threat to IDF troops." Two days later it said Thuraya belonged to Hamas and Dahdouh to Palestinian Islamic Jihad. The Washington Post recovered the footage from Thuraya's drone: "No Israeli soldiers, aircraft, or other military equipment are visible in the footage taken that day." Fourteen witnesses and colleagues gave it "no indications" that either man was doing anything but journalism, and Dahdouh had recently been approved to leave Gaza. Asked for more, the army said it had "nothing further to add." The UN special rapporteur on free expression, Irene Khan, said: "It's not enough to say that we suspected them so we killed them." Ismail al-Ghoul and Rami al-Rifi, 31 July 2024

Ismail al-Ghoul, an Al Jazeera correspondent, and his cameraman, Rami al-Rifi, were killed by an airstrike in al-Shati camp as they reported on the assassination of Hamas's leader, Ismail Haniyeh. The army called al-Ghoul a "Nukhba terrorist," citing a document from a Hamas computer that said he had received his rank in 2007. Al-Ghoul was born in 1997; in 2007 he would have been about ten. Al Jazeera pointed out that Israeli forces had arrested and released him in March 2024. Reporters Without Borders filed complaints with the International Criminal Court; asked for more, the army said it "did not have anything more to say on the topic." [cite:pr_wiki_ghoul]

Anas al-Sharif and Four Colleagues, 10 August 2025

Anas al-Sharif was Al Jazeera's best-known correspondent in northern Gaza. His father had been killed in a strike in December 2023. In July 2025 the Committee to Protect Journalists and the UN's Irene Khan warned that an Israeli campaign accusing him of being a Hamas fighter was putting his life at risk. On 10 August 2025 an Israeli strike on a press tent by the gate of al-Shifa hospital killed him, his Al Jazeera colleagues Mohammed Qreiqeh, Ibrahim Zaher, Mohammed Noufal and Moamen Aliwa, and others. The army said he had led a Hamas rocket cell, citing documents it had released in October 2024. The Guardian called them "an unconvincing dossier of unverified evidence"; the Committee to Protect Journalists said Israel had offered no "credible evidence"; the army gave no reason for killing the others. The UN Secretary-General called for an independent investigation. A message al-Sharif had written in advance was posted after his death: "If these words reach you, know that Israel has succeeded in killing me and silencing my voice." [cite:pr_wiki_sharif]

Nasser Hospital, 25 August 2025

At Nasser hospital in Khan Younis, Israel struck the hospital four times with tank shells; the first strike killed the Reuters cameraman Hussam al-Masri and one other person. As journalists and rescuers climbed an outside staircase to help, a second strike killed twenty more, among them Mariam Dagga, a visual journalist who worked for the Associated Press. In all 22 people were killed, five of them journalists. The army said the target was a suspected Hamas camera on the roof and that six of those killed were linked to Hamas, without giving evidence; Netanyahu's office said Israel "deeply regrets" the incident. A year later the army had published no findings; it said the incident was being examined and that findings would go to the Military Advocate General. An Israeli soldier told the Associated Press that the army had found no evidence linking the camera to Hamas, and that none of the six it named had been the target.
