By the end of 1935 the Mandate had been in operation for twelve years, and the frame of 1922 had been filled in. Some of what filled it was law, much of it institutions and money, and the two communities had filled it very unequally.

The legal layer was settled in practice and contested in principle. Turkey renounced the Arab provinces at Lausanne, in a treaty signed on 24 July 1923 and in force from 6 August 1924; as John Quigley stresses, it renounced them in no one's favor. [cite:rs_rd3_lausanne_fcdo] [cite:rs_rd3_quigley_2026] The League Council's minutes record the Mandate in force from 29 September 1923, and Britain, the League Secretariat and, in 1947, the United Nations treated it as the date. Quigley's challenge holds that the wording came from the Secretary-General's circular, not from a Council resolution. [cite:rs_rd3_unispal_mandate_force] [cite:rs_rd3_quigley_2026] The Citizenship Order of 1925 tied citizenship to residence on 1 August 1925. It left out Arab emigrants abroad and opened naturalization to newcomers; by 1946, on one study's figures, about 130,000 people had been naturalized, 99 percent of them Jews. [cite:rs_rd3_abualia_khalil] [cite:rs_rd3_banko_podcast] Article 11 carried Rutenberg's 70-year monopoly. [cite:zaoerv_mandate_article11] [cite:wiki_israel_electric] In 1930 a League commission ruled the Western Wall Muslim property and sacred to both parties, with Jewish access on Ottoman-era terms. [cite:rs_rd3_unispal_wall_1930]

The administrative layer stayed in British hands alone. A Currency Board appointed by the Colonial Secretary issued the Palestine pound from 1 November 1927, at par with sterling. [cite:wiki_palestine_pound] On the Jewish Agency's figures, revenue rose from LP 2,371,000 in 1922–23 to LP 5,452,000 in 1934–35. [cite:rs_rd3_horowitz_1937] Britain paid £1.5 million for the garrison in 1923–24, but in August 1929 about 150 British and 1,500 local police served the whole country. [cite:league_report_garrison] [cite:rs_rd3_ebsco_wwriots] The administration governed by inquiry and ordinance. Shaw found the tenant-protection laws had failed. Hope Simpson counted 29.4 percent of families landless in 104 villages. [cite:rs_rd3_shaw_palquest_e] [cite:rs_rd3_hope_simpson_unispal] The Passfield White Paper built on both, and MacDonald's letter of 13 February 1931 reinterpreted it away. [cite:w_passfield] [cite:rs_rd3_macdonald_unispal] Britain's one direct lever on immigration was the labor schedule, set at 8,000 for April–September 1935 and cut to 3,250 for the half-year that followed. [cite:rs_rd3_frus1936_d531]

The institutional and financial layer is where the stage's building happened. The Histadrut ran Solel Boneh, Bank Hapoalim and Kupat Holim. [cite:wiki_histadrut] The Anglo-Palestine Bank carried the movement's money. [cite:wiki_palestine_pound] The Keren Hayesod raised $20.1 million from April 1921 to December 1929, $11.0 million of it in the United States. [cite:jta_kh_decade] At Zurich in 1929 the Jewish Agency became half non-Zionist, with the Keren Hayesod as its financial instrument. [cite:israeled_weizmann_vol14] The Jewish National Fund grew from 22,363 dunams in 1920 to roughly 362,000 in 1935, leased on terms that, in its draft lease, bound lessees to cultivate "only with Jewish labour". [cite:jnf_wiki] [cite:rs_rd3_hope_simpson_unispal] Recorded Jewish immigration reached 61,854 in 1935, and the Haavara transfer moved about 105 million Reichsmarks of German goods by the outbreak of war. [cite:rs_rd3_frus1939_d828] [cite:wiki_haavara_agreement] The armed bodies divided as they grew. The Haganah came under a parity command in 1931, the Irgun broke away the same year, and the Revisionists left for the New Zionist Organization in 1935. [cite:rs_rd3_ej_haganah] [cite:rs_rd3_ej_revisionists]

The Palestinian Arab layer was a national movement without a channel into government. The Arab Executive, never recognized, dissolved in 1934 after Musa Kazim al-Husseini's death. [cite:palquest_musa_kazim] What replaced it was more varied and more divided. There was a women's congress in 1929, Istiqlal in 1932, and parties built around the Nashashibi and Husseini camps in 1934–35, which formed a short-lived united front at the end of 1935. [cite:rs_rd3_jerusalemstory_womens] [cite:rs_rd3_wiki_istiqlal] [cite:rs_rd3_palquest_nashashibi] [cite:rs_rd3_palquest_jamal] [cite:rs_rd3_frus1936_d512] Its instruments were delegations, boycotts, the courts at Wadi al-Hawarith and demonstrations. In October 1933, by Murison's count, 26 members of the public and one policeman were killed. [cite:wiki_wadi_al_hawarith] [cite:rs_rd3_murison_palquest] Al-Qassam's band, which, by one account, the Mufti declined to back, shows the other road some took. [cite:rs_rd3_palquest_qassam]

The regional layer tied Palestine to its neighbors by oil and by politics. The Iraq Petroleum Company's line brought its first oil to Haifa on 16 October 1934. [cite:rs_rd3_gulbenkian_pipeline] In December 1931 a General Islamic Congress gathered 130 delegates from 22 countries in Jerusalem. [cite:rs_rd3_mee_congress_1931] Outside Palestine, Jewish communities met state measures in Iraq from 1934, as one source reports, and killing at Constantine. [cite:rs_rd3_sephardivoices_iraq] [cite:rs_rd3_cole_constantine] Germany became a party to Palestine's economy through Haavara two years before the Nuremberg Laws. [cite:ushmm_nuremberg_laws] The American layer was money and method more than policy. American donors supplied more than half of the Keren Hayesod's first decade, the American quota act of 1924, by one account, turned emigrants toward Palestine, and in 1932 Emanuel Neumann founded a committee of prominent, mainly non-Jewish Americans. [cite:jta_kh_decade] [cite:rs_rd3_cie_1924_act] [cite:aja_neumann_bio]

What was settled, then, was the chain of authority and the institutions the Jewish community built beneath it. What was contested ran through every layer, and each side's case had documents behind it. For the Zionist leadership, the MacDonald letter corrected a breach of a Mandate owed "to the Jewish people", and the Passfield White Paper had been the breach. For Arab leaders it was the "Black Letter", and in 1936, 137 Arab officials still cited it as a cause of their lost faith in official pledges. [cite:rs_rd3_macdonald_unispal] [cite:w_passfield] [cite:rs_rd3_officials_memo_1936] The Agency argued that Arabs gained from Jewish investment through revenue, wages and land prices; Hope Simpson found families displaced and not resettled. [cite:rs_rd3_horowitz_1937] [cite:rs_rd3_hope_simpson_unispal] Haavara was a rescue and a breach of the boycott at once. [cite:jvl_haavara] What the record leaves open is no smaller. It does not say who killed Arlosoroff, or who, if anyone, stood above Tehomi in 1924. [cite:rs_rd3_jta_1985_panel] [cite:w_dehaan] It does not settle how many Jews died in August 1929, 133 or 113, how many immigrants came in any year, or whether the Mandate ever validly entered into force. [cite:rs_rd3_jerusalemstory_buraq] [cite:rs_rd3_jvl_porath_table] [cite:rs_rd3_quigley_2026] The structure that met 1936 was lopsided: one community held an apparatus without sovereignty, and the other held a majority without an institution.
