The Nag Hammadi codices were found in December 1945, divided among seven people and partly burned; one was sold to the state for three hundred Egyptian pounds, another paid for a grain merchant's shop. [cite:gn_pearse_nh] Most passed to the dealer Tano, were seized and nationalised for £E 4,000; the first codex was smuggled out, bought for the Jung Institute for 35,000 Swiss francs and kept in a Zurich bank until 1975. [cite:gn_pearse_nh] [cite:gn_pearse_jung] Scholars controlled access, in Robinson's words through "attempts to monopolize them", until the facsimile edition from 1972 and the English translation of 1977. [cite:gn_bar_robinson] [cite:vt25_wiki_nhl] Part Six follows three other Gnostic codices, and one of them leads to the biggest sum in this book.
