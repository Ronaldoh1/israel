His Claims Against His Record

Vance is the first person in this book to have held elected office, so his statements can be tested against official findings, court records and his own disclosures. This section tests eight public claims that shaped how people saw him or the people he spoke about, using the four grades the book applies to everyone. RED marks a claim contradicted by the person's own documents or sworn record. ORANGE marks a claim not delivered, or not supported by the official record. YELLOW marks a reversal or concealment, shown with the person's own explanation. GREY marks a dispute in which credible accounts conflict, with both shown. The word "lie" is used only where the record shows that the person knew a claim was false when making it; one flag below comes close to that bar, and the section explains why it is not used.

Flags on what he said about others

Pets in Springfield (9–10 September 2024): ORANGE. On the morning of 9 September 2024, as a candidate for Vice President, Vance posted that "reports now show that people have had their pets abducted and eaten by people who shouldn't be in this country," referring to Haitian immigrants in Springfield, Ohio. [cite:de12_reason_springfield] The same morning a member of his staff called the Springfield city manager, Bryan Heck, to ask whether the rumours were true. Heck told the Wall Street Journal: "I told him no. There was no verifiable evidence or reports to show this was true," and that he had said the claims were "baseless." [cite:de12_reason_springfield] The reporting does not establish whether the call came before or after the post. The next day Vance wrote that "it's possible, of course, that all of these rumors will turn out to be false," and told readers of his post to "keep the cat memes flowing." [cite:de12_reason_springfield] Ohio's Republican governor, Mike DeWine, called the story "a piece of garbage that was simply not true." [cite:de12_reason_springfield] The one police report Vance's staff supplied to support it concerned a resident's cat that was later found safe in her own basement. [cite:de12_reason_springfield] He said the claims came from "firsthand accounts from my constituents," ten of them "verifiable and confirmable"; no official body has confirmed any. [cite:de12_mediaite_bash]

This is the flag in Vance's record that comes closest to the book's bar for the word "lie": his office had the city's denial on the day he first spread the claim, he acknowledged the next day that it might be false, and he kept repeating it. It does not meet the bar, because the record does not show that he believed the claim false; he has said he relied on his constituents, and what he believed is a question of motive that only he can answer. The grade is ORANGE: a claim not supported, and directly contradicted, by the official record.

"Create stories" (15 September 2024): GREY. On CNN's State of the Union, asked about the pets claim, he said: "If I have to create stories so that the American media actually pays attention to the suffering of the American people, then that's what I'm going to do." [cite:de12_mediaite_bash] Critics read it as an admission of invention; in the same answer he gave his own meaning: "I say that we're creating a story, meaning we're creating the American media focusing on it." [cite:de12_mediaite_bash] Both readings are available from the words, and the book does not choose between them.

Springfield's Haitians as "illegal aliens" (September 2024): ORANGE. Vance repeatedly called Springfield's Haitian residents illegal immigrants, and said, "I'm still going to call them illegal aliens." [cite:de12_pf_haitians] City and state officials said most were in the country under humanitarian parole or temporary protected status, both lawful forms of temporary permission to live and work in the United States, and PolitiFact rated his description false. [cite:de12_pf_haitians] His argument, stated in his own words, was not that the permissions did not exist but that they were an illegitimate use of executive power, a "wand" waved to make unlawful entrants lawful. [cite:de12_pf_haitians] That is a real legal argument, and the administration he joined later moved to end both programmes. [cite:de12_ap_tps2025] As a description of his neighbours' legal status in 2024, however, the official record does not support the word he chose.

Kilmar Abrego Garcia "a convicted MS-13 gang member" (1 April 2025): ORANGE. As Vice President, Vance posted that Abrego Garcia, a Salvadoran man the administration had deported to El Salvador weeks earlier, "was a convicted MS-13 gang member with no legal right to be here." [cite:de12_pf_abrego] He had never been convicted of any crime. A 2019 immigration bond denial rested on an informant's allegation of gang membership, which he denied, in a proceeding that was not a criminal trial; the same year a judge barred his removal to El Salvador, and government lawyers later called his deportation an "administrative error." [cite:de12_pf_abrego] Vance's office did not respond to PolitiFact, and no correction of the word "convicted" is on record. [cite:de12_pf_abrego] After his return to the United States he was charged with human smuggling in Tennessee; on 22 May 2026 Judge Waverly Crenshaw dismissed those charges on finding a presumption of vindictive prosecution, and the Justice Department said it would appeal. [cite:de12_npr_abrego] As of October 2026 he has no criminal conviction. The gang allegation itself remains contested; the word "convicted" is contradicted by the record.

Flags on his own record

Trump, 2016 against 2021 onward: YELLOW. In 2016 he called himself "a Never Trump guy," called Trump "cultural heroin" and "reprehensible," privately weighed whether he was "America's Hitler," and said he would vote for Evan McMullin. [cite:de12_axios_trump2016] From 2021 he sought and won Trump's endorsement, and in 2024 joined his ticket. [cite:de12_wiki_oh2022] [cite:de12_cnn_vppick] His explanation is that Trump's record as President "changed my mind," that he had "bought into the media's lies and distortions," and that "you ought to admit the mistake." [cite:de12_mediaite_hannity] [cite:de12_axios_trump2016] The reversal, the explanation and the timing, which coincided with his need for the endorsement, are all documented; the motive stays contested.

Our Ohio Renewal (2016–2021): ORANGE. Vance said his charity would raise $500,000 a year to fight addiction and unemployment across the industrial Midwest. It raised $221,000 in its best year, 2018, of which $80,000 was his own money, then less than $50,000 a year until it closed in 2021, and its one notable project was a year-long residency for an addiction specialist. [cite:de12_ap_ohiorenewal] Vance has acknowledged that the groups "fell far short of his aspirations," and his campaign pointed to a key organiser's cancer diagnosis. [cite:de12_ap_ohiorenewal] The promise was not delivered, by his own account as well as the record.

The courts (2021 and 2025): no flag. His 2021 call to answer the courts with "let him enforce it" and his 2025 post that "judges aren't allowed to control the executive's legitimate power" are consistent with each other. [cite:de5_nextgov_vance] [cite:de12_ap_judges] There is no contradiction to grade. The record against him on this point is what he said, and it is set out in [scene:de12_one_heartbeat_away|the fourth section].

His acquaintance with Yarvin: no flag. Vance named Yarvin once in public, in 2021; Kofman reported that they met in 2015 through Thiel; Yarvin described one encounter at a party in January 2025; and a Vance adviser said in 2025 that they had met "like once." [cite:de5_wbur_vance] [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] [cite:de5_politico_ward] [cite:de6_cnn_2025] Citing an author is not meeting him, and the accounts of contact are loose but not contradictory.

Accounts of his venture career conflict, from colleagues who recalled no significant deal at Mithril to a former managing director who called him "a great VC"; with no dated claim of his own for them to contradict, it is not graded. [cite:de12_fortune_mithril]

Yarvin's flags in [scene:de6_claims_vs_record|Part Six] were mostly GREY. Vance's are mostly ORANGE, and three of the four concern immigrants, in claims official records contradicted and he did not correct. None reaches RED: none is contradicted by his own documents or sworn testimony.

The worst-case reading, tested

The hypothesis this book tests holds that immigration enforcement and government cuts are the occasion, and a sovereign executive without a veto the destination. Vance's life bears on it more directly than any other in Book II, because he is the only person in the network who could become the executive. Documented: that a man whose first campaign was financed mainly by Thiel, who worked for and was invested in by Thiel, and who still shares a fund-level financial interest with him, is first in the line of succession. [cite:de12_foxbiz_pov] [cite:de12_techcrunch_narya] [cite:de12_oge_2025] [cite:de12_cornell_3usc19] Documented: that in 2021 he named Yarvin, endorsed firing the civil service and replacing it "with our people," and endorsed answering the Supreme Court with "let him enforce it"; that as Vice President he restated the core of that view of judicial power; and that he used his deciding vote to defeat two attempts to limit the President's emergency and war powers. [cite:de5_nextgov_vance] [cite:de12_ap_judges] [cite:de12_wiki_ties] Documented as well: that three of his four ORANGE flags concern false or unsupported claims about immigrants, the occasion the hypothesis names.

Probable: that the 2021 statements still describe how he understands the limits of executive power, since he has restated their core and has not withdrawn them. What he would do with that understanding as President is not documented, and the book does not grade it.

Not supported: that Thiel directs Vance, that Thiel chose him for the ticket in 2024, or that any act of Vance's office has benefited Thiel or a Thiel company. The record runs against the first two: in 2024 it shows Vance asking Thiel for money and Thiel declining to give it to the campaign, and the fullest reconstruction of the choice does not mention Thiel. [cite:de12_dbeast_ft] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de12_cnn_vppick] Not supported either: that Yarvin's program is Vance's policy. The 2021 remark is rhyme and citation, not a line of command; Vance holds no power to retire the civil service, and the 2025 cuts were run by others, as [scene:de17_money_to_office|Book Three] shows. And not supported: that succession is being planned. In law the presidency passes to him only by death, resignation, removal or a contested procedure that needs the Cabinet and two-thirds of Congress. [cite:de12_crs_25th] [scene:de15_vance_and_masters|Part Fifteen] sets his first race beside the one Thiel financed for Blake Masters, and [scene:de40_worst_case_scorecard|Part Forty-Three] carries these grades into the book's final account.
