Part Two ended with three signatures on 11 February 1929. This part reads what was signed, and then follows the bargain to the present. It sets out, article by article, what the Holy See and Mussolini's government each took from the Treaty, the Concordat and the Financial Convention, and how quickly the bargain was tested when the regime closed Catholic youth groups in 1931. It then turns to the Republic: the Constitution that kept the Pacts, the 1984 revision signed by Bettino Craxi and Cardinal Agostino Casaroli that ended Catholicism's status as the state religion, and the 1985 law that replaced state salaries for priests with the otto per mille, a share of income tax that taxpayers direct. The last section follows a property-tax exemption from Rome to the courts in Luxembourg and back to Italian tax offices in 2026. Together the four sections answer a plain question: who benefits from the 1929 settlement today, and what it costs Italy.
