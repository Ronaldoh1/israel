A reader who has come this far holds three kinds of knowledge: what the record settles, what it leaves in dispute, and the questions the history ahead has to answer.

The present is settled in its outline, though not in any final agreement. Israel is the state within the 1949 armistice lines. The West Bank was divided by the Oslo II agreement into Areas A, B and C, about 18, 22 and 61 percent of the territory, in an arrangement written as temporary that has lasted three decades. Israel annexed East Jerusalem under a law that the Security Council, by 14 votes to none, declared null and void.[cite:anera_areas_abc] [cite:unsc478_text] About 14.8 million Palestinians live in four legal situations, and at the end of 2023 Arabs made up 21.3 percent of Israel's population.[cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees] [cite:rs_ch01_jiis_pop] The Law of Return extends a right of immigration to every Jew, while the residence route of 1952 decided, by dates and registers, which Arabs who stayed in 1948 became citizens.[cite:rs_ch01_lor_text] [cite:rs_ch01_nationality_1952] On 19 July 2024 the International Court of Justice advised that Israel's continued presence in the occupied territory is unlawful; no court has decided whether genocide has been committed in Gaza.[cite:icj_press_19jul24] [cite:jta_un_coi_genocide]

The deep past is settled as two documented histories, not one. A people called Israel is named on an Egyptian stele of about 1207 BCE, and a Jewish presence survived Rome. An Arabic-speaking majority lived on the land for centuries and was counted by the Ottoman state: 381,954 people in 1871–72, excluding the Bedouin, about 85 percent Muslim, 11 percent Christian and 4 percent Jewish.[cite:rs_ch01_cojs_merneptah] [cite:cjpme_historical_demography] A Jewish community lived in the four holy cities before Zionism. Modern national identity took formal shape late on both sides, at Basel in 1897 and, in Rashid Khalidi's account, in the late Ottoman years and sharpening in 1917–1923.[cite:rs_ch01_nli_oldyishuv] [cite:cup_khalidi_palestinian_identity]

Several words are settled too. "Semitic" names a family of languages that includes Hebrew and Arabic, and "antisemitism" has meant hostility to Jews since the 1880s, a hatred documented long before the word.[cite:wiki_antisemitism_etym] [cite:rs_ch01_ushmm_bloodlibel] The claim of wholesale Khazar descent was tested and failed, and genetics decides no one's rights.[cite:behar_2013_khazar] Zionism and Judaism are distinct, and Jews from every lineage have argued over the difference. Jewish life in the Muslim world was long, unequal and productive, and the frame of a permanent, ancient hatred does not describe it.[cite:stillman_jews_arab_lands]

Much remains disputed, and the Baseline has set out each dispute with the strongest case on both sides. No ancient source says why Rome renamed Judaea.[cite:rs_ch01_totf_hadrian] Whether Jews formed Jerusalem's majority depends on whether the city or the sub-district is counted, and the two 1922 totals for the city, 62,578 and 52,081, do not agree.[cite:rs_ch01_un_s10138] [cite:rs_ch01_jvl_jerpop] The family-unification ban is defended as security and attacked as demography, and its 2022 text names the Jewish majority among its aims.[cite:rs_ch01_idi_citizenship] The IHRA definition and the Jerusalem Declaration give different answers to whether anti-Zionism is antisemitic.[cite:peacenow_ihra_faq] [cite:jda_text] Who planted the bombs in Baghdad in 1950–51 is unproven, and the fates of 42 Yemenite children remained unaccounted for after the state's own inquiry.[cite:meforum_exodus] [cite:wiki_kedmi_commission] Whether some 850,000 or about 900,000 Jews left Arab lands, and whether they should be called refugees, is argued by named authors with stated positions.[cite:rs_ch01_beker_jpsr] [cite:rs_ch01_ahram_amayreh] Historians divide over how far the expulsions of 1948 were planned, and over whether Zionism is best described as settler colonialism or as national liberation.[cite:wiki_deir_yassin] [cite:massad_settler_colonial]

The volumes ahead test what the Baseline has cleared. The next volume follows the road to 1948 in order. It asks how a land of which Jews were about 11 percent at the British census of 1922 came, by the United Nations committee's estimate for late 1946, to be about a third Jewish, and through which laws, purchases and institutions; the change in land title begins with [scene:ot_s01|the Ottoman Land Code]. It asks whether the expulsions of 1948 followed a design, the argument that turns on [scene:pd_s01|Plan Dalet], and what became of the property the refugees left, governed by [scene:ap_s01|the Absentees' Property Law]. It tests the claims the Baseline met in miniature, about consent, about promises and about who decided, against the documents of each stage.

The rule for that reading is the one this volume has used throughout. Read the document first and the interpreter second, keep what is shown apart from what it is said to show, and hold both peoples' claims to the same standard. Neither history erases the other, and the story that follows is about what was done with both.
