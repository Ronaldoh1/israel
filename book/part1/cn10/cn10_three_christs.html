The Three Christs

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

Three different figures go by the name Christ in the material on this shelf, and much of the conflict described in Parts One to Nine comes from keeping them apart or running them together.

The first is the man of history. Outside the New Testament, the Roman historian Tacitus wrote that "Christus, the founder of the name, had undergone the death penalty in the reign of Tiberius, by sentence of the procurator Pontius Pilatus", and the Jewish historian Josephus mentions "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, whose name was James". [cite:vt20_tacitus_ann15] [cite:vt20_josephus_ant20] His name, Yeshua, was common in his time and place. [cite:vt20_wiki_yeshua] This is the Christ the record can see: a Galilean teacher executed by a Roman governor.

The second is the Christ of the councils. At Nicaea in 325 the emperor Constantine urged the insertion into the creed of the word homoousios, "of the same substance", to define how the Son relates to the Father, and the man whose teaching the creed condemned was banished and his writings ordered burned. [cite:vt22_vc3] [cite:vt21_socrates19] This Christ is defined by doctrine and guarded by an office. Belief in the right definition, confessed in the right words, marked the boundary of the church, and the chain of bishops that Irenaeus traced was the guarantee that the words were right. [cite:cn_iren_3_3]

The third is the Christ within. The canon itself names him: Paul writes to the Colossians of a mystery hidden for ages, "which is Christ in you, the hope of glory". [cite:cn_esv_col127] The excluded Gospel of Philip makes the same idea the goal of the whole path: "You saw the Spirit, you became spirit. You saw Christ, you became Christ. You saw the Father, you shall become Father." [cite:gn_ecw_gphil] The Gospel of Thomas has Jesus say that the kingdom "is inside of you, and it is outside of you", and that what a person brings forth from within will save them. [cite:gn_ut_thomas] The word "Christ" means "anointed", and Philip lists anointing with chrism among the rites of its community. [cite:vt24_gphil]

The inner reading draws a pattern from this. The canon kept the sayings of the third Christ. "Christ in you", "the temple of God", "the kingdom within" are all in the list the bishops closed. What it did was place them inside the second Christ: a doctrine about Christ, defined at councils and taught by an office, so that the saying about the Christ within became a statement about him rather than an invitation to become him. The excluded books put the third Christ at the centre and made the office unnecessary, and that, more than any single doctrine, is why they were dangerous to the office. The Canon has traced where the line was drawn. The reading suggests why it was drawn there.

The record does not show which of the three Christs the man of Galilee would have recognised. The historical sources are too thin to say what he taught about himself, and both the councils and the Gnostic teachers claimed to speak for him. What the record does show is that the canon contains all three, and that every reader who meets the phrase "Christ in you" must decide whether it describes a doctrine to be believed or a state to be reached.
