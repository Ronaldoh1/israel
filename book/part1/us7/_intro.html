Part Seven compares how each state handles religion. In Israel there is no civil marriage: religious courts inherited from the Ottoman and British systems decide who may marry and divorce, the Chief Rabbinate decides who is Jewish for that purpose, and the state built exits abroad and online rather than a civil alternative at home. The part then follows the exemption from military service for men in full-time Torah study, from about 400 students in 1949 to tens of thousands today, the court rulings against it, and the budget money that sustains the institutions. On the American side it traces the First Amendment from the "wall of separation" of 1947 to the Supreme Court's turn toward religious exemptions and public money for religious schools, and ends with the two systems side by side, as of October 2026.
