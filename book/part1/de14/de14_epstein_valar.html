Epstein and Valar

Jeffrey Epstein pleaded guilty in Florida on 30 June 2008 to two state solicitation charges and served thirteen months of an eighteen-month sentence; he was arrested on federal sex-trafficking charges in July 2019 and died in a Manhattan jail cell on 10 August 2019, a death ruled a suicide. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] Every contact described in this section took place after the 2008 conviction. This section sets out what the released records show about Epstein's money in Valar Ventures and his contacts with Peter Thiel, what they do not show, and the state of the Epstein files as of October 2026. It applies the strictest standard in the book. Thiel has not been charged with any crime connected to Epstein, and the presumption of innocence holds. A claim that any living person took part in Epstein's crimes would need court records or sworn testimony behind it; no such claim about Thiel exists in the record, and none is made here. The wider Epstein record, including the claims about intelligence services, is in [scene:ow12_ties|The One-Way Mirror].

The investment

Valar Ventures is a New York venture fund specialising in financial technology, co-founded by Thiel and led by Andrew McCormack, who stood in the back row of Fortune's 2007 PayPal photograph, and James Fitzgerald. [cite:de14_nyt_valar] [cite:de13_fortune_2007] According to the New York Times, Epstein put $40 million into two Valar funds in 2015 and 2016, and by 2025 the stake was worth nearly $170 million, the largest asset still held by his estate; the fund's spokesperson said a Valar representative had met Epstein in 2014, when he was seen as a "well-known adviser to world leaders, top universities and philanthropic organizations," and that the firm hoped the eventual distribution could help his victims. Thiel's spokesperson declined to comment. [cite:de14_nyt_valar]

Documents released by the Justice Department in early 2026 fill in the sequence, as reported by Byline Times, an independent British outlet, which did not give document numbers: an email of 15 November 2014 seeking $10 million to $20 million for Valar; a commitment of $15 million by June 2015 through Epstein's Southern Trust Company, as a limited partner, growing to about $40 million; fund statements sent to Epstein and his accountant; a June 2017 message offering him "super confidential" investment opportunities; and a further request for capital in November 2018. [cite:de14_byline_valar] The Stanford Daily, citing a specific file number, confirms the $40 million investment. [cite:de14_sd_files] A limited partner, in a venture fund, supplies money and receives a share of the returns but has no say in running the fund; that is the role Thiel's side says Epstein had.

The contacts

The released files show a relationship over several years. Emails from 2014 into early 2019 show the two men arranging meetings several times a year, at Epstein's New York house and at Thiel's office and home in San Francisco; earlier reporting counted about six scheduled meetings between 2014 and 2016. [cite:de14_sd_files] [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] In June 2014 Epstein offered advice on Thiel's taxes, and in another file he considered buying Palantir shares; no record shows that he did. [cite:de14_sd_files] Epstein called Thiel a "great friend" in messages to others; the phrase is Epstein's. [cite:de14_sd_files] He tried, on at least six occasions, to bring Thiel together with Ehud Barak, the former Israeli prime minister. [cite:de14_jacobin] Epstein repeatedly invited Thiel to his island; to one invitation Thiel answered, "When are you there?" [cite:de14_sd_files] When Epstein proposed in February 2016 to meet in Europe, on the island or in Saudi Arabia, Thiel replied: "Certainly not in Saudi — just think I will avoid the Middle East for the next decade or so." [cite:de14_jacobin] Thiel's spokesperson told the New York Times that Thiel "never went to Epstein's Island," and no document shows a visit to the island or a flight on Epstein's aircraft. [cite:de14_sd_files] [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein]

The contested claim: "owned by Peter Thiel and JE"

One document has done more than any other to shape public accounts. In February 2016 Barak wrote to Epstein and an associate describing two people as "seniors in a new Investment Fund owned by Peter Thiel and JE." [cite:de14_byline_valar] Thiel's spokesman denied that Epstein was a co-owner and confirmed that he was a limited partner. [cite:de14_byline_valar] The fund's own statements, the Times's account of a $40 million limited partnership and Valar's spokesperson all describe an investor, not an owner. [cite:de14_nyt_valar] Barak's phrase is a third party's description in a private email, not a record of ownership, and it is contradicted by the documents that record the investment. The book grades the co-ownership claim as not supported; the headline that rests on it, "Jeffrey Epstein and Peter Thiel co-owned venture fund," states as fact what the underlying documents do not show. [cite:de14_byline_valar]

A second, lesser chain runs through Carbyne, an Israeli emergency-call technology company first called Reporty. Barak chaired it, and his investment partnership put money into it in 2015; Epstein put $1 million into that partnership in 2016. [cite:ow12_toi_carbyne] [cite:ow10_ctech_barak] Epstein and Barak planned to pitch the company to Valar, and Valar declined it in 2016. [cite:de14_byline_valar] [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] Founders Fund, Thiel's separate firm, is also among Carbyne's backers. [cite:ow10_ctech_barak] Two investors in the same company are co-investors, not partners; the record shows no joint venture between Thiel and Epstein in Carbyne, and Barak's own record is his, not Thiel's.

Appearing in the files is not evidence of wrongdoing, and the same rule applies to everyone named in them. The files record, for example, an August 2015 dinner in Palo Alto that brought Epstein together with Thiel, Mark Zuckerberg, Elon Musk and Reid Hoffman, among others. [cite:de14_sd_files] Attendance at a dinner is not a relationship, and each person's documented dealings with Epstein are their own; Musk's emails with Epstein are treated in [scene:de11_money_and_influence|his life], and nothing in one person's record transfers to another. The Justice Department's review, released in July 2025, said it "did not uncover evidence that could predicate an investigation against uncharged third parties." [cite:ow12_abc_memo]

Status of the Epstein files as of October 2026

Congress passed the Epstein Files Transparency Act in November 2025, and the president signed it on 19 November, setting a deadline of 19 December for release. [cite:de14_gma_engelmayer] The deadline was missed; in January 2026 a federal judge in New York, Paul Engelmayer, declined to appoint a special master to supervise the release, acknowledging "legitimate concerns" but finding he lacked jurisdiction. [cite:de14_gma_engelmayer] At the end of January the department released more than 3 million pages, 2,000 videos and 180,000 images, about 3.5 million pages in all under the law, and the deputy attorney general called the review "very comprehensive." [cite:de14_sn_3m] On 27 August 2026 the department published in the Federal Register its report under the Act, covering what was released and withheld, the legal basis for redactions, and the officials and politically exposed persons named in the material. [cite:de14_fr_report] Members of Congress from both parties have disputed whether the release is complete. [cite:de14_gma_engelmayer] The Thiel and Valar material described above comes from these releases; further documents may yet change the account, and this section reflects the record as of October 2026.

The worst-case reading, tested

This part's material bears on the hypothesis in one way: whether the money behind the people who hold power in 2025 and 2026 was organised to build an executive without a veto. Documented: that Thiel's money, about $32 million in 2022, carried the political careers of two of his own employees, one of whom became Vice President; that Musk spent more than $290 million in 2024 and then ran DOGE; that people from the network took posts that shape AI, crypto, science and Arctic policy; and that parts of the money around them, through DonorsTrust, Per Aspera and the Rockbridge Network, cannot be traced. [cite:de14_cnbc_2022] [cite:de14_cnn_musk290] [cite:de13_wiki_sacks] [cite:de14_dg_donorstrust] [cite:de14_wapo_rockbridge] Probable: that this money bought access as well as votes, since the Post's account of Rockbridge describes exactly that; the record shows the access, not what was done with it. [cite:de14_wapo_rockbridge]

Not supported: that any of this money flowed to the theorists beyond Tlon and one fellowship, or to a programme for dismantling checks and balances; that any political loop closes on documents; or that the opaque channels carried money anywhere in particular, a claim the opacity itself makes impossible to prove either way. [cite:de5_wiki_urbit] [cite:de5_audit_tc2011] Running against the hypothesis: the network split in 2024, Thiel declining to fund Trump while Hoffman funded his opponent, and the contracts most often cited as returns began under earlier administrations. [cite:de13_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de13_badger_hoffman] [cite:ow9_mj_icm2019] On Epstein, the documented record is a $40 million limited partnership and years of meetings after his conviction, a matter of judgment the reader may weigh; it is not evidence of crime, and it does not bear on the hypothesis at all.
