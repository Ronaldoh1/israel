Part Two established that the papal state began as a documented exchange, Pippin's grant of 756 in return for religious legitimacy, later dressed in a forged imperial title that Lorenzo Valla exposed in 1440. [cite:vt2_britannica_pippin] [cite:vt2_britannica_constantine] That state lasted only as long as Catholic armies defended it: Austria put down the revolts of 1830 and 1848, France restored Pius IX in 1849 and left Rome in 1870. [cite:vt2_wiki_papalstates] [cite:vt2_wiki_romanrep1849] Pius IX refused Italy's unilateral Law of Guarantees and its endowment, and his successors stayed inside the Vatican for fifty-nine years. [cite:vt2_ce_guarantees] [cite:vt2_ewtn_ubinos] [cite:vt2_wiki_prisoner] The Lateran Pacts of 11 February 1929 were designed by named people, Pius XI, Cardinal Gasparri and the lawyer Francesco Pacelli, with Mussolini on the other side. Italy paid 750 million lire in cash and 1 billion lire in 5 per cent bonds, and recognised the Holy See's sovereignty in exchange for recognition of Rome as Italy's capital. [cite:vt2_lateran_text] [cite:vt2_vn_pacelli2019] The Catholic Partito Popolare had already been dissolved under the fascist laws of November 1926, and Article 43 of the Concordat kept Catholic Action out of party politics. [cite:vt2_wiki_ppi] [cite:vt2_museo_cronologia] Control of the money passed from Nogara's Special Administration to APSA, and sovereign power passed from Pius XI to Leo XIV under fundamental laws of 1929, 2000, 2023 and 2026.
