Why Support Persists

The previous sections showed wide condemnation and narrow consequence. This section sets out the documented reasons the material support, above all American support, has held: the law, the money, the industry, the intelligence, the politics and the strategy. It then sets out the evidence that support is eroding. What the record establishes is kept apart from arguments about motive, which are labelled as such and given with the case against them.

The documented drivers

Law and the memorandum. American support is written into statute and into a ten-year agreement. The memorandum of understanding signed on 14 September 2016 commits $38 billion for fiscal years 2019 to 2028, $33 billion in Foreign Military Financing and $5 billion for missile defence, and Congress has since written a floor of not less than $3.3 billion a year in military financing through 2028 into the defence authorisation act for 2021. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] The Naval Vessel Transfer Act of 2008 defined Israel's "qualitative military edge" in law, required it to be assessed every four years and provided that arms sales to other states in the region may not adversely affect it. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] After 7 October Congress added a supplemental appropriation in April 2024 of $3.5 billion in military financing, $4 billion for missile defence and $1.2 billion for the Iron Beam laser. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] In March 2025 the State Department used emergency authority to expedite some $4 billion, with nearly $12 billion in arms sales approved since January. [cite:ow1_state_rubio2025] A government that wished to stop this flow would have to override law as well as policy. The amounts, and the test of whether they free Israeli money for operations, are set out in Part Fifteen. [scene:gi16_aid|Part Fifteen: the aid] [scene:ow1_partnership|The One-Way Mirror: the partnership]

Industry. Arms run both ways. Israel's defence exports reached a record $14.8 billion in 2024, 54 percent of them to Europe and about 48 percent in air defence. [cite:hm_globes_exports] Germany supplied 30 percent of Israel's major arms imports from 2019 to 2023. [cite:gi17_aje_germany] In court, two European governments defended continued supply by pointing to industrial ties rather than to Israel: Britain argued that suspending F-35 parts would affect the "whole F-35 programme," and the Netherlands, which hosts a regional warehouse for American-owned F-35 parts, argued that the United States would supply them anyway. [cite:gi17_aje_f35uk] [cite:gi17_es_dutch] Co-production of Iron Dome, David's Sling and Arrow with American firms is described in The One-Way Mirror. [scene:ow8_coproduction|The One-Way Mirror: co-production]

Intelligence. Under a memorandum agreed in principle in March 2009, published by the Guardian in 2013, the National Security Agency shares raw intercepts with Israel's signals intelligence unit, including communications of Americans; a 2013 NSA document describes "a far-reaching technical and analytic relationship" with a dedicated line for "the exchange of raw material." [cite:ow_aj_nsamou] [cite:ow_int_cash2014] [scene:ow4_memorandum|The One-Way Mirror: the memorandum]

Politics and lobbying. AIPAC and its super PAC, the United Democracy Project, spent $95.1 million combined in the 2024 cycle, up from $44 million in 2022, and the super PAC had raised about $104 million for the 2026 cycle by June. [cite:pm_israelite_2024] [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] It spent more than $30.6 million in Michigan's Democratic Senate primary of 4 August 2026, its largest outlay on one race; its opponent, Abdul El-Sayed, won. [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] [cite:pm_itt_elsayed] The volume on that lobby sets out the money cycle by cycle. [scene:pm2_turn|The Pro-Israel Money Architecture: the turn] [scene:pm10_costs|The Pro-Israel Money Architecture: what it costs] These figures are disclosures. That money was spent is documented; that it caused any member's vote is a separate claim, which the disclosures alone do not establish.

Strategy against Iran. In January 2021 the Pentagon moved Israel from European Command to Central Command, saying "the Abraham Accords paved the way," which placed Israel's forces in the same command as America's Gulf partners. [cite:ow_inss_centcom] The United States joined Israel's war with Iran in June 2025 and again from 28 February 2026, when President Trump told Iranians, "When we are finished, take over your government." [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] [scene:ir13_why|The Iran Architecture: why Iran] The cost of that alignment in American money and lives is in Part Fifteen. [scene:gi16_americans|Part Fifteen: the Americans]

Three readings

Three explanations of why the drivers above hold are commonly argued, and none is settled by the documents. The first is that Israel is a strategic asset: a military partner in a region where the United States fights Iran, whose aid largely returns to American factories. The case against it is that the alliance's costs, set out in Part Fifteen, are also documented, and that officials such as those quoted in The Iran Architecture have questioned whether the threats were as great as claimed. [scene:gi16_costs|Part Fifteen: the costs] [scene:ir13_why|The Iran Architecture: why Iran] The second is that domestic politics and lobbying keep support in place. The case for it is the scale of the spending and the defeat of incumbents it targeted, such as Jamaal Bowman and Cori Bush in 2024; the case against is that its candidates also lost, as in Michigan in 2026, and that public sympathy, not money, long ran in Israel's favour. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] [cite:pm_itt_elsayed] [cite:gz_gallup_2026] The third is that religious belief, above all among American evangelicals, sustains it: in a 2017 survey 63 percent of Americans with evangelical beliefs said they supported Israel because "God gave the land of Israel to the Jewish people." [cite:gi11_ct_lifeway] [scene:gi11_christian|Part Eleven: Christian Zionism] The case against is that the survey measures attitude, not policy, and that support in Congress and the executive has been bipartisan, as the recorded votes in The One-Way Mirror show. [scene:ow2_votes|The One-Way Mirror: the votes] Whether American support serves the expansion programmes this volume documented is a further argument: the support is given for Israel's security, and the American president who did most to accelerate it also said, in September 2025, "I will not allow Israel to annex the West Bank." [cite:gi6_ap_trump0925] [scene:gi6_votes|Part Six: the annexation track]

Erosion

The clearest change is in American opinion. In February 2026, for the first time in Gallup's annual measurement since 2001, more Americans said they sympathised with the Palestinians (41 percent) than with the Israelis (36 percent), against 31 and 54 percent three years earlier; Gallup noted that the five-point gap was not statistically significant. About two-thirds of Democrats and half of those aged 18 to 34 sympathised more with the Palestinians, and about seven in ten Republicans with the Israelis. [cite:gz_gallup_2026] [cite:gz_ap_gallup2026] In July 2025 Gallup found that 8 percent of Democrats approved of Israel's military action in Gaza, down from 36 percent in November 2023. [cite:pm_gazette_reject] [scene:gz12_coverage|Gaza, Documented: coverage and opinion]

Congress followed more slowly. A measure by Senator Bernie Sanders on Israel's conduct drew 11 votes in January 2024; his resolution against about $8.8 billion in arms sales drew 15 in April 2025. [cite:ow2_jta_sanders2025] On 30 July 2025, 27 Democrats, a majority of the party's senators, voted to block a sale of 20,000 assault rifles and 24 to block a $675 million sale of bombs; every Republican voted against, and both resolutions failed. The previous high had been 18 Democratic votes in November 2024, or 19 by another count. [cite:gi17_bg_senate2025] [cite:ow2_jta_sanders2025] Sanders said Democrats were responding to "an Israeli government which is currently starving children to death"; the Foreign Relations chairman, Jim Risch, said Hamas uses Gaza's people "as human shields." [cite:gi17_bg_senate2025] [scene:ow2_votes|The One-Way Mirror: the votes] Abroad, erosion took the forms set out in the previous section: recognitions, sanctions on two ministers and on settlers, and Germany's temporary arms curbs. [scene:gi17_states|What states did]

The record therefore shows support that is anchored in law, contracts, industry, intelligence and a shared war, and opinion that has moved against it, most among Democrats and the young. As of October 2026 the opinion had changed faster than the law: the memorandum still runs to 2028 and no arms-blocking resolution had passed either chamber.
