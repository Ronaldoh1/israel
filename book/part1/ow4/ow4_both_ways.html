What Each Side Gets, and Who Watches Whom

The 2009 memorandum governs one flow. The NSA's own papers, published from the Snowden archive in 2013, 2014 and 2015, describe the whole relationship: what Israel receives, what the United States receives, how the NSA itself judged the balance, and how the same agency classed Israel as a counterintelligence threat. They also show the United States collecting on Israel. Read together, they test the claim that the signals relationship runs one way.

The NSA's own description. An NSA document of 13 April 2013, titled "NSA Intelligence Relationship with Israel" and published by The Intercept in August 2014, says: "NSA maintains a far-reaching technical and analytic relationship with the Israeli SIGINT National Unit," "sharing information on access, intercept, targeting, language, analysis and reporting." [cite:ow_int_cash2014] [cite:ow_york_nsadoc] It describes "a dedicated communications line between NSA and ISNU [that] supports the exchange of raw material." [cite:ow_int_cash2014] On Israel's side of the ledger, it says Israel gains "expanded geographic access to world-class NSA cryptanalytic and SIGINT engineering expertise" and "controlled access to advanced U.S. technology and equipment via accommodation buys and foreign military sales." [cite:ow_int_cash2014] The geographic targets named include North Africa, the Middle East and the Persian Gulf, and Israel's priorities include what the NSA calls "Palestinian terrorism." [cite:ow_int_cash2014] Documents from 2003–04 negotiations over a proposed agreement code-named Gladiator include receipts for cash payments of $500,000 to Israeli officials for unspecified purposes; the agreement appears never to have been finalized. [cite:ow_int_cash2014] The NSA's public statement was that such cooperation "mutually strengthens the security of both nations." [cite:ow_int_cash2014]

The NSA's judgment of the balance. An internal NSA history of 2007, "History of the US – Israel Sigint Relationship, Post-1992," published by The Guardian, put the agency's view in two sentences: "Balancing the Sigint exchange equally between US and Israeli needs has been a constant challenge," and "In the last decade, it arguably tilted heavily in favor of Israeli security concerns." [cite:ow4_alternet_guardian] This is the strongest documentary support for the one-way thesis, and it comes from the NSA itself. It is a judgment of balance, not a statement that nothing flowed to the United States.

What the United States received. The clearest documented American return is operational. Olympic Games, the attack on Iran's enrichment plant at Natanz, depended on intelligence and expertise from Unit 8200: David Sanger reported that the beacon which mapped the plant "phoned home, back to the National Security Agency and the Unit 8200," and that the American aim was to give Israel an alternative to bombing Iran. [cite:ow3_npr_sanger] [cite:ow3_sw_sanger] The CIA channel described in Part Three supplied Cold War intelligence and captured Soviet equipment. [cite:ow3_meq_siskind] The 2013 document names geographic access as something Israel gains, which implies that the regions in question were ones where Israeli collection was valued; it does not list Israeli products supplied to the NSA. [cite:ow_int_cash2014]

"Israel also targets us." The same archive records the NSA's view of Israel as an intelligence threat. A 2008 NSA document, an interview headed "Which Foreign Intelligence Service Is the Biggest Threat to the US?", said: "Israel also targets us," and noted that "a NIE [National Intelligence Estimate] ranked [Israel] as the third most aggressive intelligence service against the US." [cite:ow_td_spying2015] A senior NSA official quoted in a 2008 document said: "One of NSA's biggest threats is actually from friendly intelligence services, like Israel." [cite:ow4_alternet_guardian] The intelligence community's 2013 budget request listed Israel among the "most threatening cyber-adversaries" and as a "hostile" foreign intelligence service. [cite:ow_td_spying2015] After the Wall Street Journal reported in March 2015 that Israel had spied on the American negotiations with Iran and passed material to members of Congress, Prime Minister Netanyahu's office replied: "The state of Israel does not conduct espionage against the United States or Israel's other allies." [cite:ow4_statewatch2015] [cite:ow_td_spying2015] The denial and the documents stand side by side; the documents are the NSA's assessments, not findings tested in any court.

The mirror reflects back. The United States also collects on Israel. The Wall Street Journal reported in December 2015 that the NSA kept intercepting Netanyahu's communications after President Obama announced, in January 2014, curbs on monitoring the leaders of allied countries; Netanyahu was kept off the protected list because monitoring him served a "compelling national security purpose." [cite:ow4_aj_wsj2015] The intercepts gave the administration an inside view of Israel's campaign to turn Congress against the Iran agreement, and, according to the report, swept up communications with members of Congress; a 2011 NSA directive requires such communications to be destroyed unless the NSA director waives the rule for "significant foreign intelligence." [cite:ow4_aj_wsj2015] The National Security Council said the United States does not conduct foreign surveillance "unless there is a specific and validated national security purpose"; the Israeli embassy declined to comment. [cite:ow4_aj_wsj2015] Melman and Raviv describe American surveillance of Israel as routine. [cite:ow3_tablet] The documented asymmetry is not that only one side watches. It is in what is handed over by agreement.

What the record does not settle. No document published so far lists what ISNU sends to the NSA, in volume or in kind, so the balance can be judged only from the NSA's own words. The purpose of the Gladiator cash payments is not explained in the documents. The National Intelligence Estimate that ranked Israel third has not been published. And neither government has officially confirmed Sanger's account of Olympic Games.
