This volume compares how two states serve, and fail, the people under their authority: the United States and Israel. It was written to answer a question now asked in Congress as well as at kitchen tables: why does the United States send money to Israel when Americans lack things that Israelis have? To answer it, the book first asks what the aid actually is and what it frees up, and then asks the questions underneath. How is each state built, and how far do its courts check its government? How free and fair are its elections, its press and its protests? Whom does each police, imprison and detain without trial, and who is killed by violence and by guns? Who may become a citizen, and who is governed without citizenship at all? How does each treat religion, and its own minorities, women and disabled citizens? And on the measures households feel, wages, prices, housing, health care, schools, family benefits, pensions and the burden of military service, how has each system done since 1980?

The record answers most of these questions, measure by measure, and does not reduce to one verdict. It shows the aid as $3.8 billion a year of military money under the 2016 agreement, spent mostly with American contractors, which has fallen from about 12.7 per cent of Israel's GDP in 1985 to about 0.6 per cent in 2025 and is about 0.05 per cent of American federal outlays; it does not pay for Israel's health funds. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:us1_fred_ilgdp] [cite:us1_crfb_fy2025] [cite:us1_cmwf_israel] It shows Israel covering every citizen and resident's health care by law since 1995 and moving from level life expectancy with the United States in 1980 to about four years ahead, while 26.7 million Americans were uninsured in 2025. [cite:us16_wiki_ilhealth] [cite:us16_wb_le] [cite:us16_census_2025] It shows the United States paying higher average wages, imprisoning about two and a half times as many people per head, and recording its lowest homicide rate on record in 2025 as killings in Israel's Arab communities reached a record 252. [cite:us14_oecd_avwage] [cite:us4_wpb_us] [cite:us4_wpb_il] [cite:us5_fox_fbi2025] [cite:us5_yahoo_herzog] And it shows that Israel's national figures leave out the 5.56 million Palestinians of the West Bank and Gaza, roughly a third of the people under its control, who hold no Israeli citizenship. [cite:us9_pcbs_2025] [cite:us1_fh_israel2026]

The book is in twenty-two parts. Part One states the aid precisely and sets the rules; Parts Two to Ten compare the state, democracy, justice, public safety, citizenship, religion, equality, the people governed without citizenship and surveillance; Parts Eleven to Eighteen compare the economy, industries, labour, the cost of living, housing and infrastructure, health, education and the safety net; Parts Nineteen and Twenty turn to the military and to the money that flows in both directions; Part Twenty-One sets every measure in one ledger and gives its answer measure by measure; and Part Twenty-Two grades both systems on 43 measures, including a family-budget area, publishing every goalpost and weight, and gives a final score. [scene:us22_method|The scorecard] [scene:us1_aid|The aid, stated precisely] [scene:us21_ledger|The ledger] It shows both countries' gaps, and it compares systems, laws and governments, never peoples. Every status is stated as of October 2026, days before Israel votes on 27 October and the United States on 3 November; governments, coalitions, budgets and court cases described as current may change within weeks, and the talks on what replaces the aid agreement after 2028 will be conducted by whoever those elections choose.
