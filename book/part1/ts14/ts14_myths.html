The Ledger: Claims About Christian Zionism, Judged

This section gives a verdict on the claims about Christian Zionism a reader is most likely to meet. Documented means the record supports the claim. Contested means named sources stand on both sides and the record does not settle it. Unsupported means no source was found for it. Myth means the record contradicts it. Each verdict rests on documents cited in Parts One to Thirteen, with the stated position of those concerned beside each critical finding. [scene:ts4_rumours|Part Four: the rumours tested]

Origins

"Scofield created Christian Zionism." Myth. Christian belief in a Jewish return to the land was in print three centuries before his Bible: in Thomas Brightman's commentaries, first published in 1609, in Henry Finch's book of 1621, which James I treated as a libel, in Increase Mather's Boston sermons of 1669 and in the London Society, founded in 1809 to convert Jews. [cite:ts2_crome_cdamm] [cite:ts2_wiki_finch] Darby set out his system at Geneva in 1840, the Niagara Creed of 1878 declared that "Israel shall be restored to their own land," and Blackstone put the case to President Harrison on 5 March 1891. [cite:ts3_darby_hopes1] [cite:ts3_ak_niagara] [cite:ts6_moorhead_jets] What Scofield added was reach: the system printed beside the verse, in about two million copies by 1945. [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] Two related claims fail with it. The system was not "substantially non-existent" in America before 1909; it had a creed, conferences and Bible institutes. [cite:ts3_bt_niagara] [cite:ts3_weber_ct1999] And neither Darby nor the 1917 notes made a political return of Jews a precondition of Christ's coming: Darby's rapture was "any-moment," and Scofield's sequence at Deuteronomy 30 puts "The return of the Lord" before "Restoration to the land." [cite:ts3_weber_ct1999] [cite:ts5_sac_deut30]

"A land without a people for a people without a land" as a Zionist coinage. Myth; its later use and meaning are contested. Every documented use before 1901 is by a Christian writer: the Methodist Richard Watson before 1833, Alexander Keith in 1843, a Free Church review in 1844 and Shaftesbury in 1853, who wrote of "a country without a nation." The first recorded Zionist use was Israel Zangwill's in 1901, and by about 1904 he was saying the country "has already its inhabitants." [cite:ts2_wiki_landwithout] [cite:ts2_muir_meq] How widely Zionists later used it divides historians (Diana Muir against Anita Shapira, Boaz Neumann and Rashid Khalidi), and so does whether it meant the land was empty or that its inhabitants did not count as a nation. [cite:ts2_muir_meq] [cite:ts2_wiki_landwithout] [scene:ts2_slogan|Part Two: the phrase]

The man and the book

"Scofield was a convicted forger who served a prison sentence." Myth; the jailing is documented. He was held in the St. Louis jail from 8 October 1878 on forgery charges, and on 6 November 1879 the prosecution entered a nolle prosequi, dropping them for lack of evidence; there was no trial, verdict or sentence. [cite:ts4_rushing] [cite:ts4_doj_ks] The rest of the unflattering record holds: the Justice Department says he was "forced to resign" as US Attorney in 1873 over "questionable financial transactions," he deserted his wife and daughters, and he styled himself a doctor of divinity with no degree on record. The embellishments fail: one divorce, not two; no Canadian prison term on any record; no medal for Antietam. [cite:ts4_doj_ks] [cite:ts4_wiki_kellogg] [cite:ts4_wiki_scofield] [cite:ts4_sfiles] His own account was that grace changed him in 1879; no later charge is recorded. [cite:ts4_trumbull]

"Samuel Untermyer, or the Rothschilds, paid for the Scofield Bible." Myth. The only documented link is that both men belonged to New York's Lotos Club: Untermyer from 1894, Scofield from 3 March 1900, proposed by Horatio Fraser and seconded by John Elderkin. No letter, meeting or payment is recorded. [cite:ts4_sfiles] The story begins with Joseph Canfield's hostile biography of 1988, which named Untermyer as a likely sponsor with the words "we assume"; David Lutz in 2005 and the Washington Report on Middle East Affairs in 2015 restated it as fact without citing a document; and Michael Collins Piper's The High Priests of War (2004) was the first text to say the Rothschilds paid. [cite:ts4_sfiles] [cite:ts5_ocathail] [cite:ts5_wiki_piper] Check Your Fact rated the Rothschild claim false on 12 March 2025, and the writer Mike Rothschild found no primary source for it. [cite:ts4_cyf] [cite:ts4_mrothschild] The documented backers were the Brethren-linked businessmen Alwyn Ball, John Pirie, John Buss and Francis Fitch, with a reported $1,000 from the oilman Lyman Stewart in 1908; Untermyer's leading Zionist office began in 1921, and in 1905 Scofield's own consulting editor called Zionism "a movement ... of unbelief." [cite:ts4_sfiles] [cite:ts5_dunlap] [cite:ts5_wiki_lstewart] [cite:ts3_wiki_gaebelein] The claim has the shape of one of the oldest antisemitic accusations, that Jewish financiers secretly steer what Christians believe. It is rejected because the record contradicts it, and named because it travels faster than the record. [scene:ts5_funding|Part Five: who paid]

"The 1917 edition referred to the Balfour Declaration." Unsupported. The reference work cited for the claim does not say so, and the 1917 notes examined for this volume still describe Israel as "now in the third dispersion." The declaration is dated 2 November 1917, which left a revision prepared during that year at most weeks to absorb it. [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] [cite:ts1_sac_gen15] [cite:wb_bl_addms41178]

"Oxford sold tens of millions of Scofield Bibles." Unsupported. The citable figures are 1,925,000 copies in thirty years and more than two million by 1945. [cite:ts5_dunlap] [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] The tens of millions belong to two later books: Hal Lindsey's The Late Great Planet Earth, an estimated 28 to 35 million, and the Left Behind novels, 50 to 80 million by the reported counts. The "65 million" for Left Behind in earlier library material has no source. [cite:ts7_wiki_lgpe] [cite:ts7_wrap_lindsey] [cite:ts7_cp_lahaye] [cite:ts7_sa_lahaye]

Aims and influence

"Christian Zionists want Jews to die, or to convert." Contested; as a statement of any organisation's stated aim, the first half is a myth. The theology's texts foresee a judgment and a conversion. Darby said in 1840 that after the return "there will only be a remnant saved" and that "Two thirds" would be cut off; Scofield's 1917 notes place a "remnant" turning to Jesus "During the great tribulation" and list "National conversion" after the restoration; most restorationists, and Blackstone as late as 1918, hoped for the conversion of Jews. [cite:ts3_darby_hopes10] [cite:ts5_sac_rom11] [cite:ts5_sac_deut30] [cite:ts2_crome_cdamm] [cite:ts6_moorhead_jets] A forecast in a theology is not a stated wish, and the organisations state the opposite aim. CUFI has an official policy against proselytising Jews, Hagee says the support is "a debt of gratitude," David Brog says his members reject replacement theology, and CUFI's spokesman argues that no human can hasten the end. [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] [cite:ts1_jvl_cufi] [cite:ts9_j_brog2017] [cite:ts9_jta_morgenstern2010] Critics answer with documents of their own: interfaith leaders question how that fits Hagee's teaching of salvation through Christ alone; his 1990s sermon calling Hitler "a hunter" led John McCain to repudiate his endorsement in 2008; Sarah Posner calls Israel "just a pawn" in the prophecy; and in 2001 Israel's Chief Ashkenazi Rabbi condemned Christian money used to "expand Christian missionary propaganda." [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] [cite:ts8_cnn_hagee] [cite:ts9_dn_posner] [cite:ts8_jta_eckstein]

"Evangelicals set American policy on Israel." Contested. Their presence is documented: Pence promised the embassy move at CUFI's summit in July 2017, Jeffress and Hagee prayed at its opening on 14 May 2018, and Trump said in August 2020 that the move was "for the evangelicals." [cite:ts12_ncr_pence2017] [cite:ts12_cnn_pastors] [cite:ts9_almon_trump2020] The decisions' own texts gave other reasons: the 1995 Act and failed waivers for Jerusalem, security and Iran for the Golan, law and the stalled peace process for the settlements statement, which Blinken reversed in 2024. [cite:ts12_toi_trump2017] [cite:gi8_ucsb_procl9852] [cite:ts12_toi_pompeo2019] [cite:wb_axios_blinken] It also met limits: Huckabee called his "take it all" answer "hyperbolic," Trump said he would "not allow Israel to annex the West Bank," Brazil opened a trade office instead of an embassy, and in Thomas Massie's 2026 primary, where CUFI's Action Fund bought billboards, the Associated Press judged Trump's opposition the key factor. [cite:gi11_aje_huckabee] [cite:gi6_ap_trump0925] [cite:ts7_pti_brazil] [cite:ts8_ji_massie] [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways] [scene:ts12_officials|Part Twelve: the officials]

Churches, members and stories

"Kairos Palestine calls Christian Zionism a heresy." Unsupported. The word is not in the passages checked; the document of 2009 rejects "any theology that legitimizes the occupation" and targets "Biblical fundamentalism." [cite:ts11_transcend_kairos] [cite:ts11_jcr_kairos] The condemnation belongs to the Jerusalem Declaration of 22 August 2006, which called Christian Zionist doctrines "false teaching"; in January 2026 the Heads of Churches named it among "damaging ideologies." [cite:ts1_wiki_jerdecl] [cite:ts11_osv_heads2026] Nor did Rome rule against "Scofield's entire prophetic system": paragraph 676 of the Catechism rejects millenarianism in general. [cite:ts11_usccb_ccc676]

"Israel gave Falwell a Learjet," and "Begin telephoned Falwell to explain the Osirak strike." Unsupported. Neither was found in a contemporary report or document. What is documented is Falwell's 1979 meeting with Begin, the Jabotinsky medal of November 1980 and Rabbi Marc Tanenbaum's criticism in 1981 of Begin's choice of Falwell. [cite:ts8_lib_falwell] [cite:ts8_wiki_jabmedal] [cite:ts8_djn_tanenbaum]

Four further claims. That Darby took the secret rapture from Margaret MacDonald's vision of 1830 is unsupported: he dated his idea to 1827, her text places believers inside the "fiery trial," and Kenneth Strand found the case "falls apart." [cite:ts3_wiki_macdonald] [cite:ts3_strand_auss] That the Balfour Declaration was an act of Christian Zionism is contested between Donald Lewis's argument from formation and the war aims in the cabinet's minutes. [cite:ts6_wiki_cz] [cite:ts6_wiki_balfourdecl] CUFI's ten million members, announced in December 2020, are its own uncounted figure. [cite:ts8_hfl_10m] Truman's "I am Cyrus" is known from later accounts, and Paul Merkley and John Judis read it in opposite ways. [cite:ts6_merkley_cyrus] [cite:rs_cw2_judis_truman_cyrus]

The claims that fail are those that give the movement one author, one patron or one secret aim; the documented record is less flattering to its founders than their admirers allowed and more ordinary than their critics supposed. What stays contested is motive and effect, taken up next. [scene:ts14_record|What the record settles]
