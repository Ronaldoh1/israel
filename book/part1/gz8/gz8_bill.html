The Bill

The weapons. According to a study by William Hartung for Brown University's Costs of War project, the US government spent at least $21.7 billion on military aid to Israel between October 2023 and September 2025, not counting the tens of billions of dollars in arms sales committed for later years. The same project estimated that the United States spent a further $9.65 billion to $12.07 billion on its own military operations in Yemen and the wider region in the same period. [cite:gz_cow_aid] The library's account of [scene:pm10_costs|what the architecture costs] sets this against the longer history of American aid.

The rebuilding. In April 2026 the World Bank, the United Nations and the European Union published a joint assessment of the damage done in Gaza between October 2023 and October 2025. It put the physical damage at $35.2 billion and the economic and social losses at $22.7 billion, counted 371,888 housing units destroyed or damaged, found that the economy had contracted by 84 percent, and estimated that recovery and reconstruction would need $71.4 billion over the next decade, $16.2 billion of it for housing alone. [cite:gz_rdna2026]

Who pays the second bill is not settled. After 2014 the pledges came from donor governments and the materials passed through a mechanism Israel controlled; the US plan announced in September 2025, under which the October ceasefire took hold, places a Palestinian technocratic committee under an international "Board of Peace."
