# Volume II, Stage Three (1922–1936): research notes

Verified 2026-10-09. Every claim in `rd3.json` comes from a page fetched in this session. WebFetch returns a model-written summary of each page, not raw text, so quoted phrases are short. Check any quotation that will be printed against the original page before publication.

## 1. Lausanne, the Mandate in force, citizenship (1923–1925)
**Agree:** Lausanne was signed on 24 July 1923 and came into force on 6 August 1924. The League Council minutes of 29 September 1923 record the Palestine and Syria mandates entering into force. The Palestinian Citizenship Order took effect on 1 August 1925.
**Disagree:** John Quigley (Opinio Juris, July 2026) argues that the 29 September 1923 entry into force has no legal basis: the minutes were in the future tense, Drummond recast them in the past tense, and Lausanne gave Britain no title. One scholarly chapter (Abu Alia and Khalil) gives 12 August 1922, not 24 July 1922, as the League approval date.
**Not verified:** I could not fetch the text of Lausanne Articles 16 and 30. The figure of about 100 of 9,000 naturalization applications accepted is "reportedly" in the source, and its period is unclear.

## 2. Immigration, 1923–1936
**Agree:** Immigration was low in 1927–1931, then surged to a 1935 peak. The Fourth Aliyah brought about 82,000 people, of whom 23,000 left. Tel Aviv grew from about 15,000 (1922) to 120,000 (1935).
**Disagree:** There are two annual series. The State Department's compilation from British returns (FRUS 1939) gives 30,327 for 1933 and 61,854 for 1935; the JVL/Porath table gives 37,337 and 66,472. Scene pf_s01 rounds to the first series. None of the sources explains the gap.
**Not verified:** No neutral source gives the scale of Arab immigration into Palestine in the 1930s. The only fetched evidence is the US consul's 1939 statement that Arab growth was almost entirely natural increase. The Joan Peters-type claims were not researched, so the prose should not take them up.

## 3. Hebrew University and Brit Shalom (1925)
**Agree:** The university opened on 1 April 1925. Brit Shalom was founded in 1925 on Ruppin's initiative, never had more than 200 members, and was defunct by 1933.
**Disagree:** The Encyclopaedia Judaica says Magnes was never a member. Scene s00b3 calls him a founder.
**Not verified:** Arab protests against Balfour's 1925 visit (strike, mourning) and his reception in Damascus. The one contemporary page I fetched reports only "excitement" over a rumour.

## 4. The Western Wall and August 1929
**Agree:** The Yom Kippur screen of September 1928; the demonstrations of 15–16 August 1929; the outbreak on 23 August; Hebron on 24 August; Safed on 29 August (18–20 dead). The Shaw findings: not premeditated, and the Mufti bore "a share in the responsibility". The 1930 League commission found the Wall to be Muslim property, with Jewish rights of access.
**Disagree:** The total Jewish dead is 133 (EBSCO, JVL) or 113 (Jerusalem Story). Hebron is 67 dead (HRW, dated 23 August) or 1 + 64 (EBSCO). The trigger is told differently in Palestinian, Jewish and British accounts. All three are recorded as disputes.
**Not verified:** The names of the three men hanged on 17 June 1930. Wikipedia lists Fuad Hijazi, Ata al-Zir and Muhammad Jamjoum next to the date but does not state that they were the three. The Snell reservation to the Shaw report. A breakdown of Arab deaths by cause, beyond the JVL's statement that most were caused by British fire.

## 5. Hope Simpson, land and labour (1930)
**Agree (primary text):** 8,044,000 cultivable dunams; 29.4% of families in 104 villages landless; 688 displaced families, of whom 309 joined the landless; a ceiling of 100,000 dunams; the JNF Jewish-labour lease clause.
**Disagree:** The Jewish Agency published a statistical rebuttal in 1931. I fetched its title and catalogue page only, not its arguments.
**Not verified:** The details of the Agency's rebuttal.

## 6. The MacDonald letter (1931)
Taken from the primary text only. There is no disagreement about what the letter says. The disagreement is over what it meant, which is covered in pf_s01.

## 7. Palestinian politics, 1928–1935
**Agree:** The Seventh Congress (June 1928); the Women's Congress (26 October 1929); the Islamic Congress (opened 7 December 1931); Istiqlal (August 1932); the Murison findings on October 1933; the National Defense Party (December 1934); the Palestine Arab Party (27 March 1935).
**Disagree:** Whether the October 1933 killings by police were justified. Murison says the police showed restraint; all 26 civilian dead were shot by police.
**Not verified:** Istiqlal's exact founding day: Wikipedia gives 13 August 1932; Palquest gives only August 1932. Membership numbers for the parties. The Islamic Congress figures (130 delegates, 22 countries) rest on one press source.

## 8. Al-Qassam
**Agree:** Born in 1882 in Jabla; in Haifa from late 1920; went to the hills on 12 November 1935; killed near Ya'bad on 20 November 1935; a mass funeral in Haifa.
**Disagree:** How many followers he had (50–200, or 800), and how many died with him (two, or four). Palquest and the US consul agree on four.
**Not verified, kept out:** The Black Hand attacks: Yagur (11 April 1931, three killed), Nahalal (22 December 1932, father and son), and "at least eight Jews killed". These rest on Wikipedia alone, so the numbers are excluded. GlobalSecurity's claim that he killed "more than 15 British soldiers" is also excluded.

## 9–10. Revisionists; Arlosoroff and the London agreements
**Agree:** The movement grew from 4 delegates in 1925 to 52 in 1931 and 17% of the Yishuv vote. The Arlosoroff accused were acquitted in 1934 and cleared in 1985. The labour pact was rejected in the Histadrut plebiscite of 25 March 1935.
**Disagree:** "600,000": EJ counts 1934 petition signatures, while scene s07a3 counts members. One source (CIE) says Haavara transferred $100 million; scene hv_s01 says about $40 million.
**Not verified:** Final referendum totals (JTA gave early returns only). The exact dates of the London agreements in 1934.

## 11. Haganah, 1929–1935
**Agree:** A few hundred members in the 1920s; a parity national command in 1931; Tehomi's secession that led to the Irgun; 537 barrels seized at Jaffa.
**Disagree:** The seizure date is 16 October 1935 (Wikipedia) or 18 November 1935 (EJ).
**Not verified:** The founding date of a Haganah intelligence body in 1929. Scene cia_s01 asserts it, but the same scene dates the Shai to 1940, and EJ gives no date.

## 12. The economy (1922–1936)
**Agree:** The IPC pipeline reached Haifa on 16 October 1934.
**Disagree:** Horowitz's 1937 figures are the Jewish Agency's own advocacy for the Peel Commission. Hope Simpson's findings cut the other way. Horowitz's 1922 Jewish share (13%) differs from the census (11%).
**Not verified:** The Haifa harbour opening date (1933); no neutral page was fetched. Independent wage series.

## 13. Legislative council, 1935–36
Sourced to JTA dispatches and FRUS. **Not verified:** The outcome of the March 1936 Commons debate, and the details of the Lords debate.

## 14. Jews of the Arab world
**Reported only:** The Iraqi measures of 1934–36 come from one source, Sephardi Voices UK. "All Jews dismissed from the civil service" in 1936 is a strong claim that needs a second source before it is stated without attribution. Constantine (25 Jews and 3 Muslims killed) is from Joshua Cole.
