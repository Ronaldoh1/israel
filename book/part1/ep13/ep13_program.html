The Program, in Its Authors' Words

The Dark Enlightenment is a body of writing, not an organisation. Its two authors are the software engineer Curtis Yarvin, who wrote as Mencius Moldbug, and the philosopher Nick Land, who named the movement in a serial published in 2012. [cite:de1_land_gwern] [cite:de2_land_ebook] Its claims, in their words, are these.

First, that real power in a democracy sits with a network of officials, journalists and academics. Land, describing that network, wrote: "Moldbug calls it the Cathedral." [cite:de1_land_gwern] Second, that a state should be run as a company: "To a neocameralist, a state is a business which owns a country," with shareholders, a board and managers, and residents as customers. [cite:de4_land_de] Third, that the citizen's remedy should be leaving, not voting. Yarvin's 2008 design called for small states "each governed by its own joint-stock corporation without regard to the residents' opinions", and said that residents who did not like their government "can and should move". [cite:de2_le] Land summarised it as "No voice, free exit." [cite:de1_land_ebook] Fourth, a route to power. In a 2012 talk Yarvin told an audience they would have to "get over their dictator phobia" and accept "a national CEO", and named a first step: "Retire All Government Employees". [cite:de6_pein_2014] [cite:de2_wiki_yarvin] Asked by the New York Times in 2025 whether the word for what he proposes is monarch, he said, "Monarch is good." [cite:de1_nyt_interview]

The program has a famous sympathiser in Peter Thiel, whose 2009 sentence "I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible" Land quoted; but the library's volume on the movement found that Thiel's own program was one of exit, not seizure, and that much of the movement's supposed reach rests on resemblance rather than documents. [cite:de1_land_gwern] [cite:de1_cato_thiel] This part takes the texts at their word as a design, and asks only what the design would do to the tools in the previous section.
