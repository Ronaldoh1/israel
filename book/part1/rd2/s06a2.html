"Jordan Is Already Palestine": A Claim and Its Withdrawal

In the early 1920s the British government set Transjordan apart from the rest of the Palestine Mandate, and that decision outlived the Mandate itself as an argument. A familiar version runs that Jordan already is the Palestinian state: the partition of 1921 gave the Arabs their homeland, so a second Palestinian state would be redundant. The claim is often treated as a purely pro-Israel talking point. The documented record is more complicated. Jordan's own rulers made versions of it, for their own reasons, before Jordan's government formally and explicitly renounced it.

The administrative history beneath the argument is real. In 1921 Britain brought Transjordan within the scope of the Palestine Mandate through Article 25, while exempting it from the provisions dealing with the Jewish National Home. Amir Abdullah became ruler of Transjordan, which had been officially separated from Palestine by 1923. [cite:wiki_borders_of_israel] [cite:wiki_transjordan_memorandum] The historian Bernard Wasserstein has noted that this decision came to be known, in later ideological use, as "the myth of Palestine's first partition." His phrase does not deny that the separation happened or that it mattered. It marks the distance between a documented administrative act and the political conclusion later drawn from it, a conclusion that remains contested.

Some of the strongest statements of that conclusion came from Amman itself. In 1948 King Abdullah I declared:

"Palestine and Jordan are one, for Palestine is the coastline and Transjordan the hinterland of the same country."

In 1959 the Jordanian Prime Minister, Hazza Majali, said: "We are the government of Palestine, the army of Palestine, and the refugees of Palestine." King Hussein made comparable statements at several points. [cite:meforum_jordan_west_bank] These were not concessions to Israeli arguments. They were Jordanian claims, made to support the kingdom's annexation of the West Bank and its influence over Palestinian politics. Nor were they a permanent or unanimous Jordanian position, as later events showed.

On July 31, 1988, King Hussein announced the dissolution of Jordan's legal and administrative ties with the West Bank. [cite:springer_jordan_palestine_history] Here the Jordanian state itself, acting formally and on a specific date, renounced any continuing claim to represent or administer Palestinian territory or population. The decision reversed the direction of the statements of 1948 and 1959. The full range of political and strategic considerations behind its timing lies beyond what the sources here document.

Jordan's present position is just as plain. Asked in a televised interview whether Jordan should be considered the de facto Palestinian state, King Abdullah II answered: "Jordan is Jordan." [cite:davidsinger_jordan_palestinian_state] A large share of Jordan's population is of Palestinian origin, descended from those displaced in 1948 and 1967, and that remains a significant factor in the country's politics. But the Jordanian government does not treat this population as a separate Palestinian polity within its borders.

The record therefore cuts more than one way. Those who dismiss the "Jordan is Palestine" claim as an argument invented by outsiders must reckon with the fact that Jordan's own kings and ministers once advanced it. Those who rely on it must reckon with the fact that the state in question has formally and repeatedly rejected it since 1988, most recently in the words of its current monarch. Whatever historical weight the 1921 separation carries, a state's identity is not conferred on it by others' arguments alone. Jordan's government has withdrawn its consent to this one. [cite:davidsinger_jordan_palestinian_state]

The question belongs in an account of the years 1917 to 1922 because it began there. The decision recorded in [scene:s06a|the Churchill White Paper] was an act of British administration. What it would later be made to mean was decided by others, over decades, and that meaning has shifted with the interests of those invoking it. [cite:wiki_borders_of_israel]
