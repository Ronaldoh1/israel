The Watch List

A grade in this book is a statement about the record on a date, 28 October 2026, not a forecast. The record keeps moving, and the scorecard in [scene:de40_worst_case_scorecard|the previous section] is only useful if a reader can see what would change it. This section lists the triggers: dated or pending events, each checkable in a public docket, a contract database, a federal register or an election result, with the row it would move and in which direction. Some would push a grade toward the worst case; as many would push it away. The list builds on the four missing pieces set out in [scene:de34_missing_pieces_and_triggers|Part Thirty-Six] and was rechecked for this section on 28 October 2026.

The courts

The nearest date is a conference. On 6 November 2026 the Supreme Court is due to consider whether to hear GEO Group's challenge to the Washington verdict that required it to pay the state's minimum wage to detainees who worked in its Tacoma centre, instead of the $1 a day the federal programme has set since 1950. The petition was filed on 9 January 2026; the Court asked for the government's views in May, and the United States filed its brief on 15 September. [cite:de40_scotus_25828] [cite:de32_wa_nwauzor] [cite:de32_pogo_dollar] A refusal to hear the case would leave the $23.2 million judgment standing and keep state wage law as a check on the "compel" rung of [scene:de35_the_consequence_ladder|the consequence ladder]; a grant followed by a reversal would weaken it. Neither would make compelled labour a policy.

Early in 2027 the Court is expected to hear argument in U.S. DOGE Service v. U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, No. 25-1103, which it agreed to decide in October 2026. The question is whether the service is an agency subject to the Freedom of Information Act or an adviser to the President outside it, and whether the watchdog CREW may take discovery, including the deposition of its administrator, Amy Gleason. [cite:de40_ncc_doge] [cite:de40_kmph_doge] A ruling that the service was outside the Act would settle in law the opacity that [scene:de21_the_ending|Part Twenty-One] graded probable as a matter of design; a ruling the other way would open DOGE's records to the public.

Mahmoud Khalil, a lawful permanent resident arrested in March 2025 and found removable on the Secretary of State's determination that his advocacy harmed American foreign policy, petitioned the Supreme Court in October 2026 after a divided appeals court held that he could not litigate his claims before a judge until his immigration case ended. Justice Alito had twice extended his time to file, the second time to 19 October. ABC News reported that the justices were likely to consider the petition in January; he was free pending it and has not been charged with any crime. [cite:de40_scotus_26a90] [cite:de40_abc_khalil] [cite:de34_ccr_khalil] The case decides how quickly a non-citizen critic can reach a federal judge, the remedy row in the scorecard's second table.

Four appeals bear on the turn against critics. In AAUP v. Rubio the government appealed the vacatur of its speech-based deportation policy to the First Circuit on 9 February 2026, and the plaintiffs cross-appealed; the district court refused to stay most of its judgment. [cite:de40_cl_aaup] The challenge by four law firms to the executive orders against them was argued in the D.C. Circuit on 14 May 2026, where the panel appeared inclined to keep the orders blocked; no decision had been published by 28 October. [cite:de40_chn_lawfirms] [cite:de40_bl_lawfirms] In the case of Francesca Albanese, the UN rapporteur sanctioned in July 2025, a district judge enjoined the sanctions on 13 May 2026, and the same appeals court stayed that injunction pending appeal on 15 June 2026 without ruling on whether the sanctions are lawful. [cite:de36_audit_albanese_pi] [cite:de40_jc_albanese] [cite:de34_jurist_albanese] And a Minnesota observer's suit over the loss of her Global Entry card remained pending in the Southern District of California. [cite:de25b_bu_cleland] An appellate ruling upholding any of these measures would move row 19 or row 20 toward the worst case; rulings against them would hold it where it is.

Two cases decide how far the state's own records travel. In California v. HHS, Judge Vince Chhabria's injunction of 29 December 2025 limits what Medicaid data HHS may give ICE to five fields on people not lawfully present; at a hearing in September 2026 he said holders of valid visas should be protected and told the parties to agree, category by category, on who else is. No further ruling had been found by 28 October. [cite:de40_bl_medicaid] [cite:de40_chn_medicaid] In the welfare case, Chief Judge James Boasberg refused on 28 September 2026 to block an HHS notice letting TANF recipients' information go to DHS, and the notice took effect on 30 September. [cite:de24b_bl_tanf] The first documented enforcement use of TANF records would move row 14 from possible toward documented. In PEER v. Trump, the challenge to the new civil-service schedule, motions to dismiss and to amend were pending, with no injunction sought. [cite:de40_cl_peer]

Contracts and systems

The integration layer runs on orders with end dates. The Army's order for Palantir to sustain its Personnel Data Environment, $3.68 million, runs to 15 November 2026. [cite:de24b_govly_pde] ICE's newest Palantir order, $86.3 million to modernise the case system of its removal operations, runs from 5 May 2026 to 4 May 2027. [cite:de27_hg_ero] ImmigrationOS itself was added in 2025 as a $29.9 million modification to an older order for ICE's investigative case system, which can be extended to 26 September 2027. [cite:de40_os_immos] [cite:de40_hg_icm] Renewals at larger scope, or a new order joining HART or welfare data, would move row 13 or 14; a lapse would not, because the data already joined stays joined. DHS's biometric system, HART, was scheduled to reach initial operation no later than September 2026; no public confirmation that it had was found by 28 October. [cite:de40_bu_hart] Its operation would supply the nearest thing to the identity spine [scene:de34_missing_pieces_and_triggers|Part Thirty-Six] found missing.

Oversight, directives and elections

Three questions put to the executive in 2026 were unanswered at the date of this book. The DHS inspector general opened an audit of biometric data security in February 2026 and had published no report. [cite:de28_warner_audit] Thirty-three members of Congress asked DHS on 14 April 2026 for every dataset in its Palantir systems and any use against observers and protesters, with no published reply. [cite:de28_goldman_letter] And the Attorney General confirmed on 11 February 2026 that a list of domestic terrorist organisations exists under her December 2025 memorandum, but declined to share it. [cite:de34_int_list] Senators reported in September 2026 a Treasury review of three named nonprofits; no revocation is on record. [cite:de30_wyden_letter] The publication of the list with legal effect, or a first revocation, would move row 21; a report finding no misuse would move row 22 down.

The midterm elections fall on 3 November 2026. Federal law makes it a crime to station troops or armed men at polling places. [cite:de32_votebeat_polls] Asked by TIME about an emergency before the vote, the president said he did not rule "anything out or in," and of the Insurrection Act, "I've never used it. I could use it." It had not been invoked as of 28 October 2026. [cite:de40_dbeast_time] [cite:de32_hill_time] An invocation, or troops near the polls, would be the largest single move on the scorecard, against rows 23 and 25. An election held and certified in the ordinary way would be the strongest evidence that row 25 stays where it is. The vice president, the one person in this book's network in the line of succession, has said he will decide on 2028 after the midterms. [cite:de12_cnn_2028]

Two kinds of trigger are missing from the dated list because they cannot be dated: a document and a refusal. A memo, a sworn account or a disclosed message showing that someone in power set out to build what the texts describe would move the not-supported rows faster than any ruling, and [scene:de38_the_honest_nulls|Part Forty-One] lists where such a document would be found if it existed. [cite:de17_li_oped] The other is the moment someone asked to carry out an unlawful order declines, which military law expects of an order that is "manifestly illegal"; no refusal of that kind is on the public record for 2025 or 2026. [cite:de32_stripes_orders] [scene:de40_the_garden|The last section] turns to those people.
