The Money After 2021

From the 2022 cycle the record of pro-Israel money changes in two ways at once. The amounts rise steeply, and the measures multiply, because money now flows through channels the old series did not count. This section sets out the figures for 2020 to 2026 channel by channel, with the source and date of each, and explains why figures that look as if they describe the same thing do not.

Contributions to candidates. The Washington Report on Middle East Affairs, an advocacy publication critical of the lobby that tabulates FEC data, counted pro-Israel PAC contributions to House and Senate candidates of $12.7 million in the 2020 cycle, $17.2 million in 2022 and $44.7 million in 2024. [cite:pm_wrmea_2024] Those figures are larger than OpenSecrets' PAC-only series, which put 2020 at $8.7 million, [cite:pm_jvl_pacs2020] most likely because they count earmarked money and a wider set of committees, though the publication does not set out its method; the two series should not be joined into one line. WRMEA's count rose about three and a half times between 2020 and 2024; AIPAC's PAC alone reported more in earmarked contributions in 2024, $55.2 million on Sludge's count, than WRMEA counted for all pro-Israel PACs, so the series measure different things. By AIPAC's own account its PAC passed more than $53 million from over 10,000 members to candidates in the 2024 cycle, [cite:pm_aipac_memo_2024] and Sludge's count of the final filings found more than $55.2 million donated to federal candidates by the PAC. [cite:pm_sludge_final2024] In the 2026 cycle the PAC had given $45.9 million by the end of July, almost all of it earmarked, with particular attention to members of the House and Senate defense and appropriations committees. [cite:pm_sludge_pac2026]

AIPAC's two committees together. Adding the super PAC's spending to the PAC's gives the measure most often quoted. On OpenSecrets' count the two committees spent $44 million in 2022 and $95.1 million in 2024. [cite:pm_israelite_2024] Sludge, counting all disbursements in the final 2024 filings, including the super PAC's operating costs and its transfers to six other committees, put the 2024 total at nearly $126.9 million. [cite:pm_sludge_final2024] For 2026, Sludge counted $104.3 million by early August, and the super PAC alone had raised about $104 million by June. [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] The differences are of scope and date, not of fact: whether transfers and overheads are counted, and when the filings were read.

The other committees. AIPAC was not the only pro-Israel group whose spending grew. OpenSecrets' figures for 2022 and 2024 show the Republican Jewish Coalition and its super PAC rising from $4.5 million to $19.7 million, J Street from $8.6 million to $14.6 million, and NORPAC from $1.6 million to $2.7 million; Democratic Majority for Israel was the only major pro-Israel group to spend less, $4.8 million against $7.7 million. [cite:pm_israelite_2024]

The shift in kind. The figures show two changes beyond size. The first is in direction. In 2018 the money as a whole favored Democrats about two to one. [cite:pm_tablet_lobbying] [cite:pm_guardian_2019] By AIPAC's account, 206 of the 326 general-election winners it backed in 2024 were Republicans, [cite:pm_aipac_memo_2024] while its super PAC's largest sums were spent against Democrats in Democratic primaries. The second is in method. Until 2021 almost all pro-Israel money was given to candidates; in 2022 the largest sums were spent around them, and in 2024 contributions and outside spending were of similar size, the outside spending in advertising that, as Part Five shows, rarely mentioned Israel, and from 2026 increasingly through committees with unrelated names. [cite:punchbowl_udp_no_israel] [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026]

What is not yet counted. Two gaps remain. Per-cycle lists of the largest recipients in each party, built from the FEC's own files rather than a tracker's compilation, are not yet available; Part Four therefore uses the career totals compiled by Track AIPAC and the 2018 figures above, and says so. And the 2026 figures stop at the dates given. The general election of November 2026 and the final reports that follow it will change every 2026 number in this section.
