What the Record Settles

The alliance and the arms trade. Iran voted against partition in 1947 and recognised Israel de facto on 6 March 1950, never de jure; the CIA's own history says the 1953 coup that restored the Shah was "carried out under CIA direction." [cite:ir1_iranica_israel] [cite:ir1_afp_cia2013] Under the Shah the two states exchanged intelligence from 1957, built the Eilat–Ashkelon pipeline as equal partners and in 1977 signed oil-for-arms contracts that included a missile project; Israel has not paid the $1.1 billion award for the last Iranian oil that a Swiss court upheld in 2016. [cite:ir1_alpher_fa] [cite:ir1_wiki_flower] [cite:ir1_ih_swiss2016] [cite:ir1_shomrim_2024] The revolution handed Israel's mission to the PLO in February 1979, yet Israel sold the Islamic Republic tyres, spare parts and arms through the war with Iraq, and in 1985, with American acquiescence, 504 TOW missiles and 18 HAWKs. [cite:ir2_wiki_embassy] [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] [cite:ir2_walsh18]

Iran's Jews: two documented facts. The constitution names Jews as a recognised minority and reserves them a seat in the Majlis. [cite:ir3_const_en] The same state shot Habib Elghanian for "friendship with Israel" in 1979, convicted ten Jews as spies in 2000, hosted Holocaust deniers in 2006, and questioned at least 35 Jews about relatives in Israel after the June 2025 war. [cite:ir3_tablet_hakakian] [cite:ir3_ap_shiraz2000] [cite:ir3_wiki_conf2006] [cite:ir3_toi_summoned]

The two nuclear records. Iran joined the NPT in 1970; the IAEA found that it concealed activities and ran a coordinated effort relevant to a nuclear explosive device until the end of 2003, with no credible indications of such work after 2009. [cite:ir4_aca_timeline] [cite:ir4_wnn_pmd2015] After the United States left the 2015 deal, which the IAEA found Iran had kept until July 2019, Iran enriched to 60 percent and by May 2025 held 408.6 kg of it, a stockpile the agency called unique among states without nuclear weapons. [cite:ir7_iaea_impday] [cite:ir7_ap_may19] [cite:ir4_ap_may2025] Israel never signed the treaty, has kept Dimona outside IAEA safeguards under the opacity agreed with Nixon in 1969, and is estimated by SIPRI to hold about 90 warheads, which it neither confirms nor denies. [cite:ow11_aca_opacity] [cite:ir4_fas_israel] [cite:ir4_ynet_sipri]

The predictions, as tested. Every dated forecast of an Iranian bomb given without a condition passed without one: Netanyahu's three to five years in 1992, Peres's warheads by 1999, Netanyahu's "one or two years" in 2009, and the Mossad's window of 2010–11. [cite:hz11b_week_1992] [cite:ir6_csm_peres1992] [cite:hz11b_intercept_wolf] [cite:ir6_csm_yaalon2010] US intelligence judged in 2007 that weapons work had halted in 2003, and in March 2025 that Iran was "not building a nuclear weapon." [cite:hz11_wh_nie2007] [cite:hz11_bg_gabbard] The UN red line of 2012 and the warning of 13 June 2025 were conditional, and events overtook both before they could be tested. [cite:ir5_li_un2012] [cite:ir5_dcnf_jun2025]

Iran's own record. Khamenei called Israel a "cancerous tumor" to be removed and predicted in 2015 that it would not exist in 25 years. [cite:ir9_rferl_tumor2020] [cite:ir9_cnn_25yrs] Iran funds and arms Hezbollah by Hezbollah's own account, a US court convicted a man for a Quds Force plot to kill the Saudi ambassador in Washington, and Argentina's Cassation Court held in 2024 that Iran ordered the Buenos Aires bombings. [cite:hz6_alarabiya_2016] [cite:ir9_doj_arbabsiar] [cite:hz4_bat_cassation] At home its forces killed at least 5,000 prisoners in 1988 by Amnesty's count, 551 protesters in 2022 by the UN mission's, and thousands in January 2026, a figure Khamenei himself acknowledged. [cite:ir9_amnesty_1988] [cite:ir9_un_ffm2024] [cite:ir9_npr_jan2026]

The wars' tolls, by each count's method. The twelve days of June 2025 killed 28 people in Israel and between about 1,060 and 1,190 in Iran, by the Iranian government's and HRANA's counts. [cite:ir10_toi_numbers] [cite:ir10_ap_toll] In the forty days from 28 February 2026, Iran's government counted 3,468 dead and HRANA at least 3,636, including 1,701 civilians; Amnesty found that American Tomahawks killed more than 150 people at a school in Minab; Iranian strikes killed between 24 and 28 people in Israel and at least 23 in the Gulf states. [cite:ir11_afp_tolls] [cite:ir11_amnesty_minab] [cite:ir11_wiki_strikesisrael] [cite:ir11_amnesty_beitshemesh] By 9 October 21 American service members were dead and 865 wounded. What Is Contested

The fatwa. Iran cited Khamenei's ruling against nuclear weapons at the IAEA in 2005; no written text has been published, MEMRI concluded that none existed, and Juan Cole disputed that. [cite:ir4_wiki_fatwa] [cite:ir4_hudson_fatwa]

The 2003 fax. A Swiss outline of comprehensive talks reached the State Department; Iran's diplomat reportedly said the leadership agreed to most of it, while American officials and Michael Rubin argue it was the Swiss ambassador's own work. Washington did not answer it. [cite:ir12_ips_bargain] [cite:ir12_nr_rubin]

The words of 2005. Ahmadinejad's sentence reads as "vanish from the page of time" to Cole and Jonathan Steele and as "wiped off the map" to the New York Times and its translators; both readings agree he called for the end of Israel as a state, and differ on whether he threatened war. [cite:ir9_atl_translation] [cite:ir9_steele_2006]

Motives for 2018. Trump cited the deal's sunsets and missiles, not a breach; Eisenkot, Ya'alon and Pardo later called the withdrawal a mistake. That the exit caused Iran's later enrichment, rather than Iran's own choices, is argued both ways. [cite:ir7_toi_trump] [cite:ir7_toi_eisenkot] [cite:ir7_nl_zilber]

Motives for 2025 and 2026. Israel's government cited an existential nuclear and missile threat and the IAEA's breach finding; Jon Hoffman of the Cato Institute argued Netanyahu also had domestic reasons; AP found Iran had been "months or years away" with no decision to build. [cite:ir13_pbs_bibi2026] [cite:ir5_aj_iaea2025] [cite:hz12_cato_hoffman] [cite:ir5_ap_jun2026]

The damage. Trump said the sites were "obliterated"; a leaked preliminary DIA assessment said months, the CIA said years, the Pentagon "probably closer to two years," and Grossi said enrichment could resume within months. [cite:ir10_gov_address] [cite:ir10_ng_cia] [cite:ir10_ss_parnell]
