The Kingdom and the Cause, 1945–1991

On 14 February 1945 President Roosevelt met King Abdulaziz Ibn Saud aboard the USS Quincy in the Suez Canal. According to the American memorandum of the conversation, Roosevelt raised a Jewish home in Palestine and the king opposed it, arguing that the Jews should be given land in Germany, whose people had persecuted them, rather than in Palestine. [cite:quincy_meeting_1945] [cite:ibn_saud_bavaria_quote] In a letter weeks before his death, Roosevelt assured the king that he would take no action "hostile to the Arab people" on Palestine. [cite:fdr_letter_ibnsaud] Three years later his successor recognised Israel within minutes of its founding.

Saudi Arabia sent only a small contingent, under Egyptian command, to the war of 1948, never opened relations with Israel, and joined the Arab League boycott, which barred trade with Israel and with foreign firms that traded with it. [cite:wr_wiki_saudi_israel] [cite:wr_wiki_boycott] After the war of 1967 the Arab League met at Khartoum and adopted the "three no's": no peace with Israel, no recognition, no negotiations. [cite:w_khartoum] That remained the formal position for 35 years.

Oil as a weapon

When the United States resupplied Israel during the war of October 1973, King Faisal led the Arab oil producers in an embargo on the United States and other states that backed Israel. It lasted until March 1974 and helped quadruple world oil prices. [cite:oil_embargo_1973] [cite:w_1973_embargo] After Egypt made peace with Israel in 1979, Saudi Arabia joined the Arab states that cut relations with Cairo, and the Arab League moved its headquarters to Tunis. In 1991 the kingdom hosted more than half a million coalition troops to expel Iraq from Kuwait; Israel, struck by Iraqi Scud missiles, stayed out of the war at American request so as not to break the Arab coalition.
