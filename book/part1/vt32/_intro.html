Part Thirty-Six closes Book Two and the volume. It adds almost no new evidence; it gathers what Parts Twenty to Thirty-Five found and weighs it. The first section sets out a ledger of every documented act against the people and books of the inner traditions, from Irenaeus's refutation of about 180 to the Holy See's 2023 note on Freemasonry, together with the acts of Parts Thirty-Two to Thirty-Five against the mystery schools, the books and sun cults of the Americas and the mother goddesses, from Theodosius's temple laws of 391 to the Tiber in 2019, naming in each case who acted, in what document, against whom and with what result, and sets the most common popular versions of that history beside the record. The second gives the verdict on the thesis Book Two tests, that institutional religion built a mediated, taxed and obligatory system over teachings of direct knowledge of God and then condemned, burned or absorbed those who kept them, in its narrower form and in the wider one the last four parts tested, split into its parts and graded, as Part Nineteen graded Book One's question, into what is documented, what is probable and what is not supported, with the platform's interpretive lenses set out in one labelled passage. The third follows what survived, buried, preserved by its enemies, kept alive or found again, from Nag Hammadi to the four Maya codices, the Florentine Codex and the revived Inti Raymi, names the questions that remain open, and ends the volume.
