Part Three follows the system that turned an old Protestant hope into a detailed map of the future. It begins with John Nelson Darby, the Anglo-Irish clergyman who left the Church of Ireland, helped found the Plymouth Brethren and set out, at the Powerscourt conferences of the early 1830s, a way of reading the Bible in which Israel and the church are two separate peoples with two separate destinies; it weighs the long-running claim that his doctrine of a secret rapture was borrowed from a young Scottish visionary, Margaret MacDonald. It then explains in plain terms what the system teaches about Israel and the church, and tests the common claim that Darby made a Jewish return to Palestine a condition of Christ's coming. Last, it follows the system across the Atlantic: Darby's own journeys, the St. Louis pastor James H. Brookes and the Niagara Bible Conferences with their 1878 creed, the evangelist Dwight L. Moody and his Bible institute, and Arno Gaebelein, who would supply the prophetic notes for the Scofield Reference Bible.
