The tools of the previous six parts operate inside a body of law, and this part examines the law itself as it changed in 2025 and 2026. The first section follows a single idea, making career civil servants easier to remove, from Curtis Yarvin's 2012 proposal to retire them all, through the Schedule F order of 2020 and J.D. Vance's remark of 2021, to the rule and order of 2026 that moved about 8,000 senior officials into a new category, and sets each against the others clause by clause. The second traces the legal walls built in the 1970s between the government's separate stores of personal data, and how each was breached for immigration enforcement and tested in court. The third reads the September 2025 directive on domestic terrorism and its implementing memorandum, and the Army's commissioning of technology executives, and closes Book Five by testing the book's worst-case reading against everything the toolkit parts have documented.
