Iran became a theocracy in 1979; before that it was a constitutional monarchy with an elected parliament, the Majlis. On March 15, 1951, the Majlis voted to nationalize the British-controlled oil industry, and Mohammad Mosaddegh became prime minister. [cite:w_iran_oil_nationalization] Britain answered with a worldwide embargo on Iranian oil; production fell from 242 million barrels in 1950 to 10.6 million in 1952. Mosaddegh's own record was not spotless: in August 1953 he dissolved parliament by a plebiscite his opponents called rigged. [cite:loc_mossadeq]

Days later, on August 19, 1953, a coup organized by the CIA and Britain's MI6 removed him and restored the Shah's power. [cite:w_1953_coup] The CIA formally acknowledged its role only in 2013. [cite:nsarchive_cia_confirms_role] For the next quarter century the Shah ruled as an autocrat and a close US ally, and as Israel's main oil supplier. The Islamic Republic arrived twenty-six years after the elected government was removed: the end of that sequence, not its beginning.
