By 1592 the Western canon had two forms, and both bore the marks of the conflict that produced them. The Catholic list of 73 books was made dogma at Trent on 8 April 1546, by 24 votes to 15, at a council convened by a pope who had made a fourteen-year-old grandson a cardinal; its official Latin text was a papal edition recalled after its editor's death and re-issued with a preface blaming the printers. [cite:vt14_wiki_trentcanon] [cite:cn_wiki_paul3] [cite:cn_wiki_sixtine] The Protestant Bible set apart the books Jerome had doubted, among them the Maccabees that Catholic teaching cites for prayer for the dead, and a Bible society's funding decision made the 66-book edition standard. [cite:vt14_wiki_protbible] [cite:cn_va_ccc1031] Behind both lay a transaction: forgiveness sold to repay a bank.
