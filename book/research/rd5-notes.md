# Volume II, Stage Five (1945–1948): research notes

Verified 2026-10-09. Every claim in `rd5.json` comes from a page fetched in this session. WebFetch returns a model-written summary of each page, not raw text, so quotations are short. Check any quotation that will be printed against the original page before publication. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so several gaps listed below could not be researched in this pass.

## 1. Bevin, 1945–1946 (`bevin_1945_1946`)
**Agree:** Bevin became Foreign Secretary when Attlee formed his government on 26 July 1945 (Britannica). On 13 November 1945 he announced the Anglo-American Committee (statement text via the NYT); the US embassy reported the same day that he would "stake his political future" on a solution (FRUS, primary). Langham (Jewish Historical Studies, 2012) documents the "head of the queue" press-conference remark and the June 1946 "too many Jews in New York" remark.
**Disagree:** Historians disagree on motive. Some say antisemitism (Mayhew, Weizmann; Weiler says he held such views but that they did not drive policy). Some say anti-Zionism without antisemitism (Bullock, Ben-Gurion, Attlee). Some say strategic interest (the Foreign Office/Beeley briefing, Louis). Langham concludes he was probably not driven by antisemitism.
**Not verified:** Hansard was not reachable. The 13 November statement comes from a reprint of the NYT text. The monthly immigration figure ("present monthly rate") is not given in that text.

## 2. The Jewish Resistance Movement (`jewish_revolt_1945_1946`)
**Agree / single source:** Formation (September 1945), the Night of the Trains (1 November 1945), the Night of the Bridges (17 June 1946, eleven bridges), and the halt (August and December 1946). All of this comes from the Lehi heritage site only, so attribute it.
**Not verified:** Casualties. I found no independent scholarly source for these dates.

## 3. Britain's exit, 1947 (`britain_exit_1947`)
**Agree:** The 14 February 1947 Cabinet decision comes from FRUS (primary). A/286 (2 April 1947) and Creech Jones's statement of 26 September 1947 are primary. The Exodus details (more than 4,500 passengers; three dead; Port-de-Bouc; Hamburg) appear in both USHMM and Yad Vashem.
**Disagree:** Yad Vashem dates the attack to 17 July and arrival at Haifa to 18 July. USHMM dates the clash to 18 July. Historians also weigh the sergeants affair differently: Bell and Creech Jones give it heavy weight, while Britain's own stated reason was the deadlock.
**Not verified:** The 18 February 1947 Commons text comes from COJS, which gives no archival citation. All sergeants-affair facts come from Wikipedia only (dates kept, figures dropped). I also could not confirm which UNSCOP members saw the Exodus.

## 4. UNSCOP (`unscop_1947`)
**Agree:** The UN Yearbook and the UNSCOP report (via JVL) agree on the resolution, members, chair, itinerary, the boycott letters, the Beirut hearing of six Arab governments, the DP-camp visit and the 7–3 split, with Australia abstaining.
**Disagree:** The Center for Israel Education says eight members backed the majority plan, but the report says seven. The simulation's own s07i says the committee heard testimony "almost exclusively" from the Zionist side, which fits Palestinian bodies but not the Arab governments heard in Beirut. The writer must correct this.
**Not verified:** Ben-Gurion's testimony date (the scene has it; the report excerpt I read did not).

## 5. Resolution 181 (`res181_1947`)
**Agree:** The Ad Hoc Committee vote was 25–13–17, and the final vote 33–13–10. The full abstain and against lists, the timetable and the Commission's members all come from the UN Yearbook.
**Disagree:** The population of the Jewish state: Yusuf (Birzeit) gives about 498,000 Jews and 494,000 Arabs, Sachar (via JVL) 538,000 and 397,000. The land share: Yusuf 56%, the simulation 55%, JVL roughly 5,500 square miles, about 60% of it Negev.
**Not verified:** The text of the resolution's boundary annex, and UNSCOP's own population table (not reached).

## 6. Palestinian Arab leadership and the Arab League (`arab_leadership_1945_1948`)
**Agree:** Shlaim (JPS 1990) and the Dictionary of the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict agree that the League reconstituted the AHC, that the Mufti escaped to Cairo, that the League refused a government-in-exile, and on the All-Palestine Government and Jericho.
**Notes:** The Arab League session list comes from JVL, which cites League records. Treat it as reported.
**Not verified:** The date of the 1946 reconstitution. The Dictionary entry wrongly calls Resolution 181 a Security Council resolution, so that claim was not used.

## 7. The civil war (`civil_war_1947_1948`)
**Agree:** The Office of the Historian describes irregulars attached to the Arab Liberation Army. Palquest gives Abd al-Qadir's death on 8 April 1948. Pogońska-Pol says the two Arab forces did not cooperate.
**Not verified:** The Arab Liberation Army's entry date and strength, the date of the Jewish Agency building bombing, and the fall of Jaffa and Safed.

## 8. Trusteeship (`trusteeship_reversal_1948`)
All primary: Austin's statement (Avalon), FRUS notes (Lie, the Arab reaction), and Silver and Ben-Gurion (via JVL and the Israel MFA). I did not verify Truman's own reaction (his diary or memoir).

## 9. Plan Dalet (`plan_dalet_primary`)
The plan's text comes from Khalidi's 1988 JPS appendix. The operation list and objectives are Khalidi's translations; attribute them. Khalidi's own footnote corrects his "no village vacated" line. Morris's reading is in the simulation (pd_s01), not in this research.

## 10. Haifa (`cities_april_1948`)
**Disagree:** Khalidi (JPS 2008, reprinting 1959) argues expulsion and British collusion, and gives "some 50,000" expelled. A Jewish Chronicle opinion piece (2026) recounts Mayor Levy's plea and gives about 60,000 who left, but cites no archive. Khalidi says Morris changed position on evacuation orders.
**Not verified:** British police reports, and the Arab National Committee's own records.

## 11. Abdullah (`abdullah_1947_1948`)
**Agree:** The CIE and Shlaim's notes agree on the November 1947 understanding and the 10 May 1948 meeting. Jevon (Cambridge 2017, abstract) corroborates Britain's "green light".
**Disagree:** Collusion (Shlaim, Jevon) is set against a partial agreement that broke down (Shimoni's contemporary verdict; Abdullah's "one of five").
**Not verified:** The exact date of the February 1948 meeting, and Bevin's words at it.

## 12. The Declaration (`declaration_1948`)
The text comes from JVL, via the Israel MFA. The reproduced text carries both 14 May and 15 May (5 and 6 Iyar) in different places. The "Rock of Israel" phrase is not in the English text as fetched.

## 13. Invasion and war (`invasion_war_1948`)
**Agree:** S/745 is primary. The Office of the Historian is the official US summary.
**Disagree:** Force numbers: Shlaim gives 35,000 Israeli against 20,000–25,000 Arab troops on 15 May, while s10b has the July 1948 US figure of about 90,000. For Lydda and Ramla, Palquest gives about 70,000 expelled and more than 400 killed in Lydda, while Lockman gives a combined population of 50,000–70,000 and 250–400 civilians killed. The Office of the Historian's "February 1949" for all the armistices is too compressed (Lebanon 23 March, Syria 20 July, per simulation scenes).
**Not verified:** Security Council Resolutions 46 and 50 (texts), Arab dead, Operations Yoav, Hiram and Horev, and the date of the US embargo.

## 14. Refugees (`refugees_1948`)
**Agree:** The UNCCP technical estimate of about 711,000 (primary, 1949).
**Disagree:** PRRN gives the Commission's figure as 726,000. Other estimates run from 520,000 (Israeli) to 850,000 (Palestinian researchers), with 957,000 on UNRWA's 1950 rolls. On causes, Morris (via Lockman) finds no general expulsion order and Arab evacuation orders at no more than 5%, while Khalidi and Shlaim assign far more responsibility to Israel. Village counts depend on method: 369, 399, 418, 443, 472 and 531.
**Not verified:** The cabinet decision of 16 June 1948 as a primary record (Lockman gives only "June 1948").

## 15. Jews of Arab countries (`arab_jews_1947_1948`)
**Disagree:** Aden: 82 killed and 76 wounded (CIE), against 76 killed and 78 wounded (Anderson 1951). Cairo, 20 June 1948: 20 killed (Anderson) against 22 (Wikipedia, citing Beinin).
**Notes:** The Anderson study (1951) was a set of proposals submitted to the UN General Assembly. Treat it as a contemporary compilation from press reports, not an official UN finding. The JVL Aden page credits Wikipedia.

## 16. Jewish dissent (`jewish_dissent_1948`)
The Einstein–Arendt letter is primary (archive.org scan). Arendt's arguments are summarized from Robert Manne (2026) because the Commentary text was not reached; attribute them to Manne. The letter's Deir Yassin toll (240) is the 1948 figure; set it against current scholarship's figure of about 110 (ma_s01).
