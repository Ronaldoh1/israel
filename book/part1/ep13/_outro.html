The tools that actually moved the Epstein case were all at the edges of the system: a local police force, survivors' lawyers, a judge, a newspaper, a territory, inspectors general, a Congress forced to vote, a state that reopened its case and a journalist's lawsuit. The Dark Enlightenment's texts, quoted in their authors' words, propose a state run as a company by a sovereign answerable to its owners, with "no voice, free exit", no permanent civil service and no place for the press it calls the Cathedral. Run forward, the design removes nearly every one of those tools and leaves the sovereign's discretion, the tool that failed longest in this case. The authors' counter-argument, that an owner would be more accountable than a democracy, is stated in full; the present record, in which those checks are still working, is the strongest evidence that the worst case has not arrived.

Part Fourteen closes the book: the architecture, stated plainly, layer by layer, with what holds and what does not.
