Part Nine steps back from the chronology. It begins with the Roman families whose place in the story is most often misunderstood, the so-called black nobility, and sets what they documentably held, rank and the right to confer legitimacy, against the money that came from bankers. It then names the mechanism that repeats across eighteen centuries, with six documented cases and the points where it failed. It ends with a section, marked as a reading, that looks at the same architecture through the excluded books themselves.
