Sosúa: A Dictator's Offer, 1938–1944

At the Evian conference of 1938, called to find homes for the refugees of Nazi Germany, only one state offered to take Jews in large numbers.

At the Evian conference of July 1938 the Dominican Republic was the only state to offer to take a large number of refugees, 50,000 to 100,000 Jews. The reported motives of its dictator, Rafael Trujillo, were to repair his image after his army's massacre of about 25,000 Haitians in 1937, and to "whiten" the population. [cite:a_jvl_dr]

The Dominican Republic Settlement Association, backed by the Joint Distribution Committee, signed its contract on 30 January 1940; it bought about 26,000 acres at Sosúa, and each settler received 80 acres. The contract guaranteed "full freedom of religion" and protection from persecution. [cite:a_jvl_sosua]

About 50 refugees arrived in the first year and the community peaked at about 500 before the Nazis stopped Jewish emigration in October 1941. Most later left for the United States or Israel; about 25 families remain. [cite:a_jvl_sosua] [cite:a_jvl_dr]

The likely path. What happened: a refuge of about 500 people that saved lives and ended as a dairy town. A haven under a dictator, not a homeland.

The cost. No conflict over the land; the offer itself came from a regime that had just massacred its neighbours.
