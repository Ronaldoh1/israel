Sacred Number

Pythagoras, in the sixth century BCE, taught in a philosophical community that the soul is immortal and passes into new bodies, and some of the school's teaching was kept secret; a letter attributed to one of its members urged that the master's doctrines not be presented to the uninitiated. Late sources describe a division between those who followed oral rules for living and the mathematikoi, who studied mathematics. [cite:vt33_sep_pythagoreanism] For this school, number was not only a tool for counting but a way into the order of the world.

In Hebrew, letters also served as numbers. Gematria, in Britannica's definition, is "the substitution of numbers for letters of the Hebrew alphabet": the first ten letters count from 1 to 10, the next eight from 20 to 90 in tens, and the last four 100 to 400. Medieval Kabbalists made it "a favourite method of exegesis", a way of finding hidden meaning in sacred texts. [cite:fa_brit_gematria] Britannica gives an example. The ladder Jacob saw in his dream, sullam, has the same value as Sinai, 130, so interpreters read the Law given at Sinai as the ladder by which people reach heaven. [cite:fa_brit_gematria]

The practice drew criticism from inside the tradition that used it. Some critics, Britannica records, "condemned its use as mere toying with numbers"; others valued it as a way to resolve passages that were otherwise unclear. [cite:fa_brit_gematria] Gematria was never banned by an outside authority in the way astrology or geomancy were. Its limits were argued over by the scholars who practised it, which is a different kind of history from the others in this book.

The same warning applies today, when numerical readings of names and dates circulate widely. A sum of letters can join any two words that happen to share a total; the tradition's own critics knew that. What the Kabbalists claimed was not that numbers prove anything, but that a sacred text written with care might carry more than one layer of meaning. The Kabbalah book on this shelf follows that tradition further.
