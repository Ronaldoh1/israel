Rothschild money built early settlements, but the political movement that became the state was launched after a Rothschild refused to back it. From 1882, after the Russian pogroms, Baron Edmond de Rothschild financed roughly thirty Jewish agricultural colonies in Ottoman Palestine, including Rishon LeZion and Zikhron Ya'akov [cite:wiki_edmond_james_de_rothschild]. That is the kernel of truth: Ben-Gurion later called him the "builder of the Jewish Yishuv" [cite:betweenmyths_rothschild_israel]. But in July 1896 Theodor Herzl asked Edmond to back political Zionism and was turned down; Herzl's own diary records that "from Rothschild's negative attitude I must salvage everything" [cite:herzl_diaries_vol2_archive]. The First Zionist Congress met in Basel in 1897 without him.

Herzl's movement then built its own bank, the Jewish Colonial Trust of 1899, on £1 shares sold in installments so ordinary people could buy in; about 250,000 shares sold [cite:jct_wiki]. Later funding came from mass giving: the 1948 United Jewish Appeal raised a record $150 million from more than a million contributors [cite:jta_uja_1948].
