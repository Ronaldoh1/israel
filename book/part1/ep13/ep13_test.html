The Test

The question this part set was which of the tools that caught Epstein would survive the program as written. The answer, from the texts themselves, is that almost none would. What would remain is the discretion of the sovereign, which in this case was the tool that failed most often and longest.

That is a finding about a design, not a forecast. The Dark Enlightenment is a shelf of writing, and nothing in this book shows any official carrying it out. The library's volume on the movement found its influence on present policy to rest largely on resemblance, and that work is not repeated here. What this part adds is a reason the question matters for this case in particular. The Epstein record shows, in detail, how a well-connected network survives inside a system that still has a free press, independent judges, a Congress that can be forced to vote, and states that can act on their own. Each of those was slow and partial. Each was also, at some point, the only thing that worked. A program that removes them is not a neutral proposal about efficiency. For the survivors of this case, it is a proposal to remove the tools that eventually heard them.
