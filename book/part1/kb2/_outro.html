The Zohar presented itself as the teaching of a second-century sage; it appeared in Spain in the 1280s, and the wife and daughter of the man who circulated it said he wrote it himself. [cite:kb_brit_deleon] [cite:kb_wiki_isaacacre] Printed in Italy from 1558, it became Kabbalah's central book. [cite:kb_wiki_zohar] Part Three follows the school that built a new system on it, in a small town in Galilee.
