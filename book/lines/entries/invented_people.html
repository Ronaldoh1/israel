Palestinian national identity is documented decades before the 1960s; historians date its emergence to the late Ottoman and British Mandate periods, the same era in which Jordanian, Syrian and Israeli identities formed. On 15 January 1911, two Arab Christian cousins in Jaffa, Issa and Yousef El-Issa, founded a newspaper called Filastin, "Palestine." [cite:wiki_falastin] By 1914 it was openly opposing Zionist land purchases, and it published until 7 April 1948. Israel's own National Library holds 6,692 issues. [cite:nli_filastin_archive]

From 1919, Muslims and Christians organized together in seven Palestine Arab Congresses. Their central demand was a national government answerable to an assembly elected by the country's inhabitants. [cite:wiki_palestine_arab_congress] Britain's 1922 census counted 757,182 people in Palestine. [cite:jvl_census_1931] The PLO was founded in 1964, but the people it claimed to represent were already on the record.
