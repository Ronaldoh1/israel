The Library in the Caves

About two hundred of the manuscripts are copies of books of the Hebrew Bible. "Of the 24 books of the Hebrew Bible, all but Esther are represented." [cite:ds_vq_nature] [cite:ds_loc_library] A second group are religious books outside the later Jewish canon: Jubilees, 1 Enoch, the Genesis Apocryphon, the Aramaic Levi Document, the Temple Scroll, Ben Sira, Tobit and others. [cite:ds_vq_nature] The third group are the writings of the community itself: the Rule of the Community, the Damascus Document, the War Scroll, the Thanksgiving Hymns, a legal letter known as MMT, and commentaries on the prophets. [cite:ds_vq_nature] [cite:ds_brit_dss]

The community's own writings describe a group that had separated itself from the Jerusalem establishment. It called itself the "Sons of Light", against the "Sons of Darkness". [cite:ds_vq_nature] Its rule book describes a dualistic doctrine and strict regulations; its War Scroll describes the final defeat of the children of darkness by the children of light. [cite:ds_brit_dss] Its founder is a figure called the Teacher of Righteousness, to whom, the commentaries say, God revealed the hidden meaning of the prophets. [cite:ds_enc_dss] His chief opponent is the "Wicked Priest", a high-ranking priest, probably the Hasmonean ruler Jonathan, of whom the commentary on Habakkuk says that when he ruled over Israel "his heart became proud." [cite:ds_enc_dss] The commentary implies that the Wicked Priest kept a different calendar, so that he could travel and confront the community on what was, by their reckoning, the Day of Atonement. [cite:ds_enc_dss]

The community expected more than one messiah. Its rule tells members to keep its early precepts "until there shall come the Prophet and the Messiahs of Aaron and Israel": a priestly messiah and a royal one, keeping apart the offices of high priest and king that the ruling Hasmonean family had combined. [cite:ds_enc_dss] Here, as in so much on this shelf, a religious claim is also a claim about power. The community's objection to the rulers in Jerusalem was not only that they were wicked, but that one family held both the priesthood and the throne.

The library also changes what can be said about the canon. Britannica notes that the scrolls let scholars date a "stabilized Hebrew Bible" to no later than 70 after Christ. [cite:ds_brit_dss] Before that date, the caves show, the community copied and studied books that the later Jewish and Western Christian lists left out, side by side with those that went in. Jubilees, for example, survives at Qumran in fourteen or fifteen Hebrew copies, more than many books of the Bible. [cite:ds_vq_nature] The question The Canon asked of the Christian lists, who decided what counted as scripture, can be asked here too, and the caves show a library in which the line had not yet been drawn.
