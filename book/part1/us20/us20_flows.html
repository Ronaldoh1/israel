Money in Both Directions: Donations, Bonds, Investment and Arms

The government grant is one stream among several, and it is not the only one that crosses the Atlantic in Israel's favour, nor is every stream one-way. This section sets the private and commercial flows beside the public ones: tax-deductible gifts from Americans to Israeli organisations, including settlements; Israeli government bonds bought by American states and cities; investment and trade; and the weapons and defence work that move from Israel to the United States. Sums are converted to September 2026 dollars with the consumer price index as in the previous section; the conversions are this book's arithmetic. [cite:us20_bls_cpi_annual] [cite:us20_bls_cpi_sep26]

Tax-deductible giving: how the law routes it

American tax law lets a donor deduct a gift to a foreign charity only if it passes through an American organisation. Since 1938 such gifts have been deductible when they go to a not-for-profit incorporated in the United States with a mostly American board, when the foreign recipient is the equivalent of an American tax-exempt body, and when the American organisation oversees its foreign grants. The usual vehicle is an "American Friends" organisation, set up to raise money in the United States for one Israeli institution. [cite:us20_brandeis_philanthropy] The deduction is a public cost as well as a private gift, because the Treasury collects less tax on every dollar deducted; this book found no official estimate of the revenue forgone on gifts to Israeli causes.

The giving has a long series. A Brandeis University study by Eric Fleisch and Theodore Sasson, built from Internal Revenue Service filings, counted $283 million in 1975, $461 million in 1985 and $774 million in 1994, in the dollars of each year, rising to $2.06 billion raised in 2007, of which $1.65 billion was donated after fundraising costs and endowment activity. [cite:us20_brandeis_philanthropy] The number of American Friends organisations grew from 265 at the end of the 1980s to 436 in 2000 and 667 in 2010. [cite:us20_brandeis_philanthropy] A later study by researchers at New York University and Tel Aviv University found more than $2.65 billion given by American donors to Israel's non-profit sector in 2021, from about 1,200 American organisations, of which 161 gave more than 80 per cent; Friends organisations gave $824 million in 2015 and more than $1.2 billion in 2021. [cite:us20_ejp_giving2021] The two studies measure slightly different things, so the series is approximate. In September 2026 dollars, by this book's arithmetic, the giving was about $1.4 billion in 1985, $1.8 billion in 1994, $3.3 billion in 2007 (amount raised) and $3.3 billion in 2021. [cite:us20_bls_cpi_annual] During the war the Jewish Federations' Israel Emergency Campaign alone raised $683 million in 2024, according to federation figures. [cite:us20_bjf_iec2024] In 2021 private American giving, $2.65 billion, equalled about 70 per cent of that year's $3.8 billion military grant, by this book's arithmetic, though the two pay for entirely different things. [cite:us20_ejp_giving2021] [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025]

Giving to settlements

Most of that money goes to universities, hospitals, welfare and religious institutions inside Israel. A part goes to settlements in the occupied West Bank. Fleisch and Sasson counted about $40 million raised for settlements in 2007, against $167 million for religious education and $46 million for progressive causes. [cite:us20_brandeis_philanthropy] A Haaretz investigation by Uri Blau, published in December 2015, found that some 50 American organisations raised $281 million in tax-deductible donations in 2009–2013 and transferred $224 million of it to settlements through Israeli non-profits, about $320 million in today's dollars at 2013 prices. It named the Central Fund of Israel, the Hebron Fund and Honenu, which in 2013 gave a grant to the family of Ami Popper, convicted of murdering seven Palestinian labourers in 1990. A White House official told Haaretz that the administration "never defended or supported any activity associated with the settlements". [cite:us20_toi_settlements] The contrast is between two arms of the same government: in 1993–2005 Washington cut loan guarantees by the amount Israel spent on settlements, while the tax code continued to subsidise private gifts to them. [cite:us20_jvl_loans] [cite:us20_toi_settlements]

Israel's law treats foreign money by its source. A 2016 law requires non-governmental organisations funded mainly by foreign governments to say so publicly, a rule that falls mainly on human-rights and left-leaning groups, while private foreign donations, including the American gifts to settlement bodies, are not covered by it. [cite:us3_fh_il2025] A 2025 bill to tax foreign-government donations to non-profits at 80 per cent won government backing in February 2025. [cite:us3_toi_ngotax] Part Three sets out those laws. [scene:us3_speech|Part Three: speech and NGO laws]

Bonds, investment and trade

Israel Bonds are loans, not gifts: the Development Corporation for Israel, registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission, sells Israeli government debt in the United States and has raised $52 billion since 1951, repaid with interest. American states and municipalities bought more than $1.7 billion of it between October 2023 and July 2024, about $1.8 billion in today's dollars; Palm Beach County in Florida held about $700 million of a $4.67 billion portfolio. [cite:pm_icij_bonds] The Follow the Money volume examines the sales methods and the legal challenges. [scene:pm8_bonds|Israel Bonds]

Investment runs in both directions and is larger than the grant. The Commerce Department's SelectUSA office put the stock of American direct investment in Israel at $59.7 billion in 2025, with $16.6 billion flowing in that year, and Israeli direct investment in the United States at $22.2 billion, down from $24.5 billion in 2021. [cite:us20_selectusa_israel] Single companies account for much of the American side. Intel says it invested about $27 billion in Israel from 1974 to 2021, and Google completed its $32 billion purchase of the Israeli cloud-security firm Wiz on 11 March 2026. [cite:us12_intel_israel] [cite:ow4_globes_wiz] Goods trade, under the first American free trade agreement, in force since 1985, came to $34.4 billion in 2025: American exports of $13.7 billion and imports of $20.7 billion, a deficit for the United States of $7 billion. [cite:us11_ustr_israel] Part Twelve covers the industries behind those numbers. [scene:us12_tech|Part Twelve: technology]

Arms and defence work, Israel to the United States

The defence flow runs both ways too. Nearly all of the FMF grant is now spent with American contractors, which makes it at once a transfer to Israel's defence budget and a purchase from American industry. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] [cite:gi16_inss_aid] Part of that money returns to Israel through industrial cooperation: when Israel bought the F-35, paid for with aid, Lockheed Martin pledged about $4 billion in purchases from Israeli firms, and Israel Aerospace Industries makes F-35 wings. [cite:ow7_wiki_f35i] Israel also sells to the American government. The Stockholm International Peace Research Institute found that the United States took 7.8 per cent of Israel's arms exports in 2021–2025, while the United States supplied 68 per cent of Israel's imports. [cite:us12_sipri_at2025] Israel's Defence Ministry reported a record $19.2 billion in export deals in 2025, 13 per cent of them with North America, about $2.5 billion by this book's arithmetic, a figure that includes Canada. [cite:us12_mod_exports2025] Elbit Systems' American subsidiary, renamed Twenty-Six Defense in September 2026, employs about 3,300 people in the United States and holds Customs and Border Protection contracts worth more than $370 million. [cite:ow10_dn_26] The US Army's $373 million Iron Dome purchase of 2019 belongs in the same column. [cite:us20_toi_armyirondome]

What the two-way ledger shows

The common claim is that the money runs one way, from American taxpayers to Israel. The record supports a narrower statement. The government grant does run one way, and Israel does not raise that money itself; it is military money spent mainly in the United States, and it is now worth about a third, in real terms, of what American aid was worth in 1985. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] The larger flows are commercial and private, and several of them run toward the United States: Israel sells more goods to America than it buys, and Israeli firms invest and sell weapons there. [cite:us11_ustr_israel] [cite:us20_selectusa_israel] Each system has a gap the ledger exposes. In the United States a tax deduction subsidises private gifts to settlements that the government's own policy opposed, with no published estimate of its cost. [cite:us20_toi_settlements] In Israel the law scrutinises foreign-government money for advocacy groups while private foreign money for settlement bodies falls outside that rule. [cite:us3_fh_il2025]
