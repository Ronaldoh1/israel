Where It Began, and Who Vouched for Him

The earliest points the record holds come in a clear order. Epstein was hired to teach at the Dalton School in 1974 without a degree, and by Bear Stearns in 1976 after a Dalton parent's call; he left in 1981. In 1987 Steven Hoffenberg began paying him a consultancy, and a press release named him chairman of a company in Hoffenberg's failed bid for Pan Am. Wexner gave him power of attorney over his finances in 1991, the year from which the map measures the operation.

The earliest accounts of abuse come before that. In a lawsuit filed in December 2019, nine women made claims against his estate; one alleged abuse from about 1985, when she was 23, and another from about 1990, when she was 14. These are allegations in a lawsuit, not findings. [cite:ep16_irish_1985] The earliest sworn account of Epstein and Maxwell together approaching a minor is from the summer of 1994: the first witness at Maxwell's trial, "Jane", testified that the two approached her at the Interlochen arts camp in Michigan when she was 14. [cite:ep16_aj_jane] Interlochen says Epstein was a donor from 1990 to 2003 and that its records hold no complaint about him. [cite:ep16_yahoo_interlochen]

Was someone running it before him? Every claim of that kind the record holds was checked: that a British defence contractor, Douglas Leese, was a mentor, which rests on his son's account; that Epstein was introduced to Adnan Khashoggi and Robert Maxwell, which rests on Hoffenberg's claim; and the claims of intelligence ties, which are not established for any state. None documents an operation that existed before him and that he took over.

Wexner's money and Wexner's choice. Wexner's fortune was his own company's. He opened the first Limited store in Columbus in August 1963 with a $5,000 loan from his aunt and a matching bank loan, took the company public in 1969, and grew it by buying Mast Industries in 1978, Lane Bryant and Victoria's Secret in 1982, Lerner and Henri Bendel in 1985 and Abercrombie & Fitch in 1988; its sales passed $1 billion in 1984. [cite:ep16_wiki_wexner] [cite:ep16_enc_limited] In his written statement to the House Oversight Committee in February 2026, Wexner said Bob Meister, a former vice chairman of the insurer Aon, introduced them in the mid-to-late 1980s, and that he checked references with Ace Greenberg and Jimmy Cayne of Bear Stearns and with Élie de Rothschild, who he said "highly recommended" Epstein for work done for his family. [cite:ep16_wexner_statement] [cite:ep16_fox_wexner] Élie de Rothschild, a banker of the family's Paris branch who ran Château Lafite until 1974, died in 2007. [cite:ep16_rotharchive_elie] The recommendation rests on Wexner's account alone, and no record of the work itself has been published. He belonged to a different branch from the Edmond de Rothschild group, whose documented contract with Epstein dates from 2015.

Epstein signed the incorporation papers of the New Albany Company, Wexner's development company for the town where he lives, as its president. [cite:ep16_co_newalbany] The philanthropic Study Group that Wexner founded with Charles Bronfman in 1991 was checked for a tie to Epstein, and none is documented. [cite:ep16_wiki_studygroup]
