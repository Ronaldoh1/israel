Who Serves: Conscription, the Volunteer Force and the Veterans

Israel and the United States raise their armies by opposite methods. Israel conscripts most Jewish citizens at eighteen and calls them back as reservists for two decades; the United States has filled its ranks with volunteers since 1973. The method decides who carries the weight of a war, who is excused, and what the state owes afterwards. This section compares the two systems over time: the laws, the size of each force against its population, the exemptions, the burden on reservists, and the care each state gives to those wounded in its service. The money is counted in the next section. [scene:us19_spending|Defence spending and who pays]

Israel: a conscript army with a reserve behind it

The Knesset passed the Defense Service Law in 1949, giving the army authority to enlist citizens over eighteen. The law set service at 30 months for men and 18 for women; a temporary order in 1968 raised it to 36 and 24 months; men have served 32 months since 2015, and discharged soldiers remain liable for reserve duty to about age 40. Druze and Circassian men are conscripted, the Druze since 1956; other Arab citizens are not conscripted, though they may volunteer; religious Jewish women are exempt and many do civilian national service instead. In 2019, 69 per cent of Jewish men and 59 per cent of Jewish women were drafted; in 2013 the army exempted 26 per cent of potential conscripts, 13.5 per cent on religious grounds. [cite:us19_wiki_conscription] The International Institute for Strategic Studies counted 169,500 active troops and 465,000 reservists in 2025. [cite:hm_almon_idf] Against Israel's population of about 10.3 million, that standing force is about 1.6 per cent of residents, by this book's arithmetic. [cite:us1_jpost_ilpop]

The reserve is the army's mass. In the days after 7 October 2023 Israel mobilised about 360,000 reservists, some 300,000 of them in 48 hours, about 4 per cent of the population. [cite:hm_adn_360k] The Taub Center's July 2026 status report found that the average reservist had served 233 cumulative days since the war began, and that more than 100,000 fathers had served between 290 and 320 days. Its conservative estimate of their long-term wage loss was at least 4.7 per cent for the average reservist and 5.8 to 6.4 per cent for those fathers; on the average of the research literature the loss would be 19 to 21 per cent. Thirty per cent of reservists' salaried wives saw their wages cut and 3 per cent were dismissed, and about 70,000 students served in the 2024/25 academic year. The National Insurance Institute pays reservists' benefits; the report gives no total cost. [cite:us19_taub_miluim]

The Haredi exemption and the 2024 ruling

The largest exemption is the deferral for full-time yeshiva students, which Part Seven traces in full. [scene:us7_haredi|Part Seven: the Haredi exemption] About 400 students were exempted in 1949; by the end of June 2023 the state counted about 63,000. On 25 June 2024 a panel of nine High Court justices ruled unanimously that no law distinguished yeshiva students from anyone else liable to serve, that the government had "instructed the military authorities to apply illegitimate selective enforcement," and that state funding for yeshiva and kollel students without a lawful exemption or deferral must stop. [cite:us19_globes_ruling2024] The earlier statutory route, the Tal Law of 2002, had been struck down on 21 February 2012, and the 2014 enlistment amendments were struck down on 12 September 2017. [cite:us7_wiki_haredidraft]

Enforcement since the ruling has been slow. On 20 January 2026 an army officer told the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defence Committee that about 30,000 people were designated draft evaders, half of them Haredi, that about 80,000 Haredi men aged 18 to 24 were eligible but had not enlisted, and that the army urgently needed 12,000 more recruits. The chief of staff, Eyal Zamir, asked to restore men's service to 36 months. The government's bill, drafted by committee chairman Boaz Bismuth, would restore broad exemptions and half the lost yeshiva funding; critics including the army's leadership and the attorney general objected that it would not raise Haredi enlistment, by The Times of Israel's account. [cite:us19_toi_evaders2026] On 13 July 2026 the Knesset passed Basic Law: Torah Study, which declares Torah study a "foundational value" and which petitioners called "a draft exemption law through the back door." [cite:us2_toi_torahlaw] On 14 July it passed, by 58 votes to 54, a temporary amendment to the Security Service Law cancelling arrests of Haredi draft evaders; the next day Justice Ofer Grosskopf suspended it pending a hearing before an expanded panel. [cite:us19_globes_arrestlaw] A nine-justice panel kept the freeze after a hearing on 28 July that the government boycotted; the attorney general's representative argued that "a law that creates discrimination in criminal enforcement cannot stand, even if it is temporary." [cite:us19a_toi_hearing] In early August the public broadcaster KAN reported that Netanyahu had told officials he would not advance the law after the election, an account neither his office nor Likud confirmed. [cite:us19_jpost_netanyahu] On 3 September 2026 the court unanimously struck the law down for a defective legislative process, eight of the nine justices also finding it unconstitutional in substance; the ruling did not direct the army to make particular arrests. [cite:us19a_jpost_struck] As of October 2026 no replacement conscription law had been reported in the sources this book found, and Israel votes on 27 October 2026 with the draft one of the questions before voters. The coalition's stated position, written into the Basic Law, is that Torah study is a "foundational value"; on the record above, the arrangement it defends leaves the burden of service with those who are called up repeatedly. That is a description of how the law allocates duty, not of any community.

The United States: a volunteer force since 1973

The United States ended conscription in 1973 after the Gates Commission recommended an all-volunteer force. [cite:us19_cfr_avf] No draft has been activated since then. Men aged 18 to 25 must still register with the Selective Service System, and the 2026 National Defense Authorization Act made that registration automatic, from as early as December 2026. [cite:us19_kmph_sss]

The force has shrunk over time. Active-duty strength never fell below 2.0 million during the Cold War and peaked above 3.5 million during the Korean and Vietnam wars; it was 2.1 million in 1989 and fell to about 1.4 million by 1999. [cite:us19_crs_size2006] Congress authorised 1,284,500 for 2024 and 1,276,700 for 2025. [cite:us19_crs_endstrength] Against a population of 341.8 million in 2025, that is about 0.4 per cent of residents, by this book's arithmetic. [cite:us1_wa_uspop] Veterans made up about 18 per cent of American adults in 1980 and 6 per cent in 2022, by Census figures compiled by the Pew Research Center. [cite:us19_pew_veterans] A volunteer force must compete for recruits in the labour market: the Army missed its 2022 recruiting goal by 15,000. [cite:us19_cfr_avf] Military service in the United States is therefore concentrated in a small share of the population, chosen by enlistment rather than by law, and the burden of the 2026 war with Iran fell on that force: the Pentagon counted 21 service members killed in it by October 2026. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026]

What each state owes afterwards

The United States cares for its veterans through a cabinet department. Congress enacted $445.49 billion for Veterans Affairs for fiscal year 2026, of which $312.30 billion was mandatory spending, mostly benefits; the total was up 21 per cent from $367.71 billion in 2025. VA appropriations were $37.47 billion in 1995; in 2025 dollars they rose from $72.20 billion to $391.03 billion between 1995 and 2025. Of about 17.6 million veterans, about 9.2 million are enrolled in VA health care, and VA expected to pay disability compensation to about 6.46 million in 2026; recipients rose from 3.3 million in 2011 to 5.8 million in 2024. The 2022 PACT Act's fund for illnesses linked to toxic exposures had received $108.90 billion by 2026. [cite:us19_crs_va2026]

Israel's care for wounded soldiers sits in the Defence Ministry's Rehabilitation Division. By December 2025 it was treating more than 82,000 people, 22,000 of whom had joined since 7 October 2023. [cite:us19_jns_rehab] In July 2025 the ministry said more than 10,000 were in treatment for war-related mental trauma, 3,769 had been recognised with post-traumatic stress, and it expected about 100,000 wounded and disabled veterans by 2028. [cite:us19_ynet_rehab] No published total budget for the division was found for this book; that gap is stated rather than estimated. The two systems differ in scale and in form, a separate department beside a ministry division, and both are growing: the American disability rolls since 2011, the Israeli rehabilitation caseload since 2023.
