Jabotinsky and "Both Banks of the Jordan"

The first Zionist party to make a fixed territorial claim the centre of its programme was the Revisionist movement of Ze'ev (Vladimir) Jabotinsky. Its claim was not the biblical maximum and not the Paris line of 1919. It was the whole of the Mandate as originally conceived, on both sides of the Jordan river, including the territory Britain had separated in 1922 as the Emirate of Transjordan. The party, its youth movement, its song and the underground army that took its emblem carried that claim from 1925 to 1948, and its political heirs carried part of it into the State of Israel. [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] [cite:gi2_wiki_tjmemo]

The Iron Wall: method, not borders

Jabotinsky's best-known text is about how, not where. "The Iron Wall", dated 4 November 1923, argued that the Arab population would not consent to Zionist settlement and that "Zionist colonisation must either stop, or else proceed regardless of the native population." It could proceed only "behind an iron wall, which the native population cannot breach"; agreement would come later, and "the only way to reach an agreement in the future is to abandon all idea of seeking an agreement at present." [cite:gi2_jvl_ironwall] In the same essay Jabotinsky described his programme as one of equal rights, said he was ready to take an oath binding Jews and their descendants that "we shall never try to eject anyone", and drew no border. [cite:gi2_jvl_ironwall] A companion essay, "The Ethics of the Iron Wall", appeared in the Paris Russian-language weekly Rassviet on 11 November 1923; it names Transjordan only once, in passing, as one of the places where Zionism met opposition. [cite:gi2_jvl_ironwall] The Iron Wall is cited, rightly, as the root of a doctrine of force until acceptance. It is not a map.

The party and its programme

The founding conference of the Union of Zionists-Revisionists took place in Paris in 1925. Its programme called for a Jewish majority in Palestine "on both sides of the Jordan" and for the re-establishment of the Jewish Legion as part of the British garrison. [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] The movement "hotly opposed the separation of Transjordan," and its slogan was "a Jewish state on both sides of the Jordan." [cite:gi2_ej_partition] According to the historian Ya'akov Shavit, a resolution Jabotinsky moved in 1931 sought to define the whole Mandatory territory, Transjordan included, as the Jewish state to be achieved. [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] After a plebiscite in 1935 the Revisionists left the World Zionist Organization and formed the New Zionist Organization, which "opposed and combated the partition of Palestine as proposed in 1937 by the Palestine Royal Commission." [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists]

Jabotinsky's youth movement, Betar, had been founded in Riga in 1923. By 1934 it claimed 70,000 members, 40,000 of them in Poland. [cite:gi2_wiki_betar] In 1929, while in Paris, Jabotinsky wrote the poem known as "The East Bank of the Jordan" (in Hebrew Smol HaYarden, "the left bank of the Jordan"). A first version was given to a student union in Kaunas on 18 November 1929, and the version known today was printed in the Jerusalem daily Do'ar HaYom on 11 April 1930. Its refrain, Shtei gadot la-Yarden, says in substance that the Jordan has two banks and both are ours. It became one of Betar's best-known songs. [cite:gi1_wiki_eastbank] English renderings of the refrain differ slightly; the meaning does not.

The emblem

The Irgun Zvai Leumi, an underground force that broke from the Haganah in 1931, took its policy from Revisionist Zionism and drew most of its members from Betar and the Revisionist party; Jabotinsky was its supreme commander until his death in 1940, and Menachem Begin led it from 1943 to 1948. Its emblem showed a hand holding a rifle over a map of Mandatory Palestine and Transjordan, with the motto "only thus." [cite:gi2_wiki_irgun] Like the song, the emblem carried the territorial claim and the method together: the map was the two banks, and the rifle the means. The claim was not attached to any biblical border; it was the Mandate as first drawn.

A minority claim

The Revisionists were a minority within the Zionist movement on exactly this question, and their claim lost every formal test before 1948. The mainstream Zionist Organization had asked in 1919 only for a strip east of the Jordan up to the Hejaz Railway (see [scene:gi2_map1919|the 1919 map]), accepted the Mandate without Transjordan, and in 1937 authorised its executive to negotiate on partition, which the Revisionists fought (see [scene:gi2_peel|the next section]). [cite:gi2_ej_partition] [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] When the State of Israel held its first election, Irgun veterans led by Menachem Begin had founded a new party, Herut, in 1948 (in June by most accounts; the Encyclopaedia Judaica's article on the Revisionists gives October), while the rump Revisionist list won 0.7 percent of the vote. [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] [cite:gi3_jvl_herut] Herut was the predecessor of the Likud. [cite:gi2_wiki_irgun] What Herut and Likud later said about the land, including the 1977 Likud platform's claim to sovereignty "between the Sea and the Jordan", which dropped the east bank, is examined in [scene:gi5_likud|Part Five]. [cite:gi1_jvl_likud1977]

Two other records bear on the claim. The first is that the Hashemite rulers of Transjordan made their own "one country" argument, joining the West Bank to their kingdom in 1950; that history, and the later Israeli slogan "Jordan is Palestine", are told in [scene:s06a2|the separation of Transjordan] and [scene:gi8_jordan|Part Eight]. The second is that the claim to the east bank, which was the Revisionists' defining demand, was given up by their heirs: Israel and Jordan signed a peace treaty in 1994, and in March 2023 Israel's Foreign Ministry restated its commitment to it and its recognition of "the territorial integrity of the Hashemite Kingdom." [cite:gi8_ynet_mfajordan] Jordan's position is that, in King Abdullah II's words of January 2004, "Jordan will never be an alternative homeland to anybody." [cite:gi8_king_althomeland] Both are covered in [scene:gi8_jordan|Part Eight] and [scene:gi12_withdrawals|Part Twelve].

The finding for this section is precise. "Both banks of the Jordan" was the stated programme of a named movement, the Revisionist Zionists, from 1925, sung by its youth movement and drawn on its army's emblem. It was opposed within the Zionist movement, was never adopted by the Zionist Organization, and did not become the policy of the State of Israel. What did pass from the Revisionists into Israeli politics was a party lineage, through Herut to the Likud, and the habit of treating the land west of the Jordan as one undivided unit, which later chapters follow.
