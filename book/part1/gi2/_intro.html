Part Two goes back to the founders and asks what borders they actually claimed, in their own documents, between 1896 and 1937. It reads Theodor Herzl's diary, where an authentic but little-understood note of 1898 records a plan, never carried out, to ask the Ottoman sultan for land from the Egyptian frontier to the Euphrates. It sets out the boundary the Zionist Organization formally proposed to the Paris Peace Conference in February 1919, and the narrower lines the powers drew instead. It follows Ze'ev Jabotinsky's Revisionist movement, whose programme, song and underground emblem claimed both banks of the Jordan, and the mainstream that opposed it. And it closes with the Peel Commission's partition map of 1937, the Zionist Congress's answer, and David Ben-Gurion's letter to his son, with the disputed sentence in it given in both readings.
