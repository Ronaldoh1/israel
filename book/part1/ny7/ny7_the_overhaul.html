The Overhaul, 2023–2025

Israel has no written constitution. The Knesset elected in 1949 deadlocked over one and chose instead to pass Basic Laws chapter by chapter, and most of them can be amended by a simple majority of the 120 members. [cite:basiclaw_britannica] [cite:us2_wiki_basiclaws] With no second chamber, no federal structure and a government that always commands a Knesset majority, the Supreme Court has been the main check on executive power. That is what the overhaul set out to change.

In January 2023 Justice Minister Yariv Levin announced a programme to give the coalition control over the appointment of judges, to let a simple majority override the court's rulings and to abolish review of government decisions for "reasonableness". [cite:judicial_protests_wiki] The court had used that standard weeks earlier to void Netanyahu's appointment of a convicted former minister, Aryeh Deri, to the cabinet. [cite:timesofisrael_hcj_strikedown] Week after week, hundreds of thousands of Israelis protested; reserve pilots and soldiers said they would not serve a government that took the courts. [cite:judicial_protests_wiki]

On 25 March 2023 the defence minister, Yoav Gallant, publicly asked for a halt. Netanyahu fired him the next day; protests broke out across the country that night; on 27 March Netanyahu paused the legislation, and on 10 April he kept Gallant after all. [cite:ny_axios_gallant]

The law and the ruling

On 24 July 2023 the Knesset passed an amendment to Basic Law: The Judiciary barring all courts from reviewing the reasonableness of decisions by the government, the prime minister or ministers; the vote was 64 to 0, the opposition walking out. [cite:us2_loc_reasonableness] [cite:us2_toi_reasonableness] On 1 January 2024 the Supreme Court struck it down by 8 to 7, the first time it had annulled a Basic Law; 13 of the 15 justices held that it may review Basic Laws at all. [cite:us2_toi_reasonableness]

After the war began

The overhaul did not end with 7 October. On 27 March 2025, with the opposition boycotting, the Knesset passed a law changing the committee that chooses judges, giving the coalition and opposition each a veto over Supreme Court appointments, to take effect in the next Knesset. [cite:us2_toi_selection2025] In August 2025 the cabinet voted to dismiss the attorney general, Gali Baharav-Miara, who was overseeing the prosecution of the prime minister; on 14 December 2025 a seven-justice panel annulled the dismissal unanimously, and Levin called the ruling an "unlawful intervention". [cite:us2_toi_agfiring]

Supporters of the overhaul argued that an unelected court had taken powers no constitution gave it, and that a majority elected by the voters should govern. Opponents argued that in a system with no constitution and no second chamber, the court was the only brake on a government whose leader was on trial. Both arguments are documented; the vote counts, the ruling and the firing are not in dispute.
