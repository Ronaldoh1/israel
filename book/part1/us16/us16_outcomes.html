Outcomes Over Time, Medicines and Mental Health

Spending is an input. This section turns to what the two systems produce, measured over four decades, to the gaps each leaves among its own citizens, and to mental health care, where both systems record long waits or low treatment rates. Health outcomes depend on far more than health care, including income, diet, violence, road deaths and drug overdoses, so the figures below describe the record of each country, not the effect of its insurance law alone. Life expectancy, 1980–2024

In 1980 the two countries stood level. On the World Bank's series, which uses the United Nations' model estimates for both, life expectancy at birth was 73.6 years in the United States and 73.9 in Israel. By 2000 it was 76.6 and 79.0; by 2010, 78.5 and 81.6; and in 2019, before the pandemic, 78.8 and 82.8. [cite:us16_wb_le] A Taub Center study found the same starting point: in 1980 Israeli Jews, Americans and the average of other OECD countries all lived about 74 years, while Israeli Arabs lived more than two years less; over the following three decades life expectancy rose by more than seven years for both Israeli Jews and Israeli Arabs, against about four years in the United States. [cite:us16_taub_healthy]

The pandemic widened the gap. American life expectancy fell to 76.3 years in 2021, while Israel's dipped only to 82.5. [cite:us16_wb_le] The American figure has since recovered: the National Center for Health Statistics put it at 79.0 years in 2024, up from 78.4 in 2023, with 76.5 for men and 81.4 for women. Deaths from unintentional injuries, the category that includes drug overdoses, fell by 14.4 per cent in age-adjusted terms that year. [cite:us16_cdc_db548] Israel's figure on the same World Bank series was 83.2 in 2024, so the gap stood at about four years, against about a quarter of a year in 1980. [cite:us16_wb_le] A reported fall in Israeli life expectancy after 7 October 2023 could not be confirmed from an official series and is not used here.

Infants and mothers

Infant mortality shows the clearest reversal. In 1980 Israel's rate, 15.5 deaths per 1,000 live births, was higher than America's 12.6. By 2000 Israel's was lower, 5.6 against 7.1, and by 2024 it was half the American rate, 2.7 against 5.5. [cite:us16_wb_imr] The American rate barely moved after 2019; the National Center for Health Statistics recorded 5.53 per 1,000 in 2024 and 5.60 in 2023, a change it judged not statistically significant. [cite:us16_cdc_db548] Maternal deaths, in the World Health Organization's modelled estimates, rose in the United States from 12 per 100,000 live births in 2000 to 22 in 2020 before falling to 17 in 2023; Israel's fell from 9 to 2. [cite:us16_wb_mmr] These are model estimates built to compare countries; national counts use different definitions and should not be mixed with them.

Gaps inside each country

Both systems leave measurable gaps between groups of their own citizens, and both governments publish them. Israel's Health Ministry, in a report on inequality in the health system based on 2022 data, put national life expectancy at 82.7 years, the same as the World Bank figure for that year. The highest regional figure for Jewish and other non-Arab citizens was 84.3 years, in greater Tel Aviv; the highest for Arab citizens was 80.1, in the north. Infant mortality was 5.1 per 1,000 among Arab citizens and 2.1 among Jewish citizens. The ministry also reported fewer doctors and specialists in the periphery, more emergency-room use in poorer areas because community services are weaker, and hospital beds cut over the decade mainly in the large cities while the north and south did not gain ground. [cite:us16_toi_moh_gaps] [cite:us16_wb_le] Part Eight sets these figures beside the other measures of equality among Israel's citizens. [scene:us8_israel|Part Eight: equality in Israel]

In the United States the national infant mortality rate in 2023 was 5.61 per 1,000; it was 10.93 for babies born to Black non-Hispanic mothers, 9.20 for American Indian and Alaska Native mothers, 5.03 for Hispanic mothers, 4.48 for white non-Hispanic mothers and 3.44 for Asian mothers. [cite:us16_cdc_imr2023] The rate for Black infants was more than twice the white rate, and above Israel's rate for Arab infants. Part Eight takes up the American gaps by group. [scene:us8_us|Part Eight: equality in the United States] Neither set of figures describes the people in a group; each measures how a system's services, income and geography reach them.

Mental health

Israel brought mental health into its insurance law late. Until July 2015 the state itself was responsible; a reform that month moved the insurance responsibility to the four funds, with NIS 2 billion added to their budgets in 2015–2016. From 2016 to 2024 the only dedicated addition was a one-off NIS 100 million during COVID-19, and the State Comptroller found an average wait of about 150 days for psychotherapy after diagnosis, on 2018 data. [cite:us16_boi_mental] The war that began on 7 October 2023 raised demand sharply. In the Central Bureau of Statistics' social survey, the share of adults reporting depression rose from 23–25 per cent before the war to 34 per cent in 2024; the share in treatment rose from 14 per cent in January 2024 to 18 per cent in October. In March and April 2024 the average public wait was about six and a half months, and 42 per cent of those who sought treatment went to private practitioners against 38 per cent to the funds. [cite:us16_boi_mental] The government answered with a National Mental Health Program in January 2024, about NIS 1.4 billion a year, partly paid for by a 2025 rise in the health tax worth about NIS 1 billion a year. Israel had about 0.17 psychiatrists per 1,000 people in 2023 against an OECD average of about 0.21, and very few therapists in the periphery and in Arab towns. [cite:us16_boi_mental] [cite:us16_cwf_israel]

In the United States mental health care is covered, if at all, through the same patchwork of plans. In 2024, 52.1 per cent of adults with any mental illness received mental health treatment in the previous year, by the National Survey on Drug Use and Health; a change in the questionnaire means this cannot be compared with earlier years. [cite:us16_samhsa_tx] Suicide was the tenth leading cause of death in 2024, with 48,824 deaths, an age-adjusted rate of 13.7 per 100,000. [cite:us16_cdc_db548]

The record therefore cuts both ways. Israel spends less than a third as much per person, covers every resident by law and has pulled ahead on life expectancy and infant mortality since 1980, but its residents wait long for specialists and for mental health care, pay a larger share out of pocket, and buy private cover in large numbers; and its Arab citizens and its periphery have worse outcomes. The United States spends more than three times as much per person, covers about 92 per cent of its people, and records large gaps by group and drug prices far above those of its peers.
