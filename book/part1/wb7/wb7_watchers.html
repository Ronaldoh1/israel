The Watchers, and the Word Apartheid

Blue Wolf. In 2021 the Washington Post reported that the army had been building a database of the Palestinians of Hebron: soldiers photographed residents, children among them, with a phone app called Blue Wolf that matched faces against a large database, and former soldiers said units were rewarded for taking the most photographs. [cite:wb_toi_redwolf]

Red Wolf. In May 2023 Amnesty International reported that since 2022 the army had used a facial-recognition camera system called Red Wolf at checkpoints in Hebron, relying on databases made up exclusively of Palestinians' data. Asked about it, the army said it carries out "necessary security and intelligence operations" and that "naturally, we cannot refer to operational and intelligence capabilities." [cite:wb_toi_redwolf] For the Palestinians of central Hebron, the checkpoint between their home and the rest of their city is now also a camera that knows their face.

The apartheid findings. Taking the two legal systems, the movement restrictions and the land policies together, three human rights organizations reached a conclusion that Israel rejects. In January 2021 the Israeli organization B'Tselem concluded that the regime governing Israel and the occupied territories "has become an apartheid regime." In April 2021 Human Rights Watch found that the crimes against humanity of apartheid and persecution were being committed in the occupied territories. In February 2022 Amnesty International argued that apartheid had been practised against Palestinians across all territory under Israel's control. [cite:wb_oj_apartheid] Israel's foreign minister, Yair Lapid, called Amnesty's findings "divorced from reality," said that "Amnesty quotes lies spread by terrorist organisations," and the foreign ministry asked it to withdraw the report. [cite:wb_vibes_amnesty]

Whether the system is apartheid is a legal judgment, and the record holds the findings and the rejection side by side. What is not in dispute is the description underneath both: two populations in one territory, under two systems of law, with movement, building and surveillance applied to one of them. Part Ten returns to the International Court of Justice, which addressed the question in 2024.
