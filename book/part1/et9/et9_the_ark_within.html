The Ark Within

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

At the centre of Ethiopian Christianity is an object no one sees. The Ark at Aksum is kept behind a door that only its guardian may pass, and the tabot in every church is hidden from all but its priests. [cite:et_wiki_zion] [cite:et_wh_solomonic] The closing reading of Part Eight called it the hidden centre. The Bible itself, in a passage the Ethiopian church reads in its canon like every other, goes further.

The prophet Jeremiah foresaw a day when the Ark would not be needed at all: "in those days, declares the Lord, they shall no more say, 'The ark of the covenant of the Lord.'" The verse goes on to say that it will not come to mind, will not be remembered or missed, and will not be made again. [cite:et_esv_jer316] In its place, Jeremiah says, Jerusalem itself "shall be called the throne of the Lord". [cite:et_esv_jer316] And later in the same book God promises a new covenant: "For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the Lord", in which the law is placed within the people and written on their hearts. [cite:et_esv_jer3133] Paul's letter to Corinth draws the conclusion that the inner traditions draw: "ye are the temple of God". [cite:cn_bh_1cor316]

The inner reading puts these passages together. The Ark held the tablets of the covenant. The prophet says that one day no one will need to see it, because the covenant will be written within. The hidden tabot in every Ethiopian church then becomes a teaching that does not need its object to be shown. What is holiest is present, guarded and unseen, because it is not finally in the chapel at all. It is in the person who stands outside the door.

Read this way, the history this book has told takes on a second meaning. Every outside power that came to Ethiopia, from the Roman emperor to the Derg, sought the institution and its objects: its bishops, its primacy, its tabots, its land. [cite:vt27_wiki_frumentius] [cite:vt27_wiki_mendes] [cite:et_bm_maqdala] [cite:et_cs_land] Eleven tabots taken from Maqdala are held in the British Museum, kept from public view out of respect for their sacred nature. [cite:et_bm_maqdala] None of those powers could take the covenant that Jeremiah said would be written on the heart, because it was never in their reach. That is the reading. The record says only who came, what they took and what the keepers kept.

The record also limits it. The Ethiopian church does not teach that the Ark is a symbol; it teaches that the Ark is at Aksum, and the reading here is not its teaching. [cite:et_wiki_zion] Jeremiah was speaking of a restored Jerusalem, not of an inner state, and Paul was writing to a church, not to each reader alone. The inner reading sets another meaning beside theirs, drawn from their own words.
