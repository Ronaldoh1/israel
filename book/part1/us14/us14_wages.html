Wages Against Prices, 1980–2026

The plainest test of a cost of living is whether pay keeps up with prices. Both countries publish the series needed to answer it: Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) has measured consumer prices monthly since 1951 and average wages per employee post since the 1960s, and America's Bureau of Labor Statistics and Census Bureau have measured prices since 1913 and household income since 1967. This section reads each country's wages against its own prices first, in its own currency, and only then compares the two, using one source for both: the OECD's average annual wage in dollars of equal purchasing power. The two countries' figures are not converted at market exchange rates, which move with capital flows and say little about what a wage buys at home.

Israel: from hyperinflation to fifteen flat years

Any Israeli series that starts in 1980 runs through the worst inflation in the country's history. Prices rose 445 per cent in 1984, by the Bank of Israel's count, so that the average price level multiplied about five and a half times in a single year. The 1985 Economic Stabilization Plan froze the exchange rate, prices and wages for an extended period, drew on an American grant to cover part of the state's deficit, and was followed by a new shekel worth 1,000 old ones. Inflation then ran at about 20 per cent a year until the mid-1990s, when the central bank began setting annual targets; a long-term target of 1–3 per cent was fixed in the early 2000s. [cite:us14_boi_hyper] Nominal comparisons across 1980–1986 therefore mean little; the turn itself is set beside America's in Part Eleven. [scene:us11_turn|The 1985 plan and the American turn]

Once prices settled, the record shows pay barely moving. In a 2014 review, the Bank of Israel found that the gross real wage per employee post grew 0.8 per cent a year on average from 1996, fell 0.2 per cent a year from 2001 and grew 0.7 per cent a year from 2003, while output per employee grew about 1 per cent a year from 1990. The bank found no long-term gap between wages and productivity; the problem it identified was that productivity itself grew slowly, and in 2012 Israel's hourly wage was about 75 per cent of the OECD average in purchasing-power terms. [cite:us14_boi_wages2014] The Taub Center put it more bluntly in 2016: Israel's consumer prices rose 32 per cent from 2000 to 2014 and nominal wages rose by about as much, so there had been no rise in real wages for at least fifteen years. [cite:us14_globes_taubwages]

Pay has risen since, though unevenly. On the OECD's measure, in 2024 dollars of equal purchasing power, the Israeli average wage fell from $49,340 in 2000 to $46,050 in 2010, then rose to $51,659 in 2020 and $54,736 in 2024: a gain of about 11 per cent over the whole period, all of it after 2010. [cite:us14_oecd_avwage] In shekels, the CBS put the average monthly wage per employee post at NIS 14,672 in March 2025 and NIS 13,907 in April; April's pay was 3.6 per cent higher than a year earlier, the same as inflation, so the real gain was nil. In the technology sector, about a tenth of employee posts, the average was NIS 36,731. [cite:us14_globes_wage2025] An average is pulled up by high earners; the CBS does not publish a monthly median wage in the same release, which is one reason Part Thirteen measures low pay through the minimum wage. [scene:us13_wages|Part Thirteen: wages]

The United States: record household income, uneven gains

America's longest consistent income series is the Census Bureau's median household income, adjusted for inflation, from 1967. In 2025 it reached $87,460, up 2.6 per cent from $85,210 in 2024 and the highest on record; after taxes, the median was $76,060. Over the whole series the gains were not shared evenly: from 1967 to 2025, pre-tax household income rose about 56 per cent at the tenth and fiftieth percentiles and about 121 per cent at the ninetieth, and the ratio of the ninetieth to the tenth widened from 9.23 to 13.06. [cite:us14_census_story2025] [cite:us14_census_p60] A household measure counts every earner in the household; it is not the same as a wage.

On the OECD's wage measure, the American average rose from $64,928 in 2000 to $71,439 in 2010, $82,783 in 2020 and $82,933 in 2024, all in 2024 purchasing-power dollars: about 28 per cent over the period. [cite:us14_oecd_avwage] The Israeli average was 76 per cent of the American in 2000 and 66 per cent in 2024.

Prices now: two inflations in 2026

In 2026 the two countries' prices moved in different directions. Israel's annual inflation fell to 1.6 per cent in June, a five-year low and inside the Bank of Israel's 1–3 per cent target, and stood at 1.6 per cent in September, with the housing component at 3.8 per cent. [cite:us14_globes_cpi_june26] [cite:us14_te_cpi_sep26] American consumer prices rose 3.7 per cent in the year to September 2026. Most of the rise was energy: energy prices were up 20.4 per cent and petrol 34.2 per cent, in the year of the war with Iran, while food rose 2.5 per cent, shelter 3.2 per cent and prices excluding food and energy 2.6 per cent. [cite:us14_bls_cpi_sep26] What that war cost each treasury is set out elsewhere in the library. [scene:ir14_bill|The American bill, 1979–2026] [scene:gi16_costs|What the wars cost]

What the series show, and what they do not

Measured this way, the American average wage both started higher and grew faster after 2000, and American median household income set a record in 2025, while Israeli pay was flat in real terms for much of the 2000s and recovered only after 2010. [cite:us14_oecd_avwage] [cite:us14_globes_taubwages] Two cautions apply. First, these are averages and medians of pay; they say nothing yet about what that pay buys, because a purchasing-power wage already adjusts for each country's general price level but not for the particular things families must buy, such as housing, which the next section measures separately. Second, the American gains were concentrated at the top of the distribution, and the Israeli average is lifted by a technology sector paying more than twice the national figure. [cite:us14_census_story2025] [cite:us14_globes_wage2025] The Israeli series from 1980 to 1996 exists, but runs through hyperinflation and wage indexation; this book did not rebuild it year by year, and no figure here compares Israeli real wages before 1996.
