Seeing Stones: Story, Scripture and the Fused View

This section is interpretation throughout. It sets four images, from a novel, a letter of Paul, a Gnostic text and a writer on out-of-body experience, beside facts earlier parts documented. Each reading belongs to the Sophia platform unless named as Peter Thiel's own, and each is followed by the strongest counter-reading. The readings add no fact and move no grade. One discipline governs them: the images are pointed at offices, instruments and mechanisms, never at a person as a villain, and never at a people or a faith.

Interpretation, not finding: the names

The starting point is documented. At least five entities linked to Thiel carry names from J.R.R. Tolkien's fiction: Palantir Technologies, after the seeing stones; Valar Ventures, registered in New Zealand in 2009, after the powers that shaped Middle-earth; Mithril Capital, founded in 2012, after the rare metal, which a partner said "protects" and is "transformative"; and Rivendell One LLC and Lembas LLC, vehicles named in a 2016 filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission and used to sell Facebook shares. [cite:de39_qz_names] The reporting does not say who chose each name. Anduril, the defence company, is often added to the list, but it is Palmer Luckey's company and Luckey named it after the sword; Founders Fund was an investor. [cite:de39_cm_rings] [cite:de39_qz_names] Thiel has been reported as reading and rereading the books in his teens, and in 2023 told The Atlantic that he had read the series at least a dozen times. [cite:de39_cm_rings] [cite:de39_lithub_fan]

The Sophia platform reads the naming as a vocabulary chosen by a reader who knew the story well, so that the story's warnings travel with the names whether or not anyone intended them. The strongest counter-reading is that names from a beloved book are common in technology, that a name is a gesture of taste and not a programme, and that nothing shows the founders meant the darker parts of the story to apply. The platform accepts this as the plain reading; what follows reads the story, not anyone's mind.

Interpretation, not finding: who looks, and who is seen

In Tolkien's fiction the palantíri are seeing stones made by the Elves and used by Men. They show distant things to whoever holds them. Their danger lies in what happens when a stronger mind is on the other end: the stone in Orthanc beguiled Saruman, and Sauron, holding another stone, used it to deceive his enemies and to drive Denethor, the steward of Gondor, to despair by showing him true things chosen to make defeat seem certain. Denethor burned himself holding his stone. The same stone of Orthanc was later used by Gandalf and Aragorn against Sauron. [cite:de39_arda_stones]

The Sophia platform reads the stone as an image of a fused data view, a single screen on which records from many sources appear joined around one person. Earlier parts documented such views being built: Palantir's case-management system for ICE "structures and links data collected from these vendors and other commercial entities", and ImmigrationOS gathers the agency's data streams into a central hub. [cite:de28_brennan_consol] In the platform's reading the stone has two sides that never meet. The one who looks chooses what to search; the one who is seen does not know a search has happened. And the user can be corrupted by the stone without any villain behind it, as Saruman and Denethor were: a view that shows true fragments, selected and joined, can produce false certainty. The record holds an example of the mechanism, not of any person's corruption: when a federal citizenship-check system was run against 18 million Texas voter records, 2,724 people were flagged, and about a fifth of those were later found to be citizens. [cite:de19_npr_save] The record was true in each fragment and wrong in its conclusion.

The strongest counter-reading starts with the story itself. In Tolkien the stones are not evil; they were made for communication between allied kingdoms, and the rightful heir used one to good effect. The corruption came from a hostile mind on the other end, not from the stone. Palantir says it does not collect, store or sell personal data, that it acts as a processor for customers who decide what can be done with their data, and that its software records what each user does by default. [cite:de27_pal_notdata] [cite:de27_pal_pcl] Thiel's own stated purpose, in 2016, was the reverse of the platform's image: helping a government swamped with data to "make sense" of it so that it could "end indiscriminate surveillance". [cite:de10_fortune_palantir] [scene:de27_capability_not_intent|Part Twenty-Nine] sets out both cases at length.

Interpretation, not finding: the restrainer and the stone

This reading begins with Thiel's own. The katechon, "the one who withholds" in Paul's second letter to the Thessalonians, is the force that holds back the Antichrist; tradition has identified it with the Roman Empire, the Church and other powers, and Paul never explains it. [cite:de10_wiki_katechon] [cite:de10_rns_christensen] Thiel has said that the slogan of the Antichrist is "peace and safety" and that the danger is a "one world totalitarian state". [cite:de10_douthat_tx] In his 2025 lectures, as reported from leaked audio, he called the American empire "the natural candidate for Katechon" and also "ground zero of the one-world state", and named financial surveillance and the sanctions architecture among the tools of such a state. [cite:de10_reason] [scene:de10_girard_and_the_katechon|Part Ten] covers the argument in full.

The Sophia platform reads the two images together. On Thiel's own terms the restrainer and the danger can live in the same empire. The platform's reading is that they can also live in the same instrument: a system that sees enough to restrain catastrophe also sees enough to rule, and the stone that watches for the Antichrist watches everyone. The strongest counter-reading is Thiel's: his named fear is a global authority, not a national government doing its lawful work; he has argued against concentration at the world level, not for it at home; and no document shows him directing how any customer uses Palantir's software. [cite:de10_douthat_tx] [cite:de10_reason] The platform's reading turns his own warning back on his tools; it does not show that he made that turn.

Interpretation, not finding: the archons and the copy

The Apocryphon of John survives in three copies among the Nag Hammadi codices, found in Egypt in 1945 and probably made in the fourth century, and in a fourth copy in Berlin. In it the visible world is made by Ialdabaoth, "a lesser being, a ruler (archon)", who is "unaware of any power above him" and rules the cosmos "with subordinate powers who are his own imperfect offspring". The material world is "a copy of the perfect spiritual realm", and birth into it brings "a forgetting" of the divine origin. [cite:de39_brit_apjohn]

The Sophia platform reads the archons as offices, not persons: administrators of an order made of copies. A modern administrative state decides on the record of a person, not the person, and the record is a copy. The platform's reading is that the danger the text names is a ruler who does not know there is anything above him, and in institutional terms that is a process that acts on the copy and no longer checks it against the person or against the law above it. The Texas flags read above are the copy mistaken for the person. [cite:de19_npr_save] The platform adds a warning the lens requires. Images of hidden rulers have a long history of being turned against a people or a faith; this reading names no group and refuses that use entirely.

The strongest counter-reading is that the Gnostic texts concern the cosmos and salvation, not government, and that reading them as an account of records offices is anachronism. Records also protect rights: a court docket, a birth certificate and an audit log are copies too, and they let a citizen prove a mistake and hold an office to account. In the Texas case, it was a check of the copy against the person that found the error. [cite:de19_npr_save]

Interpretation, not finding: the second extraction

Robert Monroe (1915–1995), a radio executive who wrote three books on out-of-body experience, coined the word "loosh" in Far Journeys (1985) for a kind of energy produced by beings in intense fear, grief or despair, which in his account non-human beings harvest. His claims are not scientifically established. [cite:de39_wiki_monroe]

The Sophia platform uses the word only as a lens and only on measured outcomes. Its reading is of a double extraction: a surveillance system takes data from the people it watches, and the fear it creates does further work of its own, changing behaviour without any officer acting. The measured outcomes are these. In the KFF and New York Times survey of 1,805 immigrants, taken between 28 August and 20 October 2025, 41 per cent feared that they or a family member could be detained or deported, up from 26 per cent in 2023, with the rise largest among lawfully present immigrants and naturalised citizens; 30 per cent said they avoided travel, work, public places or medical care; and 40 per cent reported effects on their health such as stress, anxiety or sadness. [cite:de39_kff_2025]

The strongest counter-reading is plain. Monroe's harvesting beings have no evidence behind them, and an effect is not a design. Deterrence has been an openly stated aim of border enforcement since at least the Border Patrol's strategic plan of 1994, adopted under President Clinton and used since; an aim stated in public is not a hidden harvest. [cite:de39_wiki_ptd] The survey measures what people report, not what caused it. And no document shows anyone gaining from fear as such. The platform keeps the word for what it is, a writer's image applied to a measured cost.

Each reading is offered because it makes a documented structure easier to see, not because it shows anything the record does not. [scene:de39_the_runtime|The next section] turns from story to code.
