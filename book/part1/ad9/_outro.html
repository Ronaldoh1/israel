Part Nine established that American households owed $18.771 trillion in June 2026, $1.263 trillion of it on credit cards, and that required debt payments took 11.1 per cent of after-tax income, well below the 2007 peak, while card balances moved into serious delinquency at about 7 per cent a year. [cite:us14_nyfed_q2_2026] [cite:ad9_fred_tdsp] Card rates have averaged about 21 per cent since 2023 under a framework the Supreme Court built in 1978 and 1996, which lets issuers export their home state's rates and fees; at those rates a $5,000 balance paid at the minimum takes more than nineteen years to clear. [cite:ad9_fred_ccall] [cite:ad9_marquette] [cite:ad9_smiley] Credit scoring made lending faster and more consistent, but one consumer in twenty had a report error likely to raise their costs and about 7 million adults had no credit file in 2020. [cite:ad9_fed_scoring2007] [cite:ad9_ftc_2013] [cite:ad9_cfpb_invis2025] Overdraft revenue at large banks halved between 2019 and 2023 through the banks' own changes, but the federal overdraft and late-fee limits written in 2024 were repealed by Congress and vacated in court in 2025, so fee levels are again set by each bank; 4.2 per cent of households remained unbanked. [cite:us22_cfpb_od23] [cite:ad9_plaw_119_10] [cite:ad9_hk_late] [cite:ad9_fdic2023] Payday loans cost about 391 per cent a year and are mostly renewed, wage-advance apps work out at similar rates on California's data, buy-now-pay-later loans go mostly to subprime borrowers, and medical cards charged patients about $1 billion in deferred interest in three years; as of October 2026 most federal limits proposed for these products have been withdrawn, while twenty-one states and the District of Columbia cap payday rates.
