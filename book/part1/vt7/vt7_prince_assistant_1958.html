The Prince Assistant and the Fall of 1958

The one formal office that tied the Orsini to the person of the pope in modern times was that of Prince Assistant to the Papal Throne (Principe Assistente al Soglio Pontificio). Its history is the clearest test of how much power the old Roman families held, because it has a beginning, a documented holder, a documented removal and a documented end. Popular accounts of it give incompatible dates and lengths of tenure. This section rebuilds the timeline from the sources.

What the office was

The office is believed to have been instituted by Julius II in 1511, the year of his bull ending the Orsini–Colonna feud, and it was the highest honour a layman could hold at the papal court. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt7_wiki_colonna] It could be granted to an individual, but it belonged by hereditary right to the heads of the Colonna and the Orsini. The rule that the two families would share it in alternation was set by Benedict XIII, himself an Orsini pope (1724–1730); the sources give the Orsini tenure as running "from 1735 until 1958". [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt7_wiki_pa] The Catholic Encyclopedia, written in the early twentieth century, says simply that the head of the Orsini "always enjoys the dignity of assistant at the papal throne". [cite:vt7_ce_orsini]

The duties were ceremonial. A Prince Assistant served under the Prefect of the Apostolic Palace, did the honours of the house at solemn civil ceremonies such as the reception of sovereigns, heads of state and ambassadors presenting their credentials, stood beside the papal throne at great liturgies, and took precedence immediately after the College of Cardinals. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt7_wiki_pa] Nothing in the description of the office gave its holder a vote, a signature or a budget. Its value was proximity and rank. The office dates from 1511, and the Orsini shared it with the Colonna rather than holding it alone.

January 1958: a pope removes a prince

Filippo Orsini (1920–1984), head of the Gravina line, held the title from 1950. [cite:vt7_wiki_orsini] In 1957, while the British actress Belinda Lee was filming in Italy, the two began a much-reported affair; both were married to other people, and Lee left her husband in September 1957. [cite:vt7_wiki_blee] In late January 1958 Italian newspapers reported that Lee had taken an overdose of sleeping pills; three days later Orsini was reported in hospital with cuts to his wrists. Lee said the overdose was an accident, and Orsini would not tell the police why he had hurt himself. [cite:vt7_wiki_blee] The Vatican let it be known that he would lose his title if it were proven that he had attempted suicide. [cite:vt7_wiki_blee]

In January 1958 Pius XII removed Filippo Orsini from the office of Prince Assistant; TIME reported the case under the headline "The Papal Prince" on 10 February 1958. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] On 19 February 1958 the London Daily Mail reported that the pope had condemned suicide in a speech that was widely read as a reference to the scandal, although neither Orsini nor Lee was named. [cite:vt7_wiki_blee] Lee left Rome in February 1958; by December the couple were reported to be living together in Paris; she announced the end of the relationship in December 1959, and she died in a car crash in California on 12 March 1961. [cite:vt7_wiki_blee]

The episode matters for this volume for one reason: it shows the direction of power. A pope removed the head of the oldest family of the black nobility from its last court office because of his private conduct, and the family had no recourse. The removal was a disciplinary act within a court the pope controlled. It is the opposite of what the "Orsini control the papacy" claim predicts.

After 1958: Torlonia, then the end of inheritance

The office did not pass to another Orsini. The sources disagree on when the second chair was filled: the Liturgical Arts Journal says John XXIII appointed Prince Alessandro Torlonia in 1962, to restore the complement of two for the Second Vatican Council, while Wikipedia's article on the Torlonia gives 1958. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt7_wiki_torlonia] This volume leaves the date open. Either way, the new holder came from the banking family that the papacy had ennobled at the turn of the nineteenth century, not from the old baronial line. [cite:vt7_wiki_torlonia]

Ten years later Paul VI ended the hereditary basis of every court office. His motu proprio Pontificalis Domus, dated 28 March 1968, reorganized the Papal Court as the Papal Household, divided into a Papal Chapel and a Papal Family, and declared that in the Papal Household "no office whatever is to be regarded as hereditary" (nullum prorsus munus est veluti hereditarium existimandum). [cite:vt7_pd_latin] The same document suppressed a long list of court posts, among them the Palatine Cardinals, the Master of the Horse, the General Superintendent of the Posts and the hereditary Quartermaster General, and kept the Prince Assistants in the Papal Family. [cite:vt7_wiki_pd] The two living holders, Aspreno Colonna and Alessandro Torlonia, were allowed to keep the title for life; Colonna died in 1987, and Torlonia, president of the family's Banca del Fucino, died at the end of December 2017. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] [cite:vt7_br_torlonia] The title has not been conferred since and has not been formally suppressed. [cite:vt7_wiki_pa]

The office was not abolished by the Lateran Treaty of 1929: it was held and filled after 1929, its Orsini holder was removed in 1958, and its hereditary basis was ended in 1968. Nor does any document found make the 1958 scandal the catalyst for the reform of 1968: Pontificalis Domus reformed the whole court in the period after the Second Vatican Council, presents itself as a reorganization to stress the pope's "essentially spiritual" mission alongside his civic and international role, and does not refer to the Orsini case. [cite:vt7_wiki_pd] The two events are ten years apart and the second applies to every court family, not to one.

What the families hold now

As of October 2026, the record shows no member of the Orsini, Colonna, Torlonia, Massimo or any other black-nobility family holding a hereditary or ex officio post at the Holy See. [cite:vt7_wiki_pa] [cite:vt7_pd_latin] What these families keep is what old Roman wealth keeps anywhere: names, palaces, art collections, marriages and in some cases banks, which are private property and private influence, not offices of the Holy See.
