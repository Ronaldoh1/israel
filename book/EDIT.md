# Editorial pass: make it read like a great historian wrote it

These book sections are accurate and sourced. Your job is to make them read like the work of a renowned historian who writes for the general public: clear, vivid, authoritative, easy to follow for a reader who knows nothing about the subject. Think of the best popular historians: concrete scenes, plain words, sentences that carry the reader forward.

## What to improve
- **Clarity first.** Break long sentences. Prefer the active voice and plain verbs. Cut throat-clearing ("It is worth noting that", "In this context"). One idea per sentence where possible.
- **Define on first use.** The first time a term, organization or acronym appears in a section (Mandate, Yishuv, Haganah, JNF, UNSCOP, dispensationalism…), give a short plain gloss in the same sentence, drawn only from what the text or its context already says.
- **Lead with the human or the concrete.** Where a paragraph opens abstractly, open instead with the person, the date, the document or the place already in the text.
- **Flow.** Make each section open with a sentence that orients the reader (when, where, who) and end with a sentence that shows why it mattered or leads on. Smooth transitions between paragraphs.
- **Myth vs. record.** Each section will be shown with a boxed "What you were told / What the record shows" callout above it, so the reader already sees the contrast. In the prose, don't restate the box; instead make the turn explicit where the record departs from the familiar account ("The familiar version stops there. The documents do not."), then give the context.
- **Hedges stay, but lighter.** Keep every qualification about disputed or uncertain claims, but say it once, cleanly ("Historians disagree about…", "According to…"), not repeatedly.
- **Keep the length roughly the same** (within ±15%). Don't pad.

## Hard rules (a script will check them)
- **Do not add any fact.** No new names, dates, numbers, places, quotations or events. Don't change any number, date or name. Keep every quotation exactly.
- **Keep every `[cite:key]` marker**, attached to the sentence carrying the claim it supports. If you split a sentence, the marker goes with the clause holding that claim. Don't drop or invent markers.
- **Keep every `[scene:ID|label]` cross-reference** (you may reword the label).
- Allowed tags only: `<h2>` (first line, the heading, which you may improve), `<p>`, `<em>`, `<strong>`, `<blockquote>`.
- No internal vocabulary: tier, CONFIRMED, entity, scene, node, simulation, module.
- Never generalize about a people or religious group; keep claims tied to named institutions, movements, states and people, as the text does.
