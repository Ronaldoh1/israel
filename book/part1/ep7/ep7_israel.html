Israel in the Record

No question in this case draws more heat than whether Epstein worked for Israel. It deserves the same treatment as every other question in the book: list what the documents show, list what is claimed, and keep the two apart. It also needs the rule set out in the introduction, because this is where the slide from a named official to a state, and from a state to a people, happens most often. The book follows the documents about named officials and dated acts of a government, and goes no further.

What is documented

Four things are on the record. The first is Ehud Barak's long relationship with Epstein after the conviction, including the business they did together: brokering surveillance systems to the government of Côte d'Ivoire, and Epstein's money placed through Barak's partnership in Carbyne. [cite:dropsite_epstein_cote_divoire] [cite:aljazeera_epstein_ivory_coast_2026] Part Five sets that record out in full. The second is that in 2016 and 2017, when Barak stayed in an apartment at 301 East 66th Street linked to Epstein, security staff from Israel's Permanent Mission to the United Nations installed and maintained alarms and sensors there. [cite:ep7_aj_apt66_2026] That is the protective detail a state provides for a former prime minister. It is a documented act of the Israeli government, and it shows the state protecting Barak; it does not show the state employing Epstein. The third is that Robert Maxwell, Ghislaine Maxwell's father, was buried in Jerusalem in 1991 with the honours of a state funeral, a fact Part One sets out with what it does and does not show. [cite:jta_maxwell_funeral_1991] The fourth is not about Epstein at all: documents leaked by Edward Snowden showed that the US National Security Agency had agreed to share raw signals intelligence with Israel's signals unit, including material that could contain Americans' communications, with Israel undertaking not to target Americans deliberately, in an agreement that was not legally binding. [cite:ep7_irishtimes_nsa2013] That agreement is real and important in its own right. Nothing in the released record connects it to Epstein.

What is claimed

The claims rest on a small number of sources. One is a 2020 FBI report of what a single confidential informant said, released in January 2026, in which the source was "convinced that Epstein was a co-opted Mossad agent" and claimed he had been trained under Barak. [cite:ep5_mee_chs2026] [cite:ep7_toi_mossad2026] It is one person's account, recorded by agents; nothing in the release shows it was checked or corroborated, and Part Five shows how the same report places influence in a religious movement, a step this book refuses. Another is the account of Ari Ben-Menashe, a former Israeli military-intelligence officer who has claimed that Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell worked with Israeli intelligence through her father; it is a single witness whose credibility has long been disputed. [cite:wiki_ari_ben_menashe] A third is Steven Hoffenberg's claim, from prison, that Epstein was introduced to Robert Maxwell in the 1980s; the reporter who recorded it wrote that she could not prove it. [cite:ep7_rs_ward2021]

What the files themselves say

The released files cut in both directions, and both deserve to be quoted. In 2017 Barak asked Epstein whether an acquaintance had "got to the Mossad guys through you?". In 2018 Epstein wrote to Barak, "You should make clear that I don't work for Mossad," followed by a smiley face. Elsewhere he wrote, "I do not like Israel. AT ALL." A recording of more than three hours of conversation between the two men does not mention the Mossad. Israel appears in the files fewer times than Russia, China, Canada or France, and most mentions of any intelligence agency are news articles Epstein was sent. [cite:ep7_toi_mossad2026] The Israeli prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, Barak's long-time rival, has argued that Barak's ties prove Epstein did not work for Israel. [cite:ep5_toi_netanyahu_barak] A joke in an email is not a denial, and a rival's argument is not evidence; but neither is an informant's conviction.

The honest finding is narrower than either side of the argument wants. A former Israeli prime minister had a close, documented business relationship with Epstein, and the Israeli state protected that former prime minister while he used an Epstein-linked apartment. Whether any Israeli agency used Epstein is not shown by anything released. It is not ruled out either; the withheld files have not been seen.
