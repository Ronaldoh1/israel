Part Four follows the money. It sets out the five court decisions, from Buckley v. Valeo in 1976 to McCutcheon v. FEC in 2014, that made independent political spending unlimited while leaving gifts to candidates capped, and traces how a dollar now reaches an election ad. It then measures who spent in 2024 and in the 2026 cycle so far, putting the party leaders' super PACs, the presidential super PACs, billionaires on both sides, the crypto industry, AIPAC and organised labour on the same footing, and asks what the research shows that money buys, in elections and in policy, and where the heaviest spenders lost.
