The Armed Movements

"Iran was always a theocracy"

Before 1979 Iran was a constitutional monarchy with an elected parliament. In 1951 the parliament made Mohammad Mosaddegh prime minister and voted to nationalise the British-controlled Anglo-Iranian Oil Company; in August 1953 an operation run by the CIA and Britain's MI6 removed him and restored the Shah's power, a role the CIA acknowledged in documents released in 2013. [cite:nsarchive_cia_confirms_role] [cite:npr_declassified_1953_coup] Israel, five years old, had no documented part in the coup. It did benefit: Mosaddegh's government had closed Iran's consulate in Jerusalem in 1951, while after his fall Israeli and Iranian trade grew, Israel's intelligence service helped train the Shah's secret police, SAVAK, from 1957, and Israel bought much of its oil from Iran. [cite:iranica_israel_iran] [cite:globalsecurity_savak_cia_training] [cite:mag972_israel_shah] The theocracy came in 1979, twenty-six years after the elected government was removed. Verdict: false.

"Israel's army exists because of Hamas"

The Israel Defense Forces were established on 26 May 1948. [cite:wiki_altalena_affair] Hamas was founded in December 1987, twenty years into the occupation of Gaza, from the Islamist association Mujama al-Islamiya, which Israeli authorities registered in 1979. Israeli officials later called that a mistake: Avner Cohen, who handled religious affairs in Gaza, said Hamas was, "to my great regret, Israel's creation". [cite:npr_hamashistory] [cite:portside_higgins_hamas] [cite:toi_hamasblogs] Others answer that Hamas grew from the Muslim Brotherhood, founded in Egypt in 1928, and that the "Israel created Hamas" line overstates a registration decision. [cite:israel_institute_hamas] In scale, Israel reports about 170,000 active personnel and 465,000 reservists; ACLED estimated Hamas at 9,000 to 12,000 fighters in late 2024. [cite:katv_military_numbers_2024] Verdict: false on the order; disputed on how much Israel's tolerance helped Hamas grow.

"Hezbollah just hates Israel"

Hezbollah emerged after Israel's invasion of Lebanon in June 1982, built by Lebanese Shia clerics on the model of Iran's revolution with the help of about 1,500 Revolutionary Guard instructors, and announced itself in 1985; its first stated aim was ending the occupation of the south, which lasted until 2000. [cite:hezbollah_founding] [cite:wiki_hezbollah_mirror] In 2006 the former prime minister Ehud Barak said that when Israel entered Lebanon "there was no Hezbollah" and that Israel's presence created it. An Israeli intelligence research centre argues that Iran's export of its revolution was the cause and the occupation only a catalyst. [cite:terrorism_info_hezbollah_birth] The record supports both halves: the occupation was the trigger and first target, Iran was the engine. Verdict: false as stated.

The pattern behind every armed movement

Set the founding dates side by side and one pattern appears. The Arab revolt of 1936 followed the sharpest rise in Jewish immigration under the Mandate; the Irgun and Lehi formed in response to Arab attacks and British limits on immigration; Fatah and the PLO formed among the refugees of 1948; Hamas formed twenty years into the occupation; Hezbollah formed after the invasion of Lebanon. [cite:npr_hamashistory] [cite:hezbollah_founding] Each group had other ingredients too, ideology, sponsors and rivalries. But each founding date sits just downstream of an event the group was reacting to, and the pattern holds for Jewish, Palestinian and Lebanese movements alike. Explaining when a group arose does not excuse its attacks on civilians.
