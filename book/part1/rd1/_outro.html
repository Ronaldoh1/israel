By the end of this stage, the movement launched at Basel had become a working structure. The choice of territory had been settled: the Uganda offer was rejected in 1905, and Herzl had already explained in 1896 why Palestine carried a weight Argentina could not. In Jaffa, the Palestine Office, opened in 1908, coordinated land purchase and settlement with the Jewish National Fund, while the Palestine Land Development Company had handled roughly 50,000 dunams by the outbreak of war. Named agents closed the deals.

Beyond Palestine, two separate bases of support had taken shape in the United States. A Christian one, rooted in the Scofield Reference Bible of 1909, would grow into a large Christian Zionist constituency. A Jewish one, built through Hadassah, Louis Brandeis and the Zionist Organization of America, had more than 147,000 members by 1918 and a direct line to President Wilson. During the war, NILI's intelligence and the Jewish battalions in British uniform gave the movement standing in London as the cabinet weighed the Balfour Declaration.

Opposition was also in place. From 1911, Filastin was reporting Zionist land purchases to every village in the Jaffa district. And one popular claim, that the Federal Reserve's founding was tied to Zionist finance, finds no support in the record.
