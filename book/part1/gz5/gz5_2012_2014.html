2012, and the War of 2014

Pillar of Defense, November 2012. On 14 November 2012 an Israeli airstrike killed Ahmed Jabari, the head of Hamas's military wing in Gaza, and began an eight-day operation. Israel cited more than a hundred rockets fired in twenty-four hours and attacks on its soldiers near the fence, and said its aim was "to halt rocket attacks against civilian targets originating from the Gaza Strip"; Palestinian armed groups cited the blockade of Gaza and the occupation of the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. The UN High Commissioner for Human Rights counted 174 Palestinians killed, 107 of them civilians; B'Tselem counted 167, of whom 62 had taken part in hostilities. Six Israelis were killed, four civilians and two soldiers. More than 1,456 rockets were fired into Israel, and Israel's new Iron Dome system intercepted about 421. [cite:gz_wiki_2012]

The Egyptian-brokered ceasefire of 21 November required Israel to stop "all hostilities in the Gaza Strip land, sea and air including incursions and targeting of individuals", required the Palestinian factions to stop all attacks including rockets, and provided for the opening of the crossings to be taken up within 24 hours. The fishing limit was raised to six nautical miles; in March 2013 it was cut back to three. [cite:gz_wiki_2012]

The road to 2014. On 12 June 2014 three Israeli teenagers, Naftali Fraenkel, Gilad Shaer and Eyal Yifrah, were abducted in the West Bank; they were found dead on 30 June. Israel blamed Hamas, though, the record notes, "no evidence of Hamas involvement was offered by Israeli authorities at the time"; the Hamas leader Khaled Meshaal later acknowledged that Hamas members were responsible. Israel's search operation in the West Bank killed about eleven Palestinians and arrested between 350 and 600, including nearly all of Hamas's West Bank leadership. After the teenagers' burial, a Palestinian teenager, Mohammed Abu Khdeir, was murdered in revenge. Rocket fire from Gaza resumed and Israel struck targets in Gaza between 4 and 6 July; on 7 July, after an explosion in a Khan Yunis tunnel killed seven Hamas militants (an Israeli strike, by Hamas's account; its own munitions, by the Israeli army's), Hamas claimed the rocket fire. [cite:gz_wiki_2014]

The war. Israel began its air campaign on 8 July with the stated aim of stopping the rocket fire, and sent in ground forces from 17 July to 5 August with the stated aim of destroying the tunnels dug from Gaza toward Israel. An open-ended ceasefire took effect on 26 August, ending what the UN inquiry called "the 51 day operation". [cite:gz_wiki_2014] [cite:gz_un_coi2015]

The dead. The UN's humanitarian office counted 2,220 Palestinians killed, 1,492 of them civilians; the UN Human Rights Council counted 2,251, with 1,462 civilians. Israel's Foreign Ministry counted 2,125 dead and classed about 36 percent as civilians. Sixty-seven Israeli soldiers were killed, and six civilians in Israel, one of them a Thai worker. More than 7,000 homes were razed and about 89,000 damaged, and at the height of the war the UN estimated that up to 520,000 people, about thirty percent of the population, might have been displaced. [cite:gz_wiki_2014]

The inquiry. A UN commission of inquiry chaired by the American judge Mary McGowan Davis reported in June 2015 that it had found "substantial information pointing to the possible commission of war crimes by both Israel and Palestinian armed groups." On Israel's side it recorded more than 6,000 airstrikes and about 50,000 tank and artillery shells, and 142 families who lost three or more members in attacks on homes, 742 deaths in all; it asked whether Israel's unchanged targeting practice reflected a policy "at least tacitly approved at the highest level of government." On the Palestinian side it recorded 4,881 rockets and 1,753 mortars, fired indiscriminately in a way that "appeared to have the intention of spreading terror among civilians", and the tunnels that traumatised Israeli communities near the fence. Israel did not respond to the commission's requests for access. [cite:gz_un_coi2015] A week before it reported, Israel published its own account of the war, which said its army had done "the utmost to avoid civilian casualties" and blamed Hamas for firing from residential areas; Netanyahu said that "whoever wants a baseless, automatic accusation against Israel can waste their time reading the UN report." [cite:gz_euronews_2015]
