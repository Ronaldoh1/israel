Six Days, 1967

In May 1967 Egypt moved troops into Sinai, had the UN peacekeeping force withdrawn and closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping, which Israel had long said it would treat as an act of war. On the morning of 5 June Israel struck first: its air force destroyed most of Egypt's air force on the ground within hours. In six days Israel took Sinai and Gaza from Egypt, the West Bank and East Jerusalem from Jordan, and the Golan Heights from Syria. [cite:wiki_six_day_war]

Two accounts of the war's origin remain in dispute. The account long taught in Israel and America holds that Israel faced annihilation and struck in self-defence. Israeli historians working in the state's archives, among them Tom Segev, document that Israeli military planning for such a war predated the crisis, and that some Israeli leaders judged Egypt was not about to attack. [cite:segev_1967] [cite:wiki_six_day_war] Both accounts accept the closure of the straits and the Egyptian build-up; they differ on whether Israel's survival was at stake.

Resolution 242

On 22 November 1967 the Security Council adopted Resolution 242, calling for "withdrawal of Israeli armed forces from territories occupied in the recent conflict" and for the right of every state in the area to live in peace within secure and recognised boundaries. [cite:unsc_242] The English text omits "the" before "territories"; the French text includes it. Israel and the United States have read the omission as allowing less than full withdrawal; Arab states and most of the world read the resolution as requiring withdrawal from all the territories. [cite:wiki_unsc_res242] [cite:wiki_constructive_ambiguity] The formula of land for peace became the basis of every negotiation that followed. Only the Sinai has been returned.

Israel annexed East Jerusalem soon after the war and, by law, the Golan Heights in 1981; the United States recognised the Golan annexation in 2019. [cite:wiki_golan_heights_law] [cite:wiki_us_recognition_golan] The West Bank and Gaza were placed under military rule. [cite:wiki_israeli_occupation_wb]
