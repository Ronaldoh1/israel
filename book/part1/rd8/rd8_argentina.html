Argentina: Land Without Sovereignty, 1891–1930

The first serious alternative to Palestine was not a Zionist plan at all. It was a philanthropist's answer to the persecution of Jews in the Russian Empire, and for a time it moved more people onto more land than anything in Palestine.

In Der Judenstaat (14 Feb 1896) Herzl named two possible lands, Argentina and Palestine. Both, he wrote, would fail by slow "infiltration": immigration was pointless without a sovereign right to keep it going. [cite:a_wp_judenstaat]

Baron Maurice de Hirsch founded the Jewish Colonization Association in 1891 to move Jews out of the Russian Empire and settle them as farmers in Argentina; he spent nearly his whole fortune on it. At his death in 1896 there were 6,757 colonists. [cite:a_wp_hirsch_arg]

By 1923 the Association had settled 30,725 people (5,360 families) on 590,000 hectares, more than three times all the land Jews held in Palestine on the eve of statehood (about 173,000 hectares, of which the JNF held 936,000 dunams). [cite:a_jta_ica24] [cite:a_wp_jnf]

The colonies peaked at over 20,000 colonists in 1930. Progress was "hampered by insufficient land for extensive cultivation and the unfavorable location of many of the colonies"; by 1966 only 8,000 Jews remained in them. [cite:a_jvl_ica]

Argentina was an independent republic that welcomed European immigrants as citizens. No Argentine government offered self-rule to the colonies, and none was sought. [cite:a_wp_judenstaat] [cite:a_wp_hirsch_arg]

January 1919, the Tragic Week in Buenos Aires: during the suppression of a general strike the Argentine Patriotic League singled out the Jewish neighborhood of Once, in what has been called the first pogrom in the Americas. [cite:a_jc_tragic]

The likely path. A large community of citizens, not a state. That is what happened: farm colonies of up to 20,000 people, most of whose children moved to the cities, inside a Jewish community that became one of the largest in the Americas.

The cost. No war over sovereignty, because sovereignty never changed hands. There was violence against Jews all the same (the 1919 Tragic Week).
