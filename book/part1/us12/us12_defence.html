Defence Industry and Arms Exports

Both countries are among the world's largest arms exporters, and their defence industries are joined by money, co-production and ownership. The United States is the largest exporter by a wide margin; Israel, with about a thirtieth of America's population, ranks seventh, and its Defence Ministry says it relies on exports to help pay for its own forces. [cite:us11_wb_pop] [cite:us12_sipri_at2025] This section measures each industry's exports over time, using two kinds of figure that must not be confused: the value of deals signed or authorised in a year, which each government announces, and the volume of major weapons actually delivered, which the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI) estimates on a common scale. It then follows the ties between the two industries.

Israel: five years of records

Israel's Defence Ministry, through its export agency SIBAT, announces the value of export deals signed each year. It reported $12.5 billion in 2022 and a record $13.1 billion in 2023. [cite:us12_na_exports2023] The figure for 2024 was $14.8 billion, up 11.7 per cent, more than half of it to Europe and almost half of it air defence. [cite:hm_globes_exports] On 2 June 2026 the ministry announced $19.2 billion for 2025, up nearly 30 per cent and the fifth record in a row; it said exports had more than doubled in five years and quadrupled in a decade. Government-to-government deals made up about $10 billion. [cite:us12_mod_exports2025] The war did not reduce sales. Air-defence systems were 36 per cent of deals in 2023 and about 48 per cent in 2024, when a single sale of Arrow 3 to Germany of about $3.5 billion lifted Europe's share to 54 per cent; in 2025 air defence fell to 29 per cent, surveillance and optronics rose to 22 per cent, and states of the Abraham Accords took 15 per cent. [cite:us12_globes_exports2025]

The ministry's own statements tie exports to the defence budget. Its director-general, Amir Baram, said that "our force buildup budget relies heavily on these partnerships". [cite:us12_mod_exports2025] The head of SIBAT, Yair Kulas, observed that Israel was no longer the only country selling arms with a "combat-proven" label, since Ukraine now markets battlefield-tested products. [cite:us12_globes_exports2025] The phrase names a selling point that rests on use in war, and the ministry and its critics read it differently; this book records it as the industry's own description.

The United States: the largest seller

American arms sales are counted through two channels. Foreign Military Sales (FMS) are government-to-government sales managed by the Pentagon; Direct Commercial Sales (DCS) are licensed sales by companies. The State Department reported FMS of $51.9 billion in fiscal year 2022 and $80.9 billion in 2023, and DCS of $153.6 billion and $157.5 billion. [cite:us12_usni_fy2023] Its figures for fiscal 2025, as reported, were $331.18 billion in all, $104.4 billion of it FMS and $226.8 billion DCS, against $318.7 billion in fiscal 2024. [cite:us12_da_fy2025] These are authorisations and notified sales, many of which are delivered over years; they are not comparable with Israel's signed-deal figures, and both are far larger than what is shipped in any one year.

SIPRI's delivery-based measure gives the common scale. In 2021–25 the United States supplied 42 per cent of the world's major arms exports, up from 36 per cent in 2016–20, and its exports rose 27 per cent between the two periods; its largest customers were Saudi Arabia (12 per cent), Ukraine (9.4 per cent) and Japan (8.9 per cent). Israel was the seventh-largest exporter, with 4.4 per cent of the world total, up from 3.1 per cent, after its exports grew 56 per cent; India took 29 per cent of Israeli exports, Germany 21 per cent and the United States 7.8 per cent. [cite:us12_sipri_at2025] For 2019–23 SIPRI had ranked Israel ninth, with 2.4 per cent, and India's share of its exports at 37 per cent. [cite:pm_globes_exports] The five-year periods overlap, so they show a direction rather than separate points.

How the two industries are joined

The ties run in both directions. Israel is the fourteenth-largest arms importer, and 68 per cent of its major imports in 2021–25 came from the United States. [cite:us12_sipri_at2025] Much of that is paid for with American aid: the Foreign Military Financing grant of $3.3 billion a year must be spent on American equipment, and the share Israel may spend at home falls to zero in fiscal year 2028. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] The aid is therefore, in large part, a payment to American manufacturers for weapons Israel uses; its scale and what it frees up are measured in the volume's first part and in The Bill and America's Role. [scene:us1_aid|What the aid is] [scene:gi16_aid|The aid]

Missile defence is co-produced. From 2016 American production funding for David's Sling, built by Israel's Rafael with Raytheon, and Arrow 3, built by Israel Aerospace Industries with Boeing, was split half and half with the American partners. [cite:ow8_dn_coprod2016] On 9 October 2026 the Raytheon–Rafael joint venture delivered the first Tamir interceptors, the missile used by Iron Dome, built at Camden, Arkansas, its first plant outside Israel. [cite:ow8_rtx_camden] Israel's F-35s carry Israeli electronic-warfare equipment by Elbit and Israeli software. [cite:ow7_wiki_f35i] The One-Way Mirror follows this pipeline. [scene:ow8_coproduction|Co-production]

Israeli firms also sell inside the United States. Elbit's American subsidiary won a $145 million border-tower contract from Customs and Border Protection in 2014. [cite:ow10_globes_ift] Renamed Twenty-Six Defense on 8 September 2026, it is based in Fort Worth, employs about 3,300 people and held border contracts worth more than $370 million running to May 2029. [cite:ow10_dn_26] Israel's government says it has banned defence exports to Myanmar since 2017. [cite:ow10_aj_cognyte] Both governments license what their companies export, and in both the decision rests with the executive; how each answered challenges to sales during the Gaza war is set out in the volume's part on the courts and other states. [scene:gi17_courts|The courts]

Who these industries serve can be stated from the record. In Israel defence exports help pay for the army itself, as the ministry says, and the industry's growth since 2021 has come with the wars. In the United States the industry sells to many governments, Saudi Arabia above all, and Israel is one customer among many, though one whose purchases are financed by American grants. The costs of the wars these weapons are used in, for both treasuries, are set out in later parts. [scene:us19_spending|Defence spending] [scene:us20_flows|The money flow]
