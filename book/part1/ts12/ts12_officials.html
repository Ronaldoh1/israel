The Officials and Their Stated Reasons

Between 2017 and 2026 a number of senior American office-holders spoke about Israel in religious terms. This section takes them one at a time and records what each said, where and when, in his own words. It does not infer a private motive from a public remark, and it does not treat a statement of faith as proof that the faith set a policy. Where an official gave a secular reason beside a religious one, both are given. Where earlier material made a claim about an official that could not be found in a named source, the claim is listed at the end and not used.

Mike Pence, Vice President, 2017–2021

Pence put the case most directly in religious language. At the Christians United for Israel summit on 17 July 2017 he described "the hand of heaven" beneath Israel's history and promised the embassy move: "It is not a question of 'if.' It is only 'when.'" [cite:ts12_ncr_pence2017] He addressed the summit again in 2019, alongside Prime Minister Netanyahu. [cite:gi11_wiki_cufi] Earlier material calls Pence and Pompeo "documented, self-identified Christian Zionists"; no source was found in which either used that label of himself, and it is not used here. What is documented is Pence's own account of faith as a ground of his support.

Mike Pompeo, Secretary of State, 2018–2021

In an interview with the Christian Broadcasting Network published on 21 March 2019, during a Middle East trip, Pompeo was asked whether Trump might have been raised "for such a time as this," like Queen Esther in the Bible, to help save the Jewish people from "the Iranian menace." He answered: "As a Christian, I certainly believe that's possible," and added, "I am confident that the Lord is at work here." [cite:ts12_huff_pompeo] [cite:ts12_jn_pompeo] The Book of Esther is set in Persia, which made the comparison pointed. Eight months later, announcing the settlements decision, Pompeo gave no religious reason at all: his statement rested on Reagan's view, on the failure of the illegality finding to advance peace and on the role of Israeli courts. [cite:ts12_toi_pompeo2019] The two statements show the same official speaking in two registers, one for a Christian audience and one for the formal record.

David Friedman, Ambassador to Israel, 2017–2021

Friedman's best-known act was archaeological and political at once. On 30 June 2019, with the envoy Jason Greenblatt and in the presence of Sara Netanyahu, he swung a sledgehammer through a specially built wall to open the "Pilgrimage Road," an excavated ancient stairway at the City of David site in Silwan, East Jerusalem, described as a route by which Jews went up to the Temple Mount. He called it "a once-in-a-century discovery," said "No political message was intended," and said his view of Jerusalem "does not prejudge the outcome of future negotiations." The Palestinian Authority, several Israeli NGOs and Jordan, which warned of "Israeli efforts to Judaize the Holy City," condemned it. [cite:ts12_toi_friedman] After leaving office Friedman backed Cruz's reading of Genesis in June 2025: "This is the same nation of Israel referred to in the Bible." [cite:ts1_cp_cruz]

Mike Huckabee, Ambassador to Israel, 2025–

Huckabee, a former governor of Arkansas whom AP described as a well-known evangelical, was confirmed on 9 April 2025 by 53 votes to 46. He had long used "Judea and Samaria" for the West Bank, opposed a two-state solution and supported Israel's right to annex the territory, but said carrying it out would not be his "prerogative"; in 2024 he said he would not call Arab descendants of the Mandate's population "Palestinians." At his hearing he said he would "carry out the president's priorities, not mine." [cite:ts12_ap_huckabee] In June 2025 he told Bloomberg he did not think an independent Palestinian state remained a goal of US policy and told the BBC that "Muslim countries have 644 times the amount of land" Israel controls; the State Department said he "speaks for himself" and that the President sets policy. [cite:ts12_newser_huckabee]

The fullest meeting of theology and office came in February 2026. Asked by Tucker Carlson in an interview aired on 20 February whether Israel had a right to the land promised in Genesis from the Nile to the Euphrates, Huckabee said: "It would be fine if they took it all," then, "Not sure we'd go that far." Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Jordan, the Arab League and the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation condemned the remarks. [cite:gi11_abc_huckabee] Huckabee then called his answer "somewhat of a hyperbolic statement," said Carlson's version "edited out my full response," and said "Area C is Israel"; the State Department did not publicly address it. [cite:gi11_aje_huckabee] [scene:gi11_christian|The Huckabee interview in the Greater Israel volume] In mid-2026 he was a public voice of the talks to replace military aid with a new memorandum, writing that it "ends aid & will be based on trade." [cite:gi16_wiki_nextmou] His February statement did not become US policy.

Mike Johnson, Speaker of the House, 2023–

Johnson has used the Genesis blessing in public. In July 2025 he said: "I'll bless the nation that blesses Israel and curse the nation that curses Israel." [cite:ts10_ww_kaylor] On 4 August 2025 he made what the Times of Israel called a landmark visit to the settlement of Ariel; according to the municipality's statement, he said that "Scripture teaches us that the mountains of Judea and Samaria were promised to the Jewish people," that they "belong to them by right," and that the land is "an integral part of our faith." The trip was organised by the US Israel Education Association and designated private. [cite:ts12_toi_johnson] Johnson has also received $1,134,227 in direct career contributions from pro-Israel donors and committees, fifth among House Republicans, according to Track AIPAC, an advocacy compiler, as of July 2026. [cite:trackaipac_congress] Donations are a disclosure record, not evidence that they caused his positions; his stated reason is scriptural.

Pete Hegseth, Secretary of Defence, 2025–

Before he took office, Hegseth spoke at a conference in Jerusalem in 2018. He said there was "no reason why the miracle of re-establishing the temple on the Temple Mount isn't possible," and, "I don't know how it would happen, you don't know how it would happen, but I know that it could happen." He told the audience that Israel should act while there were "true believers" in Washington. [cite:ts12_mee_hegseth] [cite:ts12_cp_hegseth] No response from Hegseth to the 2024 reports of the speech was found. He still led the Pentagon in October 2026. [cite:gi15a_ibt_minab] The remark is a private citizen's hope voiced before an Israeli audience; no Defence Department policy on the Temple Mount has followed from it, and US policy as stated in 2017 supports the status quo at the holy sites. [cite:ts12_toi_trump2017] [scene:gi11_temple|The Temple Mount]

Ted Cruz, Senator from Texas

Cruz gave the clearest account of a religious motive and its limit. In an interview with Carlson released on 18 June 2025 he said he had come to Congress intending to be "the leading defender of Israel in the United States Senate," and that "as a Christian, growing up in Sunday school, I was taught from the Bible" that those who bless Israel will be blessed; pressed on whether that meant the government of Israel, he said the verse "doesn't say the government of" but "the nation of Israel." [cite:ts10_cp_cruz] [cite:ts10_cl_cruz] He also said, "Not everyone I represent is a Christian, so I don't use my faith as the reason we should support Israel," and his main argument in the interview was security: Israel was "doing a massive favor to America" against Iran's nuclear programme. [cite:ts10_cp_cruz] [cite:ts10_ji_cruz] Part Ten analyses the exchange as the mechanism at work and the point at which it was challenged. [scene:ts10_mechanism|Part Ten: how the shield works]

What the record shows, and what was dropped

The record shows that, from 2017 to 2026, a vice president, a secretary of state, an ambassador, a Speaker of the House, a defence secretary and a senator each stated, in public and in their own words, a Christian or biblical ground for supporting Israel, and that two of them, Huckabee and Johnson, applied it to the West Bank. It shows equally that the formal texts of the decisions most often credited to that theology rested on legal, diplomatic and security reasons, that Huckabee's widest statement was withdrawn as hyperbole within two days, and that Cruz himself declined to make faith the public argument. Several claims in earlier material could not be found in a named source and are not used: that Huckabee said in 2008 "there's really no such thing as a Palestinian" (the sourced 2024 statement is given instead); that Hegseth holds that "biblical Israel includes what is now the West Bank"; that Kristi Noem and Marco Rubio have Christian Zionist positions or "strong CUFI ties"; that Johnson has "ties to CUFI"; and that Trump framed the Golan as prophecy. Michele Bachmann appears in no source examined. [cite:ts12_ap_huckabee] [cite:gi8_ucsb_procl9852]
