Two Employees, Two Super PACs

In the 2022 election cycle Peter Thiel financed most of the outside money behind two of his own former employees' campaigns for the United States Senate, in two states, at the same time, by the same method. In Ohio the candidate was J.D. Vance, who had worked at Thiel's venture firm Mithril and whose fund Thiel had backed. In Arizona it was Blake Masters, who had worked directly for Thiel for years. In each state Thiel gave the first $10 million to a super PAC formed to support one man before that man had declared, added more around Donald Trump's endorsement of his candidate, stopped giving after the primary, and saw his candidate win the Republican primary. Then the two races diverged: Vance won and became Vice President; Masters lost, and lost again two years later. This part sets the two cases side by side and separates what the record documents from what it is often taken to show.

Masters: from a Stanford classroom to Thiel Capital

Blake Masters was born on 5 August 1986 and studied at Stanford, taking a bachelor's degree in political science in 2008 and a law degree in 2012. [cite:de15_wiki_masters] In the spring of 2012 Thiel taught a class at Stanford on start-ups, and Masters's detailed notes of it, posted online, became popular in the technology industry; they grew into Zero to One, published in September 2014 under both men's names. [cite:de15_wiki_masters] [cite:de15_ap_endorse] He went to work for Thiel, rising to chief operating officer of Thiel Capital, the investment firm that manages Thiel's money, and president of the Thiel Foundation, his charity. [cite:de15_ap_endorse] He resigned both posts in March 2022 to run for the Senate. [cite:de15_wiki_masters] Where Vance's ties to Thiel were several and of different kinds, Masters's was simpler and closer: he was, until his campaign, one of Thiel's most senior employees.

Saving Arizona

Saving Arizona PAC was formed in April 2021 with the stated aim of unseating the Democratic senator Mark Kelly, and on 26 April 2021 The Federalist reported that Thiel had given it $10 million while Masters, still at Thiel Capital and the foundation, was expected to run. [cite:de15_federalist_2021] The timing is the same as in Ohio, where Thiel's $10 million to Protect Ohio Values was reported on 15 March 2021, more than three months before Vance declared. [cite:de12_foxbiz_pov] In both states the money came first and the candidate second.

Thiel added $3.5 million in May 2022, and Trump endorsed Masters on 2 June 2022, calling him a "great modern-day thinker." [cite:de15_wiki_masters] [cite:de15_ap_endorse] By late July NBC, from Federal Election Commission filings, reported that the PAC had "received over $13 million from Thiel alone"; CNBC later put his primary-season total at $15 million, the last of it given in July. [cite:de15_nbc_mtp] [cite:de15_cnbc_fundraiser] An Arizona television station, from the same filings, calculated that Thiel's $15 million made up 92 per cent of the PAC's money. [cite:de15_12news] On 2 August 2022 Masters won the primary with 40.2 per cent, against 28.1 for the businessman Jim Lamon and 17.7 for the state attorney general, Mark Brnovich. [cite:de15_wiki_az2022] During the race Masters said, "I think Trump won in 2020," a claim the investigations of that election did not support. [cite:de15_ap_endorse] [cite:de12_pbs_ap]

The general election: the money steps back

In both states Thiel stopped giving after the primary, and the two campaigns' fortunes then depended on whether other money stepped in. In Ohio it did: the Senate Leadership Fund, the super PAC aligned with the Republican Senate leader Mitch McConnell, booked $28 million of advertising for Vance. [cite:de12_vindy_q3] In Arizona it did not. On 20 September 2022 the Fund cancelled $9.6 million of television time it had reserved for Arizona, and Axios reported that Thiel had not personally given to Saving Arizona since the primary. [cite:de15_axios_slf] According to reporting in the Washington Post, summarised by Axios, McConnell and the Fund's president had asked Thiel to re-engage, and he had refused an earlier proposal to split the cost of the general election, citing his early investment and his concern that the spending would become a Democratic talking point. [cite:de15_axios_double] On 12 October Axios reported that he had offered a matching arrangement instead, at least $10 million between them, Thiel paying half. [cite:de15_axios_double] Whether it was agreed was not reported; CNBC reported after the election that Thiel gave $2.5 million more in October, bringing his total for the cycle to $17.5 million. [cite:de15_cnbc_results]

The candidate's own money could not fill the gap. Through the end of September Kelly had raised more than $52 million and Masters just under $5 million. [cite:de15_cnbc_q3] On 8 November 2022 Kelly won by 51.4 per cent to 46.5. [cite:de15_wiki_az2022] In September, at the National Conservatism conference, Thiel had already judged the year: "My scoring on the '22 cycle is we are doing even less well than '94." [cite:de15_cnbc_fundraiser] In 2024 Masters ran for an open House seat in Arizona's 8th district and lost the Republican primary to Abe Hamadeh, after Trump had first backed Hamadeh and then endorsed both men. [cite:de15_wiki_masters]

The other donors, and the complaints

Thiel was not alone, and the overlap among the other donors is itself a documented connection. Richard Uihlein gave $3 million to Saving Arizona, and the Uihlein family also gave to Protect Ohio Values; the Mercer family gave to the Ohio PAC; David Sacks, Thiel's PayPal colleague, gave $1 million to a pro-Vance super PAC; and Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss gave $1 million to the Arizona one. [cite:de15_cnbc_q3] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] Over the whole cycle Saving Arizona reported receipts of about $21.6 million and independent spending of about $25.5 million. [cite:de15_propublica_sa] Thiel's total federal giving in the 2022 elections was reported at $32 million by CNBC and $35 million across sixteen races by Forbes; the two Senate PACs took most of it. [cite:de15_cnbc_results] [cite:de12_ballotpedia_thiel]

Both PACs drew regulatory attention, and neither produced a finding against them. The Federal Election Commission wrote to Saving Arizona in its first year about the timing of its reports of independent spending, a letter that a lawyer at the Campaign Legal Center called "a standard notification" rather than a sign of enforcement. [cite:de15_azmirror_fec] In June 2022 the Center complained that Protect Ohio Values had given the Vance campaign more than $1 million of polling and targeting data through a hidden website; in May 2024 the Commission dismissed the complaint "in light of the public nature of the communications at issue." [cite:de15_clc_complaint] [cite:de15_fec_mur8009]

What is documented and what is inferred

Documented: the employment ties, the amounts, the dates of the gifts, the fact that each first gift preceded the candidacy, the further gifts after Trump's endorsements, the withdrawal of Thiel's money before each general election, and the outcomes. These are the spine of the case, and they show a repeated method. Inferred, and not documented: that Thiel recruited either man to run, since no document records him asking either to; that his money, rather than Trump's endorsement, won the primaries, since the two arrived together and the record cannot separate them; that the PACs were coordinated with the campaigns, since the one complaint was dismissed; and what Thiel wanted from either senator, since he declined to comment to CBS News on his giving and has not publicly stated a purpose for it. [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman] Rhyme is not provenance here either. The two races look alike because the same donor used the same legal vehicle in the same year; that is a pattern in his behaviour, not evidence of a plan beyond it.

The worst-case reading, tested

The book's hypothesis treats the network around Thiel as a means of placing allies in power on the way to an executive without a veto. On this part's material, documented: that Thiel twice financed a former employee's Senate race through a single-candidate super PAC, giving most of each PAC's money, and that one of the two is now first in line to the presidency. [cite:de15_12news] [cite:de15_cnbc_results] [cite:de12_cornell_3usc19] Probable: that Thiel's money made both men competitive in their primaries, since in Ohio the PAC carried most of Vance's advertising and in Arizona the PAC spent about $25.5 million over the cycle while Masters's campaign raised about $12 million. [cite:de12_forbesau] [cite:de15_propublica_sa] [cite:de15_wiki_az2022] The share of credit that belongs to Trump's endorsements cannot be separated from the record.

Not supported: that the method is a machine that delivers office. It worked in Ohio and failed in Arizona, and the decisive general-election money in both states came from, or was withheld by, the party, not Thiel. [cite:de15_axios_slf] [cite:de12_vindy_q3] Not supported either: that Thiel directed either candidate, or that either race was illegally coordinated; the one formal complaint was dismissed. [cite:de15_fec_mur8009] And the record runs against the most extreme version of the reading in one respect: in the autumn of 2022 Thiel declined, at first, to keep paying for the candidate who was losing, citing his own investment and the political cost. [cite:de15_axios_double] [scene:de16_the_valley|Part Sixteen] turns from the money to the wider adoption of these ideas in the technology industry, and [scene:de14_loops|Part Fourteen] has already tested the network's candidate loops.
