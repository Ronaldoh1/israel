Part Six examines the reform most often offered for the weaknesses of plurality voting: ranked-choice voting, in its American single-seat form, the instant runoff. It shows how the count works, with an invented example and the official round-by-round tables from Maine in 2018 and New York City in 2021 and 2025, and is exact about what kind of majority the system produces. It then sets out where the method runs, where eighteen states have banned it, and what voters said when it was put to them directly, including Alaska's second repeal vote on 3 November 2026. It closes with the strongest criticisms, from exhausted ballots to the Alaska election in which the candidate who would have beaten both rivals head to head was eliminated first, and with the larger reform of proportional representation.
