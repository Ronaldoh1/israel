Pensions, Disability and Care in Old Age

Both countries run a social-insurance system founded in the twentieth century: the United States its Social Security programme, Israel its National Insurance Institute (NII). Both pay old-age, survivors' and disability pensions from payroll contributions; both sit beside a private layer of workplace pensions. They differ in how the private layer is built, in what the public pension is worth on its own and in how each state cares for older people who can no longer manage alone. This section compares them on each, with the limits of the record stated. The effect of all transfers together on poverty is the subject of the next section. Old-age pensions: the public floor

Social Security is the largest anti-poverty programme in the United States by the Census Bureau's measure: in 2025 it lifted 28.8 million people above the Supplemental Poverty Measure's line. [cite:us18_census_p60290] It paid more than 71 million beneficiaries in 2025, at a total cost of $1,609.5 billion, 99.2 per cent of it in benefits. [cite:us18_crs_ss2026] Its finances are the main American pension question as of October 2026. The trustees' 2026 report projects that the old-age and survivors' trust fund will be exhausted in the fourth quarter of 2032 and the combined old-age and disability funds in the third quarter of 2034; after that, incoming payroll taxes would cover about 83 per cent of scheduled benefits, falling to 65 per cent by 2100, unless Congress acts. The 75-year shortfall grew from 3.82 per cent of taxable payroll in the 2025 report to 4.42 per cent in 2026. [cite:us18_crs_ss2026]

Israel's public pension is smaller relative to wages. The NII pays a flat old-age allowance, with increments, and a means-tested income supplement for older people with little else; a Taub Center brief of January 2014 estimated that the public pension alone would replace about 22 per cent of the wage of an average-wage man then entering the labour market (about 78 per cent with his workplace pension added), and found that only 24 per cent of the elderly received the supplement, most of them older immigrants, because strict means tests limited access for others. [cite:us18_taub_elderly] The same brief recorded official retirement ages of 67 for men and 62 for women, and a Knesset decision to postpone a planned rise in the women's age. [cite:us18_taub_elderly]

Workplace pensions: compulsory in Israel, voluntary in America

The larger difference lies in the second layer. Israel rebuilt its occupational pensions in stages: in 1995 it closed the old defined-benefit funds to new members; it moved new savers to defined-contribution funds; and in 2008 it made workplace pensions compulsory. [cite:us18_cup_pension] Under the 2008 order every salaried employee must be enrolled, and from January 2014 contributions reached at least 17.5 per cent of salary up to the average wage, about two-thirds of it paid by the employer. [cite:us18_taub_elderly] By the account of two Israeli economists, these reforms made long-term pension saving the main component of Israeli household saving. [cite:us18_cup_pension]

The United States has no equivalent federal requirement. The Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA) regulates workplace plans that employers choose to set up, but, in the Labor Department's words, it "does not require any employer to establish a retirement plan." [cite:us18a_dol_erisa] Whether a worker has a workplace pension therefore depends on whether the employer offers a plan and whether the worker joins it. In March 2025, 72 per cent of private-sector workers had access to a retirement plan and 53 per cent took part; access ranged from 38 per cent in the lowest-paid tenth of jobs to 93 per cent in the highest-paid tenth. [cite:us18a_bls_ret2025] This book has not rebuilt an earlier American coverage series or an Israeli participation figure on the same basis, so the table below compares the two designs; Social Security is the one pension federal law provides to all who qualify by their earnings record. [cite:us18_crs_ss2026]

Disability

In both countries disability pensions come from the same social-insurance system as old-age pensions. In the United States the disability insurance trust fund is one half of the combined fund whose exhaustion the trustees now date to 2034. [cite:us18_crs_ss2026] In Israel the adequacy of the NII's general disability pension became a political fight. Disabled activists blocked roads and the Knesset entrance through 2017 and early 2018, demanding that the pension be raised to the level of the minimum wage; a bill to do so was rejected in committee for the third time in March 2017. On 13 February 2018 the Knesset passed, 84 to 0, a four-year plan costing NIS 4.3 billion ($1.23 billion) that raised monthly benefits by NIS 470 to 770, depending on the degree of disability, bringing the maximum to nearly NIS 4,000. The welfare minister, Haim Katz, called it "the first step to bringing it up to minimum wage"; protesters called it too small. [cite:us18_toi_disability] The NII's own figures credit disability pensions with reducing family poverty by 13.3 per cent in 2023, up from 12.3 per cent in 2022. [cite:us18_nii_pov2023]

Care in old age

Israel pays a long-term care benefit through the NII to older people who need help with daily living. A reform in force from November 2018 raised the number of benefit levels from three to six, let recipients choose cash, care hours or a mix and change the mix as their condition changes, and widened eligibility. In the four years after the reform the number of recipients grew by about 131,400; the NII now counts 309,435 older people receiving the benefit, about 55,000 of them in a serious nursing condition. About 14.5 per cent take cash, 57 per cent a hybrid and 28.5 per cent services in kind. [cite:us18_nii_ltc] The NII itself notes a weakness: because old-age pensions have not risen much in recent years, it believes some families use the cash care benefit to meet basic needs rather than for care. [cite:us18_nii_ltc] This part has not sourced a comparable American series on long-term care; Part Sixteen sets out how Medicare and Medicaid cover older and poorer Americans. [scene:us16_systems|Part Sixteen: who is covered] [cite:us16_census_2025]

Unemployment

Israel pays unemployment benefit nationally through the NII. The benefit is progressive: lower earners get a higher share of their last wage, from 33 to 80 per cent, capped at the average wage for the first five months and two-thirds of it after that; in 2015 the average benefit was 49.2 per cent of the average wage. [cite:us18a_nii_ui2015] It lasts 50 to 175 days, depending on age and the number of dependants: 50 days for a claimant under 25 with up to two dependants, 175 for one aged 45 or over. [cite:us18a_nii_uiperiod] The cuts of 2002–03 lengthened the qualifying period from six months' work in the previous year to twelve of the previous eighteen and halved the maximum for those under 25 from 100 days to 50; in 2015 about 35 per cent of the unemployed, some 70,000 of 202,000, received benefit. [cite:us18a_nii_ui2015] In the United States unemployment insurance is a federal-state system in which each state sets its own rules. Most states pay for up to 26 weeks; as of September 2026, 16 paid for fewer, and Florida, Louisiana, North Carolina and Tennessee for as few as 12. [cite:us18a_cbpp_uiweeks] The Center on Budget and Policy Priorities, a left-leaning research group, calculated that the average benefit replaced 46.6 per cent of prior earnings in 2013, from 33.9 per cent in Alaska to 54.3 per cent in Hawaii, and that outside recessions fewer than half of unemployed workers have received benefits since the late 1950s: about 40 per cent at the end of 2013 and under 30 per cent in early 2014. [cite:us18a_cbpp_uiintro] These are snapshots from different years and on different definitions (Israel's figure is the average benefit against the national average wage; the American one is against each recipient's own prior earnings), so they show the designs rather than a measured gap. Both systems pay a minority of the unemployed.
