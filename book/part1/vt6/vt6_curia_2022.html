The Pope, the Conclave and the Curia since 2022

The question this part answers is the one most often asked about Rome: who actually decides. The answer begins with what the law says, because the Holy See is unusual in having written its chain of command down. Canon 331 of the Code of Canon Law of 1983 describes the Roman Pontiff as head of the college of bishops holding "supreme, full, immediate, and universal ordinary power" in the Church, and canon 333 adds that "no appeal or recourse is permitted against a sentence or decree of the Roman Pontiff." [cite:vt6_cic_331] In Vatican City State the same person holds the fullness of legislative, executive and judicial power under the Fundamental Law, most recently reissued by Leo XIV on 31 July 2026; that text and its delegations are set out in [scene:vt4_fundamental_law|Part Four]. [cite:vt4_vn_fl2026] This section follows the power from the moment a pope is chosen to the offices that work in his name.

How a pope is chosen

The cardinals form a college whose first legal task is to elect the pope; otherwise they assist him, collectively when he convokes them or individually through the offices they hold. [cite:vt6_cic_331] When the See falls vacant, the College governs only for ordinary or urgent business and to prepare the election. The Camerlengo, in 2025 Cardinal Kevin Farrell, administers the temporal goods of the Holy See; the heads of the curial departments lose office, with a few named exceptions such as the Major Penitentiary. [cite:vt6_vn_sedevacante] The rules of the election are those of John Paul II's constitution Universi Dominici Gregis (1996) as amended: only cardinals under 80 on the day the vacancy begins may vote, the constitution caps the electors at 120, voting is by secret ballot in the Sistine Chapel, and election needs two-thirds of the electors present, a rule Benedict XVI restored in 2007 and confirmed in 2013. [cite:vt6_vn_sedevacante]

The last application of these rules is recent and well documented. Francis died on 21 April 2025. The conclave opened on 7 May 2025 with 133 of the 135 eligible electors; two stayed away for health reasons. The two-thirds threshold was set at 89 votes, Cardinal Pietro Parolin presided as the senior elector, and on 8 May 2025, on the fourth ballot, the cardinals elected Robert Francis Prevost, who took the name Leo XIV and was inaugurated on 18 May. [cite:vt6_wiki_conclave2025] Born in Chicago on 14 September 1955, he is an Augustinian friar, a former Prior General of his order, bishop of Chiclayo in Peru from 2015, Prefect of the Dicastery for Bishops from 30 January 2023 and a cardinal from 30 September 2023; the Holy See describes him as the first Augustinian pope. [cite:vt6_vn_leobio] His previous post matters for this part: the office that screens every candidate for bishop was the one he ran before the conclave.

The electorate that will choose his successor is also on the record. As of May 2026 Leo XIV had not created any cardinals; 119 were of voting age, just under the 120 limit that his two predecessors had exceeded, and 29 would pass 80 within three years. [cite:vt6_aleteia_firstyear] He used the College instead as a consultative body, calling consistories for discussion on 7–8 January and 31 May 2026 at which no cardinals were created. [cite:vt6_ncr_consistory] No later creation of cardinals was found in the reporting checked for this volume; the count is given as of May 2026.

The Curia after Praedicate Evangelium

The Roman Curia is the set of offices through which the pope governs the Church. Canon 360 says it acts "in his name and by his authority." [cite:vt6_cic_331] Its present shape was set by Francis's apostolic constitution Praedicate Evangelium, promulgated on 19 March 2022 and in force from Pentecost, 5 June 2022, which replaced John Paul II's Pastor bonus of 1988. [cite:vt6_vn_pe] The constitution groups the Curia into the Secretariat of State, sixteen dicasteries and a set of economic and judicial bodies. The dicasteries run from Evangelization and the Doctrine of the Faith through Bishops, Clergy and Consecrated Life to Legislative Texts and Communication. [cite:vt6_va_pe] The first of them, the Dicastery for Evangelization, "is presided over directly by the Roman Pontiff", with two pro-prefects doing the daily work. [cite:vt6_vn_pe]

Three general rules in the constitution bear on who decides. First, any member of the faithful, lay man or woman included, can head a dicastery, because the authority exercised there is the pope's, received from him. Second, prefects and members are appointed for five-year terms. Third, when the See is vacant "all heads of curial institutions and members cease from their office," except the Major Penitentiary and the Almoner. [cite:vt6_ewtn_pe] Curial power is therefore delegated, time-limited and personal to the pope who granted it; it does not survive him as an independent authority.

At the centre sits the Secretariat of State, which the constitution calls "the Papal Secretariat" providing "close assistance to the Roman Pontiff." It has three sections: General Affairs, headed by the Substitute, which handles the pope's daily business and coordinates the dicasteries; Relations with States and International Organizations, the foreign ministry; and Diplomatic Personnel. [cite:vt6_ewtn_pe] The Holy See's dealings with Israel and Palestine in later parts run through this office.

The money offices

Before 2020 the Secretariat of State also managed large funds of its own. By a motu proprio of 26 December 2020, in force on 1 January 2021, Francis moved the management of its investments, property and bank holdings, including the administration of Peter's Pence, to the Administration of the Patrimony of the Apostolic See (APSA), under the oversight of the Secretariat for the Economy; he had announced the decision to Parolin by letter in August 2020. [cite:vt6_vn_apsa2020] The investment that preceded the change, the London property, is examined in [scene:vt10_london_trial|Part Ten]. Praedicate Evangelium then fixed the economic structure: a Council for the Economy, the Secretariat for the Economy, APSA, an Auditor General, a Commission for Confidential Matters and a Committee for Investments. [cite:vt6_va_pe] The Secretariat for the Economy "functions as a Papal Secretariat for economic and financial matters": it supervises the administration of curial bodies and Peter's Pence, prepares the annual budget and the consolidated balance sheet of the Holy See, and must consult the Council for the Economy. [cite:vt6_va_spe] Founded on 24 February 2014, it was headed by Cardinal George Pell to 2019, Fr Juan Antonio Guerrero Alves to 2022 and, since 1 December 2022, by a layman, Maximino Caballero Ledo. [cite:vt6_gc_spe] How these bodies hold and spend money is the subject of [scene:vt9_apsa_governorate|Part Nine].

Who holds which office, October 2026

Leo XIV has changed personnel slowly. He kept Parolin as Secretary of State and Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández at the Doctrine of the Faith, named Archbishop Filippo Iannone to his own former dicastery in September 2025, and told the Curia on 24 May 2025 that "popes pass, the Curia remains." [cite:vt6_aleteia_appts] On 30 March 2026 he made Archbishop Paolo Rudelli Substitute in place of Archbishop Edgar Peña Parra, who became nuncio to Italy. [cite:vt6_vn_rudelli] Parolin was still signing as Secretary of State on 26 September and 1 October 2026. [cite:vt6_va_parolin2026] The Governorate of the city has been headed since 1 March 2025 by Sister Raffaella Petrini, the first woman in the post, whom Leo XIV confirmed on 9 May 2025. [cite:vt6_vn_petrini] [cite:vt6_wiki_petrini]

How a decision moves

The clearest way to see the division of labour is to follow one routine decision, the appointment of a bishop, through the offices. Bishops of a region propose names; the nuncio investigates the candidates and the diocese and sends three names, the terna, with his preference to the Dicastery for Bishops; the dicastery discusses and votes, and may accept the nuncio's choice, pick another name or ask for a new list; its prefect presents the result to the pope, usually at a Saturday audience; the pope decides; the nuncio asks the candidate; the appointment is announced. The process often takes six to eight months. [cite:vt6_usccb_bishops] The pope signs, but by the time a file reaches him the nuncio and the dicastery have shaped which names he sees. That is the practical form of the answer this part gives in [scene:vt6_grey_pope_myth|its last section]: the decision is the pope's in law, and the offices that frame it are named, appointed by him and removable by him.
