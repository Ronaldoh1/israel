Part Twenty-One established that the church's alliance with Roman power was built in documented steps. The state burned Christian scriptures in 303; ten years later the Milan letter of 313 granted liberty to "the Christians and all others" and returned church property. [cite:vt21_lact_dmp] [cite:vt21_lact_dmp48] The earliest account of Constantine's conversion is a dream reported around 315; the sky vision appears only in Eusebius's life of the emperor written after 337, and why Constantine converted remains a matter of interpretation. [cite:vt21_lact_dmp44] [cite:vt21_vc1] [cite:vt21_wiki_relpol] He kept Sol on his coins until about 325 and gave the church a civic day of rest in 321, but the popular claims of a Mithraic Christmas and a papal title inherited from him fail: emperors held the title Pontifex Maximus until Gratian gave it up after 379, and popes took it up as an honorific in the fifteenth century. [cite:vt21_wiki_sol] [cite:vt21_wiki_mithraism] [cite:vt21_cameron_jrs] [cite:vt21_wiki_pontmax] The bishops gained immunities, courts and imperial basilicas; Constantine's edict closed the meeting houses of Valentinians and four other groups and ordered their books sought, and Theodosius's edict of 27 February 380 made the faith of the bishops of Rome and Alexandria the legal standard. [cite:vt21_hist_x7] [cite:vt21_bateman] [cite:vt21_vc3_65] [cite:vt21_wiki_thessalonica] Part Twenty-Two enters the council Constantine called in 325 and tests what it did and did not decide.
