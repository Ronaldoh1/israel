# Democracy Hasn't Been Tried
## (working title; alternative: *The Unfinished Republic*)
### Outline v1.0 for review, 28 October 2026

**What it is.** It's the counter-argument to the Dark Enlightenment's claim that democracy has failed. It accepts that the American system is not a full democracy and maps exactly where it isn't: the Electoral College, primaries and gerrymanders, money, and corporate speech. It then tests whether finishing democracy answers Thiel's and Yarvin's critique better than a CEO with no veto. It's a companion to *The Dark Enlightenment Architecture* and links to its parts on "the other diagnosis", "where the argument breaks" and "who picks the CEO".

**Rules.** Same standard for every party, lobby and viewpoint. Design facts are kept separate from outcome judgments. Every reform is shown with its evidence for and against. Each part closes with **"The test"**, which asks whether the Thiel/Yarvin claim survives this part's facts, graded documented / probable / not supported.

---

### Part 1 · The Claim
- **What they actually said:** Thiel in 2009 ("I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible") and his 2009 follow-up; Yarvin's "the Cathedral" and "I don't believe in actually voting at all" (2025); Land's "No voice, free exit".
- **Where they're right about the symptoms:** falling trust, weak responsiveness to voters (the Gilens & Page finding and its critics), and money.
- **The book's question:** is the failure democracy's, or the failure of a system that was never made fully democratic?

### Part 2 · What We Actually Have: The Electoral College
- **The architecture:** Article II, the 12th Amendment, and state-by-state winner-take-all (adopted by the 1830s). Maine and Nebraska split their votes by district.
- **Hamilton's design:** Federalist 68 says electors would screen out unfit candidates. *Chiafalo v. Washington* (6 July 2020, 9–0) let states bind and penalise electors, so that check now exists only where state law allows it. This is the documentable core of the "it failed in 2016" argument. Whether the country is worse off is a contested judgment, shown with measures and both readings.
- **The record:** popular-vote/Electoral College splits in 1824, 1876, 1888, 2000 and 2016, with margins. 2016 had 7 faithless electors. 2024 went 312–226, with Trump at 77.3M votes to Harris's 75.0M.
- **Where campaigns go:** 96% of 2020 events were in 12 states and 94% of 2024 events in 7 (pro-reform sources, flagged as such).
- **Fixes:** the National Popular Vote compact (18 states plus DC, 222 electoral votes after Virginia in April 2026, 48 short of 270), a constitutional amendment, and the 2022 Electoral Count Reform Act.
- **Public opinion:** Gallup 58% for a popular vote (Democrats 82%, Republicans 32%); Pew 63%.

### Part 3 · Who Really Chooses
- **Primaries:** low turnout, closed primaries, and the "two-party trap".
- **Gerrymandering:** *Rucho* (2019) took it out of federal courts. The 2025–26 mid-decade redistricting war is mapped state by state (Ballotpedia projects about a 9-seat Republican net gain; Missouri and Virginia were blocked; Indiana rejected its map).
- **Safe seats:** Cook rates 87 swing seats on the pre-redraw maps.

### Part 4 · The Money
- **The case law:** *Buckley* (1976), *Citizens United* (2010: 5–4 on the spending ban, 8–1 upholding disclosure), *SpeechNow*, *McCutcheon* (2014), *Arizona Free Enterprise v. Bennett* (2011).
- **2024:** a record $4.5bn in outside spending, over $1bn of it from undisclosed donors. The top spenders on the same footing: Future Forward ($517M), MAGA Inc. ($377M), CLF, SLF, Musk's America PAC, Preserve America, Fairshake (crypto) and AIPAC's United Democracy Project.
- **2026 so far:** $197.7M from dark-money groups into the four party-leadership super PACs (58% to Republican groups, 42% to Democratic).
- **What money buys:** the research and the disputes about it, as a money trail (input → mechanism → who gains → who pays).

### Part 5 · Free Speech for Whom
- **The asymmetry:**
  - The First Amendment binds government, not companies (*Halleck*, 2019).
  - Companies' spending is protected speech (*Citizens United*).
  - Companies' moderation is protected editorial choice (*Moody v. NetChoice*, 2024).
  - So corporations get speech rights in both directions, and users get none against them.
- **Documented moderation, every direction:**
  - **Palestine-related content:** HRW's 2023 report (self-reported cases, with its own caveat), Meta's own commissioned BSR study (2022), and the Oversight Board rulings on "shaheed" and "from the river to the sea". Also Meta's 2024 policy on "Zionist", Musk's opposite stance on the same slogan, and the 2026 TikTok ownership change.
  - **The other direction:** the Hunter Biden laptop story (both companies later called their handling wrong), the Trump suspension, and the Twitter Files.
  - Each company's stated position sits beside each case.
- **Where users do have rights: government pressure.** *NRA v. Vullo* and *Murthy v. Missouri* (2024). The Biden-era record (Zuckerberg's 2024 letter) and the Trump-era record (EO 14149, the FCC chair's Kimmel remarks, the Paramount merger conditions) are shown on the same footing.
- **Overturning *Citizens United*:** what it would take (an amendment such as Democracy for All, which failed cloture 54–42 in 2014; or a court reversal), and what states are doing now (Montana's I-194 on the November 2026 ballot).

### Part 6 · Ranked-Choice Voting
- **The architecture:** how instant runoff works, with a worked example.
- **NYC:** adopted in 2019. The official 2021 rounds (8 rounds, decided by 7,197 votes, 140,202 inactive ballots) and the 2025 rounds (3 rounds).
- **Maine:** the 2018 2nd District race (first-round leader lost) and the ruling that upheld it.
- **Alaska:** the 2024 repeal failed at 49.88% yes, and a new repeal (Measure 2) is on Tuesday's ballot. Also the 2022 special election, where a study finds the eliminated candidate would have won head-to-head.
- **The bans:** 18 states ban it.
- **The honest critiques:** exhausted ballots, complexity, slower results, and "majority only among ballots still counting".
- **Proportional representation:** the Fair Representation Act.

### Part 7 · The Other Fixes
- **Public financing:** NYC matches the first $250 at 8 to 1; Seattle renewed its democracy vouchers with 59.1% in 2025.
- **Disclosure:** the DISCLOSE Act failed 49–49 in 2022.
- Independent redistricting commissions, open and top-two primaries (Massachusetts Question 3 is on the 2026 ballot), lobbying and revolving-door limits, term limits, and votes at 16 and automatic registration (both sides of each).
- Each fix comes with its evidence, its critics, and what it needs: a state ballot, Congress, or an amendment.

### Part 8 · The Test
- Thiel's and Yarvin's argument held to the same standard. Their texts never engage ranked-choice voting, the popular-vote compact or proportional representation, in the material reviewed.
- **Their logic:** the US is a democracy, it fails, so democracy fails. This holds only if today's system is democracy at its best.
- The book scores which of their charges the reforms would answer, which they wouldn't (for example, voter knowledge and speed of decision), and where Yarvin's "exit" remains a stronger offer.
- The verdict in three registers.

### Part 9 · What It Would Take
- A table of every reform with its legal route (city charter, state ballot, statute, amendment, court), what's already moving, and dates to watch: 3 November 2026 (Alaska Measure 2, Massachusetts Question 3, Montana I-194), compact joiners, and pending cases.
- It closes in the plain, warm register used in "The Garden".
