Who Washington Watched, 1938–1945

In 1938 Congress passed the Foreign Agents Registration Act. The law did not forbid anyone from working in the United States for a foreign cause. It required disclosure: a person acting in America as the agent of a foreign principal had to register with the Justice Department and say whom he served. It was a transparency law, and in practice its force depended on whom the government chose to ask. That choice is the subject of this section. Through the war years, foreign-born organizers and Palestine-based movements worked on American soil to shape American policy, and the question is what the government did about them, and how evenly.

A familiar claim holds that the United States government simply never noticed or questioned foreign-directed Zionist operations in America. The record does not support it. Washington noticed. The State Department noticed, the FBI investigated, British officials sent warnings, and the wartime intelligence service kept a desk devoted to the subject. What the record shows is not blindness but selection: scrutiny fell hard on one wing of the movement, the Irgun-aligned fringe, while the main political apparatus operated in the open. [cite:djn_bergson_fara_1944] [cite:jta_bergson_fbi]

The wing that drew the heat was the Bergson group, whose campaigns are told in [scene:bg_s01|the previous section]. Its leader, Hillel Kook, was a Lithuanian-born member of the Irgun who had worked in the United States since 1940 under the name "Peter Bergson." He built a network of committees, and in 1944 he opened a "Hebrew Embassy" in Washington. In June 1944 his Hebrew Committee of National Liberation registered with the Justice Department's foreign agents section. It named its foreign principal as the "Hebrew nation." [cite:djn_bergson_fara_1944] [cite:ushmm_peter_bergson] At the same time deportation proceedings were opened against Kook himself. British officials had already warned the State Department that he was a "bomb-thrower." [cite:djn_bergson_fara_1944] [cite:ushmm_peter_bergson]

The fuller story of the surveillance came out decades later. FBI files obtained under the Freedom of Information Act by the Wyman Institute show that the investigation began at the State Department's instigation. Agents planted informants inside the Bergson group, opened its leaders' mail and searched their trash. [cite:jta_bergson_fbi] [cite:jewishpress_fbi_spied_bergson] One internal memo noted that Bergson had "been in the hair of" Secretary of State Cordell Hull. [cite:jta_bergson_fbi] The phrase suggests irritation at a lobbyist as much as a security concern, though the files as reported here do not let a reader separate the two.

The pressure did not come only from the government. Leaders of established Jewish organizations urged officials to deport Kook or to draft him into the army. They included Morris Waldman of the American Jewish Committee and Nahum Goldmann. [cite:jta_bergson_fbi] [cite:jewishpress_bergson_part2] It is a record of the era's internal divisions. The rivalry between the Bergson group and the mainstream Zionist leadership was not only a matter of competing advertisements and rival rallies. At least some mainstream leaders were prepared to ask the state to remove the competitor from the country.

The mainstream apparatus itself was not treated this way. The Jewish Agency and the Zionist Emergency Council operated openly through the 1940s. [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order] They were not ordered to register as foreign agents. Their successor body, the American Zionist Council, received such an order only in November 1962, and the order was later withdrawn. [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order] [cite:jta_fulbright_jewish_agency_hearing] That episode belongs to the Cold War, and this library tells it there. For the war years, the contrast is the point: the insurgent group registered under pressure and its leader faced deportation, while the established organizations were not asked to register for nearly two decades.

There was a second watcher, and it should not be merged with the first. The Office of Strategic Services, the wartime predecessor of the Central Intelligence Agency, ran a Foreign Nationalities Branch, and within it a Palestine desk that tracked Zionist movement activity during the Second World War. [cite:forward_oss_palestine_desk] Its intelligence came largely from Jewish officials who volunteered information. They saw it as a patriotic act in wartime, not as betrayal, and the record describes it as volunteered, not coerced. [cite:forward_oss_palestine_desk] Its list of people tracked reportedly included Moshe Shertok, later Moshe Sharett, Israel's second Prime Minister. [cite:forward_oss_palestine_desk] A 2016 review of declassified documents by the Forward described a wide surveillance effort aimed at Jewish organizations and Zionist political activity, with some informants highly placed in Jewish agencies, building a picture of which American Jewish leaders aligned with which Zionist faction. [cite:forward_oss]

The two agencies had different reasons and different chains of command. The OSS had a wartime foreign-intelligence interest; the FBI's interest was domestic security. The records of the two are documented separately, and they should not be folded into a single claim about "the intelligence community." [cite:forward_oss_palestine_desk] A reader who imagines one coordinated American operation watching Zionist activity in these years is reading more unity into the record than it holds.

Meanwhile the Yishuv was building intelligence of its own. In 1940 the Haganah established the Shai (Sherut Yediot, the Information Service) as its intelligence and counter-espionage arm. [cite:shai_wiki] One account in the record goes further than the documents cited above. It holds that the Shai had American branches, Zionist networks in New York and Detroit serving as nodes for procurement and intelligence, that these were not registered under the 1938 Act, that the FBI knew, and that the Justice Department, asked repeatedly to prosecute or to compel registration, declined every time. [cite:medium_zionist] [cite:ips_espionage] The same account describes the Haganah's arms-buying branch, Rekhesh ("Acquisitions"), as an illegal procurement network in the United States, funded by American Jewish donors and buying surplus military equipment from demobilized American stocks and from black-market dealers, with smuggling routes that often relied on organized crime to get past export controls and the arms embargo on the region. [cite:medium_zionist] [cite:ips_espionage] It calls the Justice Department's restraint "deliberate," a political decision dressed as prosecutorial discretion, made because the Truman administration was moving toward support for a Jewish state. [cite:forward_oss] [cite:medium_zionist]

These are that account's interpretations, and they should be read as such. Three cautions apply. First, Rekhesh's main activity in the United States came in the mid-1940s and after the war, and the account ties the restraint to the Truman administration's later policy; the bulk of the story it tells belongs to the next stage of this library, told in [scene:cia_s01|the Shai and the OSS]. Second, the account's own evidentiary note marks one question as contested: whether the Justice Department's decisions reflected explicit political direction from the White House or independent prosecutorial judgment. [cite:medium_zionist] Third, it marks the full extent of Zionist intelligence operations on American soil in the 1940s as only partly documented and partly still classified. [cite:forward_oss] The documented core is narrower: a wartime OSS desk that watched, an FBI that investigated the Bergson group, and a mainstream apparatus that was not asked to register.

Nor is it true that no one was ever prosecuted. The arms operation was taken to court after the war, under the Neutrality Act. Charles Winters was convicted in 1949 and served 18 months. Herman "Hank" Greenspun and Al Schwimmer were convicted and served no prison time. All three were later pardoned: Greenspun by President Kennedy in 1961, Schwimmer by President Clinton in 2000, and Winters posthumously by President George W. Bush in 2008, after an appeal campaign that included a letter from Steven Spielberg. [cite:nbc_bush_pardons_winters] [cite:ina_schwimmer_obit] [cite:wilmerhale_winters_pardon] Those convictions sit uneasily with the claim that the Justice Department declined every case, and the two parts of the record should be read side by side.

The 1938 law would go on to carry a longer argument about even enforcement. A later chapter sets the federal court order compelling an Irish republican fund-raising group to register as an agent of the Provisional IRA against a Justice Department inquiry into a pro-Israel lobby that closed without public explanation: [scene:df_s08|the Act enforced against one group and not another]. [cite:wiki_noraid] [cite:justice_gov_fara_report_1980] That comparison belongs to decades after this chapter. Its starting point is here, in a law written in 1938 and applied, in its first years, to the loudest outsider rather than to the established machine.

So the answer to "did anyone notice?" is yes. The State Department, the FBI and British officials noticed, and the wartime intelligence service kept its own file. But the scrutiny fell on the Irgun-aligned fringe, often at the urging of mainstream organizations themselves, while the main state-building apparatus operated openly for nearly two decades; and the later convictions of individual smugglers were erased by pardon. [cite:jta_bergson_fbi] [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order] [cite:nbc_bush_pardons_winters] What the record does not settle is why. It is possible to read the pattern as political favor toward the mainstream movement, as an establishment using the state against a rival, as a department settling a score with a lobbyist who had irritated its Secretary, or as ordinary security worry about a man the British called a bomb-thrower. The sources here document the pattern. They do not document the motive behind it, and much of the intelligence record from these years remains, by the account that relies on it most, partly classified.
