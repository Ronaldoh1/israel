The Network Map

Books and articles about the Dark Enlightenment often contain a diagram: Peter Thiel at the centre, lines radiating out to Curtis Yarvin, J.D. Vance, Elon Musk, Blake Masters, David Sacks and the companies around them. Most such diagrams draw every line the same way. This part draws them differently. Each line below is a documented transfer of money, a job, an investment, a board seat or an appointment, with the amount where one is known, the date, the type of connection and its degree: direct (one party paid, hired or appointed the other), one step removed (the tie runs through a fund, company or committee), or shared interest only (no transfer at all). A line with no document behind it is not drawn. The biographies that supply most of these facts are in [scene:de9_money_and_influence|Thiel's money ledger], [scene:de11_money_and_influence|Musk's] and [scene:de12_money_and_influence|Vance's]; this section brings them onto one page and adds the ties that run between them.

The political money

The largest documented flows run from Thiel to two Senate campaigns in 2022. He gave $15 million to Protect Ohio Values, the super PAC that carried most of Vance's advertising in the Ohio primary, a record for a single Senate candidate at the time. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de14_forbesau] He gave $15 million to Saving Arizona PAC, which backed his former employee Blake Masters in the Arizona primary, and $2.5 million more in October, for $17.5 million in Arizona and about $32 million across the two races. [cite:de14_axios_masters] [cite:de14_cnbc_2022] [cite:de9_cnbc_2022] A super PAC is a committee that may raise and spend unlimited sums so long as it does not coordinate with the candidate; that is why a single donor could fund most of a campaign's advertising. Vance won his seat; Masters lost to Mark Kelly. [cite:de14_cnbc_2022]

Smaller documented flows ran alongside. Per Aspera Policy, a nonprofit Thiel controlled that does not disclose its donors, sent at least $200,000 to Protect Ohio Values. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] David Sacks gave $1 million of his own money to the pro-Vance super PAC, and, with Keith Rabois, co-hosted a spring 2022 fundraiser for Vance and Masters together; Thiel, according to Politico, helped recruit about ten major donors for Vance. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] [cite:de13_wiki_sacks] [cite:de14_forbesau] The Mercer, Uihlein and Lindner families, none of them from the PayPal network, gave hundreds of thousands to the same super PAC. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] Vance's own story, and the parallel between his race and Masters's, is in [scene:de15_vance_and_masters|Part Fifteen].

Musk's money is larger than all of this together and runs on a separate line. He spent more than $290 million in the 2024 election, about a quarter of a billion of it through America PAC, his own super PAC, including more than $50 million on million-dollar giveaways to people who signed a petition. [cite:de14_cnn_musk290] No document shows Thiel money in America PAC or Musk money in the 2022 Vance and Masters super PACs; the two men's political spending meets only in the candidates they both came to support. Thiel's own spending since 2022 has been smaller and more conventional: $852,200 to Speaker Mike Johnson's committee in February 2025, $3 million against California's proposed billionaire tax in December 2025, and $1.5 million to Club for Growth Action in February 2026. [cite:de9_mj_2025] [cite:de9_fortune_cbrt] [cite:de9_bgov_cfg]

Jobs, investments and seats

Money is only one kind of line. Vance worked for Thiel at Mithril Capital before 2017, and when he founded his own venture firm, Narya Capital, in Ohio in 2019, it was reported to be backed by Thiel, Marc Andreessen and Eric Schmidt; the amounts have not been published. [cite:de14_forbesau] [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] In February 2021 Thiel brought Vance to Mar-a-Lago to meet Trump. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] Masters co-wrote Thiel's book Zero to One (2014) and was chief operating officer of Thiel Capital and president of the Thiel Foundation until he resigned both posts in March 2022 to run. [cite:de14_wiki_masters] Founders Fund invested $20 million in SpaceX in August 2008, and its partner Luke Nosek joined SpaceX's board. [cite:de13_space_ff] Founders Fund, with the firm Andreessen Horowitz, invested $1.1 million in Tlon, the company Yarvin founded to build Urbit, and in May 2011 the Thiel Foundation named John Burnham, who went on to co-found Tlon, as one of its first Thiel Fellows, with $100,000 over two years. [cite:de5_wiki_urbit] [cite:de5_audit_tc2011] No other payment from Thiel to Yarvin is documented; nor is any Thiel money to the National Conservatism conferences.

The third kind of line is government money to companies the network owns. Thiel co-founded Palantir in 2003 and is its chairman. [cite:de9_wiki_thiel] Immigration and Customs Enforcement has paid Palantir since 2014, a contract worth $41 million before the 2019 renewal; in April 2025 it agreed to pay $29.9 million for "ImmigrationOS" on the grounds that "Palantir is the only source that can provide the required capabilities," and in February 2026 the Department of Homeland Security signed a blanket purchase agreement with Palantir worth up to $1 billion over five years. [cite:ow9_mj_icm2019] [cite:ow9_reg_immos] [cite:ow9_sa_dhsbpa] SpaceX's government business, counted by the Washington Post at at least $38 billion over twenty years, long predates 2025. [cite:de11_wapo_38bn] These contracts are documented. That any of them followed from the political money above is not, and the [scene:de14_loops|next section] treats that question.

What the map shows, and what it leaves out

Drawn this way, the map has one centre for political money, Thiel, in one period, 2021 to 2022, aimed at two candidates who had worked for him; a second, much larger centre, Musk, in 2024, aimed at a presidential ticket that by then included one of the two. It also has a long-running commercial layer, Palantir and SpaceX, whose government contracts began years before any of the political spending. The people at its centre are a handful of named individuals, not a movement; and the lines between them are of different kinds, which is why the table names each.

The map also has edges it cannot draw. The donors behind Per Aspera Policy are not public. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] The Rockbridge Network, a donor group that grew out of a 2019 summit Thiel and Vance convened in Ohio, has no website or public entity; only its affiliated super PAC, Turnout for America, files with the Federal Election Commission, reporting $34.5 million spent in the 2024 cycle. [cite:de14_wapo_rockbridge] The Thiel Foundation's grants to the donor-advised fund DonorsTrust leave the foundation's books under DonorsTrust's name, and where they go after that is not disclosed. These gaps, and what can honestly be said about them, are the subject of [scene:de14_foundations_and_opacity|the third section]. Foreign money into the network's companies, such as Gulf investment in Musk's ventures, belongs to the companies and not to the theorists, and is treated in [scene:de36_foreign_money|Part Thirty-Six].
