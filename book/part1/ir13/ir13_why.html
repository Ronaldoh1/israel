What Israel Says It Wants

Two kinds of answer are kept apart here. What Israel's governments have said they want from Iran is a matter of record. Why Iran, among the states that oppose Israel, became its principal target is a question of motive, and the answers to it are arguments, given below with the case against each.

No hostile bomb: the Begin doctrine

The oldest stated aim predates the Islamic Republic's nuclear programme. On 7 June 1981 Israeli aircraft destroyed Iraq's Osirak reactor near Baghdad. Prime Minister Menachem Begin's government declared: "On no account shall we permit an enemy to develop weapons of mass destruction against the people of Israel." Begin said the strike "will be a precedent for every future government in Israel." [cite:ir13_wiki_begindoc] On 19 June the UN Security Council, unanimously and with American support, "strongly condemns the military attack by Israel in clear violation of the Charter of the United Nations" and called on Israel "urgently to place its nuclear facilities under IAEA safeguards." [cite:ir13_un_res487] Dimona, the reactor at the heart of Israel's own undeclared programme, has remained outside them. [cite:ir4_fas_israel] [scene:ir4_dimona|Part Four: Dimona] On 6 September 2007 Israeli jets destroyed a reactor under construction at Deir ez-Zor in Syria. Israel acknowledged the strike only on 21 March 2018, when its chief of staff, Gadi Eisenkot, said: "This was the message in 1981, this is the message in 2007." The Associated Press noted that the disclosure could be read as a warning to Iran. [cite:ir13_ap_kibar2018] Writing the same day, Amos Yadlin, a former head of Israeli military intelligence, warned that the doctrine might not be "eternally viable" against Iran, whose centrifuges "can be dispersed, concealed, and protected more effectively" than a reactor, and that "the attack itself might accelerate the effort." [cite:ir13_inss_yadlin] The doctrine was applied to Iran in June 2025 and again from 28 February 2026. [cite:ir13_wiki_begindoc] [scene:ir10_strikes|Part Ten: the June 2025 strikes] [scene:ir11_war|Part Eleven: the 2026 war]

Stopping an Iranian bomb

The aim Israel stated most often was narrower: that the Islamic Republic must not obtain a nuclear weapon. On 13 June 2025 Netanyahu said Iran had enough highly enriched uranium "for nine atom bombs" and that, "if not stopped," it "could produce a nuclear weapon in a very short time." [cite:ir5_dcnf_jun2025] The record he spoke into was mixed, and Part Five tests his predictions against it. [scene:ir5_test|Part Five: the test] The day before, the IAEA Board of Governors had found Iran in breach of its safeguards obligations for the first time in nearly two decades; Iran rejected the finding. [cite:ir5_aj_iaea2025] In March 2025 the US Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, had testified that Iran was "not building a nuclear weapon." A week after the strikes the IAEA's director general, Rafael Grossi, said his inspectors had not found "an active, systematic plan to build a nuclear weapon." [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard] [cite:ir5_tbs_grossi] Iran's government has always said its programme is peaceful. [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard]

Ending Iran's network

The third stated aim concerns the armed groups Iran funds and arms. Netanyahu's statement of 28 February 2026 said that "for 47 years, the evil regime in Iran has called for death to Israel, death to America," and that if Israel did not act it would face "an Iran with tens of thousands of ballistic missiles." [cite:ir13_pbs_bibi2026] Washington had made the same demand before: in May 2018 Secretary of State Mike Pompeo's twelve conditions for Iran included an end to its support for Hezbollah, Hamas and the Houthis. Iran's President Hassan Rouhani replied: "Who are you to decide for Iran and the world?" [cite:ir7_voa_pompeo] Part Nine documents that support and Iran's leaders' own statements about Israel, including Ayatollah Ali Khamenei's 2020 description of it as "a cancerous tumor." [cite:ir3_reuters_tumor2020] [scene:ir9_reach|Part Nine: Iran's reach]

Regime change

The fourth aim moved from hint to statement in eight months. In June 2025 Israel's declared war aims were the destruction of Iran's nuclear and ballistic-missile capabilities, and Israeli officials told Axios that regime change was not among the objectives the security cabinet had approved. Netanyahu nonetheless suggested on Fox News on 15 June that the war could bring a change of government. On 16 June he told ABC that killing Khamenei was "not going to escalate the conflict, it's going to end the conflict," and told Israeli reporters "we could see many changes in Iran." [cite:ir13_axios_regime2025] On 28 February 2026 he recalled being asked in 2025 whether regime change was the goal: "I replied that this was not the goal, but it could certainly be the outcome." The new operation, he said, would "create conditions that will allow the brave Iranian people to cast off the yoke of this murderous regime," and he added: "You are not our enemies and we are not your enemies." [cite:ir13_pbs_bibi2026] President Trump told Iranians the same day, "When we are finished, take over your government." [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] The UK House of Commons Library summarised the two governments' stated aims as regime change and the destruction of Iran's nuclear and missile programmes; the United States called its action self-defence, and Iran said it would close the Strait of Hormuz. [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026]

Why Iran? Five Arguments

The explanations that follow are arguments made by named analysts and officials. None is established by a document of the kind that establishes the aims above.

Military supremacy. One argument is that the Begin doctrine is, in effect, a policy of nuclear monopoly. Israel is estimated to hold about 90 warheads, which it neither confirms nor denies, outside the Non-Proliferation Treaty, and it acts to keep any regional rival from acquiring one. [cite:ir4_ynet_sipri] American law reinforces the edge: since the Naval Vessel Transfer Act of 2008, the United States has been required by statute to preserve Israel's "qualitative military edge" and to assess it every four years, and arms sales to other states in the region may not adversely affect it. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] On this reading, Iran is targeted because it is the regional power with the population, the industry and the enriched uranium to end that edge. Israeli governments reply that the doctrine targets states whose leaders call for Israel's destruction, not rivals as such, though some of their own security chiefs questioned the scale of the threat: in 2009 Defence Minister Ehud Barak said he was "not among those who believe Iran is an existential issue for Israel." In 2011 the Mossad director, Tamir Pardo, said the term "existential threat is used too freely." [cite:ir6_jta_barak2009] [cite:ir6_ahram_pardo2011]

Ideology. A second argument says the hostility comes from Tehran. Iran is not the only state in the region that refuses to recognise Israel; in November 2023 Iraq, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Syria and Yemen did not recognise it either. [cite:ir13_vc_recog] What distinguishes Iran's government is that its supreme leader has predicted Israel's end and armed groups at war with it. Karim Sadjadpour of the Carnegie Endowment calls opposition to Israel "the most enduring pillar of Iranian revolutionary ideology," a hostility that, he argues, serves no national interest, and he quotes Khamenei's 2012 call for "a true cancer tumor" to be "cut off." [cite:ir13_ceip_sadjadpour] Trita Parsi, in Treacherous Alliance (2007), argues the reverse: the rivalry is "neither about ideology nor religion" but geopolitics, and the revolutionary rhetoric covers aims little changed from the Shah's. [cite:ir13_ips_parsi]

The periphery reversed. Parsi's account also dates the turn. Until 1979 Israel treated Iran as a partner on the region's edge against the Arab states, as Part One describes. [scene:ir1_periphery|Part One: the periphery alliance] After Iraq's defeat in 1991, in the reviewer's summary of Parsi, "Israeli politicians began describing Iran as the primary threat"; Brent Scowcroft, President George H. W. Bush's national security adviser, is quoted as saying that "the pro-Israeli community turned strongly against Iran." [cite:ir13_ips_parsi] Israel has since cultivated a new periphery around Iran: from 2016 to 2020 it supplied 69 per cent of the major arms imported by Azerbaijan, which borders Iran. [cite:ir13_sipri_azer] Critics of Parsi answer that what changed after 1979 was Iran's government and its alliances, documented in Part Nine, not Israel's view of the map. [cite:ir13_ceip_sadjadpour]

Domestic politics. A third argument concerns Netanyahu himself. Some of his own former security chiefs made the charge. In 2012 Yuval Diskin, the former head of the Shin Bet, said he had no faith in "the current leadership of Israel to lead us to an event of this magnitude"; Netanyahu's spokesman declined to comment. In 2021 Yadlin said that "the Iranian issue was privatized to one person." [cite:ir6_fox_diskin2012] [cite:ir6_toi_yadlin2021] The June 2025 war began while Netanyahu faced a coalition crisis, an inquiry into 7 October and his corruption trial, and it gave him what Al Jazeera called political breathing room. The case against reading the war as a personal manoeuvre is that his opponents backed it. The opposition leader Yair Lapid supported the strikes, and former prime minister Naftali Bennett said, "There is no right, no left, no opposition and no coalition." [cite:ir13_aje_breathing]

American strategy. The last argument holds that Israel's Iran policy and Washington's converged: Pompeo's 2018 demands and Trump's 2026 aims matched Israel's own. [cite:ir7_voa_pompeo] [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] Whether Israel drew the United States in, or the United States pursued aims of its own, is tested in Parts Twelve and Fourteen.
