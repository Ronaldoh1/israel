By the end of 1948 the state that had been a proposal in November 1947 was a fact. Resolution 181 had passed 33 to 13, with 10 abstentions, after a three-day postponement in which three small states changed their votes. Ben-Gurion had declared independence on May 14, 1948, and Truman had recognized the new state de facto eleven minutes later, by his own authority and against the advice of his senior officials. The Soviet Union had extended the first de jure recognition three days after that.

The new state had an army, created by order on 26 May 1948 and consolidated by force at the Altalena and after the killing of Count Bernadotte. It had been armed in its first war not by the United States, which kept its embargo, but by Czechoslovakia with Soviet consent, paid for in part by a reported $50 million raised in American cities. The war had turned in its favor after the first truce.

It had also produced the displacement of between 700,000 and 750,000 Palestinians and the depopulation of about 500 villages. The question of their return, affirmed by Resolution 194 in December 1948, was left unresolved.
