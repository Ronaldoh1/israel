Iran: Two Wars and Their Tolls

Iran appears in no Greater-Israel map or text examined for this volume, and Israel has never claimed Iranian territory; Part Eight sets out that test. [scene:gi8_claims|Part Eight: the claims tested] Israel's wars with Iran in June 2025 and from 28 February 2026 had other stated grounds: Iran's nuclear and missile programmes, its arming of allied groups, and, by 2026, the aim of creating conditions for Iranians to overthrow their government. [cite:ir10_idf_jun14] [cite:ir13_pbs_bibi2026] The Iran volume tells both wars and weighs those reasons. [scene:ir10_strikes|The Iran Architecture: the June 2025 strikes] [scene:ir11_war|The Iran Architecture: the 2026 war] This section brings together their human and physical cost: the dead and wounded on both sides by each counting body, the damage to civilian infrastructure, and the people who fled. In 2026 the war was waged jointly by Israel and the United States, and where a death or strike is attributed to one of them, the text says which.

June 2025: twelve days

Israel opened the war on 13 June 2025. Its military said the objective was "eliminating the existential Iranian threat", meaning the nuclear and missile threat. The strikes hit Natanz and other military and nuclear sites and killed the chief of the general staff, the commander of the Revolutionary Guards and several nuclear scientists. [cite:ir10_idf_jun14] [cite:ir10_na_killed] Iran called its missile and drone barrages "Operation True Promise 3", "in direct retaliation against Israeli aggression", and said it was hitting military and fuel infrastructure. [cite:ir10_irna_tp3] Israel also struck targets that were not military. On 23 June its aircraft hit Tehran's Evin prison, where the Iranian government said 79 people were killed, among them prisoners' visiting relatives, staff, conscript guards, a child and local residents. Israeli officials called the strike "symbolic". Human Rights Watch found it "unlawfully indiscriminate" and an apparent war crime. [cite:gi15_wiki_evin]

The counts of the dead in Iran differ by method. Iran's Health Ministry gave at least 610 dead by 24 June. On 7 July the head of Iran's Foundation of Martyrs said at least 1,060 had been killed. The Human Rights Activists News Agency (HRANA), counting case by case, recorded 1,190 dead, 436 of them civilians and 435 security forces, and 4,475 wounded. [cite:ir10_toi_numbers] [cite:ir10_ap_toll] In Israel, Iranian missiles killed 28 people, all but one of them civilians. The Health Ministry recorded 3,238 people taken to hospital, and more than 9,000 Israelis were displaced from damaged homes. [cite:ir10_toi_numbers] The worst single strike killed nine people in Bat Yam on 15 June; others hit the Weizmann Institute, the Bazan refinery in Haifa, which was shut, and Soroka Medical Centre in Beersheba. [cite:ir10_aj_map] [cite:ir10_en_soroka]

The 2026 war

The second war began on 28 February 2026 with joint American and Israeli strikes that killed Iran's Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] Three counts of the dead in Iran over the first forty days exist, and they measure different things. Iran's government gave totals without dividing civilians from soldiers: 3,468 dead by 18 April, according to the Foundation of Martyrs. [cite:ir11_afp_tolls] Iran's Health Ministry, as reported by the UN, counted at least 2,362 civilians killed and more than 32,314 injured between 28 February and 7 April. [cite:gi15_ocha_iran3] HRANA counted at least 3,636 dead by 7 April: 1,701 civilians, among them at least 254 children, 1,221 military personnel and 714 it could not classify. [cite:ir11_hrana_day22] [cite:ir11_afp_tolls] The two civilian figures, 2,362 and 1,701, cannot be reconciled from the published data. The first is a government ministry's count; the second is a monitor's count that leaves hundreds of deaths unclassified.

The single strike on civilians documented in most detail was American. On 28 February missiles hit the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in Minab. Amnesty International found that US Tomahawks had struck it, killing more than 150 people, 120 of them children, and that the attack "clearly did not take all feasible precautions"; a UN fact-finding mission found in September 2026 reasonable grounds to believe the United States was responsible. President Trump said the strike was not done "on purpose," and a US admiral testified in May that the school sat on an active Iranian cruise missile base. As of 16 October 2026 the Pentagon said its investigation was ongoing and had published no findings. [cite:ir11_amnesty_minab] [cite:ir11a_almon_minab] [cite:gi15a_ibt_minab] In Israel, Iranian missiles killed between 24 and 28 people over the forty days by Israeli media counts (the lower figure may include four Palestinians in the West Bank), with more than 7,000 injured. The deadliest strike killed nine civilians in a shelter in Beit Shemesh on 1 March, which Amnesty said "must be investigated as a war crime". Amnesty counted at least 23 people killed in the Gulf states by 27 March. [cite:ir11_wiki_strikesisrael] [cite:ir11_amnesty_beitshemesh] No consolidated count of Iranian dead after the ceasefire collapsed in July 2026 was found for this book. [cite:ir11_aj_jul8]

Infrastructure and displacement

The 2026 war damaged civilian infrastructure across Iran, on figures the UN compiled from Iranian institutions. By mid-April the Iranian Red Crescent reported about 125,600 civilian units damaged, about 100,000 of them homes. The Ministry of Energy reported more than 1,000 electricity distribution points with major damage, over 470 of them in Tehran province, and the Red Crescent reported 15 bridges, railway sites and five fuel storage tanks damaged. [cite:gi15_ocha_iran3] Iran's government said about 3.2 million people, some 800,000 households, had relocated inside the country, about 60 per cent of them women and children. The UN refugee agency counted about 118,300 Iranians crossing into Turkey and Pakistan by 16 April. Most of those movements, it said, appeared routine, family-related or precautionary rather than large-scale flight. [cite:gi15_ocha_iran3] These are Iranian official figures relayed by the UN, not independent surveys, and the UN gives them as such. The American and Israeli governments said they were striking military, nuclear and missile targets; Airwars recorded more than 17,000 targets declared struck by the two. [cite:ir11_airwars]

Status, October 2026

As of October 2026 the war with Iran has not formally ended. The Islamabad Memorandum of 17 June collapsed on 7 and 8 July, an American naval blockade of Iran's ports has been in force since 14 July, and Iran's oil loadings fell to about 260,000 barrels a day in August, by Kpler's estimate, against 1.7 million a year earlier. [cite:ir11_aj_jul8] [cite:hz12a_cbs_oct] [cite:ir11_oilprice_exports] On 8 October President Trump wrote that the United States would not attack Iran before the midterm elections of 3 November. [cite:ir11_ap_midterms] The IAEA has not had access to the sites bombed in June 2025 and has lost continuity of knowledge over Iran's declared enriched uranium, the material at the centre of both wars' stated aims. [cite:ir11_iaea_sep7] [scene:ir11_now|The Iran Architecture: from ceasefire to blockade] The documented record shows two wars with stated aims about weapons and, by 2026, about Iran's government. It contains no claim to Iranian land. Treating these wars as part of a territorial programme would join two separate records without a document to connect them, the same conclusion Part Eight reached.
