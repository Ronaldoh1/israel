Foundations and Opacity

Not all of the network's money moves through channels that name the giver and the receiver. Some passes through a private foundation, which must publish its grants but not its reasons; some through a donor-advised fund, which publishes its own grants but not whose money they were; some through a nonprofit that need not publish its donors at all; and some through a private group that has no public existence. This section sets out what each of these vehicles shows and what it hides, for the vehicles that touch Thiel and his circle. Its rule is the same as the rest of the book's: where the record stops, the account stops too, and the gap is named as a gap rather than filled.

The Thiel Foundation: what it publishes

The Thiel Foundation is a private foundation registered in Miami, and like every private foundation it files an annual Form 990-PF with the Internal Revenue Service, a return that lists its assets and its grants. Its filings show assets of about $45.2 million at the end of 2024 and charitable disbursements of about $3.4 million that year; disbursements were about $4.9 million in 2023, $5.1 million in 2022 and $11.0 million in 2021, the largest year on record. [cite:de14_pp_thielfdn] Its best-known programme is the Thiel Fellowship, which from 2011 gave each fellow $100,000 over two years; one of the first twenty fellows, John Burnham, went on to co-found Tlon with Curtis Yarvin. [cite:de5_audit_tc2011] Through a programme called Imitatio, the foundation funds scholarship on the work of the French thinker René Girard, with grants in 2024 and 2025 to the theologian James Alison, the Girard scholar Benoît Chantre, the Lumen Christi Institute in Chicago and the Colloquium on Violence and Religion, among others; Girard's place in Thiel's thought is in [scene:de10_girard_and_the_katechon|Part Ten]. [cite:de14_kindora_thiel] In 2024 it gave $100,000 to the Leo Strauss Foundation. [cite:de14_kindora_thiel]

Two of the foundation's former leaders later held public roles. Jim O'Neill was its chief executive from 2009 to 2012 before becoming deputy secretary of Health and Human Services in 2025; Blake Masters was its president until he resigned in March 2022 to run for the Senate with $17.5 million of Thiel's money behind him. [cite:de13_wiki_oneill] [cite:de14_wiki_masters] [cite:de14_cnbc_2022] These are documented ties of employment, not grants; the foundation's filings show no political spending, which private foundations are barred from making.

One of the foundation's older commitments shows how even a well-documented gift can carry two figures. Thiel pledged $500,000 to the Seasteading Institute, which promotes self-governing settlements on the open sea, at its launch in April 2008. [cite:de3_wiki_tsi] A 2011 account put his total giving at $1.25 million, and quoted the institute's co-founder, Patri Friedman: "Thiel is our most generous funder, but this doesn't mean that he dictates our plans." [cite:de14_yahoo_seastead] A 2018 account said that "Thiel invested $1.7 million" and that he left the institute's board in 2011. [cite:de14_bi_seastead] The two are best read as totals at different dates; the 2018 figure gives no source of its own, and the institute's public returns do not name its donors, so the difference cannot be closed. Both are reported here. The exit projects themselves are in [scene:de22_seas_and_zones|Part Twenty-Two].

DonorsTrust: where the trail goes dark

DonorsTrust, based in Alexandria, Virginia, and founded in 1999, runs donor-advised funds: accounts in which a donor places money, takes the tax deduction at once, and later recommends grants to charities. When DonorsTrust makes a grant, the recipient's records and DonorsTrust's own filings show the money as coming from DonorsTrust, not from the donor who advised it. [cite:de14_dg_donorstrust] It is large and openly conservative and libertarian in purpose: it granted $351 million in 2023, to recipients including the State Policy Network, the Federalist Society, Cato, Reason, Heritage and America First Legal, and its chief executive has said its aim is "to preserve donor intent for certain types of givers." [cite:de14_dg_donorstrust]

The Thiel Foundation has made grants to DonorsTrust. Listings compiled from the foundation's filings show $1.4 million in 2022, $1,276,111 in 2024 and $426,775 in 2025; an earlier tally put the total for 2021 to 2024 at about $7.3 million, but the listings available do not cover every year, and that total could not be confirmed against them in full. [cite:de14_granted_thiel] [cite:de14_kindora_thiel] What can be said with confidence is that at least about $3.1 million has passed from Thiel's foundation into DonorsTrust since 2022. What cannot be said is where any of it went next. Because the grants leave the foundation's books under DonorsTrust's name, no public record links a dollar from Thiel to any end recipient. This is the single largest opacity point in the network's documented money.

The opacity cuts both ways, and the book applies it both ways. It means that no one can show the money went to a cause the Dark Enlightenment's critics would object to; it also means no one can show that it did not. A frequently drawn line runs from Thiel through DonorsTrust to the National Conservatism conferences, because DonorsTrust funds many groups in that world. That line is not documented. A shared intermediary is not a flow, and no grant from the Thiel Foundation to the Edmund Burke Foundation, which organises those conferences, appears in the listings examined. [cite:de14_kindora_thiel] The honest description is the one already given in [scene:de14_loops|the loops section]: same pipe, unknown water.

The nonprofit with no donor list

Per Aspera Policy is a nonprofit that Thiel controlled and that does not disclose its donors. It sent at least $200,000 to Protect Ohio Values, the pro-Vance super PAC, in the 2022 race. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman] The super PAC had to report Per Aspera's contribution; Per Aspera did not have to report where its own money came from. The amount is small beside Thiel's $15 million in direct, disclosed giving to the same committee, and it is not evidence of hidden donors so much as a demonstration of the mechanism: a political committee may be funded in part by an entity whose own funders are not public. [cite:de14_cbs_klaidman]

The group with no public face

The Rockbridge Network goes further. It grew out of a weekend summit Thiel and Vance convened in 2019 near Rockbridge, Ohio, attended by, among others, Rebekah Mercer, Tucker Carlson and the economist Oren Cass; it is now led by the publisher Chris Buskirk, whose magazine American Greatness Thiel had earlier funded. [cite:de14_wapo_rockbridge] The Washington Post reported in November 2025 that the group "has no website or public-facing entity"; that David Sacks and Marc Andreessen are members; that it runs pollsters, data analysts and a documentary arm; and that its affiliated super PAC, Turnout for America, spent $34.5 million in 2024. [cite:de14_wapo_rockbridge] The super PAC's spending is public because federal law requires it; the network's own membership, dues and budget are not, and the Post's account rests on its own reporting rather than on any filing. Thiel declined to comment. [cite:de14_wapo_rockbridge]

None of these vehicles is unusual. Private foundations, donor-advised funds, undisclosed nonprofits and private donor networks are used across American politics, by donors of every persuasion. What the network's use of them establishes is narrower: that some of the money around Thiel and Vance cannot be traced to its end, and that claims about where it went, in either direction, go beyond the record.
