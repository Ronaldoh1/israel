This part takes up the oldest question behind Book Five: whether the American state has ever turned its surveillance on its own lawful political life, and what stopped it when it did. The first section reads the record of the decades before the 1975 reforms, programme by programme, from the National Security Agency's copying of international telegrams and its watch list of Americans to the FBI's COINTELPRO, the CIA's Operation CHAOS, the Army's files on protesters and the tax agency's lists of activist groups, and it follows the pattern into the twenty-first century. The second lists what was exposed between 2013 and 2017, from bulk phone records and Section 702 to the CIA's hacking tools, and gives each programme's status as of October 2026, including what lapsed, what continues and what has been replaced by buying data. The third sets those who disclosed the programmes beside the officials who misdescribed them, gives both sides of the argument over the messengers, and closes by testing the book's worst-case reading against this part's record.
