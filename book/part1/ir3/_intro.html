Part Three turns to Iran's Jewish community, present in the country since the Persian empire and the subject of a question often put as a paradox: how Israel and the United States can be at war with a state whose constitution recognises its Jews and reserves them a seat in parliament. It sets out two records kept by the same state. The first is the community itself: its history, its numbers by census and by estimate, including the claim that it exceeds 20,000, its constitutional standing and representative, Khomeini's assurance of 1979 in the wordings that survive, and what its leaders said in public during the wars of 2025 and 2026, and under what constraints. The second is the record of execution, espionage trials, legal limits, state Holocaust denial and wartime questioning, together with the government's stated distinction between Jews and Zionists and the answers of its critics. It ends with what both records do and do not say about the war.
