Part Twelve examined abuse and how it was handled. Part Thirteen turns to the question of reach: how far the institutions that carry the name Catholic extend, and which of them the Holy See actually directs. It counts the schools, universities, hospitals and charities reported in the Church's own statistics, by continent; it tests the competing figures for Jesuit universities, including the claim of 228, against the Jesuit networks' own count; it builds a ledger of 33 leading American universities by the body that founded them, with the University of Virginia, founded without a professor of divinity, as the contrast case; and it ends with the instrument through which Rome's reach is most direct and best documented, the appointment of bishops through nuncios, including the agreement with China, followed by the dialogues that link the Holy See to other churches without giving it authority over them.
