The Upheaval and the Peace, 1973–1982

On 6 October 1973, the Jewish Day of Atonement, Egypt and Syria attacked Israeli positions in Sinai and the Golan. Israel was caught unprepared, recovered and pushed back on both fronts before a ceasefire; the American airlift of arms during the war began the era of large-scale aid described in Aid and Arms. Settlement as policy

Settlement in the occupied territories began under Labor governments after 1967, at first in strategic areas under plans associated with Yigal Allon; by 1977 about 4,500 Israelis lived in West Bank settlements. The religious-nationalist movement Gush Emunim, founded in 1974, pressed for settlement across the whole West Bank. [cite:mas_settlements_history] In May 1977 the Likud under Menachem Begin, a former commander of the Irgun, ended Labor's unbroken rule since 1948, and settlement expanded sharply, reaching tens of thousands within a decade. [cite:mas_settlements_history] [cite:wiki_menachem_begin] The West Bank, Documented follows the enterprise.

Peace with Egypt

The same government made the first peace. Egypt's president, Anwar Sadat, flew to Jerusalem in November 1977; at Camp David in September 1978, with President Carter, Begin and Sadat agreed two frameworks, and on 26 March 1979 they signed a peace treaty under which Israel returned the whole of Sinai by 1982. [cite:wiki_camp_david_accords] [cite:camp_david_1979_treaty] Israel removed its Sinai settlements, including the town of Yamit. [cite:wiki_yamit] The second framework, on Palestinian autonomy in the West Bank and Gaza, was never implemented. Sadat was assassinated by Islamist officers on 6 October 1981. [cite:wiki_anwar_sadat] The peace has held.
