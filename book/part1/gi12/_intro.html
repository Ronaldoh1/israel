Part Twelve sets out the record that runs against the expansion thesis, with the same care given to the record that supports it. It begins with the withdrawals: Sinai and Gaza in 1957, the disengagements of 1974 and 1975 on the Suez and Golan fronts, the return of all of Sinai and the evacuation of Yamit in 1982, south Lebanon in 2000, and Gaza with four northern West Bank settlements in 2005. Alongside them are the peace treaties with Egypt and Jordan, the Oslo agreements and two large offers that were never concluded. It then gives the critics' reply, which holds that most of these withdrawals were forced or traded and that some have been reversed. The second section turns to the Israelis who have opposed expansion: the reserve officers who founded Peace Now, the monitors and petitioners, former security chiefs, politicians and the polls. It also sets out what Israel's governments have said when accused of seeking a Greater Israel, and what those denials leave open. The third section reads the Supreme Court's rulings, from Elon Moreh in 1979 to the striking down of the settlement Regulation Law in 2020, and the limits of what the court has been willing to decide.
