Rome's first attempt came through an emperor's conversion: Susenyos acknowledged Roman primacy on 11 February 1626, the Jesuit patriarch Mendes condemned the Ethiopian Sabbath and fasts, and revolt forced the edict's repeal in 1632 and Mendes's exile in 1634. [cite:vt27_wiki_mendes] The second came with an army: the Italian church favoured the invasion, Graziani ordered "all monks without distinction" killed at Debre Libanos in May 1937, and the obelisk of Aksum stood in Rome until 2005. [cite:et_cc_debrelibanos] [cite:et_lrb_holywar] [cite:et_wiki_obelisk] Neither attempt targeted the books of the canon; both targeted the people and the institution that kept them. Part Six follows a British expedition that took the books themselves.
