Revenue Collected by Someone Else

The Paris Protocol. The economic agreement signed at Paris in 1994, as part of the Oslo arrangements, left the West Bank and Gaza inside Israel's customs system. Israel collects the tariffs, purchase taxes and import VAT on goods bound for the Palestinian territories and transfers them, minus deductions, to the Palestinian Authority. These "clearance revenues" make up about two-thirds of the Authority's public revenue. [cite:wb_shabaka_clearance] [cite:wb_mas_jun2026]

The withholdings. Israel has suspended the transfers before: after Hamas won the 2006 election, in December 2012 after Palestine gained upgraded status at the UN, and in 2015 after it joined the International Criminal Court. [cite:wb_shabaka_clearance] In July 2018 the Knesset voted, 87 to 15, to deduct from the transfers the amount the Authority pays to Palestinian prisoners and the families of those killed by Israeli forces, about $330 million that year, supporting some 35,000 families. Israel's case is that the payments reward and encourage violence; the Palestinian official Hanan Ashrawi called the deduction "highway robbery." [cite:wb_aj_payslaw2018] Those deductions came to 3.54 billion shekels between 2019 and mid-2024. [cite:wb_shabaka_clearance]

After 7 October 2023 the security cabinet withheld the part of the revenue the Authority spent in Gaza, to prevent payments reaching Hamas; Norway agreed to hold that share in an account of its own so that the rest could flow. Norway's prime minister said the money was "necessary to ensure that the Palestinian Authority does not collapse." [cite:wb_norway_2024] From June 2025 Israel withheld the transfers entirely. [cite:wb_mas_jun2026]

By the Palestine Economic Policy Research Institute's count, Israel withheld 11.7 billion shekels and deducted another 12.5 billion between January 2021 and March 2026, together 44 percent of the clearance revenue due. In June 2026 the Authority paid half of its public servants' February salaries. Its debts and unpaid bills together reached about $12.4 billion, 72 percent of the 2025 economy. The Cost of Area C

In 2013 the World Bank estimated that if Palestinians could use the land and resources of Area C, the Palestinian economy could gain $3.4 billion a year, about 35 percent of its 2011 output, and the Authority's deficit could be halved. Israel's Foreign Ministry answered that the issues belonged in negotiations, that Palestinian economic development was in Israel's strategic interest, and that Israel had approved more than 300 projects in Area C in the previous two years.
