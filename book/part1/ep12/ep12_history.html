Before the Paper Trail

People ask whether this has happened in earlier civilisations. The honest answer has two parts. The sexual exploitation of the young by the powerful is recorded in many periods. But documented, hidden networks protected by institutions are well recorded only from the nineteenth century on, when newspapers, courts and public inquiries began leaving a paper trail. Before that, most of the evidence is hostile gossip written long after the fact, or confessions obtained under threat, and the book does not stretch it further than it goes.

The cleanest early case is from London. In July 1885 the editor W. T. Stead published "The Maiden Tribute of Modern Babylon" in the Pall Mall Gazette, exposing a trade in girls sold to wealthy men; to prove how easy it was, he bought a thirteen-year-old girl, Eliza Armstrong. Within weeks Parliament passed the Criminal Law Amendment Act, raising the age of consent from 13 to 16. Stead was then convicted over how he had obtained the girl and served three months in prison. [cite:ep12_spartacus_stead] Elite demand, a supply chain, a press exposure, a change in the law, and the messenger punished: the shape of the story was already familiar 140 years ago.
