The Families and the Chair, 1012–1511

Once the bishop of Rome ruled a state, his office became the most valuable prize in Italy, and the great families of the Roman countryside competed for it as they competed for any other lordship. For long stretches of the Middle Ages the question of who guarded the canon of scripture was answered not by councils but by the outcome of feuds between baronial clans. The record is detailed enough to name them, and to show what each gained.

The clearest early case is the family of the counts of Tusculum, in the hills south-east of Rome. Theophylact, son of Count Alberic III, was born around 1012. Two of his uncles had already been popes, as Benedict VIII and John XIX, and a great-uncle had been John XII. In October 1032, at about the age of twenty, he became pope himself as Benedict IX; according to his Wikipedia biography, his father had bought the chair for him by bribing the Romans. [cite:cn_wiki_benedict9] In 1045 Benedict offered to give up the papacy to his godfather, John Gratian, if Gratian reimbursed his election expenses. Gratian paid and was recognised as Gregory VI. [cite:cn_wiki_benedict9] In December 1046 the Council of Sutri deposed Benedict and another claimant and urged Gregory to resign, because the arrangement was simony, the purchase of a sacred office. Benedict did not accept it and returned briefly as pope in 1047 and 1048, the only man to hold the office three times. He is also, his biography notes, "the only person ever accused of selling the papacy". [cite:cn_wiki_benedict9]

The Tusculan case is extreme, but it was not an aberration in kind. In the following centuries the Orsini, the Colonna and the Caetani, three baronial families with castles and lands across Lazio, fought for the chair and the cardinals' hats around it. Giovanni Gaetani Orsini, son of Matteo Rosso Orsini and of Perna of the Gaetani family, was elected pope at Viterbo on 25 November 1277 as Nicholas III, and reigned until 22 August 1280; it was he who established the papal residence at the Vatican. [cite:vt7_na_nicholas3] Within a generation Dante placed him in hell among the simonists, upside down in a hole of rock. In the poem the pope identifies himself as "of a she-bear" the son, the bear being the Orsini emblem, and says he was "so eager to advance my whelps"; he mistakes the poet for Boniface VIII, whom he is expecting to join him. [cite:cn_dante_inf19] Dante's verdict is a poet's, written by an enemy of the papal party. It records what a contemporary thought the papacy had become: a means of enriching a family.

Boniface VIII, the pope Dante's Nicholas was waiting for, was Benedetto Caetani of Anagni, of a separate baronial family. [cite:vt7_wiki_boniface8] His feud with the Colonna ended in 1303 at Anagni, where Sciarra Colonna took part in the assault on the pope in his own town. [cite:vt7_wiki_colonna] Two years later the conclave at Perugia, deadlocked for about eleven months, ended on 5 June 1305 with the election of a Frenchman, Bertrand de Got, as Clement V, the pope who settled at Avignon. The decisive backing came from Cardinal Napoleone Orsini at the head of a pro-French bloc. The opposing bloc was led by another Orsini, Cardinal Matteo Orsini Rosso, together with Boniface VIII's nephew Francesco Caetani. [cite:vt7_wiki_conclave1305] The families did not act as one. They were rivals, often within themselves, and each pursued its own advantage through the office.

The office paid in several currencies. It paid in land, through the Papal States. It paid in income from the whole of Western Christendom: from 1305 Clement V claimed the first year's revenue of vacant benefices in England, and in 1319 John XXII claimed the first fruits of benefices across Christendom for two years, a levy known as annates. [cite:vt31_wiki_annates] And it paid in appointments, because a pope could make his relatives cardinals, and a cardinal could become pope. The Colonna had their own pope in Martin V, elected on 11 November 1417, whose election ended the Western Schism. [cite:vt7_audit_brit_martin5] In 1511 Julius II's bull Pax Romana settled the long feud between the Colonna and the Orsini. The court office of Prince Assistant to the Papal Throne is believed to date from the same year, and it came to belong by hereditary right to the heads of the two families: an honour of rank beside the pope, not a share in his decisions, that lasted until the twentieth century. [cite:vt7_wiki_colonna] [cite:vt7_laj_pa]

This is where a careful reader must separate the record from the legend that has grown up around it. The record shows Roman families competing for the papacy and profiting from it for centuries. It does not show any one family controlling the institution across time. The last Orsini pope, Benedict XIII, died in 1730, and no member of the family has been elected since. [cite:vt7_wiki_orsini] The court offices the families held became ceremonial, and a pope could remove their holders, as Pius XII removed the head of the Orsini from his in 1958. [cite:vt7_laj_pa] The full test of the Orsini claims is in [scene:vt7_orsini_record|The Vatican Architecture, Part Seven]. What matters for the canon is narrower and well established. Through the centuries in which the Latin Bible was the only Bible most Western Christians could hear, the office that guarded it was repeatedly bought, inherited and fought over by families whose interest in it was land, revenue and rank.
