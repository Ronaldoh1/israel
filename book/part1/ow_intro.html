Most of what ties the American state to Israel is not secret. It sits in public laws, signed memoranda, contract notices, appropriations tables and court records, laid down one administration at a time since 1948. Because it was built slowly and in pieces, it is rarely seen whole. This volume puts the pieces together: the laws and the votes that created them; the Central Intelligence Agency's liaison with the Mossad; the National Security Agency's sharing of raw intercepts, including Americans' communications, with Israel's Unit 8200; the FBI's spy cases; Homeland Security, ICE and the police exchanges; the Pentagon and each of the armed services; the pipeline that moves American weapons; Palantir and the data contractors; the Israeli-founded spyware and security firms selling to American agencies; the legal shields in American law; what is and is not documented about Jeffrey Epstein; and, at the end, what the record shows about the asymmetry the title names. The title comes from a question: the United States hands Israel raw intelligence on its own citizens, so why can it not see what Israel does with American weapons and data in Gaza and the West Bank? The volume tests that question rather than assuming the answer. It records what the United States gets back, from missile-defence technology to intelligence on its adversaries, and gives the stated purpose of both governments beside each critical finding. Every connection in it is a documented relationship, a law, a contract, a memorandum, an appointment or a court finding, never an inference from anyone's identity. Where the record is contested, both sides are shown; where it is silent, the volume says so. It draws on the Israel Architecture simulation's agency chapters, the Sophia research files and the earlier Israel books, and corrects the errors it found in them. It is a companion to Follow the Money, which maps the lobby and the campaign money behind the votes. [scene:ow1_founding|Part One: the legal spine]
