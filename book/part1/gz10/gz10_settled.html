What the Record Settles

The strip was made by war. The Gaza Strip was the remainder of a Mandate district, about 1.3 percent of Palestine, left in Egyptian hands by the fighting of 1948 and fixed by the armistice of 1949. Its population was transformed within a year: about 80,000 residents were joined by more than 200,000 refugees from the towns and villages Israel took, and the camps built for them still stand. [cite:gz_wiki_subdistrict] [cite:gz_palquest_refugees]

It has been governed from outside for most of its history. Egypt administered it without annexing it until 1967; [cite:gz_wiki_history] Israel occupied it from 1967, took a census, put down an insurgency, built settlements and controlled who could work in Israel. [cite:gz_jvl_census67] [cite:gz_wiki_insurgency] [cite:gz_wiki_occupation] [cite:gz_carnegie_labor] Oslo brought a Palestinian Authority in 1994, while Israel kept the settlements and the crossings; in 2005 Israel removed its settlers and soldiers but kept control of its airspace, territorial waters, population registry and six of its seven land crossings. [cite:gz_wiki_gazajericho] [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement]

The closure was a system, and its effects were measured. From 2007 Israel and Egypt restricted goods, exports, people, fishing and farming; Israel said the closure was to stop weapons smuggling and rocket fire after Hamas took control. The UN recorded an economy shrinking while the West Bank's grew, 96 percent of the groundwater unfit to drink, from over-extraction and contamination, and 1.2 million people needing aid. [cite:gz_un_hostile2007] [cite:gz_wiki_blockade] [cite:gz_un_gaza10]

October 7 was a planned attack on civilians. Armed groups led by Hamas killed about 1,200 people, most of them civilians, and took 251 hostages, in an attack in which, Human Rights Watch found, killing civilians and taking hostages were central aims, and whose plan Israel had held for more than a year and judged beyond Hamas's reach. Hamas said its fighters targeted only soldiers and armed people. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7] [cite:gz_nyt_jericho] [cite:gz_aj_narrative]

The war that followed was the most destructive in the strip's history. By October 2026 the health ministry counted 74,382 Palestinians killed; about 81 percent of the strip's structures had been damaged; the IPC found famine in Gaza City in August 2025, a finding Israel rejected.
