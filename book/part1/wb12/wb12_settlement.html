Who Pays for Settlement

The state. Settlement is financed through several lines of Israel's budget. Peace Now's reading of the government's July 2026 budget requests found 335.4 million shekels for the Ministry of Settlements, 294.5 million of a defense emergency line earmarked for settlements, and 125 million for security roads, part of 550 million approved in January 2026 for 2026 and 2027; more than 220 kilometres of new roads had opened in 2024 and 2025. The farms and outposts described in Part Eight, which are unauthorized under Israeli law, received 103 million shekels from 2023 to 2025 for "security components" such as vehicles, drones, cameras, gates and fences; the legal basis given is that this is security equipment, not permanent construction. American donors. A Haaretz investigation in 2015 found that about fifty American organizations had raised $281 million between 2009 and 2013 and sent $224 million of it to settlements through Israeli nonprofits, as tax-deductible gifts. The money paid for property and amenities, and some of it, through the group Honenu, supported the families of Jews convicted of terrorism, among them the family of Ami Popper, who killed seven Palestinian labourers in 1990. A White House official said the administration did not support activity that would legitimize the settlements. [cite:wb_toi_220m]

Businesses. In September 2025 the UN Human Rights Office updated its database of businesses involved in settlement activities to 158 companies from eleven countries, most of them Israeli, others based in the United States, Britain, Germany, France, the Netherlands, Spain, Portugal, Luxembourg, Canada and China. The listed activities include supplying equipment and materials for settlement construction, demolishing Palestinian property, surveillance, and the use of natural resources. [cite:wb_un_database2025]
