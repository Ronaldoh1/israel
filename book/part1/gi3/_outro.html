Part Three established that the armistice agreements of 1949 each denied that their lines were borders, and that Israel's governments nonetheless sought to make them permanent frontiers, with Israel giving back 13 Lebanese villages under the Lebanon agreement. [cite:gi3_un_syrarm] [cite:rs_rd6_arm_egypt_text] [cite:gi3_wiki_armistice] The claim to both banks of the Jordan was the programme of Herut, an opposition party with 14 of 120 seats in 1949, and not of any government; on the Syrian front the documented pattern was pressure on the demilitarized zones, not a campaign to move the line. [cite:gi3_jvl_herut] [cite:gi3_wiki_herut] [cite:gi3_frus_dayan1953] The Sinai campaign of 1956 showed wider aims stated at the top: the Sèvres eighth point on eastern Sinai, Ben-Gurion's "fantastic" plan reaching to the Litani, a letter to the troops that, as reported, placed Tiran in a "Third Kingdom of Israel," and a government decision to hold Gaza. None of these was kept. The phrase is not in the Knesset speech of 7 November, Ben-Gurion wrote on 8 November that Israel had "never planned to annex the Sinai desert," and under United Nations and American pressure Israel left Gaza on 8 March 1957 and Sinai the same month.
