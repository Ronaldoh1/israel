The Mother on Tepeyac

On the northern edge of Mexico City stands the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe; on 11 and 12 December 2009 alone, 6.1 million pilgrims passed through it. [cite:vt36_wiki_olg] The story, as the shrine tells it, is that in December 1531 a young woman appeared four times on the hill of Tepeyac to a Nahua man named Juan Diego and asked for a church; when he opened his cloak before the bishop, roses fell from it and her image was found printed on the cloth. [cite:vt36_wiki_olg]

The earliest statement linking the hill to an older goddess comes from a hostile witness inside the Church. In 1576 the Franciscan Bernardino de Sahagún wrote that the Nahua had there "a temple dedicated to the mother of the gods, whom they called Tonantzin, which means Our Mother", and that people came from more than twenty leagues away. Now preachers were calling the Virgin "Tonantzin" too. This, he wrote, "appears to be a satanic device to conceal idolatry". [cite:vt36_sahagun1576] His complaint was about a word. "Tonantzin" was a title, "Our Mother", used for several goddesses connected with the earth and maize; the theologian Rosemary Radford Ruether doubted that a single goddess of that name had a temple there at all. [cite:vt36_wiki_tonantzin]

The documents appear late and in a disputed order. The bishop's surviving writings do not mention the apparitions; in 1556 a Franciscan preached against the devotion, saying the image had been painted by an Indian named Marcos; the full narrative first appears in print in 1648 and 1649, and whether an older Nahuatl original lies behind it is unsettled. [cite:vt36_wiki_olg] [cite:vt36_wiki_huei] The historian Stafford Poole read the silences as decisive; devotees and many Mexican scholars read the record differently. [cite:vt36_enc_poole]

The image itself carries two readings, and both are serious. One reads the sunburst, the crescent moon underfoot and the starred mantle as the woman "clothed with the sun" of Revelation, the European image of the Immaculate Conception. The other reads it as a Nahua text in images: the blue-green mantle as the colour of the divine couple, the dark sash as pregnancy, and a small four-petalled flower over the womb as the Nahua sign of the cosmos. Historians who trace the image to European models treat the second as a later interpretation. [cite:vt36_ci_immac] [cite:vt36_wiki_olg] [cite:vt36_exp_signos]

What the record shows is a devotion the institution did not erase but took up. It built the mother of Tepeyac a basilica, made her patroness of a nation and made her visionary a saint in 2002, praising Guadalupe as a model of a faith that entered Indigenous culture. [cite:vt36_va_jd2002] Its own friar's complaint, that an older mother's name was being carried into the Virgin's, stays in the record beside it.
