Part Three covers the years when Israel's borders were armistice lines, from the agreements of 1949 to the eve of the June 1967 war. It reads what the four armistice agreements said about borders, what Israel's governments said they wanted, and which named party, Herut, kept the claim to both banks of the Jordan alive in opposition. It then takes the one occasion in those years when Israel held a large area beyond the lines, the Sinai campaign of 1956, as a test. It sets out what Ben-Gurion proposed at Sèvres, checks where the words "Third Kingdom of Israel" were actually said, and follows the government from victory to its acceptance of withdrawal and, under American pressure, to its departure from Gaza and Sinai in March 1957.
