# Volume II, Stage Six (1948–1950s): research notes

Verified 2026-10-09. Every claim in `rd6.json` comes from a page fetched in this session. WebFetch returns a model-written summary of each page, not raw text, so quoted phrases are short; check any quotation against the original page before publication. Several primary pages (Avalon, some UN summary records) were blocked or truncated; where that happened, the gap is listed below.

## 1. The 1949 armistice agreements
**Agree:** The texts (Egypt, 24 February; Jordan, 3 April) state that the lines are military, not political or territorial boundaries, and that nothing prejudices the final settlement. Syria's agreement (20 July) completed the Rhodes talks (Nobel Prize biography). Bunche's 21 July 1949 letter calls them voluntary agreements amounting to non-aggression pacts, and notes that the Jordan agreement covered the Iraqi front. Israel's Foreign Ministry puts the area inside the lines at "about 78%" of the Mandate.

**Disagree:** None among the fetched sources. The 56% partition share comes from the simulation's dashboard (prestate_funding), not from this research.

**Not verified:** The Lebanon (23 March) and Syria agreement texts were not fetched; the Lebanon date comes from ilb_s02. The Avalon texts were blocked.

## 2. Bernadotte's mediation
**Agree:** Appointed 20 May 1948; truces of 11 June–9 July and from 15 July; the 28 June suggestions (FRUS text) and the 16 September report (JVL and Palestine Studies excerpts); both parties "flatly rejected" the June suggestions; killed in Jerusalem on 17 September 1948 with Colonel Sérot.

**Disagree:** The UN press release says the five permanent members appointed him as a committee of the General Assembly; Britannica says the Security Council. Britannica names the "Stern Gang"; the UN release does not name the killers.

**Not verified:** Any document on the immediate Arab and Israeli official responses to the September report. The FRUS editorial apparatus on Marshall's endorsement was not fetched (tr_s02 already cites it).

## 3. Resolution 194 and the refugee count
**Agree:** Text from Refworld; vote 35–15–8 (ECF). Morris's appendix gives the spread of contemporary estimates.

**Disagree:** The refugee total: 520,000–530,000 (Israel, public), 711,000 (UNCCP, cited in ud_s01), 726,000 (UN survey/UNRWA), "close to 800,000" (Eytan, private), 810,000 (British, 1949), 900,000–1 million (Arab officials). The UNCCP's 1951 property estimate worked from 900,000 as an approximation. Village counts: 369 (Morris), 418 (Khalidi), 531 (Palestine Land Society, which counts towns and tribal lands).

**Not verified:** Morris's own preferred figure (paywalled). Which states voted against Resolution 194.

## 4. UN admission and Jerusalem
**Agree:** Resolution 273 text (UNISPAL); vote 37–12–9 (ECF). The Ad Hoc Political Committee put Jerusalem, refugees and the Bernadotte investigation to Israel on 6 May 1949.

**Disagree:** None found.

**Not verified:** Eban's actual statements (the summary records fetched were truncated). The Jerusalem chronology (Resolution 303, 9 December 1949; Knesset vote 60–2 on 23 January 1950; no embassy in West Jerusalem until 1967) rests on one source, the German Federal Agency for Civic Education, and is marked "reported".

## 5. Barring return and the infiltration years
**Agree:** The 16 June 1948 cabinet decision (Hertzberg on Morris). Morris's Border Wars figures as reported by Shlaim: 90%+ economic or social motives, 2,700–5,000 infiltrators killed, Qibya (45 houses, 69 dead).

**Disagree:** Whether infiltration was mainly a refugee phenomenon (Morris/Shlaim) or a security campaign (the Jewish Virtual Library's account of the 1955 fedayeen). Both are presented; they describe partly different periods.

**Not verified:** Israeli casualties from infiltration, 1949–56. No source acceptable under the brief was found (the candidates were Wikipedia lists and a newspaper opinion piece).

## 6. The land regime, 1948–1953
**Agree:** Fischbach (scholarship) for the Custodian of Abandoned Property (15 July 1948), the December 1948 regulations and the 14 March 1950 law. The Development Authority dates to 1950 (Trachtenberg, Kedar and Shmueli).

**Disagree:** The regulations' date: 2 December (Fischbach) vs 12 December (a US document Fischbach quotes).

**Not verified:** The volumes and prices of the 1949 and 1950 JNF purchases. The figures of 2.4 million dunams transferred and 92% state-held by 1951 come from BADIL, and 1.2–1.3 million dunams under the 1953 law from Adalah. Both are advocacy organizations, so these are marked "reported" and must be attributed.

## 7. Military government and the Arab citizens
**Agree:** Korn (Adalah Review, scholarship): the government was established in October 1948; Regulation 125; the 1949 Security Zones regulations; conviction statistics; the Rattner Commission (1956); abolished 1 December 1966. Akevot holds the Toledano letter (primary). The population figures are CBS numbers as compiled by the JVL. The IDI gives the Democratic List of Nazareth's 1949 result.

**Disagree:** What the regime was for: security (the Rattner Commission) or control of movement and land (Korn).

**Not verified:** The number of Arab citizens placed under military government, and the total of Arab members in the first Knesset.

## 8. Law of Return and Nationality Law
**Agree:** The statutory texts (MJL; Adalah's database; Refworld for the Nationality Law). Ben-Gurion's words come from the Encyclopaedia Judaica entry.

**Disagree:** The passage date: 5 July 1950 (the law's own date, and the Encyclopedia of the Modern Middle East) vs 3 July (Encyclopaedia Judaica; Kassim says the bills were submitted on 3 July). The law's meaning: a right of return (Ben-Gurion) or legal discrimination (Kassim, a Palestinian jurist; marked "reported").

**Not verified:** How many Arab residents failed the 1952 residence test. Kassim's "stateless for about thirty years" is his claim and is attributed to him.

## 9. Mass immigration
**Agree:** Jewish Agency figures: 203,000 in the first year, 684,201 by the end of 1951, the ma'abarot at 113 camps holding 250,000 people (May 1952). The State Archives table via the JVL gives 738,891 by the end of 1952. The NLI covers austerity.

**Disagree:** Country totals differ by source and period: Iraq 121,512 (JA) vs 124,226 (Archives table); Yemen "nearly all of 45,000" (JA) vs 48,375 for Yemen and Aden (table) vs about 49,000 (ye_s01).

**Not verified:** The date austerity was declared, and the minister in charge.

## 10. Jews of the Arab world
**Agree:** The Oujda and Jérada riots of 7–8 June 1948 (5 and 38 killed). Cairo 1948: over 70 killed (Sachar via the JVL). Egypt 1956: almost 25,000 expelled (contemporary wire reports via the JVL). Mendes's "push and pull" synthesis.

**Disagree:** The causes of the exodus, in three framings, presented as such. The 1950–51 Baghdad bombings remain disputed (irq_e05).

**Not verified:** The Libyan riots of 1948; Syria's Jews after 1947. Not researched.

## 11. Arab Palestine, 1948–1951
**Agree:** Shlaim (Journal of Palestine Studies, 1990) for the All-Palestine Government, the National Council (30 September 1948, not 1 October), the recognitions and the Jericho Congress (1 December 1948). The Library of Congress study for the kingdom's renaming, the April 1950 elections, refugee numbers and Abdullah's assassination. Kramer for the wording of the 24 April 1950 resolution. A British Cabinet record of 25 April 1950.

**Disagree:** Who recognized the annexation (see the blind-spot file).

**Not verified:** The full British Cabinet text (the excerpt gives only its gist), and whether Britain's recognition excluded Jerusalem (claimed in ijr_src_s05, not confirmed here).

## 12. Financing, 1949–1953
**Agree:** The CRS aid series via the JVL. Israel Bonds via the JVL ($52 million in the first year). Reparations: Sharett's claim of 12 March 1951, the Knesset vote of 61–50 on 9 January 1952, the signing on 10 September 1952, in force 21 March 1953.

**Disagree:** The agreement's size ($845 million, JVL; 3.5 billion marks plus 450 million, NLI). The start of US military aid (1959 in the CRS table vs 1962 in iaa_s01).

**Not verified:** The Bundestag ratification date. The 3 billion DM / 450 million DM split often quoted elsewhere was not found on any fetched page.

## 13. The deferred constitution
**Agree:** Dickerman (2007): the 1 October 1948 deadline, the 14 February 1949 convening, the Harari Resolution of 13 June 1950.

**Not verified:** The vote count on the Harari Resolution, and the exact date of the Transition Law.
