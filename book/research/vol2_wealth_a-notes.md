# Volume II, Rothschild wealth (private by design): research notes

Verified 2026-10-17. Every claim in `vol2_wealth_a.json` comes from a page fetched or a search result seen in this session. WebFetch returns a model-written summary of each page, not the raw text, so quoted phrases are short and approximate. Check any quotation that will be printed against the original page before publication. Ferguson's figures are known here only through reviews (Skidelsky, Steinberg/LRB) and Wikipedia's citation of *The Ascent of Money* (2008, p. 88), not from the books themselves. Conspiracy sites were skipped. A full-text copy of Ferguson vol. 2 turned up in search results on a piracy site (erenow); it was not used.

## 1. Partnership and the 1812 will (`wa_partnership`)
**Agree:** The Archive's summary of the 1810 agreement (27 September 1810) is detailed and primary: capital in gulden per partner; profit shares out of 50; heirs barred from seeing the books and paid in company stock; private arbitration; partners male family members only, so sons-in-law could not take control. History Today and a 2022 review of Livingstone agree the will left the business to the sons only and excluded daughters and their families.
**Disagree:** None found on the core provisions. The Archive's 1810 page says Nathan was not a party (he was in London). Popular accounts often treat all five sons as 1810 partners.
**Not verified:** The terms of the later agreements (1815, 1818, 1825, 1836). The Archive only lists 'Family partnership agreements, 1815-1862' as holdings and notes Lionel's 1838 change. The well-known will provisions (no public inventory of the estate, no lawsuits, the eldest son of the eldest son as head) were found in no reliable source. Treat them as lore until checked in Ferguson vol. 1 or Elon's *Founder*.

## 2. Letters and communication (`wa_letters`)
**Agree:** Judendeutsch is German in Hebrew script. The Archive calls it 'not in itself a code' but hard to read. There are about 20,000 letters (1811-1868) in 142 boxes. Transcription began in 1989. 'Breaking the seal' records an unexplained 1818-1825 gap. The Archive was established in 1978.
**Disagree:** None.
**Not verified:** Any specific code name. The Archive mentions 'private code names' but gives no examples, and no other reliable source was found. When the Archive first opened to outside researchers is also unverified (only its 1978 founding is confirmed). The colour-coded envelopes in Spear's are unsourced. Do not use them.

## 3. Capital figures (`wa_capital`)
**Agree:** The series attributable to Ferguson: £1.77m (1818; *Ascent* rounds it to £1.8m); about 9x Barings in 1825; £7.78m in 1844, 10x Barings; £9.5m in 1852; £22m in 1862; £34m in 1874; £41m in 1899, more than the five biggest German joint-stock banks together. Nathan at death was worth 0.62% of British national income; James at death just over 4% of French GDP.
**Disagree:** Wording only. *Ascent* compares 1825 with both Barings and the Banque de France; Steinberg compares 'combined assets' with Barings' capital.
**Not verified:** 1815 and 1828 figures (including the £4.3m often given for 1828). The figures were read from secondary summaries, so check page numbers in Ferguson before printing.

## 4. Incorporation (`wa_incorporation`)
**Agree:** London became N M Rothschild & Sons Limited on 30 September 1970 (Archive, two pages). The first non-family partner came in 1960 and a corporate partner in 1941.
**Disagree:** Skidelsky says 'the partnership system ended in 1905'. That cannot refer to London's legal form. Do not use it without checking Ferguson. Paris became a limited company in 1967 or on 1 January 1968 (both dates appear).
**Not verified:** Whether the 1905 date refers to the end of the inter-house joint partnership.

## 5. Non-cash assets (`wa_assets`)
**Agree:** Mouton was bought in 1853 by Nathaniel (1,175,000 francs per Wikipedia). Lafite was bought on 8 August 1868 for 4,140,000 francs, and James died on 15 November 1868 without visiting. Waddesdon's estate was bought in 1874 (about £200,000), the house built 1874-85 with a 1889 extension, and it was bequeathed by James de Rothschild to the National Trust in 1957. Ferrières was built 1853-59 and given to the University of Paris in 1975. Mentmore was by Paxton (1851) and sold in 1977.
**Disagree:** The brief's '1874-89' for Waddesdon. Waddesdon gives 1874-1885 plus a 1889 extension, and the Archive timeline says it was completed in 1884. The Archive timeline credits Dorothy with the 1957 gift; Waddesdon's own page says James's bequest. Use James's bequest.
**Not verified:** The Mouton price from a Rothschild source (Wikipedia only). The size or value of the art collections.

## 6. Dispersion and loss (`wa_dispersion`)
**Agree:** Naples closed in 1863 (Archive, three pages). Frankfurt was liquidated in 1901 on Wilhelm Carl's death, with no male heir. Vienna closed in March 1938, and Louis was arrested after the Anschluss. Paris was one of 39 banks nationalised and was renamed Compagnie Européenne de Banque.
**Disagree:**
- Paris nationalisation date: the Archive and Wikipedia say 1982 (the law took effect then), not 1981 as in the brief. Mitterrand's programme was 1981. Use '1981-82' or 'nationalised in 1982'.
- Louis's release: the Archive says July 1939 after a $21m payment; JTA (14 May 1939) reports him already in Switzerland. Wikipedia's '$480 million inflation-adjusted' ransom is a different figure framing.
- The sale of S M von Rothschild to Merck Finck (October 1939) rests on a citation-needed Wikipedia line. The Archive says only 'sold and effectively wound up'.
**Not verified:** Probate values for Nathan (1836) and Natty (1915). Only Ferguson's 0.62%-of-national-income figure for Nathan was found.

## 7. 1832 papal loan (`wa_papal`)
**Agree:** £400,000 and a 5% contract with Torlonia (Wikipedia). The Archive confirms Rothschild loans to Rome (five between 1831 and 1850) but not their terms.
**Disagree:** Which brother: Wikipedia names Carl (Naples); Felisini's abstract says the loan was 'in partnership with James de Rothschild'.
**Not verified:** The exclusivity clause. On Wikipedia it rests on Balla, *The Romance of the Rothschilds* (1913), a popular book, not on Ferguson. No second source was found. Felisini's chapter calls the loan's financial conditions controversial, but the abstract does not give them. Record as: only Wikipedia, citing Balla 1913.
