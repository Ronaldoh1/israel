Part Twenty-Seven follows the traveller. Its first section sets out what the state learns when a person flies: the name and birth date checked by TSA's Secure Flight, the passport details and booking records sent to Customs and Border Protection and kept for fifteen years, the faces photographed at checkpoints and borders, the identity rules of REAL ID, and the private biometric lane run by CLEAR; it ends with the first documented join, the passenger lists TSA has passed to ICE since 2025. The second section asks who can already stop a person flying or leaving: the no-fly list and the court cases that opened it, passport denial for tax and child-support debt, the 2025 proposal to revoke passports for "material support" that was withdrawn, and trusted-traveller revocations used as leverage. It compares the American rule, under which a passport is the only exit permit, with the Soviet exit visa, China's exit bans and Russia's draft-linked bans, grades which travel systems could be joined to the rest of the book's map, and tests the worst-case reading.
