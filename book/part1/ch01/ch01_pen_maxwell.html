The Man Who Owned the Books

For three years at the end of the 1980s, one of the largest school-book operations in the United States belonged to a British press baron with a long, documented relationship with the State of Israel. That sentence is a matter of record. What it does and does not prove is the subject of this section, and the two have to be kept apart from the first line to the last.

Robert Maxwell was born Jan Ludvik Hoch on 10 June 1923 in Slatinske Doly, a village then in Czechoslovakia and now in Ukraine. Most of his family was murdered in the Holocaust. He escaped, joined the British Army, won the Military Cross in January 1945 for storming a German machine-gun nest, and took an English name. In 1951 he bought most of a small scientific publisher and renamed it Pergamon Press, which he built into a major academic house. He sat in Parliament as the Labour member for Buckingham from 1964 to 1970. [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell]

His business record carried a warning from early on. In 1969 the American firm Leasco, which had agreed to buy Pergamon, said he had misrepresented the profits of one of its subsidiaries, and he was removed from Pergamon's board. Government inspectors from the Department of Trade and Industry reported in 1971 that he was not, in their opinion, "a person who can be relied on to exercise proper stewardship of a publicly quoted company." He recovered control of Pergamon within a few years and kept buying. [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell]

The empire

In July 1984 he bought Mirror Group Newspapers, publisher of the Daily Mirror and five other British papers, from Reed International for £113 million, and promised editors they would be "free to produce the news without interference." [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell] Four years later he made the purchase that matters most to this part of the book. In late 1988 his Maxwell Communication Corporation paid $2.62 billion for Macmillan Inc., the American publisher. [cite:pen_csm_macmillan1988] The same year he bought the Official Airline Guides and the school-materials house Science Research Associates.

The school business was then combined with a rival's. In May 1989 McGraw-Hill and Macmillan, described in the trade press as "a division of Maxwell Communication Corporation," agreed to merge their elementary and secondary publishing into one company, owned half by each. The two units had taken in $440 million in 1988, and an analyst quoted at the time said the new firm would be second only to Harcourt Brace Jovanovich in the American school market. As part of the deal Maxwell agreed not to build up his stake in McGraw-Hill for fifteen years. [cite:pen_edweek_mmh1989] For the rest of his life, then, the man who owned the Daily Mirror also owned half of one of the two biggest suppliers of textbooks to American classrooms.

Laid out as a structure, the empire had one owner at the top, holdings aimed at three different kinds of reader, and a source of cash underneath that no reader could see. The figure below separates what the record documents about each link from what has only been claimed.

Israel: what is documented, and what is alleged

His public ties to Israel are documented and large. He bought into Israeli business, including the newspaper Ma'ariv; at his death his Israeli holdings were estimated at $300 million. [cite:pen_jta_funeral1991] He was buried on the Mount of Olives in Jerusalem on 10 November 1991 with what the Jewish Telegraphic Agency called "all the pomp of a state funeral." President Chaim Herzog gave the eulogy and said he had "scaled the heights of human endeavor"; Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir and members of the government and opposition attended. [cite:pen_jta_funeral1991] Accounts of the burial drawn on in standard reference works add that at least six serving and former heads of Israeli intelligence were present. [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell]

Beyond that, the claims are serious and contested, and they come from named authors rather than documents:

  • 1948. John Loftus and Mark Aarons, in The Secret War Against the Jews (1997), credit Maxwell's contacts with Czechoslovak communist leaders as crucial to the arms sales that equipped the Haganah, and his smuggling of aircraft parts with helping Israel win control of the air. The standard histories of the Czech arms deal do not name him, so this remains these authors' account. [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell]
  • Intelligence. Seymour Hersh, in The Samson Option (1991), reported allegations from Ari Ben-Menashe, a former employee of Israeli military intelligence, that Maxwell and the Mirror's foreign editor Nicholas Davies had long worked with the Mossad. Ben-Menashe also claimed that Maxwell told the Israeli embassy in London about Mordechai Vanunu, who had leaked details of Israel's nuclear programme in 1986. [cite:hersh_samson] Maxwell called the claims "ludicrous, a total invention." British and Israeli sources quoted by the Washington Post disputed them. [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell]
  • Software. Gordon Thomas and Martin Dillon, in Robert Maxwell, Israel's Superspy (2002), allege that he sold altered versions of the PROMIS case-management software to foreign governments and American laboratories so that Israeli intelligence could read their data. [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell]

Tom Bower's biographies, written from Maxwell's business record, are the main account of the man himself. [cite:bower_maxwell] None of these authors produced a document from an intelligence file, and the agencies concerned have never confirmed any of it. The reader should treat these as reported claims by named writers, of differing reliability, and nothing more.

The death and the money

Maxwell's body was found in the Atlantic near the Canary Islands on 5 November 1991, after he went missing from his yacht, the Lady Ghislaine. A Spanish inquest that December recorded a heart attack combined with accidental drowning; three pathologists could not agree on the cause, and the question has never been fully settled. [cite:pen_wiki_maxwell] Within weeks it emerged that he had taken about £460 million from his employees' pension funds to prop up his companies. His sons Kevin and Ian were later tried for fraud and acquitted. [cite:pen_moneyweek_maxwell] His daughter Ghislaine was convicted in 2021 of sex trafficking for Jeffrey Epstein; that story belongs to another volume, and the library tells it in full in [scene:s16|Robert Maxwell and the press empire].

What the case shows

The case shows a gap, not an operation. It shows that a man described by named biographers as an asset of Israeli intelligence, and described by British government inspectors as unfit to run a public company, could buy half of a leading American school publisher with no review of who he was. No law screened him, because no law screens anyone. Ownership of a textbook house carries no test of loyalty, honesty or interest, and the next section shows that this is still true.

It does not show that Maxwell changed a single sentence in a single textbook. This book searched for such evidence and found none: no memo, no testimony, no edited page. Nor does it show that any Daily Mirror article about Israel was written to order. Those would be separate claims needing their own proof, and nobody has produced it. Keeping the two apart is the whole lesson. The structure allowed influence without leaving any record of whether it was used, and a system that keeps no record cannot tell anyone that it was not.
