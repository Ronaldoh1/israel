Two Wars With Iran, 2025–2026

Twice in eight months the United States went from defending Israel against Iran to attacking Iran itself. This section separates three things the public argument often runs together: advocacy, meaning who argued for war and for how long; decision, meaning who ordered it and on what stated grounds; and consent, meaning what Congress and the public did. It gives the administration's stated reasons beside its critics' analysis and does not resolve the motive question, which the documents do not settle. The 2026 war was not formally over when this was written, so its figures are given as of October 2026.

The advocacy

The case for striking Iran had a long Israeli advocate. A Cato Institute analysis of April 2024 traced Benjamin Netanyahu's record: testimony to Congress in 2002 backing the overthrow of Saddam Hussein; a request, by his own account in his memoir, that President Obama strike Iran's nuclear sites in 2013, which Obama declined; and his 2015 address to Congress against the nuclear agreement. The author, Jon Hoffman, also noted Ehud Olmert's request in 2008 for American approval to bomb Iran, which President Bush refused, and argued that Netanyahu had domestic political reasons to widen the conflict. [cite:hz12_cato_hoffman] That record shows advocacy. It does not by itself show that Israel caused an American decision; each refusal in it was an American choice too.

American intelligence did not support the urgency. In March 2025 the Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, told Congress the intelligence community "continues to assess that Iran is not building a nuclear weapon"; in June 2025 President Trump said "I don't care what she said" and that Iran was "very close" to a bomb. Iran says its programme is peaceful. [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard] [cite:hz11_pbs_gabbard] Part Eleven sets out this record of warnings against assessments. [scene:hz11_pattern|The pattern of threat claims]

June 2025

Israel began striking Iran on 13 June 2025 in what it called Operation Rising Lion. On 21 June, Washington time, the United States joined with Operation Midnight Hammer, striking the enrichment plants at Fordow and Natanz and the nuclear technology centre at Isfahan with B-2 bombers carrying 14 GBU-57 bunker-busting bombs and with 24 Tomahawk missiles. Iran answered by firing at the American base at Al Udeid in Qatar, after notifying the United States in advance; American Patriot batteries fired at least 14 interceptors and no Americans were reported killed. The Costs of War project put the American cost of the June war at $2.04 billion to $2.25 billion. [cite:hz12_cow_2025] Congress was not asked to authorize the strikes. On 27 June the Senate rejected, 47–53, a motion to bring up Senator Tim Kaine's resolution directing the removal of American forces from hostilities against Iran not authorized by Congress; the administration opposed it. [cite:hz12_sen_sjr59]

The decision of February 2026

On 28 February 2026 the United States and Israel opened a joint campaign against Iran, which killed Ayatollah Ali Khamenei in its first strikes. [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] Accounts compiled from news reporting say Netanyahu had lobbied Trump in early 2026 for a joint strike on Iran's leadership, that Israeli intelligence he provided weighed in the decision, and that Trump gave the order on 27 February while nuclear talks with Iran were still under way; these accounts rest on reporting this book could not check against a primary source and are given as reported. [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] The order itself was Trump's, given on his own authority.

The stated reasons were these. Trump said the aim was to defend Americans by eliminating "imminent threats from the Iranian regime", claimed Iran was rebuilding its nuclear programme, and told Iranians, "When we are finished, take over your government." Netanyahu said the aim was to "remove the existential threat posed by the terrorist regime in Iran." [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] Trump's statement that day also cited Iran's support for Hamas and Hezbollah, its killing of protesters and its record of attacks on Americans back to the 1979 hostage crisis. [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] The British government's research service summarized the American position as self-defence, with stated aims of regime change and the destruction of Iran's nuclear and missile programmes. [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026]

The critics' analysis rested on the record. The International Atomic Energy Agency had said it had no evidence that Iran had resumed enriching uranium, which undercut the claim of a rebuilt programme. [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] In the week after the strikes 55 per cent of registered voters told Quinnipiac that Iran had not posed an imminent military threat. [cite:hz12_qu_mar2026] Whether Netanyahu's advocacy was decisive, or whether a president who had called Iran "very close" to a bomb nine months earlier needed no persuading, is a question about motive; the documents show both men wanted the war, and that only one of them could order American forces into it.

The war, Hormuz and the cost

Iran answered with missiles and drones against Israel, American bases and Gulf states, and said it would shut the Strait of Hormuz. [cite:ow7_wiki_ceasefire2026] [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] Shipping through the strait came almost to a standstill; by April "almost all shipping has ceased", the House of Commons Library reported, and the 32 members of the International Energy Agency released 400 million barrels from their reserves to hold prices down. On 13 April the United States imposed its own blockade on ships bound for Iranian ports. [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026] American gasoline averaged just over $4 a gallon by late July, against about $3 before the war. [cite:hz12_almon_ipsos] A Pakistani-mediated ceasefire began on 7–8 April, was extended, and led to the Islamabad Memorandum signed on 17 June; it collapsed on 8 July. [cite:ow7_wiki_ceasefire2026] The war's effects in Lebanon are told in Part Eight. [scene:hz8_war2026|The 2026 war in Lebanon]

The American dead came early and kept coming. By AP's count in July, six soldiers were killed by an Iranian drone at a civilian port in Kuwait soon after 28 February, one died of wounds from an attack on Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia, six died when a KC-135 tanker crashed in Iraq in March, a Navy pilot died in a helicopter crash on 1 July, and two were killed in Jordan on 18 July defending against Iranian missiles and drones. [cite:hz12_ktar_deaths] By 9 October the Pentagon's database listed 21 deaths and 865 wounded. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026] The administration declared Operation Epic Fury concluded on 7 July, but by The Intercept's reading of the official figures 444 of the dead and wounded were added after that date, about 100 of them in September; it called the count an undercount, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth called a Washington Post report of suppressed numbers "DISGUSTING and FAKE." [cite:hz12_intercept_cas] Central Command estimated the cost at $43.6 billion as of 3 September, and the Congressional Budget Office put it at $38 billion through 1 August, rising by about $3 billion a month. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026]

Congress

Congress neither declared war nor authorized it. On 3 June 2026 the House adopted, 215–208, a concurrent resolution under the War Powers Resolution directing the President to remove American forces from hostilities with Iran, with four Republicans voting for it; the Senate agreed, 50–48, on 23 June. A concurrent resolution is not sent to the President, its legal force is disputed, and the administration opposed it. [cite:hz12_clerk_hcr86] [cite:hz12_sen_hcr86] On 15 July the House voted on an amendment by Representative Thomas Massie, a Republican from Kentucky, to bar funding for Israel in the 2027 State Department and national security appropriations bill, which would have removed about $3.3 billion a year in military financing. It failed, 103 to 314, with 10 voting present; Massie was the only Republican in favour. Some earlier accounts, including earlier volumes in this series, describe the vote as an amendment adopted 314–103; that inverts it. The Democratic leader, Hakeem Jeffries, called the amendment "overly broad"; Nancy Pelosi and Katherine Clark voted for it while saying they still supported the relationship with Israel. [cite:hz12_na_massie] Massie had already lost his Republican primary on 19 May to a Trump-endorsed challenger in the most expensive House primary on record, in which pro-Israel PACs spent about $8 million against him; AIPAC congratulated the winner on "defeating anti-Israel incumbent Thomas Massie", and Massie said a "foreign lobby has fully funded" the effort. AP judged Trump's endorsement the key factor. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] [cite:mee_massie_aipac] [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways]

The public

The war began unpopular and stayed so. Quinnipiac's poll of 6–8 March found 53 per cent of registered voters opposed and 40 per cent in favour, with 85 per cent of Republicans in favour and 89 per cent of Democrats and 60 per cent of independents opposed; 74 per cent opposed sending ground troops. [cite:hz12_qu_mar2026] A Fox News poll at the same time found voters split evenly, and about six in ten said Iran posed a real national security threat. [cite:hz12_pbs_polls] By late July Reuters/Ipsos found about one in three Americans supporting the war, and 69 per cent, including four in ten Republicans, saying Trump had not clearly explained its goals. [cite:hz12_almon_ipsos] This book found no Pew survey of support for the 2026 war.

Where it stands, October 2026

As of October 2026 no agreement had formally ended the war. Fighting between American and Iranian forces had been intermittent since the ceasefire collapsed in July, American casualties were still being added in September, and the bill was rising by billions a month. [cite:ow7_wiki_ceasefire2026] [cite:hz12_intercept_cas] [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] On the other fronts, the Critical Threats Project and the Institute for the Study of War reported nothing significant from Iraq or Lebanon in late September, while the Houthis were fighting Saudi-backed forces in Yemen.
