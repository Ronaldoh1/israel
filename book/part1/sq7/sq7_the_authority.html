The Authority

"The PA pays for terrorism" or "It is only welfare"

The Palestinian Authority's payments to prisoners held by Israel, including those convicted of killing Israelis, and to the families of those killed, are known to Palestinians as the Martyrs Fund and to critics as "pay-for-slay". The PA spent about $350 million on them in 2018, roughly 7 percent of its budget, the last year it published the figure. That year Congress passed the Taylor Force Act, named for an American veteran killed in Tel Aviv in 2016, cutting economic aid while the payments continue; the Knesset passed a law deducting the same sum from the taxes Israel collects for the PA. [cite:jstreet_prisoner_payments] [cite:tv7_tax_withhold]

On 10 February 2025 President Abbas decreed that the payments would move to a needs-based welfare body; he later fired officials who resisted, including his finance minister in November 2025. [cite:axios_abbas_decree] The new body began paying in December 2025. An audit released in July 2026 found that it works on needs-based criteria, but also flagged about $105 million in old-system payments made between February and December 2025, and said auditors lacked full access. In April 2026 the State Department told Congress the reform had not been carried out and declined to certify compliance. Israel says the payments continue through other channels; the PA says no body now pays by prisoner status. [cite:sq_ipf_reform] [cite:cjn_state_report_payments] [cite:inn_taylorforce_2026] Status as of 26 November 2026: the dispute stands, and Israel continues to withhold tax revenues that make up most of the PA's budget. [cite:sq_ipf_reform] Verdict: true of the system until 2025; whether the reform is real is disputed.

"Palestinian schools teach hate"

The Georg Eckert Institute, commissioned by the European Union, studied the Palestinian textbooks of 2020. It found that they meet UNESCO standards on human-rights education while expressing a "narrative of resistance" and antagonism toward Israel, with some content of "escalatory potential" and some antisemitic depictions, but no direct calls for violence against Israelis. [cite:gei_faq] [cite:eumep_textbooks] IMPACT-se, a monitoring group, says the study confirms systemic incitement; others say it disproved the larger claims. [cite:impactse_gei] [cite:hansard_textbooks] The study did not look at Israeli textbooks, which other researchers have also criticised. Verdict: partly true; documented problems, without the calls to violence the claim implies.

"$60 billion in aid was stolen by Arafat, the PLO and Hamas"

The meme's figure has no traceable audited source. What is documented: an IMF review released in 2003 found that hundreds of millions of dollars of PA revenue had flowed between 1995 and 2000 into a special account controlled by Arafat. [cite:w_arafat] Corruption in the PA is widely documented, including by Palestinian polling, and Hamas's diversion of resources in Gaza is also on the record. Verdict: corruption true; the number not established.
