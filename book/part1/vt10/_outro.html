Part Ten has shown that the Holy See's financial rules were written in the wake of its scandals. Under Paul Marcinkus the IOR did business with Michele Sindona and Roberto Calvi, issued letters that creditors read as backing for Calvi's offshore companies, and in 1984 paid those creditors about $240–250 million while denying any liability; in 1987 Italy's Court of Cassation held that the Lateran Treaty barred the prosecution of its officials, and no one has ever been convicted of Calvi's death. [cite:vt10_avv_anfossi] [cite:vt10_time_1984] [cite:vt10_cc_609_1988] [cite:vt10_wiki_calvi] The Secretariat of State's London investment, sold at a loss in 2022, led to the conviction of Cardinal Giovanni Angelo Becciu and eight others on 16 December 2023; on 17 March 2026 the Court of Appeal found the summons defective and ordered the hearings renewed, and as of October 2026 the next hearing was set for 29 October, with every defendant presumed innocent. [cite:vt10_vn_verdict2023] [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] [cite:vt10_us_oct2026] Each exposure brought a reform: the 2010 law and supervisor, the closure of about 4,800 IOR accounts, the Secretariat for the Economy, and the centralisation of the Holy See's money in APSA and the IOR, which MONEYVAL rated as substantially or moderately effective in 2021 while faulting prosecution and confiscation.
