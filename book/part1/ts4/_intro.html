Part Four turns from the system to the man who put it in the margins of the Bible. Cyrus I. Scofield's life before his conversion has been told twice over: by an admiring biographer who left out his first family and his time in jail, and by critics who added a conviction, a prison term, a second divorce and, in the most widely shared version, a Jewish financier behind his work. The first section follows the paper record from his Confederate enlistment in 1861 through Kansas politics, a federal attorney's post he held for six months, much of a year in a St. Louis jail on forgery charges that were dropped, the wife and daughters he left, and his conversion, ordination and Dallas pastorate. The second takes the rumours one at a time and says which of them hold.
