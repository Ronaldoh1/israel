This volume began with lines that were, by their own text, not borders, and it ends on the eve of a war fought across them. The Egyptian armistice of 1949 had said its line was "not to be construed in any sense as a political or territorial boundary." [cite:rs_rd6_arm_egypt_text] What changed in the eighteen years between was everything built around the lines: who armed whom, who could restrain whom, and who was bound to fight if a neighbour was struck. By the eve of the war that structure stood in six layers, laid by many governments.

The legal layer grew dense and stayed weak. The Security Council asked Jordan in 1953 to stop the crossings, but its censures fell mainly on Israel's raids: Qibya in 1953, the raid at the lake of December 1955, when in January 1956 it held that Syrian interference "in no way justifies the Israel action," and Samu in 1966, when Resolution 228 declared that "actions of military reprisal cannot be tolerated" by 14 votes to none, with the United States in favour. [cite:rs_cw2_avalon_res101] [cite:rs_cw2_un_res101] [cite:rs_cw2_un_res111] [cite:rs_cw6_res228] [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_d349] None of these was enforced. The one instrument that changed facts on the ground after Suez was UNEF, and it stood only on Egyptian soil because Israel would not host it; U Thant held that it rested on the host's consent, and on 18 May 1967 Egypt withdrew that consent. [cite:rs_cw6_a6730] [cite:rs_cw6_s7906] The American aide-mémoire of 1957 had called the Gulf of Aqaba "international waters"; in May 1967 Israel claimed a right of innocent passage and the UAR claimed territorial waters, and no court ruled between them. [cite:rs_cw3_jvl_aidememoire] [cite:rs_cw6_s7906_t] Inside Israel the Supreme Court gave the state a doctrine of its own in 1965: "defensive democracy," which barred an Arab list over Justice Cohn's dissent that the law said a list "shall be confirmed." [cite:rs_cw6_yeredor]

The administrative layer shows power held close. Military government ruled most Arab citizens of Israel from 1949 until 1 December 1966. [cite:wiki_kafr_qasim_massacre] Its governorships ended by a civilian decision over the army's objection, but the Emergency Regulations stayed in force, enforced by a reinforced police and Shin Bet, and the adviser who planned the change called it mainly "psychological." [cite:rs_cw6_akevot_gl17005] Whether that ended military rule is still argued; the Institute for Palestine Studies dates full abolition to late 1968. [cite:rs_cw6_palquest_mg_e] In Damascus, power passed by coup in 1963 and 1966 to a party faction whose leader, Jadid, led through his party post while others held the state's offices. [cite:rs_cw6_locsyria] [cite:rs_cw6_gs_syria] In Jerusalem, a cabinet deadlocked from 23 to 27 May was widened on 1 June with Dayan and Begin, and on 4 June authorized military action. [cite:rs_cw6_isa_cabinet_t] [cite:rs_cw6_mfa_4june]

The institutional and financial layer moved furthest. American aid to Israel was economic through the 1950s, $73.6 million in 1953 and $50.8 million in 1956; the first military aid came in 1962, as loans, when the Hawk sale dwarfed a previous ceiling of $1 million. [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_usaid_table] [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] [cite:rs_cw4_kramer_hawks] Within four years Washington had financed German tanks with $24 million of kits on credit, sold 210 tanks for about $42 million and 48 Skyhawks for about $50 million, and sold Jordan 36 F-104s for cash. [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_d99] [cite:rs_cw6_komer_jul1965_t] [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_d273] Bonn turned its undelivered tanks into 140 million DM, and its reparations, by a press report that does not reconcile with the 3 billion DM agreed, ran out in 1965–66. [cite:rs_cw6_serr_bpb] [cite:rs_cw6_jta_reparations_1965] [cite:rs_cw6_bpb_lux] Egypt's food aid shrank to a $70 million credit. [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_sum_a] Beneath the arms lay the reactor that Washington judged in 1960 to have weapons as "at least one major purpose," visited by nine American teams between 1961 and 1969, none of which knew of its reprocessing plant. At Apollo, 93.8 kg of uranium went unexplained; the AEC found no evidence of theft, there is no sign it looked for any, and the editors of the primary record judge the evidence of diversion "strong but not incontrovertible." [cite:rs_cw4_ebb510] [cite:rs_cw6_jvl_burr_cohen] [cite:rs_cw6_nsa_numec] At home, Israel entered 1967 in recession, with unemployment at 10.3 percent at the end of 1966. [cite:rs_cw6_boi_1966_ch9]

The Palestinian Arab layer changed most in kind. In the 1950s Palestinians appeared chiefly as the subject of other governments' plans; by 1967 they had organizations of their own. The PLO, founded in Jerusalem in 1964 by appointed delegates under Hussein's sponsorship, adopted a Charter that called Israel's establishment "illegal and false." [cite:rs_cw6_palquest_chron] [cite:rs_cw6_charter_1964] Fatah's campaign from 1965, backed by Syria and funded, in the CIA's account, chiefly by wealthy Palestinians in Kuwait and Saudi Arabia, produced 61 incidents that Israel attributed to it by December 1966, launched from Jordan and Lebanon without their governments' approval. [cite:rs_cw6_cia_dec1966] Palestinian citizens of Israel who tried to organize outside the parties saw al-Ard banned and its list barred. [cite:rs_cw6_yeredor] The Institute for Palestine Studies records that Palestinians mark Fatah's first operation as the start of their contemporary revolution. [cite:rs_cw6_ipsp_fatah] Israel answered the raids with reprisals on West Bank targets, harsher and more frequent from May 1965, according to GlobalSecurity's account. [cite:rs_cw6_gs_jordan_f] Who created the PLO, and whose was the first operation, the record leaves open.

The regional layer was rebuilt around pacts. The Czech arms deal of 1955 brought Soviet-bloc weapons to Egypt; at Sèvres in 1956 Britain, France and Israel agreed in writing on a war; and from 1958 Israel built secret ties with Turkey and Iran, the "Outer Circle" that Schonmann says was meant to create "a phantom of a pact" and persuade Washington of Israel's regional value. [cite:rs_cw2_jvl_czech] [cite:rs_cw3_shlaim_sevres] [cite:rs_cw4_schonmann_2024] Then came the Egypt–Syria pact of November 1966 and the Egypt–Jordan pact of 30 May 1967, which, on one reading, made a clash on the Syrian or Jordanian line Cairo's affair as well. [cite:rs_cw6_locegypt] [cite:rs_cw6_gs_jordan_t] Egypt entered the crisis with part of its army still in Yemen. [cite:rs_cw6_fp_yemen] The Jews of Egypt had largely left after Suez, and more than 97,000 had left Morocco by 1964. [cite:rs_cw6_uw_egypt1956] [cite:rs_cw6_julius_fathom] [cite:rs_cw6_cie_yachin]

The American layer turned from rationing arms to supplying them. Washington, which had refused Israel arms in 1956, by 1966 was writing nuclear pledges into arms sales: "will not be the first to introduce nuclear weapons," in one wording, and "unless others do" in another. [cite:rs_cw2_shlaim_arms] [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_d185] [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_d273] It also restrained: Johnson warned after Samu that another such raid would suspend shipments, and on 26 May told Eban that Israel "will not be alone unless it decides to go it alone." [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_sum_samu] [cite:rs_cw6_frus19_d77] No document consulted records a trade of ambiguity for arms, or a green light.

What, then, did the Cold War years settle? They settled that Israel would be armed by the United States, which armed Jordan too, while Egypt had drawn on the Soviet bloc since 1955. [cite:rs_cw2_jvl_czech] They did not settle the lines, the refugees, the waters or the Strait. What was contested on 4 June was whether Israel faced an imminent attack: its ministers believed it did, Nasser had spoken, in the Israeli Foreign Ministry's translation, of destroying Israel, and American intelligence judged Egypt's deployment defensive. [cite:rs_cw6_tal_t] [cite:rs_cw6_nasser_26may] [cite:rs_cw6_frus19_d77] What the record leaves open is what Moscow and Cairo intended. Those intentions, and the lines of 1949 themselves, were tested in the war that followed. It is the subject of Volume IV, Six Days, Two Fronts, which opens with [scene:s12|the Six-Day War].
