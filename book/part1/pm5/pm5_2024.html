2024: Bowman and Bush

The war in Gaza that followed the attacks of 7 October 2023 turned the next primary season into the most expensive test yet of the super PAC's method. AIPAC's PAC more than doubled its weekly fundraising after the attacks, [cite:pm_cns_2024] and by August 2024 the super PAC had raised about $68.4 million and spent about $56 million, and its largest donors were again a small number of very wealthy individuals: Jan Koum gave $5 million, Jonathon Jacobson nearly $4.6 million, Bernard Marcus $3 million and David Zalik $2 million. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] It concentrated its money, as in 2022, in Democratic primaries, but this time its two largest targets were sitting members of Congress, both progressive critics of Israel's conduct of the war: Bowman had voted for a Gaza ceasefire resolution, and Bush had opposed sending offensive weapons to Israel. [cite:wiki_bowman_primary_2024] [cite:rollcall_bush_primary_2024]

Jamaal Bowman, New York's 16th District. The super PAC spent almost $9.9 million opposing Representative Jamaal Bowman and nearly $4.8 million supporting his challenger, George Latimer. Latimer won on 25 June with more than 58 percent of the vote to Bowman's 41. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] The advertising tracker AdImpact counted $14.9 million in super PAC advertising in the race, 59 percent of all the money spent on it. [cite:pm_adimpact_2024] The New York Times wrote that the spending "eclipsed what any interest group has ever spent on a single House race." [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] After the result AIPAC said the super PAC "will continue to support leaders who promote our partnership with Israel." [cite:factcheck_udp_2024]

Cori Bush, Missouri's 1st District. In St. Louis the super PAC spent more than $5.2 million against Representative Cori Bush and $3.3 million for her challenger, Wesley Bell, according to FactCheck.org's count of filings through September; later tallies that include all spending in the race put the super PAC's total at about $8.6 million. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] [cite:rollcall_bush_primary_2024] Bell won on 6 August. AIPAC's PAC had also steered more than $3.1 million in earmarked contributions to Bell's own campaign by the end of July. [cite:pm_sludge_2024]

The other races. The super PAC spent about $4.6 million against Dave Min, a candidate for an open seat in California's 47th District; Min nonetheless finished in the top two and advanced to the general election. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] It spent about $3.7 million for Sarah Elfreth in an open Maryland seat, who won. [cite:pm_adimpact_2024] And for the first time it spent in Republican primaries, about $3 million against four Republican candidates, two of whom lost: John Hostettler, in Indiana's 8th District, against whom it spent $1.8 million while the Republican Jewish Coalition's super PAC spent $1.2 million for his opponent Mark Messmer; and Representative Bob Good of Virginia. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] [cite:pm_adimpact_2024] Across the cycle, the super PAC spent about $12.4 million supporting some Democrats and more than $20 million opposing others. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024]

A race it chose to skip. In Minnesota, Representative Ilhan Omar faced the same challenger she had beaten in 2022. Her opponents spent only about $110,000 against her, after AIPAC reportedly judged the challenger too weak to win, against the $23.2 million spent to defeat Bowman and Bush the same year. [cite:abc538_squad_spending] [cite:washex_omar_2024] The comparison is a reminder that the money followed a judgment about which seats could be taken, not only about which members the network most opposed.

What the advertising said. In these races the super PAC's advertising generally did not mention Israel. It attacked Bowman and Bush on their records and their reliability as Democrats, and in Bowman's case on his statements. [cite:punchbowl_udp_no_israel] [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] Its supporters regard that as ordinary campaigning: an independent committee may choose whatever message it thinks will persuade voters. Its critics argue that it allowed a foreign-policy lobby to remove members of Congress without voters knowing that foreign policy was the reason. Both descriptions are consistent with the filings.

The results of 2024 were the high point of the method's reputation. AIPAC's own summary credited the super PAC with helping to decide nine races, Bowman's and Bush's among them. [cite:pm_aipac_memo_2024] What the filings cannot show is how many votes the money moved. Both races had other dynamics, and the sources used here do not separate the effect of the spending from them. They show that the two largest sums spent by any interest group in a House primary up to that time were spent to defeat two sitting members who had criticized Israel's conduct of the war, and that both were defeated.
