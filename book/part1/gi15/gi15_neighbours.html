Egypt, Jordan and the Whole Ledger

Egypt and Jordan are the two neighbours at peace with Israel, under the treaties of 26 March 1979 and 26 October 1994, and neither has been attacked by Israel in the wars of 2023–2026, though on 27 May 2024 an Egyptian member of the security personnel near Rafah was killed in an exchange of fire with Israeli troops, which both militaries said they were investigating. [cite:gi15a_aj_egysoldier] [cite:gi8_avalon_egtreaty] [scene:gi8_sinai|Part Eight: Sinai and Egypt] [scene:gi8_jordan|Part Eight: Jordan] They have still borne costs: a closed border crossing, proposals to move Gaza's people onto their soil, lost revenue, missiles crossing their airspace, and treaties strained but not broken. This section sets out those effects with each government's stated position. It ends with a single table that draws together the ledger of Parts Thirteen and Fourteen, country by country.

Egypt: the crossing and the red line

Gaza's only crossing not on Israel's border is at Rafah, on the Egyptian frontier. Israeli forces seized it in May 2024, seven months into the war, and it stayed largely shut for most of the next two years. [cite:gi15_almon_rafah] On 24 May 2024 the International Court of Justice ordered Israel, by thirteen votes to two, to halt its offensive in the Rafah governorate where it might inflict conditions of life that could bring about the physical destruction of the Palestinian group in Gaza. [cite:gz_lawfare_rafah] [scene:gz7_courts|Gaza, Documented: the courts] Reopening the crossing was part of the first phase of the October 2025 ceasefire. Israel made it conditional on recovering the last hostage's body, and the crossing reopened for people in early February 2026. Returns from Egypt were limited to Gazans who had left during the war, "only after prior security clearance by Israel". [cite:gi15_almon_rafah] It closed again when the war on Iran began and, according to Israeli authorities, reopened on 18 March 2026 for "limited movement of people". No later change in its status was confirmed in the sources reviewed. [cite:gi15_libna_rafah]

The deeper strain was over where Gaza's people would go. On 25 January 2025 President Trump spoke of wanting to "clean out" Gaza, and on 4 February he proposed that the United States "take over" the Strip and that its people be relocated to other countries. [cite:gi15_toi_sisi] [cite:gz_whyy_riviera] President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi had already answered on 29 January. Displacing Gazans, he said, was an "injustice that we cannot take part in" that "can never be tolerated or allowed because of its impact on Egyptian national security". [cite:gi15_toi_sisi] Egypt put forward its own plan instead, adopted by an Arab summit in Cairo in March 2025, to rebuild Gaza for about $53 billion while Palestinians stayed "on their land without displacement". Israel's foreign ministry had called relocation an "opportunity for the Gazans to have free choice based on their free will," and the White House said the Arab plan did not address the reality that Gaza was uninhabitable. [cite:gz_nbc_arabplan] Israeli ministers' statements in favour of Palestinian emigration from Gaza are set out in Part Thirteen. [scene:gi14_gaza|Part Thirteen: Gaza] No official count of Gazans who entered Egypt during the war was found.

The economic cost to Egypt came mostly through the Red Sea, not from Israel directly. Sisi said in April 2025 that tension in the Red Sea had cost the Suez Canal about $7 billion in revenue in 2024, as Houthi attacks diverted shipping around Africa. That is his government's figure, not independently verified. [cite:gi15_toi_suez] [cite:hz10_crs_if12587] The treaty itself was strained over Sinai. In September 2025 Netanyahu ordered that a gas export deal not proceed without his approval, after Israeli allegations that Egypt's military deployments in Sinai violated the peace treaty; Egypt denied any violation. On 17 December 2025 he approved the deal, worth about $35 billion, as "the largest gas deal in Israel's history". Egypt's State Information Service called it "a purely commercial transaction" with "no political dimensions or understandings of any kind". [cite:gi15_aje_gas] The treaty has held, and Egypt was among the mediators of the October 2025 Gaza truce. [cite:gz_wiki_timeline2025]

Jordan: airspace, tourism and the "alternative homeland"

Jordan, too, was asked to take Gazans. King Abdullah II's answer in January 2025 was the one his government has given for decades: Palestinians must stay on their land, in line with a two-state solution. [cite:gi15_toi_sisi] In 2004 he had put it in the words Jordan uses against the "Jordan is Palestine" argument advanced by some Israeli politicians: "Jordan will never be an alternative homeland to anybody." [cite:gi8_king_althomeland] [scene:gi8_jordan|Part Eight: Jordan]

The wars reached Jordan through its sky. In April 2024 Jordan shot down some of the Iranian drones crossing its airspace towards Israel. [cite:ir8_gs_tp1] In 2026 Iran launched more than 250 missiles and drones at Jordan itself, aimed mainly at the American military presence and the port of Aqaba. Most were intercepted, the physical damage was slight, and no Jordanian casualties were reported. [cite:gi15_mei_jordan] Earlier, on 28 January 2024, a drone launched by an Iraqi militia had killed three American soldiers at Tower 22, a base in north-eastern Jordan. [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] The economic toll was larger. In February 2024 the IMF cut its growth forecast for Jordan to about 2.6 per cent, noting that hotel occupancy had fallen by about a third in the first week of December 2023. [cite:gi15_jn_imf] In 2026 gas imports from Israel were suspended at the start of the Iran war, raising the cost of generating power by about $140 million a month. Visitors fell to 3.1 million in the first half of the year, in a country where tourism makes up 14 to 16 per cent of GDP. [cite:gi15_mei_jordan] Jordan's government has protested Israeli acts beyond its own borders: in October 2026 it said the Sufa 53 works in Syria violated the UN Charter and Israel's obligations under the 1974 agreement. [cite:gi9_an_sufa] Part Seven records its response to Netanyahu's "Greater Israel" remark of August 2025. [scene:gi7_interview|Part Seven: the "very much" interview]

The whole ledger

The table below brings together the countries covered in Parts Thirteen and Fourteen. Each toll is given with the body that counted it and the date; counts made by different methods are not added together. Israel's stated reason stands in its own column. The status column describes the position as of October 2026, a week before Israel's election of 27 October. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] [cite:hz8b_qna_toll] [cite:ir11_hrana_day22]

The table shows three things that the record supports and one that it does not. It shows that the heaviest tolls by far fell in Gaza and Lebanon, where Israel's wars were fought on the ground. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] [cite:hz8b_qna_toll] It shows that in Syria, Iran, Iraq and Yemen the operations were air and missile campaigns whose stated reasons concerned weapons, armed groups and attacks on Israel, and that each was met by the other side's own stated reason. [cite:gi15_aje_iraq2019] [cite:gi15_gv_ap46] [cite:ir10_idf_jun14] It shows that, as of October 2026, Israeli forces hold ground taken in these wars in three places: in Gaza, in south Lebanon and in south-west Syria. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] [cite:hz10s_eb_sufa] It does not show that these operations are steps in a programme of territorial expansion. This volume found such a programme documented, in laws, platforms and named officials' statements, for the West Bank and the Golan only. [scene:gi13_record|The verdict] Whether the zones in Lebanon, Syria and Gaza become permanent is argued both ways. One view reads indefinite "security zones" as expansion by another name. The other points to the stated security purpose, the absence of any sovereignty claim, and the withdrawals of 1957, 1982, 2000 and 2005. [cite:gi9_carnegie_ring] [scene:gi12_withdrawals|Part Twelve: the withdrawals] Part Fifteen turns from the toll to the bill: what American aid pays for, and whether it frees Israeli money for these operations.
