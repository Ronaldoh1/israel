Gun Laws, Gun Deaths and Mass Shootings

The two countries start from opposite legal premises. In the United States the Second Amendment protects an individual right to keep and bear arms, and the Supreme Court has said so in District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) and later cases. [cite:us5_duke_bsca] Israel has no such right. Its Firearms Law of 1949 says that "no person shall possess a firearm, except under license". Section 12 gives the licensing authority discretion to issue, condition, refuse or revoke a licence, and the courts treat licensing as an administrative matter rather than the exercise of a right. [cite:us5_duke_reznik] This section keeps crime, suicide and attacks classed as terrorism apart.

How the law works

To hold a private licence in Israel, an applicant has to clear threshold conditions set in regulation and also meet at least one criterion from a further list that is not fully public. As of 2023 the threshold was age 21 with two years of national or military service (one year for women), age 27 for a citizen without service, or age 45 for a permanent resident. Applicants must be physically and mentally fit, and a protective order, for example in a domestic-violence case, should bar a licence unless a court rules otherwise. The police also control which types of firearm civilians may hold. [cite:us5_duke_reznik] Licensing was widened in steps. Measures in 2015 and 2018 opened eligibility to people who had served in significant combat roles, and from 2023 any combat service counted. [cite:us5_duke_reznik]

In the United States, federal law sets a floor and the states build on it. In New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen, decided on 23 June 2022, the Supreme Court struck down New York's requirement that people applying to carry a concealed handgun show "proper cause". It also set a test under which a gun law must fit the nation's historical tradition of firearm regulation. [cite:us5_duke_bsca] Two days later, on 25 June 2022, President Biden signed the Bipartisan Safer Communities Act, the first major federal gun law in decades. It expanded background checks for buyers under 21, provided $750 million over five years for state crisis-intervention programmes including "red flag" laws, extended the domestic-violence ban to some dating partners, created federal crimes of straw purchasing and gun trafficking, and required people who sell guns predominantly for profit to be licensed dealers. [cite:us5_duke_bsca] Policy then reversed direction. On 7 February 2025 President Trump ordered Attorney General Pam Bondi to review federal rules, enforcement and court positions for "ongoing infringements" of the Second Amendment, including the reports of the White House Office of Gun Violence Prevention. [cite:us5_bloomberg_eo]

How many guns

The Small Arms Survey estimated about 857 million civilian-held firearms in the world at the end of 2017. About 393 million of them, or 120.5 per 100 residents, were in the United States. [cite:us5_sas_2018] The same survey put Israel at about 557,000 civilian firearms, or 6.7 per 100. [cite:us5_wiki_gunownership] The 2017 survey is a snapshot, and for Israel it is a narrow one: it counts civilian holdings, not the weapons the army issues to soldiers and reservists, and it predates October 2023. Israel's own longer record runs the other way from the American one until 2023. Individually licensed firearms fell from about 280,000 in 1998 (4.7 per cent of the population) to 147,000 in 2018 (1.6 per cent), according to a government report. [cite:us5_duke_reznik]

The October 2023 loosening

After the Hamas-led attack of 7 October 2023, National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir relaxed the licensing rules and pushed through issuance at an unprecedented pace. Geographic restrictions were eased, combat service of any kind qualified, and personal interviews were replaced by telephone interviews or none at all. About 250,000 applications arrived in the first 50 days, roughly as many as in the previous twenty years combined, and approvals averaged about 1,700 a day in November 2023. The stated aim was to triple the number of licence holders, and more than 800 new civilian emergency squads were set up in towns with minimal training. [cite:us5_duke_reznik] Dozens of communities were added to the list of eligible residence areas. The list was given to judges in a sealed envelope and has not been published. Rescue and fire volunteers and national-service members became eligible, the three-year wait for new immigrants in eligible communities was cancelled. [cite:us5_jpost_gunreform]

By the reform's first anniversary about 188,000 licences had been issued from 365,000 applications. [cite:us5_jpost_gunreform] Against about 170,000 licences in force before 7 October 2023, that roughly doubled the number in a year, by this book's arithmetic. [cite:us5_duke_reznik] Much of the processing was done by temporary staff, mostly young women doing national service, who worked at the ministry from 8 October to 2 December 2023 without appropriate training. Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara ordered their work halted. Police found that they had issued 23,000 temporary and 15,424 permanent licences, 1,146 of them suspected of breaching the criteria, and the Lahav 433 fraud unit opened an investigation in which people from Ben-Gvir's office were questioned. Ben-Gvir's associates called these cases negligible. [cite:us5_jpost_gunreform] The United States reportedly held back a shipment of rifles over concern that they could reach West Bank settlers. [cite:us5_duke_reznik] The UN counted 1,828 settler attacks causing casualties or damage in 2025. [cite:wb_ocha_snap2025] [scene:wb8_settlers|The West Bank, Documented: settlers]

The reform's effect is argued both ways. A former senior licensing official warned that "a weapon in inexperienced or unqualified hands could cause a disaster". The women's organisation WIZO argued that "the presence of a gun in the house is the issue". [cite:us5_jpost_gunreform] Ben-Gvir's office said that women murdered in 2024 were killed "with illegal weapons or kitchen knives", not reform-issued guns, and that armed civilians were often the first to stop attackers. [cite:us5_jpost_gunreform] The Abraham Initiatives links the shootings in Arab communities to illegal firearms, and the government's response in July 2026 targeted smuggling and the illegal arms trade, not licensed guns. [cite:us5_toi_2023toll] [cite:us5_toi_shinbet] [scene:us5_arab|The killings in Israel's Arab communities]

Gun deaths: what the American figures show

The CDC recorded 44,447 gun deaths in the United States in 2024, the third annual fall in a row but still the fifth-highest total since 1968. Of these, 27,593 (62 per cent) were suicides and 15,364 (35 per cent) homicides. [cite:us5_pew_gundeaths] The overall gun death rate was 12.8 per 100,000, against a peak of 16.3 in 1974. The gun homicide rate was 4.7, down from 6.7 in 2021 and a peak of 7.2 in 1974. The gun suicide rate was 7.6, about equal to its record of 7.7 in 1977. [cite:us5_pew_gundeaths] By state, rates ranged from 3.7 per 100,000 in Hawaii to 28.0 in Mississippi. The largest part of America's gun toll is suicide, which comparisons limited to crime leave out. [cite:us5_pew_gundeaths]

No Israeli series combining firearm homicides, suicides and accidents was found for this book; the WHO mortality database could close that gap. The comparison made with confidence covers homicide alone. [scene:us5_homicide|Homicide over time]

Mass shootings and attacks

There is no agreed definition of a mass shooting. The Gun Violence Archive counts any incident in which four or more people are shot, not counting the shooter. By that measure the United States had 690 mass shootings in 2021, 645 in 2022, 660 in 2023 and 502 in 2024, with 510 deaths in 2024. [cite:us5_pew_gundeaths] The FBI's narrower "active shooter" count rose from 3 incidents in 2000 to 61 in 2021 and fell to 24 in 2024. [cite:us5_pew_gundeaths] In Israel, the shootings with the most victims have mostly been attacks classed as terrorism, which are counted separately from crime. On 8 September 2025, for example, two gunmen from the West Bank killed six people at Ramot Junction in Jerusalem before a security officer and a civilian shot them dead. [cite:us5_aje_ramot] Multi-victim shootings in Arab towns, most of them attributed to criminal groups and some to feuds in which several members of one family were killed, are counted in the Arab-community homicide figures rather than as mass shootings. [cite:us5_toi_2023toll] [cite:us5_maariv_2025]

The two systems have moved in opposite directions. The United States combines constitutionally protected, very high civilian gun ownership with a gun homicide rate falling from its 2021 peak and a gun suicide rate near its record. Israel's discretionary licensing shrank private gun ownership for two decades, then roughly doubled the number of licences in a year after October 2023, with investigators examining how some were issued as of late 2024, while the Arab-community killings, mostly with illegal guns, reached a record in 2025.
