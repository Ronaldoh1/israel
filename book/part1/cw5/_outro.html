By the end of 1963 the two confrontations of Kennedy's last year had produced something less than either side had asked for and more than either had conceded at the start. Neither ended in a ruling, a treaty or a prosecution. Each ended in an arrangement that rested on consent, on silence or on a reading of the record that the other party did not share.

The legal layer is the thinnest. The Foreign Agents Registration Act of 1938 was a disclosure law, not a prohibition, and the order of 21 November 1962 that the American Zionist Council register under it was never tested in a court. [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order] By nearly every account the Council never registered; a single 2016 version of a Wikipedia article says otherwise, and no primary document supports it. [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_azc_2016] The Senate inquiry that surrounded the order ran under Senate Resolution 26 to 31 January 1964, with $48,600 and a duty to recommend changes to the law; the sources reached do not show its report. [cite:rs_cw5_sres26] The Attorney General's report for 1964 counted 480 registrants and no prosecutions, and a proposal, not yet law, to make registrants keep separate books. [cite:rs_cw5_doj_fara_1964] On the side of the alliance, nothing was written into law either. Kennedy's promise at Palm Beach of support "in case of an invasion" stayed a spoken assurance, and through the summer of 1963 he remained "unwilling to make 'explicit' guarantees." [cite:rs_cw5_nsa_letters]

The administrative layer held the real outcome on Dimona. The instrument was correspondence: Kennedy's warning that American "commitment to and support of Israel could be seriously jeopardized," Eshkol's reply of 19 August agreeing to regular visits in principle, Kennedy's "basic agreement" of 26 August, and the memorandum of 16 September that named the "plutonium separation plant." [cite:rs_cw5_nsa_letters] The State Department could ask; it could not compel. Israel's prime minister and cabinet decided when and how often the visitors came, and the six-month interval was declined while Kennedy was alive. Inside Washington, the estimates of January to May 1963 had turned "no evidence" into a forecast of capability, a device by late 1965 or more likely 1966, without claiming knowledge of a decision. [cite:rs_cw5_frus18_d87] [cite:rs_cw5_nsa_letters] Inside Israel the structure changed hands: Isser Harel was forced out over the German scientists, and Ben-Gurion resigned on 16 June for "personal reasons" he never explained. [cite:rs_cw5_enc_harel] [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_bengurion]

The institutional and financial layer is where the dispute is sharpest and least settled. The Jewish Agency's payments to the Council stand at $5,100,001.02 for 1955 to 1962 in the Senate record and at $1.5 million in Barda, unreconciled. [cite:jta_fulbright_jewish_agency_hearing] [cite:rs_cw5_barda_2026] The strongest case on one side, Smith's, is that a registered foreign agent's money reached American lobbying through an unregistered body. The strongest case on the other, Barda's and Neumann's, is that the documented payments, $38,000 for subscriptions, bought copies of a newsletter, ended in 1962, and left the committee's chairman finding "no wrong doing." [cite:rs_cw5_smith_podcast] [cite:rs_cw5_djn_19630816] What emerged was AIPAC's defence that it is funded by American donors, not by the Israeli government. [cite:factually_aipac_fara] Money moved in the other direction too: PL-480 aid to Egypt of $180.3 million in fiscal 1962, and, on Kenen's account, a Hawk loan of about $25 million over ten years and American aid to Israel of about $85 million in 1963. [cite:rs_cw5_frus17_d249] [cite:rs_cw5_kenen_uja_1963]

On the Palestinian Arab side, the year produced a voice more than an institution. Ahmad Shukairy, chosen by the Arab League in 1963 as Palestine's representative, told a UN committee on 14 November that 1,120,170 refugees lived on about six cents a day, demanded the return of their property and called its seizure "genocide," his charge. [cite:rs_cw5_palquest_shuqayri] [cite:rs_cw5_un_spc407] The United States answered that he had made many misstatements and would say no more. [cite:rs_cw5_un_spc407] The sources reached do not show what came of Kennedy's 1961 offer of help "on the basis of the principle of repatriation or compensation." [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_jfk_arab_letter] The organization Shukairy would lead was created only in 1964.

The regional layer was in motion. Egypt and Saudi Arabia had signed disengagement terms for Yemen and paid for UN observers, yet by Riedel's account each had told the UN mediator in April that it would not do its part, and Egypt's force had grown to about 30,000 by March 1963. [cite:rs_cw5_unyom] [cite:rs_cw5_riedel_yemen] The union proclaimed in Cairo on 17 April for "the liberation of Palestine" had collapsed by 22 July, and Iraq's Ba'athist government fell in November; whether the United States helped bring about Iraq's February coup is still argued among named scholars. [cite:rs_cw5_countrystudy_syria] [cite:rs_cw5_wiki_ramadan] [cite:rs_cw5_gibson_majalla] By Bergman's account, the German scientists had left Egypt by the end of the year for jobs in West Germany. [cite:rs_cw5_jvl_damocles]

The American layer held both policies at once. Washington fed Cairo, armed Israel on credit, and pressed Israel over Dimona out of a view of proliferation as a challenge to American power. [cite:rs_cw5_frus18_d70] [cite:rs_cw5_nsa_letters] Kennedy was killed in Dallas on 22 November 1963. That is a fact of timing. Johnson kept insisting on the visits; the foreign-agents order was never enforced to completion, and no document records a decision to stop. [cite:rs_cw5_frus6468_summary] [cite:rs_cw5_smith_ipa_2022] No official inquiry or historian reached for this book connects the death to either dispute as a cause. [cite:wiki_warren_commission]

What was settled, then, was narrow: a federal order existed and went unenforced, and Israel accepted American visits on terms it controlled. What was contested was what each meant: whether the Agency's money was a subsidy or a sale, and whether "basic agreement" was verification or its form. What the record leaves open is how much of the money reached lobbying, who decided the order would lapse, and what Israel had decided about Dimona. Those questions pass to [scene:cw6_johnson_eshkol|Johnson and Eshkol].
