Lost Years: The Case of Edmond de Rothschild

Removing a funder or a leader removes more than his ties. It removes years of work, and the question is whether anyone else would have done that work in those years. The map answers it year by year, from 1882 to 1948, and the clearest way to show how is the case that tests the rule hardest: Edmond de Rothschild of the Paris house.

Edmond did not arrive with a plan for a state, and he did not appear in Palestine overnight. His involvement began in 1882, months after the first colonies were founded. Rabbi Samuel Mohilever appealed to him to save Rishon LeZion, which was collapsing for want of water and farming knowledge, and Edmond agreed. Zikhron Ya'akov, Rosh Pinna and Mazkeret Batya followed in 1883, Ekron in 1884; he made his first visit, anonymously, in 1887. [cite:schama_two_rothschilds] [cite:rs_rd0_firstaliyah_wiki] [cite:rs_rd0_rh_visits] He was not working toward a state. When Herzl came to him in July 1896 he refused to back a political movement, and he stayed away from the Basel Congress; only after about 1914 did he draw close to the Zionist Organization, and in 1929 he became honorary president of the enlarged Jewish Agency. [cite:herzl_diaries_vol2_archive] [cite:rs_rd0_ej_edmond] His work was fifty-two years of building on the ground, from 1882 until his death in 1934, much of it through his own administrators and, from 1899, through the Jewish Colonization Association and then PICA. By 1934 he was reported to have invested over $60 million in Palestine, and PICA held about half a million dunams, twice the Jewish National Fund's holdings at the time. [cite:f_jta_baron]

The map places each tie's work in the years it was done. Edmond's money runs from 1882 to 1934. The Keren Hayesod's runs from 1921 to 1932, the American appeals' from 1946 to 1948. A tie without dates is spread over the years its actor was active, and an actor runs from its first dated tie to its last, except where its founding and end are documented. The Jewish Agency counts from 1929, when the Zionist Congress constituted it, not from earlier years its ties happen to mention; the Palestine Office from 1908; Herzl from 1895 to 1904. For each year and each channel the map then asks what work the removed actor did that year, and whether the strongest documented actor on the same side, already founded, could have done it. A substitute covers only up to the yearly work it had actually shown by then. Money raised in 1946 cannot replace money spent in 1890.

What no one covers is not lost for one year only. Work that was built stays built: a colony founded in 1883 was still there in 1948, and so was the land bought for it. The map therefore treats what no one covered as a shortfall against everything built so far, and keeps counting it each year until later work outgrows it. Losing the first colonies matters for decades; losing one donation in 1947 matters for a year. A channel starts counting once its record reaches 5 percent of its 1948 total, because before that the record is too thin to say that a year was lost rather than simply undocumented. A year counts as lost in proportion to the worst-hit channel's shortfall.

Added up, about 43 years of the movement's money work are lost without him, and a few more of its land and settlement work. Those years lower the chance of a founding on time in proportion to the 66 years of build-up between 1882 and 1948, and a later founding cannot come before they are made up. The reading is "unlikely": about 35 percent on time, about 43 percent ever. It rose from about 28 percent as more of the period's documented money was counted, most of all the Transfer Agreement of 1933, which moved about $35 million through the Anglo-Palestine Bank by 1939 and did not depend on him. The closest documented substitute in money is the Jewish Agency, from 1929, which could carry his yearly role in full only once the American appeals of 1946 and after arrived.

An earlier version of the map put the loss at about 21 years. It had measured each actor's whole lifetime of work against every year at once, so that the Jewish Agency, dated from the wrong year, appeared able to replace Edmond's spending of 1902 with money raised in 1946. The correction applies to every node on the map, not to Edmond alone. Some readings rose with it and others fell; every reading in this appendix uses the corrected rule.
