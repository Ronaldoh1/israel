How a Blind Spot Is Found

The contract between Epstein's company and a Swiss bank, set out in Part Seven, was reported in detail in February 2026 by Le Monde and in the Swiss and French financial press, and only later, in July, by an American network. That is the first kind of blind spot: a fact that is public but has not travelled.

The second kind is found by reading the network rather than the news. The companion map holds every person, company, property and document in this book, with every documented tie between them. Two simple tests show where attention has not gone. The first asks which points connect the most other points once Epstein and Maxwell are taken out; the second asks which of those points the public conversation has barely named. The answer is consistent: the strongest connectors are an agreement, a government department, an assistant, an apartment building and a banker, not the celebrities whose photographs dominate the coverage.

The third kind is a gap: a record that should exist and is not in the release. The Justice Department says it collected more than six million pages and released about three million.
