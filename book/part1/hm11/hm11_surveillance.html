The Surveillance Paradox

Israel's signals-intelligence service, Unit 8200, is counted among the most capable in the world, and the United States shares raw intercepts with it. [scene:ow4_memorandum|The One-Way Mirror: the NSA memorandum] In this war it used computerized target-generation systems. According to six Israeli intelligence officers interviewed by +972 Magazine and Local Call, a system called Lavender marked as many as 37,000 Palestinians as suspected militants, with an error rate its operators put at about 10 percent; officers spent about 20 seconds checking each name, and a companion system, "Where's Daddy?", flagged when a target entered his family home. The army said such systems are "auxiliary tools" for its officers and denied much of the account. [cite:hm_972_lavender] Palantir signed a strategic partnership with Israel's Defense Ministry in January 2024, though it denies any role in Lavender, and Israeli reporting says Unit 8200 declined its services. [cite:ow9_globes_palantir] [cite:ow9_pal_unletter]

Against that capability stand three facts from the record. The first is October 7 itself: Israel held Hamas's attack plan more than a year before and judged it aspirational. [cite:gz_nyt_jericho] The second is how the war's most-wanted man was found. Yahya Sinwar was killed in October 2024 by troops on a routine patrol in Rafah who did not know he was there; Israel had known he was hiding in that district but not his exact location. [cite:hm_wiki_sinwar] The third is the hostages: in two years of war, military intelligence located and freed eight of them alive. [cite:hm_ynet_rescued8]

In Lebanon the same state showed what its intelligence could do against an enemy it had penetrated. Between the pager explosions of 17 September 2024 and the strike that killed Hassan Nasrallah on 27 September, Israel removed much of Hezbollah's chain of command in about ten days. [cite:hm_ns_hezbollah] Two years of war in Gaza did not do the same to Hamas: by September 2026 IDF sources said Hamas was taking weapons back out of its depots. [cite:hm_jp_reclaim]

The record allows more than one explanation. Israel's commanders pointed to Gaza's tunnels, 500 to 700 kilometres by the Institute for National Security Studies' estimate, and to an enemy fighting among two million civilians and holding hostages whose presence constrained every operation. [cite:hm_inss_arsenal] Critics read the same facts differently: a system built to generate targets quickly will produce many deaths and few decisive results against an enemy underground. Both explanations fit the evidence; neither is proved by it.
