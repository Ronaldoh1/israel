Neighbours, Not the Same Thing

The Dark Enlightenment shares stages, funders and sometimes people with several other currents on the American right and in the technology industry, and commentary often folds them together. They are not the same, and several of them contradict it on the point that matters most to this book: who should rule and by what right. This section defines each neighbour from its own texts, records the documented overlaps in people, money and venues, and records the documented disagreements. A shared stage is recorded as a meeting, not as a shared belief. The last of these neighbours, the violent "accelerationism" of terrorist groups, is kept strictly apart, because it shares a word with Nick Land's philosophy and nothing else that the record shows.

Post-liberal Catholic integralism

Integralism is a Catholic political theory holding that temporal government should be ordered to the spiritual good; its members are a subset of a wider "post-liberal" circle of Catholic intellectuals who want, in the Associated Press's summary, a strong government that they control and who seek to take over institutions such as universities from within. [cite:de16_ap_postliberal] Its best-known legal text is the Harvard law professor Adrian Vermeule's essay "Beyond Originalism," published in The Atlantic on 31 March 2020, which argued that "strong rule in the interest of attaining the common good is entirely legitimate" and that the constitutional order's "main aim is certainly not to maximize individual autonomy." [cite:de16_vermeule] Patrick Deneen's 2023 book Regime Change called for "the peaceful but vigorous overthrow of a corrupt and corrupting liberal ruling class." [cite:de16_ap_postliberal]

The overlap with the Dark Enlightenment is a shared hostility to liberal elites. The disagreement is sharp. Vermeule described the bureaucracy "not as an enemy, but as the strong hand of legitimate rule," where Yarvin proposes to retire it entirely ([scene:de6_the_program|Part Six]); integralism grounds authority in natural law and the Church, where Yarvin's sovereign is a secular owner answerable to shareholders. [cite:de16_vermeule] The documented touchpoint is J.D. Vance, who has called himself "postliberal," praised Regime Change at a 2023 panel with Deneen, and said at Franciscan University of Steubenville in 2022, "You need to have a functional state that accomplishes some of the things that we care about." [cite:de16_ap_postliberal] No Thiel funding of integralist venues has been found.

Christian nationalism

Christian nationalism, as measured by the polling organisation PRRI, is a set of beliefs about American identity rather than a body of texts by named authors: that "the U.S. government should declare America a Christian nation," that "U.S. laws should be based on Christian values," and that "God has called Christians to exercise dominion over all areas of American society," among five statements. In PRRI's 2024 survey, 10 percent of Americans were adherents and 20 percent sympathisers; 38 percent of adherents agreed that "true American patriots may need to resort to violence in order to save our country," against 17 percent of all Americans. [cite:de16_prri] It is a mass, majoritarian identity, and it works through elections. The Dark Enlightenment is elitist, secular in its texts and contemptuous of elections. No documented tie links Yarvin or Land to Christian-nationalist organisations, and none of the officials in this book is documented using the label of himself.

National conservatism

National conservatism has a founding document. "National Conservatism: A Statement of Principles," published by the Edmund Burke Foundation on 15 June 2022, calls for "a world of independent nations," "much more restrictive" immigration, a public life "rooted in Christianity" where a Christian majority exists, and "accepting and living in accordance with the Constitution of 1787," with "a strong but limited state, subject to constitutional restraints and a division of powers." [cite:de16_tac_natcon] That last commitment is the disagreement: the national conservatives keep the constitutional state that Yarvin proposes to replace.

The overlaps are documented and specific. Peter Thiel gave the opening speech at the first National Conservatism conference in Washington in July 2019, and signed the 2022 statement; Michael Anton signed it too. [cite:de16_bloomberg_natcon] [cite:de16_natcon_signers] No Thiel money to the conferences or to the foundation is documented ([scene:de14_network_map|Part Fourteen]), and neither Yarvin nor Land signed. [cite:de16_natcon_signers]

Pronatalism

Pronatalism is a single policy aim, raising the birth rate, pursued by Catholic, secular and technology-industry advocates and, at its fringe, by people who openly link it to eugenics. Its main gathering, the Natal Conference, was first held in Austin in 2024; its 2025 edition met at a conference centre rented from the University of Texas, and its organiser, Kevin Dolan, has said that "pro-natalist and the eugenic positions are ... very much aligned." [cite:de16_kut] The aim also has a mainstream form: Vance told the March for Life on 24 January 2025, "I want more babies in the United States of America." [cite:de16_rns_vance] Pronatalism has no theory of sovereignty, and it is compatible with any form of government. No documented attendance by Vance or by Thiel at the Natal Conference has been found.

Rationalists and effective altruists

The rationalist community grew around the blogs Overcoming Bias, from November 2006, and LessWrong, launched on 1 February 2009 by Eliezer Yudkowsky, and its central concern became the risk that advanced artificial intelligence could destroy humanity; it is closely tied to effective altruism, a movement that tries to direct charity by evidence and, in its "longtermist" branch, toward the far future. [cite:de16_wiki_lw] Neoreaction circulated among a small share of its readers, which is why the two are often linked. The record shows the community rejecting him: Yudkowsky "has strongly rejected neoreaction"; only 0.92 percent of respondents to the forum's 2016 survey called themselves neoreactionary; and Scott Alexander, its best-known essayist, published a long rebuttal, "The Anti-Reactionary FAQ," on 20 October 2013. [cite:de16_wiki_lw] [cite:de2_ssc_faq]

Thiel's relation to this current changed. The Thiel Foundation gave Yudkowsky's institute, then the Singularity Institute and now the Machine Intelligence Research Institute, more than $1.6 million from 2006 onward. [cite:de16_wiki_thielfdn] In his 2025 lectures, as reported from recordings, Thiel named Yudkowsky among the "legionnaires of the Antichrist" for arguing for world government to stop dangerous technology. [cite:de10_reason] The documented relation, then, is a funder who became an adversary.

Effective accelerationism

"Effective accelerationism," shortened to e/acc, is an online current that first appeared in May 2022 in a newsletter by four pseudonymous accounts; in December 2023 Forbes identified the best known of them, "Beff Jezos," as Guillaume Verdon, a former Google quantum-computing engineer. It holds that unrestricted technological progress, artificial intelligence above all, is a moral good, and it opposes the AI-safety priorities of the effective altruists, whose name it parodies. [cite:de16_wiki_eacc] Its closest public expression is Andreessen's 2023 manifesto ([scene:de16_the_valley|the first section of this part]). Land has voiced support for it, which is a documented overlap; but its theory differs from his, which treats capital as an inhuman process indifferent to human ends, and a critic called it "Nick Land diluted." [cite:de16_wiki_eacc] It proposes no form of government.

Violent "accelerationism": a different thing

The word "accelerationism" is also used, in a completely different sense, by neo-Nazi terror networks. In that usage, as the Institute for Strategic Dialogue defines it, accelerationism is "an ideology focussed on the need for societal collapse to preserve the white race," to be brought about by violence; its groups include Atomwaffen Division and The Base, its key text is James Mason's neo-Nazi collection SIEGE, and the perpetrators of the Christchurch mosque massacre and the Poway synagogue shooting in 2019 cited it. [cite:de16_isd] On 13 January 2025 the State Department designated the Terrorgram Collective and three of its leaders as Specially Designated Global Terrorists, saying the group "promotes violent white supremacism, solicits attacks on perceived adversaries"; it has been linked to a 2022 shooting outside an LGBTQ bar in Slovakia. [cite:de16_state_terrorgram] [cite:de16_aj_terrorgram]

No documented organisational, funding, membership or personal link exists between these groups and Yarvin, Land, Thiel or Vance. One research group, the ISD, lists neoreaction, which it calls "a doctrine developed by Nick Land and Curtis Yarvin," as one of three ideological strands that fed into this usage, while noting that neoreactionaries blame democracy where white supremacists blame Jewish people and other minorities. [cite:de16_isd] That is one think tank's interpretive claim about the history of ideas, and it is recorded here as such, not as evidence of a tie. Land's own accelerationism, the subject of [scene:de4_ccru_acceleration|Part Four], is a philosophy of technology and capital, and the journalist Zack Beauchamp, among others, has described it as otherwise unrelated to the terror usage. [cite:de4_wiki_accel]

The worst-case reading, tested

This part asked how ideas from a small body of texts could be adopted by people with money and power. Documented: a visible part of the technology industry moved to the right in 2023 and 2024, for reasons its leaders stated in public, mostly about taxes, cryptocurrency and artificial intelligence; one of its texts names Land as a saint; and its firms supplied the administration with officials, including the director of the civil service's personnel office. [cite:de16_techcrunch] [cite:de1_a16z_wiki] [cite:de16_cnbc_picks] [cite:de16_fedsmith_kupor] Documented, too, are contacts between Yarvin and people near power: Vance's 2021 citation, Anton's recorded conversation and lunch, Andreessen's friendship and Srinivasan's 2013 email. [cite:de5_wbur_vance] [cite:de5_powerline] [cite:de5_sfstandard] [cite:de16_bi_balaji]

Probable, at most: that some staff in the administration had read Yarvin; it rests on his word and one anonymous source. [cite:de6_cnn_2025] [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] Not supported: that the officials who came through this pipeline carried the sovereign-executive program with them; none has said so, and most have said nothing about the texts at all. The record also runs against the hypothesis in a way this section makes plain. The neighbours with the most real political weight, national conservatism and Catholic post-liberalism, are documented in their own founding texts as committed to the Constitution or to a strong administrative state, and the one document Thiel and Anton signed binds them to "the Constitution of 1787." [cite:de16_tac_natcon] [cite:de16_vermeule] The coalition around the administration is many currents, several of which contradict the Dark Enlightenment's end state in writing.
