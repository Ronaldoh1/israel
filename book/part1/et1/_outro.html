The Ethiopian Bible counts eighty-one books in two reckonings, and no council ever closed it. [cite:vt27_cowley1974] Its scriptures were translated early, some before the end of the fourth century, and kept in Ge'ez, a language that ceased to be spoken by about 1200 but remained the church's. [cite:vt27_met_aksum] [cite:sm_eb_geez] The faith came down from the throne: Ezana converted and put the cross on his coins, Athanasius consecrated the first bishop, and the Roman emperor Constantius II tried to replace him around 356. [cite:vt27_met_aksum] [cite:vt27_wiki_frumentius] Part Two follows the book that bound the throne to the Bible itself.
