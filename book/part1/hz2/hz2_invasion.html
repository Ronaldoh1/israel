Peace for Galilee

On 3 June 1982 a gunman from the Abu Nidal Organization shot Shlomo Argov, Israel's ambassador in London, outside the Dorchester Hotel. Argov survived with severe injuries and died of them in 2003. [cite:hz2_wiki_1982il] [cite:hz2_inn_argov] The next day Israeli jets bombed PLO ammunition depots and training bases in Lebanon, and PLO artillery answered against northern Israel. On 6 June the Israeli cabinet launched Operation Peace for Galilee. [cite:hz2_inn_argov] [cite:hz2_begin_pfg]

The stated aim. The Israel Defense Forces' own history says the operation followed "the attempted assassination of the Israeli Ambassador to the UK by a terrorist cell". It gives the objective as "pushing the latter 40 km to the north", meaning armed Palestinian groups, to keep their weapons out of range of northern Israeli towns. [cite:hz2_idf_lw1] The Menachem Begin Heritage Center, which keeps the record of the prime minister who ordered the war, describes the same aim differently: to "ensure that the forty miles surrounding the border would be terrorist-free". Forty miles is about 64 kilometres. [cite:hz2_begin_pfg] Neither account names the Abu Nidal Organization or explains why the PLO was the target. The Begin Center calls the London attackers only "Palestinian". [cite:hz2_idf_lw1] [cite:hz2_begin_pfg]

The advance. The army did not stop at either distance. On 9 June the Israeli Air Force attacked the Syrian air-defence network in the Bekaa Valley and destroyed 17 of its 19 missile batteries. On 13 June Israeli forces reached West Beirut. [cite:hz2_wiki_1982il] The Begin Center says the army "was forced to continue north all the way to Beirut". That is the official Israeli explanation for the gap between the announced aim and where the army ended up. [cite:hz2_begin_pfg] By the IDF's account the war lasted more than three months. The siege of Beirut tightened from late June, and Israeli forces took the high ground over the city between 20 and 25 June. [cite:hz2_idf_lw1] An account of the US Marine deployment says the invasion went ahead with tacit US approval. [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks]

The evacuation. A multinational force of American, French and Italian troops landed in Beirut on 24 August 1982 to oversee the departure of PLO fighters. [cite:hz2_an_1983] The IDF says the evacuation by sea was completed on 31 August. The Begin Center says the fighting "forced the PLO leader Yasser Arafat to leave Lebanon". [cite:hz2_idf_lw1] [cite:hz2_begin_pfg] The US Marines left on 10 September, their stated task done. [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks] What grew in the space the PLO left is the subject of the rest of this part and of Part Three. [scene:hz3_question|Part Three: Would Hezbollah exist?]

Sabra and Shatila. Shortly before, President-elect Bachir Gemayel, leader of the Kataeb (Phalange) party, had been assassinated. Between 16 and 18 September, Kataeb militiamen entered the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps in West Beirut, which Israeli troops had surrounded, and killed civilians, mostly Palestinians and Lebanese Shia. The killings were presented as revenge for Gemayel. [cite:hz2_wiki_1982il] [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks] Estimates of the dead run from about 700 to 3,500 or more, and they come from different counts made by different bodies with different access. [cite:hz2_wiki_1982il] The Lebanese government said in October 1982 that 762 bodies had been recovered from the camps and that some 1,200 more had been buried privately by relatives, putting the toll close to 2,000. The figures could not be independently checked because the Red Cross had left the search after less than a week. [cite:hz2b_csm_toll762] The Israeli commission of inquiry described below, citing Israeli military intelligence, put the number at 700 to 800 and called that the figure "most closely corresponding with reality". [cite:hz2b_jvl_kahan] The Israeli journalist Amnon Kapeliouk and other researchers put it closer to 3,500. [cite:hz2b_aj_battah] The Institute for Palestine Studies' encyclopedia says "more than 3,000" were killed and notes that bulldozed rubble and mass graves make an exact count impossible. [cite:hz2b_palquest_ss] This book does not choose among them. The Begin Center's timeline says "Christian Phalanges Massacred a Muslim refugee camp". [cite:hz2_begin_pfg] In Israel, 400,000 people marched on 25 September 1982 to demand that Begin resign. [cite:hz2_wiki_1982il] Four days later the US Marines returned to Beirut, joining 2,200 French and Italian troops already in place. [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks]

The Kahan Commission. Israel's commission of inquiry had three members: Yitzhak Kahan, president of the Supreme Court, Justice Aharon Barak and Major General (res.) Yona Efrat. It reported on 8 February 1983. [cite:hz2b_jvl_kahan] It found that the killings were carried out by a Phalangist unit acting on its own, that no Israeli was directly responsible, and that Israel bore indirect responsibility because its forces controlled the area and allowed the militia in. [cite:hz2b_jvl_kahan] [cite:hz2b_csm_kahan] It found that Defence Minister Ariel Sharon bore "personal responsibility" for ignoring the danger of bloodshed and revenge when he approved the Phalangists' entry, and it recommended that he resign or, if he did not, that the prime minister remove him. It severely criticized the chief of staff, Rafael Eitan, and recommended that the director of military intelligence not continue in his post. [cite:hz2b_jvl_kahan] [cite:hz2b_csm_kahan] [cite:hz2b_wiki_kahan] Sharon at first refused to go. On 10 February 1983 a grenade was thrown into a Peace Now rally outside the prime minister's office in Jerusalem that was demanding the report be carried out. It killed Emil Grunzweig, a reserve officer, and wounded others. Yonah Avrushmi was convicted of the murder in 1985. [cite:hz2b_wiki_grunzweig] [cite:hz2b_csm_sharon] Sharon then gave up the defence ministry, which Begin took over, but stayed in the cabinet as a minister without portfolio. [cite:hz2b_csm_sharon] [cite:hz2b_wiki_kahan]

What the counts show. The war's death tolls need the same care as later ones in this book, because the published figures cover different periods. On the Israeli side, the army reported 330 soldiers killed by late August 1982 and 446 killed and 2,383 wounded between 4 June and 19 November 1982. [cite:hz2b_csm_nov82] [cite:hz2b_csm_dec82] The Ministry of Defense archive gives 353 soldiers killed "in the warfare" of 1982. [cite:hz2b_mod_1982] By June 1985, when the army pulled back to the border zone, its spokesman put the total at 654 killed, 3,195 wounded and four missing, close to the 656 killed often repeated elsewhere. [cite:hz2b_csm_jun85] [cite:hz2_njop_pfg] On the Lebanese side, the figures most often quoted come from the Lebanese police and were reported at the time. In November 1982 the Christian Science Monitor gave 17,825 killed and 30,103 wounded between 6 June and 25 August. In December it reported revised police figures of 19,085 killed and 30,302 wounded between 4 June and 31 August; the paper did not break the totals down by status, but its regional breakdowns show they included Palestinian, Syrian and Lebanese fighters as well as civilians. [cite:hz2b_csm_nov82] [cite:hz2b_csm_dec82] [cite:hz2a_csm_dec82] In Beirut, the police counted 6,775 dead, 84 per cent of them civilians; in the south about 20 per cent of the dead were civilians and the rest Palestinian, Syrian or Lebanese fighters. [cite:hz2b_csm_dec82] The paper did not explain how the police counted, and no later audit of these numbers was found. They are best read as a contemporary official estimate of about 18,000 to 19,000 dead in less than three months, not as a verified total.
