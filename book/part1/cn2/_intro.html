Part Two covers the second century, when Christianity had no emperor behind it and no agreed list of books. It follows a wealthy shipowner, Marcion, who drew up the first known closed list of Christian scripture and saw the church of Rome hand back his gift when it expelled him in 144. It then follows Irenaeus of Lyon, who answered the teachers of his day around 180 by joining two lists into one argument: four gospels, and a named line of bishops to guard them.
