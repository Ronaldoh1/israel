Part Thirteen is different from every other part of this book, and it says so at the top. It is a scenario. It asks one question: if the political program known as the Dark Enlightenment were carried out as its authors describe it, what would remain of the tools that eventually caught Jeffrey Epstein?

The rules are strict. Every mechanism is taken from the documented record, either from the Epstein case set out in the earlier parts or from the program's own texts, quoted in its authors' words; the library's volume on the Dark Enlightenment sets those texts out in full. The scenario runs the documented mechanisms forward; it does not invent new ones. It makes no claim that any named person intends anything described here, and it states the program's own best answer: that a state run like a company would be more accountable, not less. It is a stress test, not a prediction.
