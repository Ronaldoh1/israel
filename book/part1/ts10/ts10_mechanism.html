The Mechanism, Step by Step

The theology shield, as this volume defines it in [scene:ts1_defined|Part One], is not a doctrine that any church has adopted under that name. It is a way of arguing. A promise in the Hebrew Bible is read as applying to a modern state, and from there to that state's policies, so that a dispute about a policy can be presented as a dispute with God. Each step can be followed in statements that named people made on named dates. This section takes the steps in order, using what the speakers themselves said, and sets beside each step what the text and its Christian critics say.

Step one: the verse

The text is Genesis 12:3, spoken to Abram before he is renamed Abraham: "I will bless those who bless you, and whoever curses you I will curse; and all peoples on earth will be blessed through you." Its place at the centre of the argument is stated by the organisations that use it. The International Christian Embassy Jerusalem, founded in 1980, publishes an explainer by its executive director emeritus, Malcolm Hedding, which calls Genesis 12:3 the verse "most often referred to as their biblical mandate" by Christian Zionists. [cite:ts10_icej_hedding] The same page defines a Christian Zionist broadly, as "a Christian who supports the Jewish people's right to return to their homeland", and says that many Christians qualify whatever their reasons. [cite:ts10_icej_hedding] As a definition this is modest. The work is done in the steps that follow, when the verse is applied to a state and its decisions. The two Christian readings of the verse, the Christian Zionist and the covenantal, are set out in [scene:gx_s05|One verse, two readings] and in [scene:ts1_genesis|Part One].

Step two: "you" becomes the state

In the verse the "you" is one man. Christian Zionist teachers have read it as extending to his descendants, the Jewish people, and from them to the State of Israel founded in 1948. The clearest recent instance came in an interview that Senator Ted Cruz of Texas gave to the broadcaster Tucker Carlson, released on 18 June 2025, as Israel's strikes on Iran began. Cruz said he had come to Congress intending to be "the leading defender of Israel in the United States Senate," and explained: "as a Christian, growing up in Sunday school, I was taught from the Bible" that "those who bless Israel will be blessed, and those who curse Israel will be cursed." Asked where that was, he said: "I don't have the Scripture off the tip of mind." Carlson pressed: "Those who bless the government of Israel?" Cruz replied that the verse "doesn't say 'the government of.' It says 'the nation of Israel.'" [cite:ts10_cl_cruz] [cite:ts10_cp_cruz]

Two corrections came from the reporting itself. Jewish Insider noted that the wording Cruz used is closer to Numbers 24:9, where the blessing is spoken over Israel as a people, than to Genesis 12:3, where it is spoken to Abram. [cite:ts10_ji_cruz] The Baptist editor Brian Kaylor made the point from the other side: Genesis 12:3 "does not actually mention Israel since Israel didn't yet exist at that point in the story." [cite:ts10_ww_kaylor] The step from Abram to the state is therefore an interpretation, made by named teachers, and not the text's own wording. Cruz's own account was also narrower than the exchange suggested. He called the verse his personal motivation and said: "Not everyone I represent is a Christian, so I don't use my faith as the reason we should support Israel." His main public case in the same interview was strategic: "Israel is doing a massive favor to America right now by trying to take out Iran's nuclear capacity." [cite:ts10_cp_cruz] [cite:ts10_ji_cruz]

Step three: the blessing becomes a test for nations

Once the "you" is the state, the blessing and the curse can be applied to other states. Word&Way reported two such uses in 2025. Speaker of the House Mike Johnson, in July 2025, rendered the verse as "I'll bless the nation that blesses Israel and curse the nation that curses Israel", adding the word "Israel" to it; the outlet linked a video clip and did not name the venue. At a White House press conference with President Trump on 29 September 2025, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said: "It's also in the Bible. It says, 'Those who will bless you will be blessed, and those who will curse you will be cursed.'" [cite:ts10_ww_kaylor] The two statements were made in different settings and for different audiences, and neither defined which acts count as blessing. What they share is the move this part describes: a promise to one man, read as a standard for judging governments.

Step four: opposing a policy becomes opposing God

The decisive step is the one that turns a policy disagreement into a religious one. Robert Jeffress, senior pastor of First Baptist Church in Dallas, took it in plain words twice, eight years apart. After the Obama administration declined to veto a UN Security Council resolution condemning Israeli settlements in December 2016, he told CBN News that President Obama and Secretary of State John Kerry "are on the wrong side of God on this issue," and that "God will judge any nation that divides the land that God gave to Israel." [cite:ts10_cbn_jeffress2017] On 22 June 2025, in a sermon answering the Cruz–Carlson exchange, he said: "Unlike any other nation in the world, God created the nation of Israel," and: "Those who oppose Israel are always on the wrong side of history. And most importantly, they're on the wrong side of God." [cite:ts10_cp_jeffress2025]

John Hagee, the founder of Christians United for Israel, made the same move against his own government's diplomacy in a CUFI webcast on 6 January 2011: "God did not make a covenant with Washington, D.C. He made a covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob." [cite:gi11_cp_hagee2011] His stated policy line, recorded in the Greater Israel volume, is narrower: that "Israelis – and they alone – have the right to make existential decisions about land and peace," and that CUFI asks the White House not to press Israel into concessions it does not wish to make. [cite:gi11_jpost_hagee] [scene:gi11_christian|Christian Zionist land theology] On that account, the theology is aimed at American pressure on Israel's government rather than at that government's own choices.

Step five: the argument closes

The effect of the fourth step is to move a question about a resolution, a settlement or a weapons package from evidence to fidelity. The ICEJ explainer describes the setting in which this happens: "Zionism," it says, "has been turned into a negative word by Israel's enemies," and Christian Zionism is "under attack and often misrepresented in the media and in much public discourse." [cite:ts10_icej_hedding] That is the organisation's stated view of its critics, and it is entitled to it. The pattern this part records is narrower and checkable. In the statements above, a named person answered a dispute over a specific policy by appeal to God's covenant rather than to the policy's effects. Whether any of them changed a vote or a decision is a separate question, which [scene:ts12_officials|Part Twelve] takes up for officials in office; the statements show the argument being made, not its effect.

The mechanism in the other direction: the Prejean Boller case

The same machinery can be turned the other way, and one case from 2026 shows how quickly the result becomes tangled. At a hearing on antisemitism held by the White House Religious Liberty Commission on 9 February 2026, the commissioner Carrie Prejean Boller, a Catholic and former Miss California USA, told the Jewish witnesses: "Catholics do not embrace Zionism, just so you know. So are all Catholics antisemites?" She asked whether speaking about "what many Americans view as a genocide in Gaza" should be treated as antisemitic. [cite:ts10_cam_prejean] [cite:ts10_ji_prejean] At the same hearing she reiterated her belief that "Jews killed Jesus," the deicide charge the Catholic Church repudiated in 1965, and said that Candace Owens "just doesn't support Zionism" and should not be called an antisemite. [cite:ts10_cam_prejean]

On 11 February the commission's chair, Lieutenant Governor Dan Patrick of Texas, announced her removal on X: "No member of the Commission has the right to hijack a hearing for their own personal and political agenda on any issue. … This was my decision." [cite:ts10_ncr_prejean] [cite:ts10_cam_prejean] Prejean Boller told EWTN that members had asked her to resign months earlier and had tried to discourage her planned remarks; on X she called her stance "a matter of Catholic conscience" and said she would not surrender her "religious liberty" to "Zionist supremacy." [cite:ts10_ncr_prejean] [cite:ts10_cam_prejean] Both halves of the record belong together. Her question about Catholics and Zionism raised a real point of doctrine, set out in [scene:ts11_churches|Part Eleven]: the Catholic Church has never taught Christian Zionism. In the same session she repeated a charge against Jews that her own church has rejected. The case cannot be reduced either to a religious-liberty victim or to a simple instance of antisemitism; what it shows is that, once the argument is framed as fidelity to God, both sides can use the same frame.

The finding of this section is about an argument, not about believers. Named people, among them a senator, a Speaker, a prime minister, a pastor and the leader of the largest Christian Zionist organisation, have used Genesis 12:3 or the covenant to cast opposition to Israeli or American policy as opposition to God, and each statement is dated. The record examined here does not show that this is how the millions who tell pollsters they support Israel reason; the LifeWay survey of 2017 found only 23 per cent of respondents with evangelical beliefs favouring a Palestinian-state treaty and 46 per cent unsure, which is uncertainty, not a settled religious verdict. [cite:gi11_ct_lifeway] The next section turns to the places where the argument has been answered, withdrawn or overridden.
