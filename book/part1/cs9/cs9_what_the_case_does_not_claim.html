What the Case Does Not Claim

A case that claims more than its evidence gives its opponents the easiest answer. This one keeps five limits.

It names institutions, not a people. The case is against a movement's leadership, a state, its army, its governments and the powers that backed them. Zionism is not Judaism, and the record shows Jews among the movement's sharpest critics, from Magnes and Buber to the Orthodox rabbis who opposed it. [cite:wiki_ihud] [cite:neturei_karta]

It does not question who is a Jew. The Jews of the Old Yishuv lived in the land, and so did Arabs. The claim that Ashkenazi Jews are really Khazars fails the genetics: studies model their ancestry as Levantine and European. [cite:jewishpress_khazar] [cite:adl_khazar] The case needs no such claim; the expulsions of 1948 are a crime whoever carried them out.

It does not erase other violence. Arab violence against Jews at Hebron in 1929, in the revolt of 1936, at the Hadassah convoy and on 7 October is on the record, and the case does not excuse it. [cite:wiki_hebron_massacre_1929] [cite:wiki_deir_yassin] [cite:halakha_yomhazikaron_2026] The case is that the founding set the chain going, not that only one side killed.

It does not claim every leader is irreligious. The founders were secular; today's governments include observant parties. The shield is the political use of faith, whoever uses it. [cite:w_herzl] [cite:us7_globes_conversion2021]

It does not claim the whole region's wars. The Arab borders and the early revolts came before Israel, and the Iran coup of 1953 did not involve it. [cite:w_sykes_picot] [cite:w_1953_coup]

The verdict

On the record of this library, the State of Israel was built by force, on a sacred map, by a secular movement carried by an empire, a bank and a superpower, over a shared country its own dissenters proposed, and most of what has followed, the wars, the accords and the dead, follows from those choices. The record supports that case. It does not need more.
