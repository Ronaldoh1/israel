From Embargo to Memorandum: The Aid Architecture, 1949–2026

In 1948 Washington embargoed Israel's weapons. By the 2020s Israel was the largest cumulative recipient of American foreign assistance since the Second World War, and its military aid was fixed for a decade at a time by written agreement. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] The change did not happen in one decision. It was built in stages, each one a law, an appropriation or a signed memorandum, and each one passed through the federal city's machinery: a President's request, a congressional vote, an agency's disbursement. This section follows the stages and the money, using the Congressional Research Service's accounting as the main ledger.

Economic aid first, 1949–1961. For more than a decade after the founding, American assistance to Israel took the form of economic aid and loans. Washington avoided selling weapons directly, for fear of alienating the oil-producing Arab states. [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] One summary puts American aid from 1946 to 1971 at an average of about $60 million a year, about $1.5 billion in all, of which $162 million was military aid, all of it as credit sales. [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance]

Weapons, 1962–1972. The first American military assistance came in 1962, as loans, when President Kennedy approved the sale of Hawk anti-aircraft missiles over the objections of his State Department. [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance] [cite:wiki_israel_us_military_relations] President Johnson went further, approving A-4 Skyhawk attack aircraft and then, in 1968, F-4 Phantom fighter-bombers, a sale that made the United States Israel's principal arms supplier. [cite:wiki_israel_us_military_relations] [cite:jvl_1968_phantom_sale] [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance] In 1971 Congress for the first time wrote a specific earmark for Israel into the foreign aid bill. [cite:everycrsreport_israel_foreign_assistance]

The turning point, 1973–1987. The October War of 1973 was the inflection point. President Nixon ordered an emergency airlift to replace Israel's battlefield losses and asked Congress for $2.2 billion in emergency aid, roughly an eight-fold increase over previous annual levels. [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] [cite:legalclarity_when_did_us_start_funding] Aid was then converted from loans to gifts: economic assistance became all grants in 1981, and military aid in 1985. [cite:legalclarity_when_did_us_start_funding] [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] A strategic cooperation memorandum was signed in 1981 and suspended on 18 December that year after Israel annexed the Golan Heights; in 1987 Congress designated Israel a major non-NATO ally, letting its industries compete for American defence contracts on the same footing as NATO members'. [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance]

The memoranda, 1999–2028. From 1999 the aid was set by ten-year memoranda of understanding, agreements between the two executives that Congress then funds year by year. The first, for 1999 to 2008, provided at least $26.7 billion, including $21.3 billion in military aid, and phased out economic aid. The second, for 2009 to 2018, provided $30 billion in military aid and let Israel spend up to 26.3 percent of it on its own industry. The third, signed on 14 September 2016 for 2019 to 2028, pledged $38 billion: $33 billion in Foreign Military Financing and $5 billion for missile defence, subject to appropriations. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] Under that agreement Israel's right to spend part of the aid at home declines to nothing by 2028, after which the aid must be spent on American goods and services. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] [cite:israeled_mou_text]

The totals. Through the 2025 fiscal year the United States had provided Israel about $175 billion in current dollars: $124.5 billion in military aid, $34.3 billion in economic aid and $16.1 billion for missile defence. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] Adjusted for inflation, the Congressional Research Service estimates the aid obligated from 1946 to 2024 at about $298 billion in 2024 dollars; the commonly quoted figure of "more than $300 billion" is the Council on Foreign Relations' inflation-adjusted estimate from the same data. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] [cite:dv4_toi_rubio_nov2026] Both numbers are correct; they measure different things, and this volume uses the current-dollar figure unless it says otherwise.

The war years, 2023–2025. After 7 October 2023 the regular appropriations were joined by emergency ones. The supplemental passed in April 2024 (Public Law 118-50) added $3.5 billion in Foreign Military Financing, $4 billion for missile defence and $1.2 billion for the Iron Beam laser system, on top of the $3.8 billion provided under the memorandum that year. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] A war-reserve stockpile the United States keeps in Israel was drawn on to speed deliveries in the war's first six months, and Congress has authorized additions to it of up to $500 million a year through 2027. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] Brown University's Costs of War project counted at least $21.7 billion in American military aid to Israel in the two years after 7 October 2023, $17.9 billion of it in the first year, and noted that full details of American shipments were not public, so the totals are minimums. [cite:ap_cow_2yr]

What comes after 2028. The architecture is now being renegotiated, and the record moved again in the first week of November 2026. Formal talks on a successor to the 2016 memorandum opened in the first week of June 2026, with both governments describing the goal as a gradual move "from aid to a completely reciprocal partnership"; the American side was led by Ambassador Mike Huckabee and the State Department's counselor, the Israeli side by the Defense Ministry's director-general and Israel's ambassador in Washington. [cite:dv4_wiki_next_mou] Israel's prime minister had proposed in early 2026 to taper the aid to zero over ten years. [cite:ji_mou_talks_launch] [cite:dv4_wiki_next_mou] On 3 November 2026 President Trump said that military aid to Israel would end by the end of the year. On 9 November Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the President "would like for the financing portion to end as soon as possible," that Israel was now rich enough to buy American weapons, and that "we'll negotiate the timeline." The current memorandum, however, runs to the end of September 2028, and the reporter who recorded Rubio's remarks judged the President's statement almost certainly inaccurate on that point. [cite:dv4_toi_rubio_nov2026] The same day the Israeli outlet Ynet reported a draft under which Foreign Military Financing would continue at $3.3 billion a year for five years after 2028 and then be phased out. [cite:dv4_antiwar_ynet_nov2026] As of 11 November 2026 no new agreement has been signed.

What the architecture shows. American aid to Israel is not a gift from a city; it is a set of laws, agreements and appropriations made by the government the city houses, and it took a quarter of a century after 1948 to become large. Once built, it was durable: it grew through Republican and Democratic administrations and congresses alike, and it was fixed in ten-year agreements that outlast any single election. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] It was also designed, in its own text, to return most of the money to American industry. [cite:israeled_mou_text] Whether the arrangement now ends, shrinks or turns into joint production is the open question of the moment. Whatever the answer, the record to date supports one conclusion this volume needs: from the 1970s on, Washington supplied Israel with material support on a scale that made it the largest cumulative recipient of American aid since the Second World War. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025]
