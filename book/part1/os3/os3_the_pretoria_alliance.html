The Pretoria Alliance, 1975–1994

In 2010 the historian Sasha Polakow-Suransky obtained the declassification of South African government documents, published by The Guardian in May of that year. Among them were "top secret" minutes of a meeting on 31 March 1975 at which South Africa's defence minister, P. W. Botha, asked for nuclear-capable Jericho missiles and, according to the minutes, Israel's defence minister, Shimon Peres, offered them "in three sizes". A memorandum by South Africa's chief of staff said such missiles would need warheads made in South Africa "or acquired elsewhere". [cite:guardian_israel_sa_nuclear_2010] [cite:mg_israel_sa_nuclear_2010] Weeks earlier the two ministers had signed a secret military agreement, known as SECMENT, whose very existence was to be kept confidential. [cite:mg_israel_sa_nuclear_2010] The missile deal did not go ahead, reportedly because of its cost.

Israel disputes the reading. Peres's office stated that "Israel has never negotiated the exchange of nuclear weapons with South Africa" and that the report rested on a "selective interpretation" of the documents; Israel had tried to prevent their declassification. Some reporting reads "three sizes" as conventional, chemical and nuclear; the former South African foreign minister Pik Botha called a formal nuclear offer unlikely. [cite:mg_israel_denial_2010] [cite:mg_pik_botha_denial_2010]

South Africa built six nuclear weapons of its own and dismantled them under international supervision in the early 1990s. A double flash recorded by an American Vela satellite over the South Atlantic in September 1979 has been attributed by some to a joint test; it remains officially unresolved, and it is a question here, not a finding.
