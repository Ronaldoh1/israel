Part Thirty-Five established a double record. Pressure on the divine feminine is documented: a Franciscan of 1576 wanted the Tepeyac pilgrimage corrected because it kept the name of Tonantzin, colonial Spain allowed only Catholicism in Cuba, Artemis's image at Ephesus was destroyed with a boast, and the Isis festival of the ships was suppressed in Egypt in the sixth century. [cite:vt36_sahagun1576] [cite:vt36_wiki_santeria] [cite:vt36_wiki_artemision] [cite:vt36_wiki_navigium] Persistence is documented as well: the Guadalupe devotion, first narrated in print in 1648–1649, was adopted by the archbishops and crowned by the canonization of Juan Diego on 31 July 2002; Pachamama, Oshún and Yemayá live on in Andean and Afro-Caribbean devotion paired with Virgins the popes honoured; and when female figures from the Amazon synod were thrown into the Tiber in October 2019, it was lay critics who threw them and the pope who apologised. [cite:vt36_wiki_huei] [cite:vt36_va_jd2002] [cite:vt36_wiki_yemoja] [cite:vt36_cwr_pacha2019] The claim that Mary is Isis renamed fails on the dated evidence for each image and title, while the demotion of Wisdom and of Mary Magdalene is documented, and the institution's own teaching raises Mary as high as it allows any creature and no higher.
