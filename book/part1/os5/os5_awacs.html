AWACS, 1981

In 1981 President Reagan approved an $8.5 billion sale to Saudi Arabia of five AWACS airborne radar planes and enhancements for its F-15 fighters, and formally notified Congress on 1 October. AIPAC led the opposition, convening a national emergency conference; at one point 50 senators had co-sponsored a resolution of disapproval. On 14 October the House voted 301 to 111 to block the sale. [cite:martinkramer_awacs] [cite:dailyalert_awacs_bendavid]

Blocking required both chambers. The White House lobbied the Senate intensively, and Reagan said that "it is not the business of other nations to make American foreign policy". Eight first-term senators, seven of them Republicans, changed their votes, and on 28 October the Senate rejected disapproval by 52 to 48; the sale went ahead. [cite:martinkramer_awacs] [cite:ultimate70s_awacs_nyt] In the same months Reagan described Israel as a major strategic asset and committed to its military edge. [cite:revueconflits_awacs]

AWACS is remembered as both AIPAC's largest defeat and the fight that made it a permanent power in Congress: it lost by four votes in one chamber against a popular president spending all his capital, and the commitments made to win became lasting features of the relationship.
