Part Nine is about the people each state governs who cannot vote for its government. For Israel these are the Palestinians of the West Bank and Gaza, who hold no Israeli status, and the Palestinians of East Jerusalem, who hold permanent residency but not citizenship. Together they number more than five million. The part sets out the two legal systems that run side by side in the West Bank, the residency regime in East Jerusalem and how residency has been revoked since 1967, and how Israel's control of Gaza changed after 2005 and again after 2023, with the status of each as of October 2026. It then asks what the American counterpart is: the residents of the five inhabited US territories, who are citizens or nationals but have no vote for president or a voting member of Congress; people held by the US military abroad, from occupied Iraq to Guantanamo; and non-citizens held in immigration detention. The comparison is unequal, and the part says so plainly: no part of the American system governs a population of comparable size, under military law, for this long. The West Bank, Documented and Gaza, Documented volumes hold the full record; this part links to them rather than repeating it.
