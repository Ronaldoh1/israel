Part Fourteen asks what it costs to live in each country and how that has changed. It sets wages against prices from 1980, through Israel's hyperinflation and the long years of flat Israeli pay that followed, and America's long rise in household income with larger gains at the top. It then measures housing against income in each country's own terms, takes the household budget apart item by item (food, phones, childcare, college, health, fuel and debt), and builds the closest honest version of a family's monthly budget then and now from each country's expenditure survey. It ends with the summer of 2011, when a boycott of cottage cheese and a tent camp in Tel Aviv became, by many accounts, the largest social protest in Israel's history, and with what the government's Trajtenberg Committee changed and did not change. Each series is given with its source, year and definition, and the part says plainly where only a snapshot exists.
