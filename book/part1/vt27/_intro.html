Part Twenty-Seven turns to the one ancient church whose Bible keeps books the rest of Christianity set aside: the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. The first section tells how Christianity reached the kingdom of Aksum through Frumentius and King Ezana in the fourth century, how the church relates to Nicaea and to Rome, and how it counts its canon of 81 books, which it reaches in more than one way; it gives the full list. The next two sections summarise the books found in no other major church's Bible: 1 Enoch, with its fallen Watchers, its Son of Man and its 364-day year, then Jubilees, the three books of Meqabyan, 4 Baruch and the books of church order. The last section follows how Enoch fell out of use in the West and came back with James Bruce in 1773, and sets that beside what Rome actually did in Ethiopia in the seventeenth century.
