The six stages of this book follow the record in the order it happened. This appendix reads the same record another way: all at once, as a network, and with a question the stages could not ask. What if a part of it had been missing? Every person, fund, government and document the stages name is a node on the companion network map, and every tie between them carries the sentence and the source that establish it. Take a node out, and the map shows what would have been left to found a state, how many years of work would have been lost, and who, on the record, could have taken over.

The appendix explains how the map is built and how it weighs a removal, works through the hardest case for its rules, sets out the prepared tests and what they show, measures the two other lands the movement and its benefactors considered, and closes with what the map cannot know. The figures are not predictions. They are a disciplined way of asking which parts of the documented record the founding depended on.
