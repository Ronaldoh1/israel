The Kebra Nagast was written between 1314 and 1322, about forty years after Yekuno Amlak seized the throne, and scholars regard it as created to legitimise his dynasty; there is no historical evidence for its Solomonic legends. [cite:et_wiki_kebra] [cite:et_wh_solomonic] Its claim nonetheless reached the constitution of 1955, which tied the imperial dignity "perpetually" to Haile Selassie's line and declared his person "sacred", and it inspired a religion in Jamaica that held him divine. [cite:et_const1955] [cite:et_jstor_rasta] The coup of 12 September 1974 ended it as state ideology. [cite:et_jstor_rasta] Part Three turns to the object at the centre of the legend, which no one but one man may see.
