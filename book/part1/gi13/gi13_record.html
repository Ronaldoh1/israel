What the Record Settles

The last part sorts the volume's findings into the four kinds of statement Part One promised to keep apart: programmes that named actors stated or legislated, patterns that can be shown over time, arguments about intent, and myths or misreadings. [scene:gi1_phrase|Part One: how this volume reads the record] Each finding below rests on the documents already cited in Parts One to Seventeen; nothing new is introduced here.

Documented programmes

Both banks of the Jordan, 1925–1965. The Revisionist Union's founding programme of 1925 called for a Jewish majority "on both sides of the Jordan," sung by Betar and drawn on the Irgun's emblem, and Herut carried the claim in opposition until it merged into Gahal in 1965. [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] [cite:gi1_wiki_eastbank] [cite:gi3_jvl_herut] It was the programme of a named minority, never of the Zionist Organization or of an Israeli government, and Likud's 1977 platform dropped the east bank. [scene:gi2_bothbanks|Part Two: both banks]

The whole land, 1967–1977. The Movement for the Whole Land of Israel declared on 22 September 1967 that "no government in Israel is entitled to give up this wholeness"; it won 0.6 per cent in 1969 and passed into Likud. [cite:gi4_972_twoads] [cite:gi4_posen_manifesto] [cite:gi4_wiki_mgi] Gush Emunim, founded in 1974, stated as its aim the permanent incorporation of the whole land, and its teacher, Zvi Yehuda Kook, held that "no prime minister has the authority to renounce any part of the country." [cite:gi4_lustick_gush] [cite:gi11_wiki_zvikook] [scene:gi4_movement|Part Four: the Movement]

Party and state, 1977–2022. The 1977 Likud platform stated that "between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty." [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] The Knesset applied Israeli law to East Jerusalem in 1967 and entrenched it in 1980, and on 14 December 1981 applied "the Law, jurisdiction and administration of the State" to the Golan by 63 votes to 21. [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex] [cite:gi5_wiki_golanlaw] Likud's Central Committee voted unanimously in 2017 to extend sovereignty to "the areas of liberated settlement." [cite:gi5_toi_likud2017] Clause 7 of the Nation-State Law of 2018 made "Jewish settlement" a "national value" without saying where. [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw] The guidelines of the 37th government opened with "an exclusive and inalienable right to all parts of the Land of Israel," and the Likud–Religious Zionism agreement of 1 December 2022 committed the prime minister to a sovereignty policy in Judea and Samaria at a time of his choosing. [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines] [cite:gi5_cgtn_sov] [scene:gi5_nationstate|Part Five: the Basic Law and the coalition]

The annexation track, 2019–2025. Netanyahu pledged sovereignty over the Jordan Valley on 10 September 2019; the Trump plan of January 2020 assigned Israel about 30 per cent of the West Bank. [cite:gi6_ap_jv2019] [cite:gi6_crs_plan2020] Smotrich declared 2025 "the year of sovereignty," described his civil powers as a change to the system's "DNA" that would not look like annexation, and on 3 September 2025 proposed annexing 82 per cent of the West Bank on the principle of "maximum territory and minimum Arab population." [cite:gi6_aje_2025year] [cite:gi6_bg_nyt_smotrich] [cite:gi6_toi_82pct] The Knesset backed the principle by 71 to 13 and advanced a bill on all settlements by 25 to 24. [cite:wb_toi_71_13] [cite:gi6_toi_readings] [scene:gi6_votes|Part Six: the votes]

Every one of these texts names the land west of the Jordan, Gaza in the 1977 version, or the Golan. None claims territory in Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Iraq or Syria beyond the Golan. [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines]

Patterns over time

Holding and settling where the claim applied. Settlers in the West Bank outside East Jerusalem numbered 1,500 in 1972, 4,400 in 1977, 23,700 in 1983 and 60,300 in 1987. [cite:wb_peacenow_pop] When the court closed the seizure of private land in the Elon Moreh case of 1979, about 908,000 dunams were declared state land by 1992; when sovereignty was suspended in 2020, civil powers moved to a minister in 2023 and Area C land registration began in February 2026. [cite:wb_dweikat] [cite:wb_wiki_stateland] [cite:gi6_dcpd_uae2020] [cite:gi6_fmep_feb2026] Formal sovereignty has been declared over no part of the West Bank outside East Jerusalem. [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex]

Withdrawing under pressure or by treaty elsewhere. Israel left Gaza and Sinai in March 1957 after Eisenhower's public demand; pulled back on the Suez and Golan fronts in 1974–75; completed the return of Sinai on 25 April 1982 and of Taba in 1989; left south Lebanon on 24 May 2000; and removed every Gaza settlement in 2005. [cite:rs_cw3_app_20feb57] [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza] [cite:gi12_jvl_syrdis] [cite:gi12_ied_sinai2] [cite:gi1_csm_sinai1982] [cite:gi8_enc_taba] [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] Most of those steps came under pressure or as part of an exchange; Sharon called Yamit "the red line of our concessions," and his adviser called the Gaza disengagement "formaldehyde" for the peace process. [cite:gi1_csm_sinai1982] [cite:gi12_un_weisglass] [scene:gi12_withdrawals|Part Twelve: the withdrawals]

The buffer belt, 2024–2026. In Syria, Lebanon and Gaza, the first statement was temporary and later ones were not: "deployed temporarily" became "for an indefinite period"; five posts held "at the moment" became a zone kept until Hezbollah disarms; a line "frozen" pending withdrawal became, to the chief of staff, "a new border line," with the share of Gaza behind it rising from about 53 per cent to between 64 and 70 per cent. [cite:hz10s_cnn_480] [cite:hz10s_ih_hermon] [cite:hz8_national_five] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] [cite:gi9_olj_zamir] [cite:gi9_aj_maps] [cite:gi9_unsco_jun26] No government decision has claimed sovereignty over any of the three. [cite:gi9_nahar_saar] [scene:gi9_gaza|Part Nine: the three zones]

Maps without correction. In September 2023 and September 2024 the prime minister displayed maps on which the West Bank and Gaza appeared inside Israel; no government clarification was found. [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023] [cite:gi7_aje_phil2024] [cite:gi7_ynet_germany]

The ledger, the bill and the world's response

Parts Thirteen to Seventeen recorded what the wars since October 2023 cost, who paid, and how the world answered. What they settle is a record of acts and their consequences, not a further programme: the rule the ledger kept, and this part keeps, is that operations outside the West Bank and the Golan are reported with Israel's stated reason beside them, and are not counted as progress toward a "Greater Israel" that no document claims for those places. [scene:gi14_gaza|Part Thirteen: the ledger] The figures below are given with the body that counted them and the date, and counts made by different methods are never added together.

Gaza. The attack of 7 October 2023 killed 1,195 people in Israel, 815 of them civilians, by AFP's count cited by Human Rights Watch, and 251 people were taken into Gaza as hostages. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7] The Gaza health ministry, which does not separate fighters from civilians, counted 74,382 Palestinians killed by 12 October 2026, 1,493 of them since the ceasefire of 10 October 2025, and OCHA, citing it, gave 175,142 injured to 30 September 2026. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] Of the 71,444 dead the ministry had identified by name by 31 December 2025, 21,283 were children. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] A peer-reviewed estimate in The Lancet put deaths from traumatic injury at 64,260 to the end of June 2024, when the ministry's count stood at 37,877. [cite:gz_aj_lancet] Israel's military gives 474 soldiers killed in the ground operation. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] The World Bank, UN and EU assessment counted 76.6 per cent of housing units destroyed or damaged. [cite:gz_rdna2026] [scene:gi14_gaza|Part Thirteen: Gaza]

The West Bank and Lebanon. The UN Human Rights Office counted 1,001 Palestinians killed in the West Bank and East Jerusalem from 7 October 2023 to 17 October 2025, 213 of them children, and 59 Israelis killed by West Bank Palestinians in the same two years; Oxfam, counting calendar years from January 2023, gave 1,244 Palestinians killed by the end of 2025, against 1,036 in the seventeen years from 2006 to 2022. [cite:wb_ohchr_1001] [cite:gi14_oxfam_wb] UNRWA counted 41,951 Palestinians displaced as of August 2026, 34,176 of them from the northern camps. [cite:gi14_ocha_wbsnap] In Lebanon the health ministry, which does not separate civilians from fighters, counted 4,047 killed from 8 October 2023 to 27 November 2024, among them 790 women and 316 children, and 4,387 killed from 2 March to 11 October 2026, 8,954 in all since October 2023. [cite:hz7_olj_abiad_4047] [cite:hz8b_qna_toll] On Israel's side, the research centre Alma counted 45 civilians and 83 soldiers killed on the northern front in the first round, Israel gave 39 soldiers, a contractor and two civilians killed in 2026, and about 60,000 Israelis were displaced from the north. [cite:hz7_alma_summary] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] [cite:hz7_afp_north_return] [scene:gi14_lebanon|Part Thirteen: Lebanon]

Syria, Iran, Iraq and Yemen. Airwars' provisional count of civilians alleged killed by Israeli strikes in Syria since 2013 is 60 to 108; most of the 1,311 killed at Sweida in July 2025, by the Syrian Observatory's count, were killed by Syrians. [cite:gi15_airwars_syria] [cite:gi15_olj_sweida] In Iran, the June 2025 war killed 1,060 or more by the Foundation of Martyrs' count and 1,190 by HRANA's, and Iranian missiles killed 28 people in Israel. [cite:ir10_ap_toll] [cite:ir10_toi_numbers] For the 2026 war, the counts in Iran run from 3,468 dead (the Foundation of Martyrs, to 18 April) to at least 3,636 (HRANA, to 7 April, 1,701 of them civilians), with the Health Ministry, as reported by the UN, giving at least 2,362 civilians to 7 April; between 24 and 28 people were killed in Israel. [cite:ir11_afp_tolls] [cite:ir11_hrana_day22] [cite:gi15_ocha_iran3] [cite:ir11_wiki_strikesisrael] In Iraq the record shows depot strikes in 2019 and none found since; in Yemen, Israeli strikes killed at least 86 people on the lowest Houthi per-strike figures, 31 journalists and media workers among them by CPJ's count. [cite:gi15_aje_iraq2019] [cite:gi15_gv_ap46] [cite:gi15_cpj_31] Egypt and Jordan were not attacked, and their treaties held. [cite:gi15_aje_gas] [cite:gi15_mei_jordan] [scene:gi15_neighbours|Part Fourteen: the whole ledger]

The bill. The $3.8 billion a year is a ten-year grant under the memorandum of 14 September 2016, $3.3 billion in military financing and $500 million for missile defence, running to 30 September 2028, and the share Israel may spend at home falls to nothing in 2028. [cite:gi16_crs_aid2025] At least $21.7 billion in American military aid followed from October 2023 to September 2025. [cite:gz_cow_aid] The grant's weight has fallen, from about 24 per cent of Israel's security spending and 1.5 per cent of its GDP in 2011 to about 15 per cent and half a per cent in 2025–26, while the Bank of Israel put the gross budgetary cost of the war at about NIS 352 billion for 2023–2026 and the special American grant at NIS 25 billion. [cite:gi16_cato_bandow] [cite:gi16_inss_aid] [cite:gi16_boi_2025] The United States' own largest cost was the 2026 war with Iran, $43.6 billion by 3 September 2026 on Central Command's estimate. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] [scene:gi16_costs|Part Fifteen: the costs]

American lives. Twenty-eight American service members died in these theatres between October 2023 and 9 October 2026 on the Pentagon's counts as reported, a total that is this book's addition; 21 of them died in the 2026 war with Iran, by the Defense Casualty Analysis System's figure of 9 October, and 14 of the 28 deaths were hostile. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026] [cite:gi16_tp_21] [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] At least 33 Americans were killed on 7 October 2023, and ABC News counted five American citizens killed in the West Bank by July 2025, in cases where Israeli accounts and those of families and rights groups often conflict. [cite:gi16_kshb_oct7] [cite:gi16_abc_musallet] [scene:gi16_americans|Part Fifteen: the Americans killed]

The courts, the UN and the states. The International Court of Justice ordered Israel in 2024 to prevent genocidal acts, to let aid in "at scale" and to halt its Rafah offensive; it will not rule on whether genocide was committed before Israel's rejoinder, due on 22 May 2029. [cite:gz_oj_icj2024] [cite:gi17_icj_order0328] [cite:gz_lawfare_rafah] [cite:gz_icj_timelimits] In its advisory opinion of 19 July 2024 it found Israel's continued presence in the occupied territory unlawful and said settlement must cease and settlers be evacuated; Netanyahu answered that "the Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land." [cite:wb_livelaw_icj2024] [cite:wb_jurist_icj2024] The International Criminal Court's warrants of 21 November 2024 for Netanyahu and Gallant stand unexecuted, and the United States sanctions the court's officials. [cite:gz_un_icc] [cite:ow11_wiki_eo14203] The United States vetoed six Gaza ceasefire drafts and Palestine's full membership before the Security Council endorsed the American plan in Resolution 2803; the General Assembly demanded an end to the occupation by 124 votes to 14; the UN's Commission of Inquiry found genocide, which Israel called "distorted and false"; and UNIFIL's mandate ends on 31 December 2026. [cite:ow11_afp_veto2025] [cite:gi17_wiki_res2803] [cite:wb_ahram_unga2024] [cite:gz_un_coi2025] [cite:gz_ctv_coi2025] [cite:gi9_un_2790] By September 2025, 157 of 193 UN members recognised Palestine, and five governments sanctioned Ben-Gvir and Smotrich; no Arab treaty or Accords state broke with Israel. [cite:gi17_aje_recog2025] [cite:gi17_aje_sanctions2025] [scene:gi17_un|Part Sixteen: the world's response]

The programme and 1948. Every programme documented in this volume was carried by a state's cabinet, Knesset, army or ministries; the idea of a larger land was older than the state, in the Zionist Organization's map of 1919 and the Revisionists' "both sides of the Jordan," and no government adopted the whole-land claim between 1949 and 1967. [cite:gi4_cie_june19] [cite:gi2_bu_zo1919] [cite:gi2_ej_revisionists] [cite:gi3_jvl_herut] The programme therefore presupposes 1948 and 1967 together; the idea preceded both. [scene:gi18_dependency|Part Seventeen: the programme and the state] The state within its pre-1967 lines was admitted to the UN in 1949 and is recognised by most states, while the holding of land beyond them has been rejected by the Security Council, the General Assembly and, in advisory form, the International Court, a view Israel's government rejects. [cite:rs_rd6_ecf_273] [cite:gi18_icj_pr2024] [cite:gi18_idi_icj2024] [scene:gi18_legitimacy|Part Seventeen: two questions of legitimacy]

What is argument

Intent beyond the stated aims. Whether Ben-Gurion's partition as "the beginning" in 1937 and his eighth point at Sèvres in 1956 were a programme or a posture; whether Labor's tolerance of the Sebastia settlers showed consent or weakness; whether Netanyahu shares the purpose Smotrich described; whether the buffer zones are perimeters or frontiers: each rests on documents on both sides, and this volume has given both. [cite:gi2_wiki_bgletter] [cite:rs_cw3_shlaim_sevres] [cite:rs_cw3_jvl_ike_bg] [cite:gi4_lustick_gush] [cite:gi6_bg_nyt_smotrich] [cite:gi9_carnegie_ring] The disputed sentence in the Amos letter carries no finding on its own. [cite:gi2_comm_morris] [cite:gi2_hnn_karsh] Netanyahu's "very much" of August 2025 documents attachment to a vision he did not define; that it reveals a policy toward Jordan, Egypt, Lebanon, Syria or Iraq is an inference. [cite:gi7_afp_i24] [cite:gi7_olj_pendant]

"From the Nile to the Euphrates" as a programme. The phrase rests on one reading of Genesis 15:18. [cite:gi1_bg_gen1518] [cite:gi1_ebg_brookegypt] Its documented carriers are a 1898 diary note never acted on, a minority of religious nationalist groups, a rabbi's answer to a UN committee in 1947 (which he narrowed, when pressed, to exclude Egypt, Syria, Lebanon, Iraq and Transjordan), a minister's remark about Jerusalem expanding "to Damascus" and an ambassador's "It would be fine if they took it all," which he called hyperbolic. [cite:gi1_pipes_calumny] [cite:gi1_wiki_greaterisrael] [cite:gi10_djn_fishman] [cite:gi11_newarab_damascus] [cite:gi11_aje_huckabee] Hamas's covenant asserts it as Zionist aim. [cite:hm_avalon_charter] No law, platform, coalition agreement or government map found adopts it.

Whether the wars served expansion. Outside the West Bank this is an argument, and both sides rest on statements. For it: Netanyahu said in May 2026 that his "directive is to go to 70" per cent of Gaza; Smotrich spoke of an "operational plan" for the "voluntary emigration" of Gazans; Katz spoke of depopulating Lebanese border villages to create a "buffer zone"; and Netanyahu said Israel would remain in south Lebanon "as long as it is not disarmed." [cite:gi9_olj_net70] [cite:gz_jc_smotrich] [cite:gi14_npr_yaroun] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] Against it: the plan Israel accepted says it "will not occupy or annex Gaza," Netanyahu said "we don't want to govern it," Israel signed a framework in June 2026 for withdrawing from Lebanon, and no law or government decision claims any of the three zones. [cite:gz_almon_plantext] [cite:gz_mm_netanyahu_aug25] [cite:hz8b_almon_framework] [cite:gi9_nahar_saar] In Iran, Iraq and Yemen the stated aims concerned weapons, armed groups, attacks on Israel and, in 2026, Iran's government; no document in either side's record joins those wars to a territorial claim. [cite:ir10_idf_jun14] [cite:ir13_pbs_bibi2026] [cite:gi15_aje_iraq2019] [scene:gi15_iran|Part Fourteen: Iran]

The money and the "dragged in" claim. That American aid frees Israeli money for operations and settlements holds in principle, and Congress once deducted settlement spending from loan guarantees; on the numbers, the regular grant is a modest and shrinking part of a war bill Israel has mostly paid itself, and Netanyahu now proposes phasing it out. [cite:gi16_jvl_loans] [cite:gi16_boi_2025] [cite:gi16_ji_netanyahu] [scene:gi16_aid|Part Fifteen: the fungibility test] That Israel dragged the United States into war is likewise argued: the record shows Israeli action shaping when the United States fought in 2026, and American presidents deciding that it would. [cite:ir14_aje_rubio] [cite:ir14_sn_forced] Why support persists has documented drivers in law, industry, intelligence, lobbying money and a shared war with Iran; which of them decides policy is argument, and disclosure of lobbying money is not proof that it caused any vote. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] [cite:pm_israelite_2024] [scene:gi17_support|Part Sixteen: why support persists]

Without 1948. Whether a state would have been declared and fought for whatever the Assembly voted, and what would have remained had the movement settled in East Africa or Argentina, cannot be proved. The labelled argument of Part Seventeen is that refuge and legal grant could have travelled, that the whole-land claim could not, and that buffer-zone expansion for security could arise anywhere. [cite:gi18_shindler_1947] [cite:rs_rd1_israeled_sixth] [cite:gi18_ency_birobidzhan] [scene:gi18_uganda|Part Seventeen: the Uganda Test]

What is myth or misread

Part Ten's ledger stands: no one has produced the Knesset inscription, which the Christian Science Monitor withdrew in 1980; the flag's stripes come from the prayer shawl; the 10-agora coin copies a coin of Mattathias Antigonus; Yinon's essay forecast fragmentation and named only Sinai as an Israeli aim; "A Clean Break" urged keeping the Golan, not taking more. [cite:gi10_csm_1980] [cite:gi10_mfa_flag] [cite:gi10_boi_coins] [cite:ir13_mb_yinon] [cite:ir13_km_cleanbreak] [scene:gi10_myths|Part Ten: the ledger] The research also corrected claims circulating on the other side and in this library's own sources: the Herzl note is authentic, not fabricated; "Third Kingdom" is not in the Knesset speech of 7 November 1956; the United States voted for Resolution 497; the Litani clause of 1919 was the mainstream's water request, not a Revisionist frontier; and Colombia joined the United States in recognising the Golan annexation in August 2026.
