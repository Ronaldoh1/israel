Book One of this volume asked whether the Holy See's refusal of Zionism was a front, and answered it in [scene:vt19_verdict|Part Nineteen]. Book Two, "The Church Before the State: What Rome Took, What It Buried, What Survived", turns to the institution's foundations: how a movement around a Galilean teacher became an imperial church, which texts it kept and which it excluded, and how it treated the groups that taught direct, unmediated knowledge of God, from the second-century teachers of gnosis to the Cathars, Templars, mystics, fraternities, witches and the forbidden arts, ending with the excluded texts themselves laid out in full. Its working thesis, set out on Ron's platform, is that institutional religion built a mediated and obligatory system of priests, tithes and Sunday duty on top of teachings about a kingdom within; the book lays the record against that thesis, saying plainly where it holds, where popular versions overstate it, and where the evidence runs out. Part Twenty starts at the beginning, before any bishop of Rome had special standing: what the earliest Jewish, Roman and Christian sources show about Yeshua and his teaching on the kingdom, what Paul, who never met him in life, says about his own revelation and his dealings with Peter and James, and how the first communities met, led themselves and shared money in the century and a half before Constantine.
