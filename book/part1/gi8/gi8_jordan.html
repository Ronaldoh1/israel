Jordan: a Treaty Border and the "Jordan is Palestine" Current

Jordan is the neighbour most often named in Greater-Israel maps, because the Revisionist Zionist movement of the 1920s and 1930s claimed both banks of the Jordan River, east as well as west. [cite:gi1_wiki_greaterisrael] That history is told in Part Two. [scene:gi2_bothbanks|Part Two: both banks of the Jordan] After 1967 the claim to the East Bank faded from Israeli party programmes, and a different idea took its place on the Israeli right: that Jordan already was the Palestinian state, so that none was needed west of the river. This section traces that idea to the named officials who held it, sets it beside the 1994 peace treaty, which fixed the border and recognised Jordan's territorial integrity, and ends with the March 2023 episode in which a minister's podium map and his government's Foreign Ministry pointed in opposite directions.

"Jordan is Palestine": who said it

The slogan was used on both sides of the river, which is part of its history. King Hussein of Jordan said in 1981 that "Jordan is Palestine and Palestine is Jordan". On 31 July 1988 he said the opposite: "Jordan is not Palestine." Pipes and Garfinkle read the reversal as tactical. [cite:gi8_pipes_jordanpal] On the Israeli side, the clearest statement in the sources comes from Yitzhak Shamir, writing in 1982 that the Palestinian Arabs' homeland "is Trans-Jordan, or Eastern Palestine." The historians Daniel Pipes and Adam Garfinkle, who quote the passage in their 1988 Commentary article "Is Jordan Palestine?", wrote that the argument "has long underlain the policy of the Likud bloc as a whole." They also linked Ariel Sharon to the idea, without a direct quotation, and themselves judged it "historically wrong" and its implementation "extremely dangerous." [cite:gi8_pipes_jordanpal] The original of Shamir's article was not retrieved for this book; the wording is as Pipes and Garfinkle give it.

Sharon is the figure most associated with the idea. The political scientist Marc Lynch, writing for the Middle East Research and Information Project in June 2004, described the view of Sharon and the Likud right that a Palestinian state already existed in Jordan as the Sharon vision that "Jordan is Palestine", reported that Sharon had at one time favoured the overthrow of King Hussein so that a Palestinian state could be set up on the East Bank, and noted that Sharon abstained in the Knesset vote on the 1994 treaty. [cite:gi8_merip_nojordan] These are attributions by analysts, not Sharon's own texts; no statement by Sharon in his own words was retrieved for this book, and the attribution should be read with that limit. The idea, as documented, was a political argument about where a Palestinian state should be, not a claim to annex Jordanian territory to Israel, but Jordanian officials have consistently read it as a threat to the kingdom's existence.

The 1994 treaty

On 26 October 1994 Yitzhak Rabin and Jordan's prime minister, Abdelsalam al-Majali, signed a peace treaty at the Arava crossing north of Eilat, with President Bill Clinton present; the Knesset approved it by 92 votes to 3. [cite:gi8_wiki_jotreaty] In Article 2 the two states "recognise and will respect each other's sovereignty, territorial integrity and political independence." Article 3 delimits the border "with reference to the boundary definition under the Mandate" and makes it "the permanent, secure and recognised international boundary", adding that this is "without prejudice to the status of any territories that came under Israeli military government control in 1967", that is, the West Bank. Article 9 records Israel's respect for "the present special role of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan in Muslim Holy shrines in Jerusalem." [cite:gi8_incore_jotreaty] The treaty also settled water: Israel would supply Jordan with 50 million cubic metres a year. [cite:gi8_wiki_jotreaty]

Two small areas show the treaty working in Jordan's favour. At Naharayim (Baqura), land Israel had held since 1950, and Tzofar (al-Ghamr), taken in 1967, Israel recognised Jordanian sovereignty while Israeli farmers kept the use of the land under 25-year arrangements that Jordan could decline to renew on a year's notice. [cite:gi8_wiki_jotreaty] [cite:gi8_ap_baqura] Jordan gave notice in October 2018. On 10 November 2019 King Abdullah II told parliament of "the expiration of the Peace Treaty annexes on Ghamr and al-Baqura" and the restoration of "full sovereignty". Israel's Foreign Ministry said Israel "regrets Jordan's decision to terminate the annexes", and complied. [cite:gi8_ap_baqura] Land Israel had used for decades thus passed fully to Jordan under the treaty's own terms, without dispute over the outcome.

Jordan's answer: "never an alternative homeland"

Jordan's monarchy has answered the "Jordan is Palestine" idea in the same words for two decades. On 7 January 2004, addressing officers from fourteen Arab countries in Amman, King Abdullah said the question of a substitute country "is being raised from time to time by different parties" and that "Jordan will never be an alternative homeland to anybody." [cite:gi8_king_althomeland] According to Lynch, in March 2004 the king also warned Sharon in person not to encourage talk of a "Jordan option", and repeated the concern to President George W. Bush that June. [cite:gi8_merip_nojordan]

March 2023: the podium map and the Foreign Ministry

The current resurfaced in March 2023. Bezalel Smotrich, finance minister and leader of the Religious Zionism party, spoke in Paris at a memorial ceremony for a former far-right political activist from a podium bearing a map of "Greater Israel" that included Jordan as well as the West Bank. Axios dates the speech "Sunday, March 20"; that Sunday fell on 19 March. He said the Palestinian people were "an invention" of the past century and that "there is no such thing as a Palestinian people." Jordan's Foreign Ministry summoned Israel's ambassador, accused Smotrich of breaching the peace treaty with his speech and the map, and condemned "racist, inciting and extremist statements." [cite:gi8_axios_smotrich] The speech did not mention Jordan by name in the passages reported; the claim on Jordan was in the map. Who designed the map, and what Smotrich meant by displaying it, is not documented in the reporting reviewed.

Israel's government answered on Monday 20 March. Its Foreign Ministry stated: "Israel is committed to the 1994 peace agreement with Jordan. There has been no change in the position of the State of Israel, which recognizes the territorial integrity of the Hashemite Kingdom." The national security adviser, Tzachi Hanegbi, gave the same assurance to Jordan's foreign minister, Ayman Safadi. Smotrich himself did not comment on Jordan's accusation. [cite:gi8_ynet_mfajordan] [cite:gi8_axios_smotrich] The government did not disown him or remove him; it restated the treaty. What the record shows

Against Jordan, the binding record runs one way: a ratified treaty recognising Jordan's territorial integrity, two leased areas returned on request, and a 2023 Foreign Ministry reaffirmation issued within a day of a minister's provocation. [cite:gi8_incore_jotreaty] [cite:gi8_ap_baqura] [cite:gi8_ynet_mfajordan] The counter-current is also documented, but at a different level: a 1982 statement by Shamir, the Likud right's "Jordan is Palestine" argument attributed to Sharon by analysts, and Smotrich's 2023 map. Those are statements and displays by named politicians, and none has become law or government policy. Their significance lies in the reaction they draw from Amman, which reads them as a threat to the kingdom, and in the fact that the 2023 government answered its own minister with a restatement of the treaty rather than a rebuke. What Netanyahu's own August 2025 remark added, and how Jordan answered it, is in Part Seven. [scene:gi7_interview|Part Seven: the "very much" interview]
