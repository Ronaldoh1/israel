From Petition to Revolt, 1929–1939

The 1920s closed in blood, and the people of the land were among those who shed it. A dispute at the Western Wall that began on Yom Kippur 1928 ended in August 1929 in killings in Jerusalem, Safed and above all Hebron, in the Hebron Massacre of 24 August. [cite:toi_diaries_1929_officials] [cite:encyclopedia_hc_palestine] The violence undermined the hope, held by some on both sides, that Arab and Jewish leaders could share a binational country. [cite:wiki_brit_shalom] That record belongs in any account told from the Palestinian side, because it was part of their history too.

So was the organizing that went on beside it. Two months after the violence, on 26 October 1929, about 300 women met in Jerusalem as the first Palestine Arab Women's Congress, led by Salma al-Husseini and organized by Wahida al-Khalidi and Amina al-Husseini, and called for repeal of the Balfour Declaration and an end to Jewish immigration. [cite:rs_rd3_jerusalemstory_womens] By 1935 the five Arab parties had formed a united front. [cite:rs_rd3_frus1936_d512] That November the preacher Izz al-Din al-Qassam, who had taken a small band to the hills near Jenin, was killed by British police, and Haifa held the largest funeral the city had seen. [cite:rs_rd3_palquest_qassam]

The general strike of 1936 began from below. On 19 April a national committee in Nablus called for a general strike, the withholding of taxes and a boycott of Jewish goods; local committees across the country united on 25 April as the Arab Higher Committee, under Hajj Amin al-Husseini, Mufti of Jerusalem. Its three demands were the same ones the congresses had made for fifteen years: an end to Jewish immigration, an end to land sales to Jews, and an independent national government. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_mee_strike] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_origins_yehudai] The strike lasted about six months and was called off in October at the appeal of the rulers of neighbouring Arab states. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_cojs_gocorder] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_hcc]

Britain's answer to the revolt was a royal commission, and the commission's answer was to divide the country and move its people. The Peel report of July 1937 proposed partition and an "exchange of population" modelled on Greece and Turkey: about 225,000 Arabs to be moved out of the proposed Jewish state, and about 1,250 Jews out of the Arab one, compulsorily in the last resort. It was the first official British document to put population transfer in writing. [cite:wiki_peel_commission] [cite:rs_rd4_pw_mee_strike] To the people who would have been moved, it confirmed what they had feared since al-Khalidi's letter.

The second phase of the revolt was fought in the countryside and crushed there. Britain sent about 20,000 troops and used house demolitions, collective fines on whole neighbourhoods, indefinite detention, the outlawing of the Arab Higher Committee and the expulsion of its leaders. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_origins_yehudai] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_shindler_hughes] Between 108 and 122 Palestinian Arabs were hanged; two Jews were. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_shindler_hughes] Walid Khalidi's count from British statistics is 3,832 Arabs killed by the British and 1,200 in internal strife, 5,032 in all, with 14,760 wounded; reference works round the dead to about 5,000, and one summary holds that over a tenth of Arab men aged 20 to 60 were killed, wounded, imprisoned or exiled. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_shindler_hughes] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_hcc] The rebels also turned on Palestinians they deemed disloyal, and the Irgun, abandoning restraint in 1937, killed Arab civilians, at least 250 by the summer of 1939 by one count. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_unpacked] All of it is part of the same record.

The revolt failed, and the society that came out of it was broken in the ways that mattered most for what came next. Its leadership was divided and in exile; its men were dead, jailed or disarmed; and military cooperation between Britain and the Zionist movement had deepened. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_origins_yehudai] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_shindler_hughes] The Mufti, Hajj Amin al-Husseini, went on to Berlin, met Hitler and broadcast Nazi propaganda during the war. That was his record, made by his own choices; the overwhelming majority of Palestinian Arabs had no part in it and no means to remove him. [cite:wiki_amin_al_husseini] Britain's White Paper of 1939, which capped Jewish immigration at 75,000 over five years, gave the High Commissioner powers over land sales and promised an independent state within ten years, came as the revolt was being put down. [cite:rs_rd4_wp_wiki] [cite:rs_rd4_wp_cojs_text] It gave the people of the land, on paper, much of what they had asked for, at the moment they had lost the strength to hold it.
