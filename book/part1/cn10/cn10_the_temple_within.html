The Temple Within: the Skull, the Face and the Single Eye

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The list of books that the bishops closed still carries the inner teaching in its own words. Paul asks the church at Corinth: "Know ye not that ye are the temple of God, and that the Spirit of God dwelleth in you?" [cite:cn_bh_1cor316] Luke's Gospel has Jesus tell the Pharisees that the kingdom of God does not come in ways that can be observed, and then use a Greek phrase, entos hymon, that the King James Version renders "within you" and many modern versions render "among you" or "in the midst of you". [cite:vt20_audit_esv_luke17] The scholar Ilaria Ramelli has argued that "inside" is the correct sense, that "among" is practically unattested for the phrase in Greek, and that early readers such as Origen and Athanasius read it as "inside". [cite:vt24_ramelli] The translation chosen decides whether the kingdom is a community gathered outside the reader or a reality found inside the reader.

The inner traditions follow that thread to the head. Matthew names the place of the crucifixion: "a place called Golgotha, that is to say, a place of a skull". [cite:cn_bh_mt2733] The word comes from Aramaic for "skull", and the Latin Bible's phrase for it, Calvariae locus, gave English the name Calvary. [cite:cn_bh_mt2733] Why the hill had that name is unknown. Commentators have suggested its bare, rounded shape, and an old tradition reported by Origen held that Adam was buried there. [cite:cn_bh_mt2733] In Genesis, Jacob wrestles through the night and names the place Peniel, "the face of God", saying "I have seen God face to face". [cite:cn_esv_gen3230] And in the Sermon on the Mount the King James text reads: "if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light." [cite:cn_wiki_mt622]

Read together, these passages make a map. The death and rising of Christ happen at the place of the skull. The meeting with God happens at the face. The light that fills the whole body enters through one eye, not two. The inner reading proposes that these are not only places on a map of Judea but stations of an inward event: the old self dies "in the skull", attention gathers into a single point behind the brow, and what was outside, the face of God, is met within. In this reading the temple Paul names is the body, and its holy of holies is the head.

Modern readers often add a gland to this map. In 1640 the philosopher René Descartes wrote that the pineal gland, a small organ deep in the brain, was "the principal seat of the soul, and the place in which all our thoughts are formed", because it was the one part of the brain that is not doubled. [cite:cn_sep_pineal] Few accepted the idea in his lifetime and it was almost universally rejected after his death; the anatomist Nicolaus Steno showed that Descartes had the anatomy wrong. [cite:cn_sep_pineal] Science now knows the gland as an endocrine organ that produces the hormone melatonin, in amounts that follow the cycle of day and night, and an old idea that it is the remnant of a light-sensing "third eye" survives in modified form. [cite:cn_sep_pineal] The gland that responds to light, sitting at the centre of the head, has made it the favourite modern candidate for the "single eye". That pairing is a reader's image, three thousand years younger than Genesis.

The record sets clear limits on all of this. None of these passages names the pineal gland or any part of the brain. The scholars who have studied the Greek word for "single", haplous, find that it can mean "generous" as well as "single-hearted", and the verse may have been heard by its first audience as a teaching about a generous eye against a grudging one, or about undivided devotion to God; some think the double meaning was intended. [cite:cn_wiki_mt622] Golgotha was a real place outside a real city. The inner reading does not replace these meanings. It sets another one beside them, and it is strongest where the canon itself points inward, as in Paul's temple and Luke's kingdom.
