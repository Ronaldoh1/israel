"Czechoslovak Arms Saved the State"

In 1948, the guns that defended the new state of Israel came from the communist East. Many readers assume the United States armed Israel from its founding. It did not. Washington kept an arms embargo in place, and the government willing to sell to the Jewish side was communist Czechoslovakia, acting with Soviet consent. The American-Israeli arms relationship came years later. In the first year, the decisive supplier was the Soviet bloc.

The pipeline opened before the state existed. On 14 January 1948, the Jewish Agency, which represented the Jewish community of Palestine, signed its first arms contract with Czechoslovakia. The deal was coordinated through "Secretariat D" of the state engineering-works holding. [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] [cite:wiki_operation_balak] The date matters for two reasons. It came four months before independence. And it came before the UN Security Council's arms embargo, Resolution 46, of 17 April 1948. Czechoslovakia itself became a communist state in the February 1948 coup, and from then on its sales went forward with Stalin's consent. [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] The decision fits Soviet diplomacy in the same months, which backed partition and granted the new state its first de jure, or full legal, recognition ([scene:so_s01|Moscow's early support]).

The most striking deliveries came by air. Through Operation Balak, a secret airlift ran from May to August 1948 between Ekron and the Žatec airfield west of Prague, code-named "Zebra." The planes carried arms, ammunition and personnel. They also carried fighters: the Avia S-199, a Czech-built derivative of the German Messerschmitt Bf 109, dismantled and crated inside C-46 and C-54 transports. About 25 were delivered, and they formed 101 Squadron. [cite:wiki_operation_balak] [cite:machal_avia_s199] The airlift did not stay secret. A defector exposed the Zebra base, and Washington pressed the United Nations to have it shut. Operations moved to Nikšić in Yugoslavia.

More weapons came by sea. They included some 35,000 German-pattern Karabiner 98k rifles and heavy weapons, many of them first produced for the Wehrmacht. In a 1952 report, the Czechoslovak Foreign Minister, Viliam Široký, put the receipts from the sales at almost $14.5 million. [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] Part of that money came from the American fundraising of early 1948 described in the previous chapter.

The Western powers were not neutral bystanders. Britain supplied aircraft and training to the Arab air forces, while the United States kept its embargo in place. [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] The result was the reverse of the later alignment. In 1948 the communist bloc armed the new state, and the United States declined to.

The new state's founding leader left no doubt about how he weighed these shipments. In 1968, Ben-Gurion said: "Czechoslovak arms saved the State of Israel, really and absolutely." [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] It was one man's verdict, given twenty years after the fact.

The Soviet bloc's role was neither sentimental nor lasting. Within months, according to the same sources, it began shipping the same kind of weapons toward the Arab side. [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] The pipeline of 1948 reflected a brief convergence of interests, not a permanent alliance. The military bond with the United States that later defined Israel's arms supply did not yet exist.
