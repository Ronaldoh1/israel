Part Sixteen asks why the promise keeps its hold. It begins with what Americans now say about the Dream, using polls that have asked the same questions over years, and sets their beliefs about mobility beside the measured rates, with the studies that find people overestimate how often the poor rise and the one that finds the opposite. It then examines the habits of mind that lead people to read the outcome of a structure as a verdict on character, from belief in a just world to meritocracy and the survivorship bias of success stories, with the evidence against each. The second section sets the phrases both parties have used for the Dream, from "bootstraps" and "job creators" to "ownership society," "you didn't build that" and "make America great again," beside what each administration's enacted laws did to the mechanisms traced in earlier parts. The last section turns to the record in the body: the rise in deaths from drugs, alcohol and suicide among Americans without a degree, the dispute over its cause, the fall in overdose deaths since 2023, and the gap in life expectancy between rich and poor.
