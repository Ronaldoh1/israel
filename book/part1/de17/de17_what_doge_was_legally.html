What DOGE Was in Law

For most of 2025 the public called DOGE a department and its leader Elon Musk. In law it was neither. It was a renamed White House office, a temporary organization inside it with a fixed end date, and small teams placed in agencies, all created by one executive order without an act of Congress. This section sets out that form, who was in charge on paper and in practice, how the courts dealt with the questions it raised, and what remained as of October 2026.

The order

Executive Order 14158, signed on 20 January 2025, "establishes the Department of Government Efficiency to implement the President's DOGE Agenda." First, it renamed the United States Digital Service, a technology unit the Obama administration had created after the troubled launch of HealthCare.gov, as the United States DOGE Service (USDS), "established in the Executive Office of the President," with an administrator "who shall report to the White House Chief of Staff." [cite:de17_eo_text] [cite:de17_fnn_structure] Second, it set up inside USDS a "U.S. DOGE Service Temporary Organization," "dedicated to advancing the President's 18-month DOGE agenda," which "shall terminate on July 4, 2026." Third, it told every agency head to create a "DOGE Team of at least four employees" within thirty days, typically "one DOGE Team Lead, one engineer, one human resources specialist, and one attorney," who could include special government employees. [cite:de17_eo_text]

The temporary organization was built on section 3161 of title 5 of the U.S. Code, which lets a president or Congress create a body "for a specific period not in excess of three years" and lets its head hire staff outside the normal competitive rules for terms of up to three years. [cite:de17_vlex_3161] Its stated programme was a "Software Modernization Initiative." Its most consequential sentence told agency heads, "to the maximum extent consistent with law," to give USDS "full and prompt access to all unclassified agency records, software systems, and IT systems." In return, "USDS shall adhere to rigorous data protection standards"; the order did not say what those standards were. [cite:de17_eo_text] How that clause was used is the subject of [scene:de19_the_access|Part Nineteen].

Special government employees

A special government employee is a temporary federal worker, normally limited to 130 days of service in a year, who is subject to lighter ethics and disclosure rules than a full-time official. On 3 February 2025 the White House confirmed that Musk held this status: unpaid, with a government email address and White House office space, and, a White House official said, unlikely to have to file a public financial disclosure report. [cite:de17_ap_sge] Others at DOGE used the same status to keep their private jobs, as [scene:de18_the_team|Part Eighteen] records. In August 2026 the Government Accountability Office (GAO), Congress's auditor, counted at least 206 DOGE personnel in Executive Office posts between 20 January 2025 and 31 January 2026; at least 27 of them, Musk among them, were special government employees, and at least 128 had left by the end of that period. [cite:de17_gao_108403] [cite:de17_nextgov_108403] The Executive Office of the President did not give GAO its records of ethics training or financial disclosures for these staff, and ten agencies did not answer at all. The Office of Government Ethics had no plans to review USDS's ethics programme. [cite:de17_gao_108403]

Who was in charge

Who ran DOGE was answered differently in court and in public. On 17 February 2025, in a suit brought by fourteen states, the director of the White House Office of Administration, Joshua Fisher, declared that Musk was a "Senior Advisor to the President," not an employee of USDS or its temporary organization, and that "Mr. Musk has no actual or formal authority to make government decisions himself. Mr. Musk can only advise the President and communicate the President's directives." [cite:de11_audit_fisher] [cite:de17_audit_fisher] For weeks the administration did not say who the administrator was; on 24 February a government lawyer told a judge he did not know. The next afternoon the White House named Amy Gleason, who had worked at the Digital Service from 2018 to 2021 on the federal coronavirus response and returned in January, as acting administrator. [cite:de17_ap_gleason] In a sworn declaration in March she stated: "Elon Musk does not work at USDS," and "I do not report to him, and he does not report to me." She reported to the chief of staff, Susie Wiles. [cite:de17_bi_gleason]

The president described it otherwise. In his address to Congress on 4 March 2025 Trump said he had "created the brand new Department of Government Efficiency, DOGE," "which is headed by Elon Musk, who is in the gallery tonight"; lawyers suing DOGE cited the line in court the next day. [cite:de17_abc_address] Judges split on whether the difference mattered. On 18 March Judge Theodore Chuang of Maryland found that Musk's role in closing USAID likely violated the Constitution's Appointments Clause, which requires officers who wield significant authority to be appointed through the proper process: to deny the claim because Musk had no formal title "would open the door to an end-run around the Appointments Clause." [cite:de17_upi_chuang] Ten days later the Fourth Circuit stayed his order. Judge Marvin Quattlebaum, joined by Judge Paul Niemeyer, wrote that "unconventional does not necessarily equal unconstitutional" and that the evidence showed "a strong likelihood that he functioned as an advisor to the President." Judge Roger Gregory agreed to the stay only because, in his view, the plaintiffs had sued the wrong defendants. [cite:de17_hill_ca4]

Advisory committee or agency

The legal form also decided which transparency laws applied. On inauguration day three suits argued that DOGE was an "advisory committee" under the Federal Advisory Committee Act of 1972 (FACA), which requires such bodies to have a fairly balanced membership, hold open meetings and keep public records. [cite:de17_lawfare_faca] [cite:de17_fnn_structure] The executive order signed that evening placed DOGE inside the government instead, which, the New York Sun noted two days later, "may nullify those legal challenges." [cite:de17_nysun_faca] Two sets of plaintiffs withdrew in March and May 2025; in the remaining case the government argued that Musk, as a presidential adviser, fell within FACA's exclusion for bodies made up wholly of federal employees, and its motion to dismiss was still undecided in 2026. [cite:de17_ch_pubcit]

The Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) reaches agencies, not bodies that only advise the president. [cite:de17_ncc_cert] On 10 March 2025 Judge Christopher Cooper found that DOGE was likely an agency subject to FOIA, citing its "unusual secrecy" and "substantial authority over vast swathes of the federal government," in a suit by the watchdog group Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW). [cite:de17_reason_cooper] On 6 June 2025 the Supreme Court stayed his discovery orders, holding that parts requiring disclosure of USDS's internal recommendations were "not appropriately tailored" and that "separation of powers concerns counsel judicial deference and restraint." It did not decide whether DOGE is covered by FOIA; it said only that the question "cannot turn on the entity's ability to persuade." Three justices dissented. [cite:de17_scotus_order] The order is often described as exempting DOGE from FOIA; it did not. On 15 October 2026 the Court agreed to hear the dispute in full, as U.S. DOGE Service v. U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia (No. 25-1103), with argument expected early in 2027; the question is whether USDS "wields independent authority or is merely a board that exists to advise the president." [cite:de17_ncc_cert] The wider litigation is in [scene:de19_the_courts|Part Nineteen].

The end, on paper

DOGE wound down before its deadline. In November 2025 the director of the Office of Personnel Management, Scott Kupor, told Reuters that DOGE "doesn't exist" as a "centralized entity," with many of its functions absorbed by his office and some staff moved to a new National Design Studio. [cite:de17_reuters_noexist] The temporary organization ended on 4 July 2026. USDS did not: the order had renamed it permanently, and GAO noted that "the EO does not call for the termination of the broader USDS entity." [cite:de17_nextgov_108403] The 2027 budget request sought $35 million for the U.S. DOGE Service, House appropriators asked "the Administrator of DOGE" for quarterly staffing reports, and Gleason was still listed as acting administrator while leading a health-technology office at the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services. [cite:de17_ee_end] As of October 2026, then, the office survives under its new name, the FOIA question is before the Supreme Court, and the advisory-committee case is undecided. The worst-case reading, tested

On this part's material, documented: that the largest donor of the 2024 election was given an office with a mandate across the executive branch within three months of the vote; that the office was built by executive order alone, around a clause granting access to all unclassified agency systems; that its legal form, an adviser without authority, differed from its public form, a department "headed by Elon Musk"; and that the Executive Office of the President did not give Congress's auditor the ethics records of its staff. [cite:de11_cnn_290m] [cite:de7_os_donors2024] [cite:de17_eo_text] [cite:de11_audit_fisher] [cite:de17_abc_address] [cite:de17_gao_108403] Probable: that the advisory structure reduced the reach of FACA, FOIA and public disclosure while the work went on; that was its effect in 2025, and no official has stated it as an intent. Not supported: that the office was bought, since no document records an exchange; that DOGE descended from Yarvin's RAGE; or that Thiel had any role in creating it.

Parts of the record run against the reading. The office was proposed in public during the campaign, not arranged in private. [cite:de17_reuters_econclub] It had an end date written into it, and the temporary organization did end. [cite:de17_eo_text] [cite:de17_ee_end] Courts heard the challenges to it, and two of three Fourth Circuit judges found Musk's role likely to be an adviser's. [cite:de17_hill_ca4] Congress's auditor counted its staff and published both what it found and what it was refused. [cite:de17_gao_108403] Who those staff were is the subject of [scene:de18_the_team|Part Eighteen].
