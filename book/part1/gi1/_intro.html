Part One sets out what the phrase "Greater Israel" means before the rest of the volume tests what has been done in its name. It starts from the Hebrew, Eretz Yisrael HaShlema, "the Whole Land of Israel", and from the biblical verses on which the widest claims rest, Genesis 15:18 and Deuteronomy 11:24, set beside the narrower boundary lists of Numbers and Ezekiel and the old dispute over whether "the river of Egypt" means the Nile or a wadi in Sinai. It then separates three versions of the claim, a maximal one drawn from scripture, the Revisionist claim to both banks of the Jordan and the post-1967 claim to the land between the Jordan and the sea, and names the movements, parties and documents that held each and the Israelis and Zionists who opposed them. It also states the rule the whole book follows: the subject is what named actors stated, legislated and did, never a people.
