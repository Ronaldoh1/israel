Iraq, Iran and Saudi Arabia: the Claims Tested

Three more countries are regularly named in writing about Greater Israel: Iraq, because the Euphrates is the far river of Genesis 15:18; Iran, because it is Israel's principal adversary; and Saudi Arabia, because some maps circulated online extend the promised land into northern Arabia. Unlike the Golan, Sinai, Jordan and Lebanon, none of the three borders Israel, and Israel has never held territory in any of them. [cite:gi1_bg_gen1518] This section tests briefly what the record contains for each: what text or map puts the country into a Greater-Israel claim, whether any Israeli government has adopted that claim, and what Israel's relations with the country have actually turned on. It applies the volume's rule that "Greater Israel" is a finding only where a named actor states or legislates it.

Iraq: a river, a diary note and two papers

Iraq's place in the argument begins with scripture. Genesis 15:18 promises land "unto the great river, the river Euphrates", and Deuteronomy 11:24 repeats the river. [cite:gi1_bg_gen1518] [cite:gi1_bg_deut1124] The founder-era trace is Theodor Herzl's diary note of 1898, recording a plan to ask the Ottoman sultan for a territory from the Egyptian frontier to the Euphrates. The note is authentic; Daniel Pipes, who examined it, stresses that Herzl "did not in fact request" the territory from anyone and that it never became Zionist policy. [cite:gi1_pipes_calumny] Part Ten treats the note and the myths that grew around it, such as the claimed Knesset inscription. [scene:gi10_authentic|Part Ten: the authentic documents] [scene:gi10_myths|Part Ten: the myths]

Two later documents are cited as evidence of designs on Iraq, and both concern Iraq's government rather than its land. Oded Yinon's essay "A Strategy for Israel in the 1980s", published in February 1982 in Kivunim, a Hebrew quarterly of the World Zionist Organization's information department, argued that Arab states would and should fragment along ethnic and sectarian lines, with Iraq dividing into Shiite, Sunni and Kurdish centres. [cite:ir13_mb_yinon] [cite:ir13_wiki_yinon] Scholars disagree about its weight: Mordechai Nisan said the views were Yinon's own and not policy, while Ilan Peleg called the essay "an authentic mirror of the thinking mode of the Israeli Right" and Noam Chomsky called its views "ideological and geopolitical fantasies" rather than mainstream thinking. [cite:ir13_wiki_yinon] "A Clean Break", published on 1 June 1996 by a study group of the Institute for Advanced Strategic and Political Studies led by Richard Perle and written for Netanyahu's incoming government, recommended "removing Saddam Hussein from power in Iraq". [cite:ir13_km_cleanbreak] [cite:ir13_wiki_cleanbreak] Neither document proposes that Israel take Iraqi territory, and neither was adopted as government policy; how they have been read and misread is set out in the Iran volume and in Part Ten. [scene:ir13_papers|The Iran Architecture: the papers] [scene:gi10_misread|Part Ten: documents misread]

What Israel did do in Iraq was military and concerned weapons, not borders. On 7 June 1981 Israeli aircraft destroyed the Osirak reactor near Baghdad, and the government declared that it would not permit "an enemy to develop weapons of mass destruction against the people of Israel"; the Security Council condemned the strike unanimously. [cite:ir13_wiki_begindoc] [cite:ir13_un_res487] Iraq has never recognised Israel. [cite:ir13_vc_recog] Israel's more recent strikes on Iranian-aligned armed groups in Iraq are covered in the Hezbollah volume. [scene:hz10_iraq|The Hezbollah Architecture: Iraq] No Israeli law, platform, coalition agreement or government statement reviewed for this volume claims Iraqi territory. The one modern artefact that draws an outline into Iraq is commercial: the "Greater Israel" pendant sold by the company of the interviewer who gave one to Netanyahu in August 2025, which Haaretz reported as taking in parts of Egypt, all of Jordan, Lebanon, and parts of Syria and Iraq. [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] Part Seven examines that interview. [scene:gi7_interview|Part Seven: the "very much" interview]

Iran: an adversary, not a map

Iran appears in no Greater-Israel map or text examined for this volume, ancient or modern. Its conflict with Israel is documented on other grounds: Iran's nuclear programme, its arming of Hezbollah, Hamas and the Houthis, its leaders' calls for Israel's elimination, and Israel's strikes on Iran in June 2025 and from 28 February 2026. Iran, which had maintained de facto relations with Israel under the Shah, through a mission in Tehran that was never a formally recognised embassy, ended them after the 1979 revolution. [cite:ir13_vc_recog] [cite:ir2_wiki_embassy] Why Israel made Iran its principal target, set against what Israel's governments have said they want from it, is the subject of the Iran volume, which keeps the stated aims apart from the competing explanations. [cite:ir13_pbs_bibi2026] [scene:ir13_why|The Iran Architecture: why Iran] Treating that war as part of a territorial programme would join two separate records without a document to connect them; the simulation chapter on which this volume draws itself calls the territorial concept and the Iran security threat "analytically distinct", and nothing found here contradicts that.

Saudi Arabia: a partner on the map

Saudi Arabia's appearance in Greater-Israel claims rests on assertions that the maps extend into the Hejaz or ancient Midian. No source for that was found. The simulation chapter states that the pendant given to Netanyahu in 2025 includes Saudi territory; Haaretz's description of it, as reported, does not, and the statement should not be repeated. [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] The documented Israeli record on Saudi Arabia from 2020 to 2023 concerns diplomatic normalisation, not land. When Netanyahu addressed the UN General Assembly on 22 September 2023, his map of "the new Middle East" coloured Saudi Arabia green, as a partner in peace; the same map showed the West Bank and Gaza inside Israel with no Palestinian territory marked, which drew criticism and is examined in Part Seven. [cite:gi7_toi_un2023] [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023] [scene:gi7_maps|Part Seven: the maps at the UN]

The Saudi position in those months was stated by the crown prince. In a Fox News interview aired on 20 September 2023, Mohammed bin Salman said of normalisation: "Every day we get closer." He added: "For us, the Palestinian issue is very important, we need to solve that part", and said he hoped a deal would "ease the life of the Palestinians"; he set no explicit condition of statehood. [cite:gi8_cnbc_mbs] After the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023 the condition hardened. On 7 February 2024, outside this part's period but closing the episode, the Saudi foreign ministry said it had told Washington "there will be no diplomatic relations with Israel" unless "an independent Palestinian state is recognised on the 1967 borders with East Jerusalem as its capital." [cite:gi8_reu_saudi2024] Saudi Arabia has never recognised Israel. [cite:ir13_vc_recog] The argument, made in the simulation chapter, that normalisation without a Palestinian state is "Greater Israel's diplomatic expression" is an interpretation: it reads a diplomatic sequence as a territorial aim, and no Israeli document reviewed here states that aim with respect to Saudi Arabia.

What the test shows

For all three countries the Greater-Israel claim, where it exists at all, rests on scripture, on a diary note never acted upon, on papers by individuals that were not adopted as policy, or on a piece of jewellery; none rests on an Israeli law, platform, coalition agreement or official map. [cite:gi1_pipes_calumny] [cite:ir13_wiki_yinon] [cite:gi7_olj_pendant] Israel's actual dealings with the three, a strike on a reactor, a war with Iran and its allies, and a pursuit of diplomatic ties, have documented causes of their own. That does not show that no Israeli politician holds a wider vision: Part Seven sets out what Netanyahu said in August 2025, and Part Eleven the religious currents in which the broader biblical borders still circulate. It shows that, for Iraq, Iran and Saudi Arabia, the record reviewed here contains no programme to test. [scene:gi11_religious|Part Eleven: the religious engine]
