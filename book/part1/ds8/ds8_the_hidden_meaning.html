The Teacher and the Hidden Meaning

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The community believed that the words of the prophets had a meaning their own authors did not know, and that God had revealed it to one man. The Teacher of Righteousness, its founder, was the one to whom God revealed the hidden meaning of the prophets. [cite:ds_enc_dss] The community's commentaries on Habakkuk, Nahum, Isaiah and the Psalms apply that method line by line, reading old prophecies as coded descriptions of the community's own time and enemies. [cite:ds_brit_dss]

The inner traditions have always read scripture this way, and the reading on this shelf has done so too: Golgotha as the skull, the serpent as the spine, the Ark as the heart. The Teacher's method is the oldest documented example in the caves of a claim that the plain text conceals a second text, and that the key belongs to the one who has been given it.

Here the scrolls offer the inner traditions a mirror, and an uncomfortable one. A method that finds a hidden meaning can find it anywhere, and the community found its own story in every prophecy it read. The same method, turned outward, has built many of the theories this shelf has examined, including the theory that the scrolls themselves hid a secret about Jesus; that theory read a hidden meaning into a delay, and when the texts opened, the meaning was not there. [cite:ds_wiki_deception] The difference between a reading and a delusion is not whether a hidden meaning is sought, but whether the reader keeps the plain text and the record in view, says which is which, and lets the record correct the reading.

That is the discipline this shelf has kept, and the scrolls show why. The Teacher's key was held by one man and checked by no one, and it made a community certain that it alone was the children of light. The key the inner traditions offer is open to every reader and is held up against the record, not in place of it. On that reading, the hidden meaning of scripture is not a secret held by a teacher but a teaching about where to look: within, with the plain text still open beside it.

The record limits the reading. The Qumran commentaries are a specific genre with their own rules, and the community understood its interpretations as revealed truth, not as one reading among others. The comparison drawn here is the reader's.
