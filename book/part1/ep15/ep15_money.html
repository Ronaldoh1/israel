The Money Nobody Followed

Part Three traced the money that reached Epstein. Two streams in the record have drawn almost no attention.

The first is public money. According to a JPMorgan court filing, the Virgin Islands' Economic Development Commission granted Epstein's companies tax benefits of $219.8 million between 1999 and 2012 and $80.6 million between 2013 and 2018: about $300 million in all. [cite:ep7_rappler_jpm2023] The figure comes from a party defending itself in litigation and has not been tested at trial, but no one has disputed its order of magnitude. The territory later sued and recovered money from his estate and his bank. [cite:ep3_aj_usvi_jpm2023] The commission's own files, which would show who approved each renewal and on what evidence of local business activity, are not public.

The second is early technology money. In 2014 a company owned half by Epstein and half by Joi Ito, then director of the MIT Media Lab, put about $500,000 into the seed round of Blockstream, a bitcoin infrastructure company; Blockstream's chief executive says the stake was sold within months and that the company has no financial connection to Epstein or his estate. Epstein's gifts to the Media Lab, $525,000 by one account, helped MIT's Digital Currency Initiative hire three bitcoin developers in 2015, and Ito thanked him for it. [cite:ep15_logic_blockstream2026] Ito has said he knew nothing of Epstein's crimes. [cite:ep15_logic_blockstream2026] None of this is a crime. It is a convicted sex offender buying a seat near the foundations of a new financial system, and it has been covered mostly by the specialist crypto press.

A third stream ran the other way. In early 2017 Leon Black's company BV70 LLC lent Epstein two sums, $22.5 million and $8 million, through Plan D, LLC, a Virgin Islands company of which Epstein was the sole member. The review Black's firm Apollo commissioned from the law firm Dechert says the loans were for an art transaction, that Black demanded repayment in early 2018, that Epstein repaid $10 million, and that the rest was still owed when he died. [cite:ep15_dechert_apollo2021] The released files hold the note itself: $30.5 million, payable on demand, signed by Epstein on 17 April 2017. [cite:ep15_doj_plandnote] The same Plan D is listed as the holder of his Gulfstream G550. [cite:ep15_doj_aircraft] A 2006 FBI interview with one of his pilots records the pattern: a separate company for each plane, which the pilot understood to be for liability reasons. [cite:ep15_doj_rodgers] So the company that held one of his aircraft also held a debt to the man who paid him $158 million, and the estate's accounts are where that debt would show.

The money also moved through an address. Wires from accounts belonging to Epstein, Southern Trust, another of his companies and Darren Indyke's law practice were all marked "c/o HBRK Associates", Richard Kahn's accounting firm on Lexington Avenue, according to a 2019 TD Bank suspicious-activity report in the release. A suspicious-activity report is a bank's alert to the government, not a finding of wrongdoing. [cite:ep15_doj_tdsar] HBRK told the bank it served other wealthy clients too. Who they were is not in the public record.

All three streams lead to records that exist and are not public: the commission's approvals, the full investment ledgers of Epstein's companies, and HBRK's client books, which the estate's executors and Kahn's firm hold.
