Who Held the Ethiopian Bible

Set beside the Western story told in The Canon, the Ethiopian story shows the same pattern from a different angle. In the West, the institution that guarded the scriptures closed the list and enforced it. In Ethiopia, the list was never closed by a council, and the institution that kept it was itself repeatedly fought over: by the throne that protected it, and by every outside power that tried to take it.

The table shows a striking fact. None of these powers tried to change the books in the Ethiopian Bible. Constantius wanted a different bishop. The Jesuits wanted Roman primacy and a different calendar. The British wanted manuscripts as objects for a national museum. The Fascists wanted a subdued church and a trophy for Rome. The Derg wanted the land and an obedient patriarch. [cite:vt27_wiki_mendes] [cite:et_cup_maqdala_blog] [cite:et_wiki_obelisk] [cite:et_wiki_theophilos] What every one of them sought was control over the institution or the objects, not the content of the canon. The canon survived because it was not, in itself, the prize.

The Ethiopian church kept its own gates too. Its scriptures were read in Ge'ez, a language the people stopped speaking by about 1200. [cite:sm_eb_geez] Its holiest object is seen by one man, who names his successor. [cite:et_wiki_zion] Its land came with rights to tribute from peasants. [cite:et_wiki_landreform] And its throne claimed, through a book written by clerics close to power, a right to rule that no one could test. [cite:et_wiki_kebra] Ethiopia was not an exception to the pattern of knowledge held by an office. It was the same pattern, under a different crown.

What makes Ethiopia different is what the pattern preserved. Because the Ethiopian church never accepted the Western councils' lists, it kept Enoch, Jubilees and the other books that the Latin and Greek churches discarded, and when Europe wanted them back, it had to come to Ethiopia to find them. [cite:vt27_hessayon] The isolation that kept Rome out also kept the books in. The church that guarded them was a gatekeeper, but it was a gatekeeper that did not throw anything away.

The record also shows the cost of keeping. The monks of Debre Libanos were killed in their monastery on their feast day. [cite:et_lrb_holywar] The library of Maqdala was carried off on elephants and sold for four pounds a soldier. [cite:et_cup_maqdala] The patriarch who insisted on the church's right to consecrate its own bishops was strangled. [cite:et_wiki_theophilos] The eighty-one books are in print today because people paid for keeping them with their lives, their land and their libraries.
