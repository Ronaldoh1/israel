This book is about four brotherhoods that met behind closed doors: the Knights Templar, the Rosicrucians, the Freemasons and the Bavarian Illuminati. Each has a documented history, with founding dates, rules, members and, in three cases, the edicts that broke them. Each also has a second history, told about it by its enemies and its admirers, in which it survives in secret, holds a hidden wisdom or steers the world. This book keeps the two histories apart.

The documented record is precise and often harsh. The Templars were arrested across France at dawn on Friday 13 October 1307, tortured into confessions, and fifty-four of them were burned outside Paris in 1310 as relapsed heretics. [cite:vt29_barber_cup2] [cite:vt29_barber_cup1] The Illuminati were banned by their own prince within a decade of their founding, and by 1787 recruiting for them carried the death penalty. [cite:vt30_natgeo_weishaupt] Freemasonry has been forbidden to Catholics since 1738, and in 1935 Nazi Germany dissolved every remaining lodge and seized its property. [cite:vt30_wiki_ineminenti] [cite:od_ushmm_nazi]

The second history is where this book is most careful. From the 1790s the brotherhoods were cast as the hidden hands behind revolutions, and early in the nineteenth century that story was grafted onto the old hatred of Jews. [cite:vt30_wiki_barruel_memoirs] [cite:vt30_wiki_simonini] That graft is a conspiracy theory built on a fabricated letter and a proven forgery, and this book names it as such. The documented orders are institutions, with names, papers and edicts; the myth about them is a weapon, and Part Six shows who used it. The closing part, The Inner Reading, reads what the orders' own allegories taught about the work of building a self.
