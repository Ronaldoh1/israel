Part Two follows the teachers whom later writers grouped as Gnostics, and the contest between their schools and the bishops' offices in the second century. It introduces Basilides, Valentinus, who wanted a bishopric and lost it, and Ptolemy, whose letter to a woman student survives complete. It then reads the opponents' charges about money and women against the one Gnostic text on property that survives, Epiphanes' definition of God's righteousness as "a kind of sharing along with equality".
