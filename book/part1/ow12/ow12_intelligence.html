The Intelligence Question

The claim that Epstein was an intelligence asset, and specifically an asset of Israel's, is not one claim but several, resting on different kinds of evidence. Some are documented facts that are true but do not prove what they are used to prove. Some are reports from a single anonymous source. Some are theories with no document behind them. This section separates them, and sets each beside what the American government has said. The rule followed throughout is the one this book applies everywhere: an association between people, however close, is not evidence that a state directed either of them, and no claim here is extended from named individuals to any people collectively.

A documented fact: Robert Maxwell's funeral. Ghislaine Maxwell's father, the British publisher Robert Maxwell, was found dead in the sea off the Canary Islands in November 1991; a verdict of drowning was recorded. He was buried in Jerusalem. President Chaim Herzog delivered the eulogy and Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir attended, according to the Jewish Telegraphic Agency's report at the time; later accounts add that figures from Israeli intelligence were present. [cite:ow12_jta_maxwell1991] [cite:ow12_wiki_gmaxwell] A state funeral is a documented honour. It is not, by itself, evidence that Robert Maxwell worked for Israeli intelligence, and it is not evidence of anything about Jeffrey Epstein, whose own record is set out in the previous section. Claims that Robert Maxwell was an Israeli agent appear in books and articles; the sources reviewed for this volume contain no court finding and no released government document establishing it.

A single-source report: "belonged to intelligence." The most cited piece of evidence is a sentence attributed to Alexander Acosta. On 9 July 2019 The Daily Beast reported, from one anonymous former White House official, that Acosta, in interviews with President Trump's first transition team in 2017, had said he "was told Epstein 'belonged to intelligence' and to 'leave it alone'," and that Epstein was "above his pay grade." [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] The report named no agency and no country. Acosta denied saying it in a transcribed interview with the House Oversight Committee on 19 September 2025: "I never made that assertion." [cite:ow12_oversight_acosta] [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] The Justice Department's Office of Professional Responsibility, which examined the 2008 agreement in depth, faulted Acosta's judgment but found he had acted within his authority. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] The remark therefore stands as an anonymous, second-hand report, denied by the person to whom it is attributed. It may be true; nothing in the released record confirms it.

Theories. The Acosta report "has generated many theories about the connections between Epstein and intelligence organizations," promoted by writers such as Whitney Webb. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] The strongest version, that Epstein ran a blackmail operation for Israel's Mossad, rests on secondary accounts; the sources reviewed for this volume contain no court record, sworn testimony or released government document that supports it. Against it stands the government's own finding of July 2025 that there was "no credible evidence" that Epstein blackmailed prominent individuals, and a 2025 email from a federal prosecutor to FBI officials, reported by the Associated Press from released Justice Department records, stating that the seized materials contained no evidence implicating "anyone other than Epstein and Maxwell." [cite:ow12_abc_memo] [cite:ow12_ap_comey] Those official findings are themselves contested: the department withheld millions of pages until Congress forced their release, and critics in both parties have said its compliance was incomplete. [cite:ow12_wiki_efta] A withheld page is not evidence of what it contains, in either direction.

The documented Israeli connections. What is documented is narrower and, for this book's question, more useful. Epstein had a business relationship with Ehud Barak, the former Israeli prime minister and defence minister, that included a joint investment in an Israeli emergency-call company and dozens of meetings; and emails released by the House Oversight Committee, together with emails from Barak's account published by a hacker group calling itself Handala, which Canada's government has linked to Iranian intelligence, and partly corroborated by The Sunday Times, show the two discussing Israeli surveillance and security packages for Ivory Coast between 2012 and 2014, for which Barak commissioned plans from former Israeli intelligence officers. [cite:ow12_wiki_epstein] [cite:ow12_wiki_barak] These are records of two private men brokering business, one of them a former head of Israel's government and defence establishment. They show Epstein inside the commercial world of Israeli security technology. They do not show the State of Israel or any Israeli agency directing him, and an archive obtained by hacking is a source to be weighed with care, whatever its authenticity.

The line. The difference between those two kinds of claim is the finding of this part. A documented association, with a former official, in a business, through a partnership, is a fact and can be stated. An intelligence operation, meaning an agency recruiting, tasking or protecting Epstein, is a claim about a state, and on the public record as of October 2026 it is not established for Israel or for any other country. Where the American government has spoken, it has said there is no credible evidence of blackmail, and where it has been accused of hiding the answer, Congress has forced the files out and the review continues.
