What the Wars Cost

The wars fought since 7 October 2023 have been paid for in two treasuries, Israel's and America's, and each has published its own estimates. This section sets those figures side by side. The human toll in each theatre is counted in Parts Thirteen and Fourteen and is not repeated here. [scene:gi14_gaza|Part Thirteen: Gaza] The figures below are budget costs as each government or research group defines them; they are not added together, because they count different things over different periods.

Israel's bill

The most complete Israeli account is the Bank of Israel's. In its annual report for 2025 the central bank put the gross budgetary cost of the war at NIS 34 billion in 2023, NIS 133 billion in 2024 and NIS 116 billion in 2025, with a further NIS 69 billion projected for 2026: about NIS 350 billion in all. That total leaves out decisions taken after the Iran war began in late February 2026. [cite:gi16_boi_2025] The twelve-day war with Iran in June 2025 was not costed separately; the bank records about NIS 4 billion, later about NIS 6 billion, in compensation for property damage from Iranian missiles, and a NIS 31 billion addition to the 2025 budget that paid for that war and the fighting in Gaza. [cite:gi16_boi_2025b] For the 2026 war, Israel's Finance Ministry gave a "preliminary estimate" on 12 April 2026 of NIS 35 billion ($11.5 billion) in budgetary costs, NIS 22 billion of it for defence, and said the sum had already been added to the 2026 budget. [cite:gi16_aa_iranwar]

The bill shows in Israel's public finances. Defence spending, 4.2 per cent of GDP in 2022, reached 8.4 per cent in 2024 and 7.8 per cent in 2025; the bank assumes about 5.5 per cent a year for 2026–2030. The central government deficit was 6.7 per cent of GDP in 2024 and 4.7 per cent in 2025, and gross public debt rose from 61.3 per cent of GDP in 2023 to 68.5 per cent in 2025. [cite:gi16_boi_2025] SIPRI's figures, measured differently, put Israel's military spending at $46.5 billion in 2024 and $48.3 billion in 2025. [cite:hm_globes_sipri] [cite:hz9_sipri_2025] The Knesset passed the 2026 budget on 30 March 2026, with about $44.8 billion for defence. [cite:gi16_bd_budget2026] Against all of this, the Bank of Israel's accounts show a special American grant of NIS 13 billion in 2023 and NIS 6 billion in each of 2024 and 2025, NIS 25 billion in all. [cite:gi16_boi_2025] The previous section weighs what that grant means. [scene:gi16_aid|The aid]

America's bill: the Red Sea and the region

The United States paid twice: in aid to Israel, and for its own forces. The Costs of War project at Brown University, which publishes its methods, estimated American regional operations in the 2024 fiscal year at $4.86 billion. That included $2.4 billion from the April 2024 supplemental for operations, force protection and deterrence, and $2.4 billion for additional carrier groups and strikes on the Houthis; a carrier strike group cost about $8.7 million a day, and by June 2024 the Navy had fired about $1 billion of munitions in the Red Sea. [cite:hz12_cow_2024] The American air campaign against the Houthis began on 11 January 2024, after attacks on shipping that the Houthis said were in support of Gaza. [cite:hz10_crs_if12587] [cite:hz10_aje_truce] Operation Rough Rider, from 15 March to 6 May 2025, cost more than $1 billion by NBC's reporting and between $2.76 billion and $4.95 billion for the 2025 fiscal year's Yemen operations by Costs of War's estimate, which includes carrier time and three F/A-18 fighters lost at sea, worth about $210 million. [cite:hz10_nbc_rrcost] [cite:hz12_cow_2025] Airwars recorded at least 224 civilians allegedly killed by American strikes in Yemen in those seven weeks; Central Command made no public determinations. [cite:hz10_airwars_rr] The Red Sea campaign is told in the Hezbollah volume. [scene:hz10_redsea|The Red Sea]

From October 2023 to September 2025, Costs of War put the cost of American operations in the wider Middle East at $9.65 billion to $12.07 billion, and at $31.35 billion to $33.77 billion when the $21.7 billion in military aid to Israel is added. [cite:hz12_cow_2025]

America's bill: interceptors and two Iran wars

The most direct American contribution to Israel's defence has been interceptors. On 13–14 April 2024 American forces, including the destroyers Arleigh Burke and Carney, destroyed more than 80 drones and at least six ballistic missiles aimed at Israel; on 1 October 2024 two destroyers fired about a dozen interceptors at Iran's second barrage, and the United States then sent a THAAD battery and its crew to Israel. [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] [cite:ow7_usni_oct2024] [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024] In June 2025 American THAAD batteries fired more than 100 and possibly 150 interceptors, about a quarter of the stock, by CNN's reporting; Costs of War, assuming about 150, put their cost at $1.8 billion to $1.95 billion. With Operation Midnight Hammer, the strikes on Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan, the June 2025 war cost the United States $2.04 billion to $2.25 billion. [cite:hz12_cow_2025] [cite:ow7_cnn_thaad2025] The defence of Israel is set out in The One-Way Mirror. [scene:ow7_iran|Defending Israel, fighting Iran]

The 2026 war dwarfed these sums. Central Command estimated its cost at $43.6 billion as of 3 September 2026, more than half of it in munitions, with Patriot and THAAD interceptors in greatest demand; the Congressional Budget Office estimated $38 billion through 1 August. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] The war also raised petrol prices: the Energy Information Administration forecast an average of $3.91 a gallon in 2026 against $3.10 in 2025, which Part Fourteen of the Iran volume sets out with its caveats. [cite:ir14_eia_steo] [scene:ir14_bill|The American bill, 1979–2026]

What the two bills show

Two points stand out. The first is scale. The 2026 war alone cost the United States more by September 2026, on Central Command's estimate, than all its military aid to Israel and its regional operations in the two years after October 2023 combined, on Costs of War's. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] [cite:hz12_cow_2025] The second is who carried the cost. Israel bore the larger share of its own wars: the Bank of Israel's NIS 352 billion, with debt rising by about seven points of GDP. [cite:gi16_boi_2025] The United States bore most of the cost of the war with Iran it chose to join and lead in 2026, and of the Red Sea campaign against the Houthis, whose attacks on shipping the Houthis themselves tied to Gaza. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026] [cite:hz10_aje_truce] How far those costs followed from Israel's wars, and how far from decisions of American presidents, is the question the next section takes up.
