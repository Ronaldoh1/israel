"Czechoslovak Arms Saved the State"

A widespread assumption holds that the United States armed Israel from its founding. In 1948 it did not. Washington maintained an arms embargo, and under the embargo the government that would sell to the Jewish side was communist Czechoslovakia, acting with Soviet consent. The American-Israeli arms relationship came years later. In the first year, the decisive supplier was the Soviet bloc.

The pipeline opened before the state existed. On 14 January 1948, the Jewish Agency signed its first arms contract with Czechoslovakia, coordinated through "Secretariat D" of the state engineering-works holding. [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] [cite:wiki_operation_balak] The date matters for two reasons. It preceded independence by four months, and it preceded the UN Security Council's arms embargo, Resolution 46, which followed on 17 April 1948. Czechoslovakia itself became a communist state in the February 1948 coup, and from then on its sales went forward with Stalin's consent. [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] The decision fits the Soviet diplomacy of the same months, which backed partition and granted the new state its first de jure recognition ([scene:so_s01|Moscow's early support]).

The most striking deliveries came by air. Through Operation Balak, a secret airlift ran from May to August 1948 between the Žatec airfield west of Prague, code-named "Zebra," and Ekron. The aircraft carried arms, ammunition and personnel, and also fighters: the Avia S-199, a Czech-built derivative of the German Messerschmitt Bf 109, dismantled and crated inside C-46 and C-54 transports. About 25 were delivered, and they formed 101 Squadron. [cite:wiki_operation_balak] [cite:machal_avia_s199] The airlift did not stay secret. A defector exposed the Zebra base, Washington pressed the United Nations to have it shut, and operations moved to Nikšić in Yugoslavia.

Other weapons followed by sea. Deliveries included some 35,000 German-pattern Karabiner 98k rifles and heavy weapons, many of them originally produced for the Wehrmacht. In a 1952 report, the Czechoslovak Foreign Minister, Viliam Široký, put the receipts from the sales at almost $14.5 million. [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] Part of the money that paid for these weapons came from the American fundraising of early 1948 described in the previous section.

The Western powers were not neutral bystanders in this arms picture. Britain supplied aircraft and training to the Arab air forces, while the United States kept its embargo in place. [cite:wiki_czech_arms_israel] The result was an inversion of the later alignment: in 1948 the communist bloc armed the new state while the United States declined to.

The new state's founding leader left no doubt about how he weighed these shipments. In 1968, Ben-Gurion said: "Czechoslovak arms saved the State of Israel, really and absolutely." [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] His verdict is one man's retrospective judgment, given twenty years after the fact.

The Soviet bloc's role was neither sentimental nor lasting. Within months, according to the same sources, it began shipping the same kind of weapons toward the Arab side. [cite:jpost_czech_arms_saved_israel] The pipeline of 1948 reflected a moment of strategic convergence, not a permanent alliance, and the military relationship with the United States that later defined Israel's arms supply did not yet exist.
