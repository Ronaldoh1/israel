# Volume plan: The West Bank, Documented

**Working title:** The West Bank, Documented
**Subtitle:** Settlements, Separation and Expansion, 1947–2026
**Companion to:** Gaza, Documented (Vol XVI). The two volumes cross-link, and Part Eleven covers the split between them.
**Method:** the same as Gaza. Each part is researched, recorded claim by claim, written, then fact-checked by a separate agent. Every contested question is shown with both documented positions. Every figure is dated.

## The parts

1. **The Hill Country and the Partition, 1947–1949.**
   - What the 1947 plan allotted, by land and by population, with both readings of the land: the coastal plain and citrus belt, and the Negev desert.
   - What the West Bank was meant to be.
   - The 1948 war in the hills: Latrun, Lydda and Ramle, and the villages on the line.
   - The 1949 armistice (the Green Line), including the Triangle transfer.
2. **Under Jordan, 1948–1967.**
   - The 1950 annexation, which almost no state recognized.
   - Refugees and camps.
   - Border raids: Qibya 1953 and Samu 1966.
   - East Jerusalem under Jordanian rule.
3. **1967: Occupation, and the First Settlements.**
   - The war and the people displaced from the West Bank.
   - East Jerusalem's annexation and its expanded boundary.
   - UN Resolution 242.
   - The 1967 legal opinion from the foreign ministry's legal adviser, Theodor Meron, warning that civilian settlement would breach the Fourth Geneva Convention.
   - The Allon Plan, Kfar Etzion, and Hebron 1968.
4. **The Settlement Enterprise, 1977–1993.**
   - Likud and Gush Emunim; the Drobles plan.
   - "State land" declarations under Ottoman law, and the Elon Moreh ruling (1979).
   - The Jewish Underground.
   - The first intifada in the West Bank.
   - Settler population, by year.
5. **Oslo's Map: Areas A, B and C, 1993–2000.**
   - The 1995 Interim Agreement. Area C is about 60% of the West Bank.
   - The permit regime and demolitions.
   - Settler numbers growing through the peace process.
   - The Hebron Protocol (H1 and H2).
   - The 2005 Sasson report on unauthorized outposts.
6. **The Wall, the Roads and the Islands.**
   - The barrier since 2002; its route against the Green Line; the ICJ's 2004 opinion.
   - Checkpoints and roads restricted to Israelis.
   - The Jordan Valley; the "seam zone"; Shuhada Street.
   - Fragmentation into enclaves, measured, with Israel's security case beside each measure.
7. **Two Legal Systems, and the Watchers.**
   - Military courts and civilian courts in the same territory.
   - Conviction rates, administrative detention, children in military detention.
   - Surveillance: Blue Wolf, Wolf Pack, and the Hebron camera networks (Amnesty International's 2023 report, soldiers' testimony).
   - Israel's stated reasons, and the courts' rulings.
8. **Displacement, House by House.**
   - Demolition and displacement data from OCHA, B'Tselem, Peace Now and COGAT.
   - Masafer Yatta: Firing Zone 918 and the 2022 court ruling.
   - Khan al-Ahmar.
   - Sheikh Jarrah and Silwan: ownership claims and the courts.
   - Herding outposts.
   - Settler violence, with incident counts and prosecution rates. Huwara 2023.
   - Communities displaced since 2023.
9. **Since October 2023.**
   - West Bank deaths (OCHA), and Operation Iron Wall (Jenin and Tulkarm, 2025).
   - Record settlement approvals and state-land declarations.
   - E1, and the Knesset annexation votes.
   - Smotrich's civil powers.
   - US sanctions on violent settlers (2024) and their revocation (2025).
   - Status as of build date.
10. **The Law, and Why It Isn't Enforced.**
    - The rules: Fourth Geneva Article 49(6) and the Hague Regulations.
    - UN Security Council Resolutions 446 and 2334; the ICJ in 2004 and July 2024 (occupation unlawful, settlements to be evacuated); the UN General Assembly's September 2024 deadline; the ICC's Palestine investigation.
    - Israel's legal position: the Levy Report (2012) and the "disputed territory" argument.
    - US positions over time: the Hansell memo (1978); Reagan; Pompeo (2019); Blinken (2024); Trump's second term.
    - The enforcement gap, laid out mechanism by mechanism.
11. **The Split: Fatah's West Bank, Hamas's Gaza.**
    - 2006 to 2007, and the PA's security coordination.
    - No elections since 2006.
    - What Israeli leaders said about the division, on the record: Smotrich (2015), "the PA is a burden, Hamas is an asset"; the Qatari cash; the "no partner" argument.
    - Why the split mattered: one territory at war, while the other's annexation advanced with less attention.
    - Ron's thesis is presented with its documented basis and the counter-arguments.
12. **The Money.**
    - The Paris Protocol, and clearance revenues withheld.
    - The settlement economy, the UN database of businesses, and US tax-exempt donations to settlements.
    - The World Bank's estimate of what Area C restrictions cost.
    - Who pays for the infrastructure.
13. **Settled, Contested and Open.**

## Discipline notes for this volume

- **Partition land.** The 1947 plan gave the Jewish state about 55–56% of Mandate Palestine, when Jews owned about 6–7% of the land and were about a third of the population. That much is documented. The plan also put most of the Negev, which is largely desert, in the Jewish state, alongside most of the fertile coastal plain. The volume gives both, with figures: it neither repeats the "it was all desert" framing nor drops the Negev.
- **"Nobody does anything."** The record shows rulings and statements without enforcement, not silence: the ICJ in 2004 and 2024, UN Security Council Resolution 2334, the ICC investigation, and US sanctions in 2024. The volume names each, and names the specific reason it wasn't enforced.
- **"The split benefited Israel."** This is an argument about motive. The volume gives the documented statements and outcomes, and gives Israel's stated reasons. It does not assert intent the record doesn't show.
- **Settler violence and IDF conduct.** Counts come from the counting bodies (OCHA, Yesh Din, B'Tselem, the Israeli police and army). Named cases come with court outcomes. Israel's position on each policy is stated.
- **Time-sensitive material** (Parts Nine and Ten) is searched live at build time and marked "as of [date]".
