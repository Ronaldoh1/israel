The War Years: Pius XII and the 1943 Notes

The Second World War produced the fullest written statement of the Holy See's position on a Jewish homeland before 1948, and it was written in private, to governments, not in public. The documents were published by the Holy See itself thirty years later, and one of them circulates in full. They are the most important test of the front hypothesis for the period before Israel's independence, because they are exactly the kind of record the hypothesis predicts should contradict the public line. This section sets them out, then grades the one document offered for the opposite view: a note said to have been written by Pius XII in 1944.

What Pius XII knew of the destruction of European Jewry, what he said and did not say, and what the files opened on 2 March 2020 have added are treated in [scene:vt14_pius_files|Part Fourteen] and are not repeated here. This section keeps to a narrower question: what the Holy See said, in its own papers, about Zionism and a Jewish state in Palestine. The two questions must be kept apart. The same files that record the Holy See's opposition to a Jewish home in Palestine also record its interventions for persecuted Jews, and neither set settles the other.

Tardini and Maglione, spring 1943

The internal record of 1943 begins with Monsignor Domenico Tardini of the Secretariat of State, who wrote in March 1943 that the Holy See "has never approved the project of making Palestine a Jewish home." [cite:vt16_ej_vatican] In May 1943 Cardinal Luigi Maglione, Secretary of State, wrote to Archbishop Amleto Cicognani, the Apostolic Delegate in Washington, on the question. According to the extracts reported when the volume was published, Maglione wrote: "If Palestine fell under the rule of the Jews, it would give birth to new and grave international problems", and asked "how can you historically justify a criterion of bringing people back to those territories." [cite:vt16_jta1976] Minerbi adds that Maglione suggested other territories would better serve as a "Jewish Home" and wrote that Catholics "would be wounded in their religious sentiments" if Palestine became exclusively Jewish. [cite:vt16_ej_vatican]

The same line went to London. Maglione told Monsignor William Godfrey, the papal representative in Britain: "The Holy See has never approved the plan to make Palestine a Jewish homeland." [cite:vt16_jta1976] From Istanbul, the Apostolic Delegate to Turkey, Angelo Roncalli, later Pope John XXIII, wrote to Maglione that "the reconstruction of the Kingdom of Judah and of Israel is only a utopia." [cite:vt16_jta1976]

These letters are held in the Holy See's archives and were published in the multi-volume edition of wartime documents, Actes et documents du Saint Siège relatifs à la Seconde Guerre mondiale, prepared by four Jesuit historians under a mandate from Paul VI and issued between 1965 and 1981. [cite:vt1_wiki_adss] The Jewish Telegraphic Agency reported the extracts on 26 January 1976, days after the volume on the Holy See and the victims of the war for January to December 1943 was made public. [cite:vt16_jta1976] Grade: primary documents, published by the Holy See; the selection was made by the Holy See's editors, whose completeness critics have questioned and whose editor said they "did not deliberately overlook any significant document." [cite:vt1_wiki_adss]

Cicognani to Myron Taylor, 22 June 1943

The document that put the position before a government survives in full. On 22 June 1943 Cicognani wrote to Myron C. Taylor, President Roosevelt's personal representative to Pius XII, as letter no. 219/43, following "our conversation of a few days back." The text now circulates through the Simon Wiesenthal Center. [cite:vt16_cicognani1943] It covers two subjects, and the order matters. It first records that "The aid of the Holy See has recently been enlisted" for "Jewish youths and infants interned in Slovackia" (the spelling of the transcription). [cite:vt16_cicognani1943b] It then turns to Palestine.

On Palestine the letter argues from religion, from history and from international order. "Catholics the world over are piously devoted to this country", it says, and "If the greater part of Palestine is given to the Jewish people, this would be a severe blow to the religious attachment of Catholics to this land." [cite:vt16_cicognani1943] [cite:vt16_cicognani1943b] It warns that "grave new, international problems would arise." [cite:vt16_cicognani1943b] It concedes that "at one time Palestine was inhabited by the Hebrew Race", but holds there is "no axiom in history to substantiate the necessity of a people returning to a country they left nineteen centuries before." [cite:vt16_cicognani1943] It proposes an alternative: "If a 'Hebrew Home' is desired, it would not be too difficult to find a more fitting territory than Palestine." [cite:vt16_cicognani1943] On the shrines it says that "Catholics rejoice in certain rights regarding these places" and that "Repeated formal assurances that these rights will be respected are ever necessary." [cite:vt16_cicognani1943] It traces this line back through the Holy See's own record: Benedict XV in 1919 and 1921, who "did not want to interfere with any rights of the Jewish people", Gasparri's letter of 6 March 1922 and the aide-mémoire to the League of 4 June 1922. [cite:vt16_cicognani1943b]

Three things about this letter bear on the front test. It was private, addressed to a government that could not be swayed by a public stance, and it is harder than anything the Holy See said in public in those years. It is continuous with the record of 1917 to 1922, which it cites as its own precedent. And it separates the two subjects that the 1904 audience had fused: help for persecuted Jews was one question, a Jewish political home in Palestine another, and the letter answers them differently. The Holy See's stated position in the letter is that its concern was the rights and religious attachment of Catholics in the Holy Land and the risk of new international conflict. Grade: primary.

Shertok's audience, April 1945

The last contact of the war years is again reported from the Zionist side. On 10 April 1945 Moshe Shertok of the Zionist leadership in Palestine was received by Pius XII and asked for the Church's "moral support" for the Jewish community in Palestine. Minerbi records that he received none, and that the Holy See's effort went instead toward an international status for Jerusalem. [cite:vt16_ej_vatican] That effort, and the encyclicals of 1948 and 1949, are the subject of [scene:vt17_jerusalem_1947_49|Part Seventeen]. Grade: reported by a named historian.

The claimed 1944 note

The only document offered for private papal support of a Jewish homeland in this period is a note said to have been written by Pius XII in 1944. It was announced on 8 March 2012 by the Pave the Way Foundation, a New York organization led by Gary Krupp that has published tens of thousands of pages of research on Pius XII's record, through the Catholic news agency Zenit. In the Foundation's account, Tardini warned the pope against helping Jews establish a homeland and Pius XII replied in his own hand: "The Jews need a land of their own." [cite:vt16_ptwf_zenit] The Foundation said the note was unpublished, held in "the closed section of the Vatican Library", and that it had "learned of the existence" of the document; it gave no image, no transcription and no archival reference. [cite:vt16_ptwf_zenit]

The same release made other claims: that Pacelli asked the German government on 15 November 1917 to protect the Jewish community of Palestine "even with the use of arms"; that he met Sokolow again on 15 February 1925; that he urged Catholics in 1926 to join a German pro-Palestine committee; and that the Vatican encouraged Spain to recognize Israel in 1955. The release names no source for the 1926 and 1955 claims. [cite:vt16_ptwf_zenit] The 1917 intervention, if documented, concerns the physical protection of a community in wartime and is not evidence of support for a state.

The 1944 note is graded here as unverified. The papers of Pius XII's pontificate have been open to researchers since 2 March 2020, and a search made in October 2026 found no publication, image or catalogue reference for such a note in the reporting since then. [cite:vt16_ptwf_zenit] [cite:vt14_vn_open2020] Holy See papers of this kind would sit in the Apostolic Archive or the Secretariat of State's historical archive, not the Library, two institutions that [scene:vt14_apostolic_archive|Part Fourteen] distinguishes. [cite:vt14_vn_libarch2025] If the note exists and says what the Foundation reports, it would show that Pius XII held a private view different from his Secretariat of State's in one year. It would still stand against the documented letters of 1943, written in his name by his Secretary of State and his delegate, and against the Holy See's policy from 1945 to 1949.

On the first of the five tests, the private record, the years from 1904 to 1945 come out against a front. The private documents that are primary and published match the public line, and in 1943 they are harder than it. The two items that point the other way are a Zionist envoy's report of a cordial audience in 1917, which the Holy See's own conditions as Minerbi reports them do not support, and an unpublished note known only from an advocacy foundation's announcement. Neither shows money, favours or diplomatic help passing from the Holy See to the movement. The final weighing is left to [scene:vt19_predictions_tested|Part Nineteen].
