Part Three asks the question that sits under every argument about Hezbollah: would it exist if Israel had not invaded Lebanon in 1982 and stayed in the south for eighteen years? No document can prove what would have happened in a history that did not occur, so everything in this part that answers the question is argument, and is labelled as such. What the record can do is set out who has made each argument, on what evidence, and which pieces of that evidence are documented facts. The first section gives the case that the invasion and the occupation made Hezbollah, much of it in the words of Israeli officials. The second gives the case that it would have formed anyway, in this or another shape, because of Lebanese Shia politics, clerical networks reaching back to Iraq, and Iran's stated aim of exporting its revolution, and ends with what can and cannot be settled.
