The Gnostic teachers held a kind of authority that could not outlast them: a school depended on its master, while a bishopric passed from holder to holder with its property and its lists. [cite:vt24_tert_adval] [cite:cn_iren_3_3] Their opponents accused them of taking wealthy women's fortunes and of libertine rites; those charges come from competitors and are uncorroborated. [cite:vt24_ah1_13] What the record does show is that women prophesied and taught in some of these schools, that a woman, Marcellina, led the Carpocratian school in Rome, and that one text defined God's righteousness as sharing in common. [cite:vt24_ah1_25] [cite:gn_wiki_epiphanes] Part Three turns to the myth that gave the movement its most lasting image: a world governed by rulers who are not God.
