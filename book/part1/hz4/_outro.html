Part Four established that Israel held a strip of south Lebanon for fifteen years through a Lebanese militia it created, paid about $500 to $800 a man each month and equipped, and that this militia ran the Khiam prison, whose interrogators Israel's Shin Bet trained. [cite:hz4_crs_sla] [cite:hz4_hrw_khiam99] It showed how Hezbollah came out of the civil war as the one militia allowed to keep its arms, as "resistance" to that occupation, a status the agreement's text never names. [cite:hz4_wiki_taif] [cite:hz4_lbc_arms] [cite:hz4a_olj_taif] Two Israeli campaigns whose stated method was to move civilians north killed about 120 Lebanese civilians in 1993 and 154 in 1996, including about 100 at Qana, where the UN found error "unlikely" and Israel maintained it was a mistake; Hezbollah's rockets, fired as stated reprisals, killed and wounded Israeli civilians in the same years. [cite:hz4_hrw_pawns] [cite:hz4_hrw_grapes] [cite:hz4_un_qana] The withdrawal of May 2000 ended the occupation the UN certified, and the Shebaa Farms dispute gave Hezbollah a stated reason to keep its weapons. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] [cite:hz4_merip_2000] Argentine and Bulgarian courts attributed the Buenos Aires and Burgas bombings to Hezbollah, and in Argentina to Iran, in proceedings against absent defendants; both deny it.
