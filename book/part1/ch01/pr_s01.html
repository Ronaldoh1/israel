The Map: Israel, the West Bank, Gaza and East Jerusalem

Much of the confusion in this history begins with geography. Four names recur throughout this book: Israel, the West Bank, Gaza and East Jerusalem. They are often used as though they were interchangeable. They are not one place under different names. Each is a distinct territory, with its own history since 1948 and its own legal regime today. Most of the arguments that follow are, at bottom, arguments about where these lines run and who governs on either side of them.

Start with the line itself. Israel, in the narrow sense used here, is the state within the 1949 armistice lines, known as the "Green Line." The West Bank is the territory east of that line. Jordan held it from 1948 to 1967, and Israel has occupied it since 1967. In 1995 the Oslo II accord, an interim agreement, divided the West Bank into three zones, explicitly as a temporary arrangement pending a final agreement.[cite:wiki_oslo_areas] Area A, about 18 percent of the territory, went under Palestinian Authority civil and security control. It holds the major cities: Ramallah, Bethlehem, Nablus, Jenin, Tulkarem, Qalqilya, Jericho and most of Hebron. Area B, about 22 percent, came under Palestinian civil control with joint security control. Area C, about 61 percent, stayed under full Israeli control.[cite:anera_areas_abc] Areas A and B together hold some 2.8 million Palestinians.

The zones were meant to be a stage, not a destination. The plan was to transfer these areas gradually to Palestinian control. That transfer never happened, and since 2002 the Israeli army has entered Area A routinely. A temporary arrangement became, in practice, the lasting shape of the territory.

Area C is the key to the land question. Every Israeli settlement outside East Jerusalem lies within it. About 60 percent of Area C is firing zones, military land, state land or nature reserves. Palestinian building there is tightly restricted, and permits are rarely granted.[cite:wiki_area_c] [cite:anera_areas_abc] When later chapters discuss settlement growth or the prospects for a Palestinian state, the land at stake is mostly in Area C.

Gaza is a strip on the Mediterranean coast. Egypt held it from 1948 to 1967, and Israel occupied it from 1967. In 2005 Israel withdrew its settlers from Gaza but kept control of its borders, airspace and coast.[cite:jerusalemstory_area_a] Withdrawing settlers and withdrawing control are two different things, and the difference runs through every later debate about Gaza's status.

East Jerusalem was occupied in 1967 and then annexed by Israel, a step that has not been recognized internationally. Roughly 360,000 Palestinians live there.[cite:minorityrights_palestine] About 737,000 Israeli settlers now live across East Jerusalem and the West Bank together.

Everyday speech blurs these places together. The documents do not. They describe four places under four different rules. A resident of Ramallah, a settler in Area C, a resident of Gaza and a Palestinian in East Jerusalem live under different authorities and different laws. When this book names one of these territories, it means that territory under that rule. Keeping the map in mind is the first condition for reading everything that follows.
