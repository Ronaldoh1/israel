Part Three tests the promise at its most concrete: that children who work will end up better off than their parents, and that where they start will not decide where they finish. It follows the share of Americans who out-earn their parents from the generation born in 1940 to the one born in the 1980s, explains what that measure can and cannot show and what its critics dispute, sets it beside the steadier record of relative mobility, maps how sharply the odds of climbing vary from one city and neighbourhood to another and why, and compares the American ladder with those of Canada, Denmark and other rich democracies.
