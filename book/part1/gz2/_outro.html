Part Two followed Gaza through two rulers. Under Egypt, from 1949 to 1967, the strip was a stateless refugee territory and the front line of a border war: Israeli raids and the Egyptian-run fida'i units, the 1955 uprising against a plan to resettle refugees in Sinai, and the Israeli occupation of 1956, with the killings at Khan Yunis and Rafah. [cite:rs_cw2_mod_black_arrow] [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza] [cite:rs_cw3_morris_gaza] Under Israel from 1967, its 356,000 people were counted, governed by the military, and subjected after 1970 to a campaign that demolished some 2,000 homes and displaced nearly 16,000 people; its workers were drawn into the Israeli economy, and settlements were planted on its land from 1970. [cite:gz_jvl_census67] [cite:gz_wiki_insurgency] [cite:gz_carnegie_labor] [cite:gz_wiki_gushkatif]

What is settled is the sequence of rule and the main acts of each ruler. What is contested is the death tolls of 1955 and 1956, set out side by side here, and the judgment on the campaign of 1971: effective pacification, or collective punishment that stored up resentment. What the record leaves open is the strip's status, which the armistice, the Camp David framework and the peace treaty each declined to settle. Part Three follows the uprising, the Oslo years, the settlements and the withdrawal of 2005.
