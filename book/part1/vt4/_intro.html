Part Four sets aside the history and the money for a moment and answers a plainer question: who lives in the Vatican, and how is it governed from one day to the next. It begins by separating the two subjects that the word "Vatican" runs together, the Holy See, which signs treaties and sends ambassadors, and Vatican City State, the walled territory of 44 hectares that the Holy See has ruled since 1929. It then reads the state's constitution, the Fundamental Law, in its four versions from 1929 to the one Pope Leo XIV signed on 31 July 2026, and shows how a pope who holds every power of government delegates them to a commission and a Governorate. The third section explains how a person becomes a Vatican citizen and stops being one, what courts the state has, and why its convicts would serve their sentences in Italian prisons. The last section follows the ordinary machinery: the euro coins, the untaxed salaries and VAT-free shops, the water, trains, post and power that Italy supplies, the hospital the Holy See owns outside the walls, and the two small forces that keep order inside them.
