Jared Kushner

At the time of contact: property developer and owner of the New York Observer. Later: senior adviser to President Trump, his father-in-law, in the first term, and the administration's lead on the Middle East. Today: private investor.

Why he is here

Kushner's name circulates widely in connection with the Epstein files, often with a count of how many times it appears. Counts of mentions measure nothing; a name can appear in a news clipping, a guest list or someone else's speculation. This entry sets out the two documents that actually connect him to the record, and what each one is.

The record

The first is an invitation. In March 2013, four years after Epstein left jail, the New York Observer, which Kushner then owned, emailed Epstein an invitation to the paper's twenty-fifth anniversary party on 14 March. The card was issued in the names of Kushner, as publisher, and the paper's chief executive, Joseph Meyer. The guest list sent with it included Donald and Ivanka Trump, Harvey Weinstein, Matt Lauer, Senator Cory Booker and the New York police commissioner, Raymond Kelly, and Mayor Michael Bloomberg was to give a toast. Whether Epstein went is not known. The email was among those released by the House Oversight Committee in November 2025. [cite:ep5_mediaite_kushner2025] It shows that Epstein, a registered sex offender, was on the invitation list of a major New York publication in 2013. It does not show that Kushner knew him, wrote to him or met him.

The second is an FBI report. Among the files released in January 2026 is a 2020 FBI report of what a confidential source told agents during an inquiry into foreign influence. The source claimed that Trump had been "compromised by Israel", described Kushner as "the real brains" behind Trump's organization and presidency, urged the FBI to investigate the Kushner family's charities, and said Epstein had become a "co-opted Mossad agent". [cite:ep5_mee_chs2026] [cite:ep5_alternet_chs2026] A report of this kind records what one informant said. It is not a finding by the FBI, and nothing in the release shows that any of these claims was checked, corroborated or acted on. No response from Kushner has been reported.

What the report does, and what the book does not

The informant's account places the supposed influence in a religious movement, the Chabad Hasidic movement, and treats Kushner's religious practice as evidence of it. [cite:ep5_mee_chs2026] That is the step this book refuses for everyone. A person's faith, or the faith of a community, is not a documented connection to anything: a network tie is a payment, a contract, an email, a flight or an appointment. The report is part of the record and is set out here so readers know what it says, but none of its claims about Kushner meets that standard, and its claims about Epstein and Israel are examined on the evidence in Part Seven.

What is not in the record

In the reporting on the released files that this book examined, there is no flight, payment, visit, email exchange, business deal or photograph between Jared Kushner and Epstein. No survivor has named him. This book found no documented Epstein connection for his father, Charles Kushner, or his brother, Joshua. Kushner moved in the same New York and Washington circles as several people in this dictionary, but sharing a room or a guest list is not a connection, and each tie has to be documented on its own.

What remains unestablished: whether Epstein attended the 2013 party; whether the FBI did anything with the informant's report; and what the files still withheld say, if anything, about any member of the family.
