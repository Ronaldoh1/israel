The Fair Test

Ron's thesis for this part is that the warnings were false alarms: that Netanyahu said for more than thirty years that Iran was close to a bomb and it never came. A fair test sorts the statements in the previous section into three kinds before scoring them. Some were dated estimates with no condition attached: Iran was three to five years away in 1992 and 1995, "probably one or two years away" in 2009. Some were conditional thresholds: Iran would reach a stated point "at current enrichment rates," or "if not stopped." The rest were warnings with no date at all: the 1996 deadline for action, the 2015 case against the deal, the 2018 archive and the 2026 war statement. Each kind can be tested only on its own terms. [scene:ir5_predictions|The dated predictions]

The dated estimates failed on their own terms. No Iranian weapon existed by 1997, by 2000 or by 2011. The US intelligence community judged in its National Intelligence Estimate of December 2007, "with high confidence," that Iran had halted its covert weapons programme in the autumn of 2003, and with moderate confidence that it had not restarted it by mid-2007. [cite:hz11_wh_nie2007] The IAEA's final assessment of December 2015 found that Iran had carried out a range of activities relevant to a nuclear explosive device "prior to the end of 2003 as a coordinated effort," that some continued after 2003 but "did not advance beyond feasibility and scientific studies," and that there were no credible indications of such work after 2009. [cite:ir5_iaea_gov2015] Those findings show that the weapons work was real, which Iran denies, and that it stopped years before the 2009 estimate was given. The 2002 claim about Iraq failed outright: the Iraq Survey Group found that Saddam had ended his nuclear programme after 1991. [cite:hz11b_cnn_duelfer] Part Four sets out the programme's own history. [scene:ir4_programme|The programme]

The conditional warnings cannot be scored the same way, because the conditions changed. When Netanyahu spoke at the UN in September 2012, Iran had been enriching to 20 per cent at Fordow since January. The interim agreement of November 2013 rolled that stockpile back, and from 20 January 2014 the IAEA confirmed that Iran had stopped enriching to 20 per cent. [cite:ir4_wiki_timeline] [cite:ir4_aca_timeline] [cite:ir7_aca_jpoa] Iran did not cross his red line in 2013, but whether it would have done so at the 2012 rates cannot be known. A Mossad assessment of October 2012, leaked in 2015, said Iran "is not performing the activity necessary to produce weapons"; The Times of Israel argued that the two documents differed over speed, not direction. [cite:hz11_rferl_spycables] [cite:hz11_toi_spycables] The 2025 warning is harder still to test, because the strikes it announced followed at once. Its premise about material was accurate: the IAEA reported 408.6 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60 per cent in May 2025, and estimated 440.9 kilograms by 13 June; on 12 June its Board found Iran in breach of its safeguards obligations. [cite:ir4_ap_may2025] [cite:ir5_ynet_sep2026] [cite:ir5_aj_iaea2025] Its premise about intent was not supported by the agencies that watched the programme. In March 2025 the US Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, said the intelligence community "continues to assess that Iran is not building a nuclear weapon." On 19 June the IAEA's director general, Rafael Grossi, said, "We did not find in Iran elements to indicate that there is an active, systematic plan to build a nuclear weapon." [cite:hz11_bg_gabbard] [cite:ir5_tbs_grossi] A senior Israeli official later told Ynet, anonymously and in an account Ynet could not verify, that Iran's "weaponization group was very far from developing a nuclear weapon." [cite:ir5_ynet_sep2026] Iran maintained throughout that its programme was peaceful. [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard]

The warnings without dates are tests of argument, not of timing. The 2015 claim that the deal "paves Iran's path to the bomb" was never run to its end: the United States left the agreement in 2018, and Part Seven traces what Iran did after that. [scene:ir7_withdrawal|The withdrawal] Before the 2026 war President Trump said Iran was rebuilding its programme; the IAEA said it had no evidence that Iran had resumed enrichment. [cite:ow7_npr_epicfury] Netanyahu's own statement that day gave no date. [cite:ir5_jvl_feb2026]

Two Readings of the Same Record

Israel's government reads the record as a series of dangers that were real and were stopped. The argument has documentary support. The 2007 estimate itself judged that Iran's halt had been "directed primarily in response to increasing international scrutiny and pressure." [cite:ir5_wh_nie2007] In January 2011 the departing head of the Mossad, Meir Dagan, was reported as saying that Iran would not be able to build a bomb before 2015 because its programme had been delayed by unspecified "measures." [cite:ir5_ap_dagan2011] The Stuxnet cyberattack, a US operation begun in 2006 to which Israel's Unit 8200 contributed, put out of action nearly a thousand of Iran's five thousand centrifuges in its last attack, by the account of the New York Times. [cite:ow3_sw_sanger] In June 2026 Netanyahu put the argument in its strongest form: without the two wars, "Iran would have had atomic bombs." [cite:ir5_ap_jun2026] On this reading the warnings did not fail; they were the reason the bomb never arrived.

The critics read the same record as a warning repeated beyond its evidence. The Intercept's Murtaza Hussain titled his 2015 survey of the statements "Benjamin Netanyahu's Long History of Crying Wolf About Iran's Nuclear Weapons." [cite:hz11b_intercept_wolf] Iran's foreign minister used the same image in 2018. [cite:hz11b_cbs_archive] Jon Hoffman of the Cato Institute argued in 2024 that Netanyahu had pressed for decades for American military action against Iran and had domestic political reasons to widen conflict; that is an argument about motive, and Part Thirteen weighs it. [cite:hz12_cato_hoffman] [scene:ir13_why|Why Iran] In June 2026 the Associated Press set his claim of averted bombs against the public evidence: Iran had been "months or years away" from a usable weapon before either war, and there was no evidence it had decided to build one. [cite:ir5_ap_jun2026] Dagan himself, the source of the delay argument, also said Israel "should not hurry to attack." [cite:ir5_ap_dagan2011]

Both readings have a weak point. Israel's cannot be disproved: any warning that is followed by no bomb can be credited to the pressure that followed it, and the halt the US agencies identified came in 2003, before Stuxnet, the assassinations of Iran's scientists and the strikes of 2025. The critics' reading cannot account for everything either: the weapons work of the years before 2004 was real, the 60 per cent stockpile was, in the IAEA's words of May 2025, unique among states without nuclear weapons, and the agencies that rejected the dates also recorded the capacity. [cite:ir4_ap_may2025] [cite:hz11_bg_gabbard] What the record shows as a pattern is narrower than either. Every dated forecast of an Iranian bomb that Netanyahu gave without conditions passed without a bomb. His later warnings moved from dates to thresholds and to capacity, and on capacity the record largely bore him out. On intent, which the 2025 war was fought over, the American and international agencies of the time did not.
