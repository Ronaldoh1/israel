The Federal Reserve and Zionist Finance: Coincidence, Not Connection

On 23 December 1913 the Federal Reserve Act was signed into law, creating America's central banking system. [cite:federalreservehistory_fed_act] The date falls sixteen years after the First Zionist Congress at Basel and seven years before the founding of Keren Hayesod, the movement's later fundraising arm. A persistent claim holds that the Federal Reserve was founded by, or to serve, the same financial network that funded Zionism. This book meets that claim head-on, because silence about it in a history of the movement's finances could easily be read as evasion.

Start with what was checked and what was found. The primary record, including the founding documents of the Jewish Colonial Trust, the Jewish National Fund and the Federal Reserve, shows no institutional or financial link between the Federal Reserve and the Zionist Organization or its funds. Keren Hayesod did not yet exist. [cite:federalreservehistory_fed_act] A checked absence of this kind is a finding in its own right, not an unfinished search. Two events sixteen years apart, in different countries, within a small and overlapping financial world, are not connected merely by that.

The claim usually rests on one man. Paul Warburg, a partner at the New York bank Kuhn, Loeb, was a genuine architect of the Federal Reserve. He was present at the secret 1910 Jekyll Island meeting that shaped the proposal, described in [scene:s04gb|the previous section], and he served on the first Federal Reserve Board from 1914 to 1918. [cite:wiki_paul_warburg] That is the documented core of his role, and nothing beyond it is established. No primary source connects his work on the Federal Reserve to Zionist fundraising or planning.

The strongest evidence against the claim comes from inside Warburg's own family. His brother Felix Warburg and his father-in-law Jacob Schiff were prominent non-Zionists and opponents of Zionism in this very period. Schiff put his position on record:

"I cannot for a moment concede that one can be at the same time a true American and an honest adherent of the Zionist movement." [cite:schiff_zionism_wiki]

Suppose the theory is that a family banking circle was quietly coordinating both American central banking and Zionist fundraising. Then the recorded, quoted rejection of Zionism by that circle's most prominent members is about as direct a contradiction as history can offer. [cite:schiff_zionism_wiki] Views within the wider Warburg and Schiff families shifted over the decades, and not every member held the same view for life. But the quoted position belongs to this specific period, which is the period in question.

The claim itself has a traceable origin. The specific assertion of a Federal Reserve–Zionism connection goes back to Eustace Mullins's tract The Federal Reserve Conspiracy, promoted from the 1950s and recycled since by extremist groups and figures. Mainstream fact-checking and civil-rights research organizations classify "Jewish control of the Federal Reserve" as a documented antisemitic myth, not a contested historical question. [cite:adl_fed_myth] Naming the source explains where the idea came from. It does not mean that everyone who has repeated some version of it knows or endorses that origin. Myths travel independently of their authors.

What matters here is the line between documented structure and conspiratorial inference. The previous section described real and unusual facts: privately owned regional banks, a plan drafted in secret by bankers, the era's leading banking houses at one table on Jekyll Island. Those facts stand on their own evidence. The leap from them to a hidden hand behind the money supply, coded by religion or ethnicity, is a different claim, and it does not survive contact with the record.

The conclusion comes in three parts. The timing of the Federal Reserve Act relative to Basel is a coincidence. The claimed connection is a documented myth with a named, dated source. And the actual record, Schiff's and Felix Warburg's opposition to Zionism, runs the opposite way from the myth. [cite:federalreservehistory_fed_act] This section does not pretend to answer every version of the claim in circulation. It sets out the checkable facts, so that any other version can be measured against the same standard.
