The Documents People Cite

Two documents are often cited as evidence that the campaign against Iran was planned long in advance: a 1996 policy paper written for Netanyahu, and a 1982 essay by an Israeli journalist. Both are real, and both can be read. In both, Iran is a minor subject.

"A Clean Break", 1996

A Clean Break: A New Strategy for Securing the Realm was published on 1 June 1996 by the Institute for Advanced Strategic and Political Studies (IASPS), a think tank, for Netanyahu's incoming government. It came from the institute's "Study Group on a New Israeli Strategy Toward 2000," led by Richard Perle. The other members were James Colbert, Charles Fairbanks Jr., Douglas Feith, Jonathan Torop, David Wurmser, Meyrav Wurmser and the institute's president, Robert Loewenberg. [cite:ir13_wiki_cleanbreak] [cite:ir13_km_cleanbreak]

The paper's targets were the Oslo process, Syria and Iraq. It urged Israel to "make a clean break" from the peace process of the preceding years. It proposed that Israel, with Turkey and Jordan, shape its region "by weakening, containing, and even rolling back Syria", an effort that could focus on "removing Saddam Hussein from power in Iraq." It recommended "striking Syrian military targets in Lebanon" and, should that prove insufficient, targets in Syria itself. It also urged Israel to free itself from American economic aid and loan guarantees. [cite:ir13_km_cleanbreak]

Iran appears in four sentences, always in connection with Syria, Hezbollah or Lebanon's Shia, and always in the context of Lebanon or Iraq. Israel could seize the initiative on its northern border "by engaging Hizballah, Syria, and Iran, as the principal agents of aggression in Lebanon." Syria "and Iran might prefer a weak, but barely surviving Saddam." The Shia of southern Lebanon, the paper says, had long been tied to "the Shia leadership in Najf, Iraq rather than Iran," and so a Hashemite government in Iraq could help Israel "wean the south Lebanese Shia away from Hizballah, Iran, and Syria." [cite:ir13_km_cleanbreak] [cite:ir13a_km_cleanbreak] The paper proposes no strike on Iran, no regime change in Tehran and no action against Iran's nuclear programme. Some later accounts describe it as a plan to roll back Iran along with Syria and Lebanon, but its own text applies "rolling back" to Syria. [cite:ir13_km_cleanbreak]

Its significance lies in who wrote it. By 2002, as The Guardian's Brian Whitaker noted, several of its authors held key posts in Washington, in the administration of George W. Bush that went on to invade Iraq in 2003. Some critics drew a line from one to the other: Patrick Buchanan said the plan had been "imposed" on the United States, and the scholars John Mearsheimer and Stephen Walt said it called on Israel to reorder the Middle East. Others found no evidence of influence. CBS's defence correspondent David Martin said in 2005 that he "never saw any evidence that Richard Perle had any determining effect on US policy", and Netanyahu himself did not adopt several of the paper's proposals. [cite:ir13_wiki_cleanbreak] Feith says he "neither wrote it nor signed it" and doubts that he saw it before publication. He describes David Wurmser as its principal author and says that he and a few others were consulted as Wurmser researched it, his own contribution being the idea that Israel "graduate" from American economic aid. [cite:ir13_feith_cleanbreak] [cite:ir13a_feith] The record supports a narrower statement than is often made: in 1996 a group of American policy advocates urged an Israeli government to topple Saddam Hussein and confront Syria, and several of them later served a US government that did invade Iraq. It does not show a 1996 plan for war with Iran.

The Yinon essay, 1982

In February 1982 Kivunim ("Directions"), a Hebrew quarterly published by the World Zionist Organization's information department, printed "A Strategy for Israel in the 1980s" by Oded Yinon, reputedly a journalist and a former Foreign Ministry official. Israel Shahak, an Israeli critic of Zionism, translated it into English; by the translation's own note it was disseminated on 13 June 1982, a week after Israel invaded Lebanon. It circulated as "The Zionist Plan for the Middle East." [cite:ir13_wiki_yinon] [cite:ir13_mb_yinon] [cite:ir13a_mb_yinon]

Yinon argued that the Arab states were fragile mosaics and that Israel should aim at their dissolution into ethnic and religious units. Iraq would split into Shiite, Sunni and Kurdish states; Syria would "fall apart" into several; he called Egypt a "corpse" and argued for Jordan's "liquidation." [cite:ir13_mb_yinon] [cite:ir13_wiki_yinon] Iran was not among the states whose break-up he proposed. In the text of the translation reviewed for this book, which lacks the essay's closing sections, Iran appears in two sentences, both about the Iran–Iraq war, which Yinon expected to weaken Iraq: "An Iraqi-Iranian war will tear Iraq apart." [cite:ir13_mb_yinon] [cite:ir13a_mb_yinon]

Its status is contested. The scholar Nisan said the views were Yinon's own and not government policy, and Noam Chomsky called them "ideological and geopolitical fantasies" outside mainstream Zionism. Peleg, by contrast, treated the essay as "an authentic mirror of the thinking mode of the Israeli Right" under Begin, and Becker and Polkinghorn (2017) argue that its ideas were refined in "A Clean Break." [cite:ir13_wiki_yinon] The public record contains no evidence that any Israeli government adopted it. It does show the essay's later misuse: it has been offered as proof that Israel planned the 2003 invasion of Iraq, the Syrian civil war and the rise of the Islamic State. [cite:ir13_wiki_yinon] Neither document is evidence about Iran.

Oil and Hormuz

Oil runs through the Iran question in a different way. About 20 million barrels a day, roughly a fifth of world consumption, passed through the Strait of Hormuz in 2024, together with about a fifth of the world's trade in liquefied natural gas; 84 per cent of that crude went to Asia. The pipelines of Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates could carry only about 2.6 million barrels a day around the strait. [cite:ir13_eia_hormuz] That geography is Iran's strongest card. In March 2026 shipping through the strait almost stopped, and the United States answered with a blockade of Iran's ports. As of mid-October 2026 that blockade was still in force and Iran was attacking ships in the strait. [cite:hz12_hcl_iran2026] [cite:hz12a_cbs_oct] [scene:ir11_now|Part Eleven: where things stand] Israel's statements of its aims do not mention oil. The stake the record shows is global supply, which is a reason other governments had to weigh a war with Iran, not one Israel gave for it.
