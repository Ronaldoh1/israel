The Myth and the Hunters

Barruel's book of 1797 did not blame Jews. [cite:vt30_wiki_judeomasonic] The Jewish element entered through a letter dated 1 August 1806 from a man signing himself Jean-Baptiste Simonini, who claimed that Jews had founded both the Freemasons and the Illuminati and meant to take over Europe. Barruel could not trace the writer and chose not to publish it, fearing violence against Jews, but he circulated it privately. The letter is thought to have been fabricated, probably by police opposed to Napoleon's policy towards Jews; it was first printed in 1878. [cite:vt30_wiki_simonini] Its contents are believed to have fed the Protocols of the Elders of Zion, a forgery first serialised in St Petersburg in 1903 and exposed by historians and courts. [cite:vt30_wiki_simonini] [cite:hm_ushmm_protocols]

This book states plainly what the documents show. The claim that Jews secretly direct the Freemasons or the Illuminati is an antisemitic conspiracy theory with no documentary basis. It rests on a letter its own recipient would not vouch for and on a proven forgery. It is not a contested historical question, and later versions that substitute "international bankers" or a family name reuse the same trope. [cite:vt30_wiki_judeomasonic] The documented orders are institutions with names, papers and edicts; the myth turns them into a mask for a people. That is the line this shelf does not cross.

The myth had hunters on both sides of the Atlantic. In 1826 William Morgan, a former Mason in Batavia, New York, planned to publish a book describing the Masonic rituals. He was arrested on what appear to have been flimsy charges and then vanished. He was never found; the account most widely believed is that Masons drowned him in the Niagara River, though some argued he had left of his own accord. Of dozens indicted, ten men were convicted, and none received more than three years. [cite:od_wiki_antimasonic] The outrage produced the Anti-Masonic Party, the first significant third party in the United States. In 1831 it held the first presidential nominating convention, in Baltimore, a practice every major party later adopted; its candidate in 1832, William Wirt, won 7.8 per cent of the vote and carried only Vermont. By 1840 most of its members had been absorbed into the Whigs. [cite:od_wiki_antimasonic]

The fiercest hunt was in Germany. Reinhard Heydrich counted Masons, Jews and the political clergy among the "most implacable enemies" of the German race, and Nazi propaganda promoted the myth of a "Jewish-Masonic" conspiracy. [cite:od_ushmm_nazi] In the summer of 1934 police closed many lodges and confiscated their libraries and archives; a decree of 28 October 1934 labelled the lodges "hostile to the state"; and on 17 August 1935 the interior minister, Wilhelm Frick, ordered all remaining lodges dissolved and their assets confiscated. [cite:od_ushmm_nazi] In occupied Europe lodges were ransacked for membership lists, and exhibitions of stolen Masonic objects were staged in Paris in October 1940 and Brussels in February 1941 to ridicule Freemasons and spread fear of a Jewish-Masonic conspiracy. Some former lodge members were arrested and murdered; how many were imprisoned solely for being Masons is not known, because many were also Jews or political opponents. [cite:od_ushmm_nazi]
