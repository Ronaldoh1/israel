In the late summer of 1953 Israel's borders were still the armistice lines of 1949, and none of them had become a peace. Egypt governed the Gaza Strip as a military protectorate that it never annexed. On the Syrian front, small demilitarized zones whose ownership the armistice had left open were already the subject of one Security Council crisis, told in Stage One. In Cairo, the Free Officers had dethroned King Farouk on 23 July 1952, with Colonel Gamal Abdel Nasser as their leader and Major General Muhammad Naguib as their figurehead. [cite:rs_cw2_ebsco_farouk] From Washington, Israel received $73.6 million in US assistance in 1953. [cite:rs_rd6_jvl_usaid_table] In Israel, David Ben-Gurion, prime minister and defence minister, was granted a long leave on 7 July 1953. [cite:rs_cw2_mod_bg_portfolio] The next month the army appointed Ariel Sharon to establish and command Unit 101, created, in the words of Israel's Defense Ministry archive, "to carry out reprisal operations in enemy territory". [cite:rs_cw2_mod_sharon]

This stage asks what that policy produced, and what the governments outside the region did with a border that would not stay quiet. Its three years run from the founding of Unit 101 to 26 July 1956, when Nasser nationalized the Suez Canal Company. [cite:rs_cw2_state_suez] In that time the quarrel along the armistice lines became tied to the Cold War: to who would arm Egypt and who would arm Israel, to which camp each state in the region would join, and to the terms on which Washington would lend money, sell weapons or guarantee borders. The stage asks with equal weight what the governments and peoples of the region decided for themselves. Egypt chose its own suppliers. In Jordan, riots brought down a prime minister who had said he would join the Baghdad Pact, and the king dismissed Glubb. Palestinians in Gaza organized against the Egyptian administration that governed them.

The cast is wide, and most of it was divided against itself. In Israel, Ben-Gurion and his chief of staff, Moshe Dayan, led what the historian Avi Shlaim calls the "retaliation" school, and Moshe Sharett, prime minister from January 1954, led the "negotiation" school against them; Pinhas Lavon, Abba Eban and Shimon Peres each had a part. [cite:rs_cw2_shlaim_east_west] In Egypt, Nasser contended with Naguib and with the Muslim Brotherhood. In Jordan stood Prime Minister Hazza al-Majali, King Hussein and Glubb, the commander of the Arab Legion. [cite:rs_cw2_loc_jordan] In Gaza, communists and the Muslim Brotherhood formed a joint committee, and Egypt later raised Palestinian fida'i units under Lieutenant Colonel Mustafa Hafez. [cite:rs_cw2_palquest_gaza] In Washington, John Foster Dulles held the purse and the policy. Henry Byroade spoke for the State Department. Eric Johnston and Robert Anderson carried plans to the region as envoys, and James Angleton held the Israeli account at the CIA. In London there was Anthony Eden. At the United Nations there were General Vagn Bennike, General Burns and Dag Hammarskjöld. Nasser put the question of arms to the Soviet ambassador, D. S. Solod, in 1954; Egypt's arms came in the end through Czechoslovakia, and Israel's from France.

The sections follow that story roughly as it happened. They begin with [scene:cw2_free_officers|Egypt's revolution], because the power that shaped most of the stage sat in Cairo. Next come Unit 101 and Qibya, and in the same weeks the aid that Washington held back over a canal at Banat Yaacov, followed by Johnston's two-year effort to divide the Jordan. The oil settlement in Iran is followed by the contest between Sharett and Ben-Gurion, and then by the Pledge of Allegiance and the national motto, a domestic American thread whose links to Israel are examined and found undocumented. The year after Qibya brings the Scorpion Pass, Nahalin, Byroade's speeches and the Bat Galim, and alongside it Operation Susannah, Israel's covert operation in Egypt. The Angleton channel and the Anglo-American plan called Alpha come next. Then the region chooses sides at Baghdad, Bandung and Amman. The Gaza raid of February 1955 and the fedayeen lead to the arms race, the Council's censures and Hammarskjöld's mission. The stage closes with [scene:cw2_aswan|the dam offer and the Canal] and a synthesis of the structure as it stood on that July day.
