The Stars

Astrology is the reading of human affairs from the positions of the planets. Catholic theology in the line of Thomas Aquinas did not reject it outright: Aquinas allowed conjectural predictions, and the line the Church drew ran against claims to know free human acts and fortunes with certainty. [cite:vt30_ox_coeli] The Council of Trent fixed that line in law. Its Rule IX for the Index, published in 1564, "absolutely repudiated" books of geomancy, palm reading, necromancy, sorcery and the magic arts, and told bishops to keep out of circulation books of astrology that claimed to determine destiny, while expressly allowing astronomy written for navigation, farming and medicine. [cite:vt30_fordham_rule9]

Sixtus V's bull Coeli et terrae of 1586 went further than Aquinas, banning even "uncertain predictions about future human actions". [cite:vt30_ox_coeli] The most severe act was political. In 1629 the Roman astrologer Orazio Morandi circulated a prediction that Pope Urban VIII would die after a solar eclipse in June 1630. Morandi was jailed and died in prison in 1630. In 1631 Urban decreed death for anyone who predicted "the life or death of the sitting Roman Pontiff" or of his relatives. [cite:vt30_torch_popes] The ground in that case was the danger of prophecy in a papal election, not the illegitimacy of the art.

The modern position is in the Catechism. Paragraph 2116 says "All forms of divination are to be rejected", naming horoscopes and astrology among them, because they "conceal a desire for power over time, history, and, in the last analysis, other human beings" and "contradict the honor, respect, and loving fear that we owe to God alone." [cite:vt30_va_ccc2116] The text does not argue that astrology fails. It argues that seeking it is a wrong owed to God.

The record holds a quieter fact beside the bans. The same Church that forbade judicial astrology kept the astronomy: Trent allowed it in the same rule, and the Vatican Library holds and publishes one of the great surviving books of the Mexican sky, the Codex Borgia. [cite:vt30_fordham_rule9] [cite:vt35_wiki_borgia] What was forbidden was not the sky but the claim to read a person's fate in it.
