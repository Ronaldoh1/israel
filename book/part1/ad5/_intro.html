Part Five turns to the first promise of the Dream: that work pays. It begins with the floors the law sets under work, the federal minimum wage, the cash wage for tipped workers and the salary line for overtime, each written as a fixed number of dollars, and follows what happened to their value when Congress stopped changing them, along with other dollar limits in federal law that have stood still for decades and one that was finally indexed. It then asks how much Americans work, how many hold a second job, what the law guarantees in time off, and why most families with children now depend on two earners, weighing the best-known account of that change against its critics and setting out what childcare costs. It ends with the forces that weaken a worker's ability to walk away for better pay: employer concentration, noncompete contracts, occupational licensing, health insurance tied to the job, and employers who do not pay what the law requires, with the disputes over how large each one is.
