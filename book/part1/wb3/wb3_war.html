June 1967

The war. In six days in June 1967 Israel took the West Bank, East Jerusalem, the Gaza Strip, the Sinai and the Golan Heights. [cite:wb_wiki_westbank] For the West Bank, the war meant a second exodus. About 300,000 Palestinians fled the West Bank and Gaza, some 200,000 of them across the Jordan River to the East Bank; about 180,000 became refugees for the first time, and the rest were refugees of 1948 uprooted a second time. [cite:wb_prrn_1967]

The Latrun villages. Near Latrun, where the Arab Legion had held the road to Jerusalem in 1948, the army emptied three villages: Imwas, Yalo and Beit Nuba. Some 8,000 people were ordered by loudspeaker to march toward Ramallah, and when residents tried to return their homes had been razed, on the orders of the chief of staff, Yitzhak Rabin. Moshe Dayan wrote that the houses were destroyed "not in battle, but as punishment." Israel said the villagers had taken part in attacks on Jerusalem in 1948 and helped an Egyptian commando raid in the war. In 1973 the Jewish National Fund of Canada raised $5 million to build a recreational park, Canada Park, on the site. [cite:wb_wiki_imwas]

Resolution 242. On 22 November 1967 the UN Security Council adopted, unanimously, the resolution that has framed every peace plan since. It emphasized "the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war," called for the "withdrawal of Israeli armed forces from territories occupied in the recent conflict," affirmed every state's right to live "within secure and recognized boundaries," and called for "a just settlement of the refugee problem." [cite:wb_wiki_242] The English text says "territories," without "the" or "all"; the equally authentic French text says "des territoires occupés," which can be read as "the occupied territories." Its British and American drafters said the omission was deliberate; those who read it as requiring full withdrawal point to the French text and to the preamble's rule against acquiring territory by war. [cite:wb_wiki_242] The two readings, both documented, have been argued over for six decades.
