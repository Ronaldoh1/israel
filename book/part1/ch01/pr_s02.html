Who Are "the Palestinians"? One People, Several Legal Situations

When this book says "Palestinians," the word can mean any of several groups whose rights and daily lives differ sharply. The record shows a single people dispersed across several territories and several legal systems. Which law applies to any one person depends largely on where that person was born and now lives.

Begin with the total. The Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics estimated about 14.8 million Palestinians worldwide in 2024.[cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees] Their circumstances fall into several distinct categories.

The first group is the Palestinian Arab citizens of Israel, about a fifth of Israel's population. They vote in Israeli elections and sit in the Knesset, Israel's parliament. Their status has been shaped by the 2018 Nation-State Law and other measures, which later chapters examine in their own right.[cite:jta_nsl_upheld]

The second group lives in East Jerusalem, the part of the city Israel annexed after 1967. Roughly 360,000 Palestinians live there, most of them holding Israeli residency rather than Israeli citizenship.[cite:minorityrights_palestine] They live under Israeli rule, but mostly without the full standing of Israeli citizens.

The third group lives in the West Bank: about 3.4 million Palestinians as of mid-2025. The Palestinian Authority governs them in Areas A and B, and the territory as a whole remains under Israeli military rule. Israeli settlers in the same territory live under Israeli civil law. One territory thus holds two populations under two legal systems.[cite:minorityrights_palestine] [cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees]

The fourth group lives in Gaza, about 2.1 million people as of mid-2025. About two-thirds of them are refugees or the descendants of refugees.[cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees]

Beyond these four territories lies the largest single category. About 5.9 million Palestinians are registered with UNRWA, the United Nations agency for Palestinian refugees. About 40 percent of them are in Jordan, and others are in Lebanon, Syria, the West Bank and Gaza.[cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees] [cite:minorityrights_palestine] Altogether, about 6.6 million Palestinians live in Arab countries.

The familiar picture of one people in one place under one government stops here. These categories overlap with the map in the preceding section, and they explain why statements about "the Palestinians" so often talk past each other. Say that Palestinians vote in national elections, and it is true of one group and false of others. Say that Palestinians live under military rule, and the same holds. A refugee in Jordan, a resident of Gaza, a West Bank villager, an East Jerusalem resident and a Palestinian Arab citizen of Israel share a national identity. They do not share a legal status.

These differences matter beyond description. Much of the legal debate over Israel's conduct, including whether its policies amount to apartheid, turns on them. The next section takes up that question. For now it is enough to hold the four situations, and the refugee population beyond them, clearly in view.
