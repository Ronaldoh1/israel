Who Could Report, and Who Was Killed

The ban. From October 2023 Israel did not allow foreign journalists to enter Gaza independently. In January 2024 its Supreme Court upheld the ban on security grounds, accepting the state's argument that independent reporters could give away the positions of Israeli troops; the Foreign Press Association, which had petitioned the court, said the ban, then 95 days old, was "unprecedented." Almost all foreign reporters who entered went in embedded with the Israeli army, on the condition that its commanders reviewed their material and footage before publication. [cite:gz_tna_fpa] A second petition was still undecided in October 2026 after repeated postponements; Israel says independent entry is too dangerous, and three years into the war, with a ceasefire in place, the ban remained. [cite:gz_wlrn_ban]

The censor. Inside Israel, foreign correspondents work under the military censor. To receive a press card they sign an undertaking to abide by its rules, and during the war the censor restricted reporting on subjects including security cabinet meetings and information about the hostages. [cite:gz_intercept_cnn]

The journalists who were there. That left Gaza's own journalists, and they were killed in numbers without precedent. As of 24 September 2026 the Committee to Protect Journalists counted 189 Palestinian journalists killed in Gaza, and two more in Israeli detention, after a review in June 2026 removed names it found had engaged in combat; the UN counted 242 by August 2025. [cite:gz_cpj_2026] Brown University's Costs of War project found that more journalists had been killed in Gaza since October 2023 than in the American Civil War, both world wars, Korea, Vietnam, the Yugoslav wars and the war in Afghanistan combined. On 10 August 2025 an Israeli strike targeting Al Jazeera's correspondent Anas al-Sharif killed him outside al-Shifa hospital; on 25 August a strike on Nasser Hospital killed twenty people, five of them journalists, including a Reuters cameraman. Israel denies targeting journalists, and has said that some of those killed, al-Sharif among them, belonged to Hamas, often, according to NPR, without providing evidence. [cite:gz_wiki_journalists] [cite:gz_wlrn_ban]

The consequence is structural, and it is documented: the war was reported to the world by local journalists who were being killed at a historic rate, and by foreign reporters who could see it only from Israel or from inside Israeli army units, under rules that Israel set. Much of what Parts Six to Ten record reached the outside world through UN agencies, human rights groups and Palestinian reporters rather than through the correspondents of American networks.
