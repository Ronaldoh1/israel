What Is Built: An Inventory, Layer by Layer

Book Seven tests the book's working hypothesis against everything the first six books documented. The hypothesis is the Sophia platform's, and it has never been a finding: immigration enforcement and government cuts as the occasion, and a sovereign executive without a veto, the end state Curtis Yarvin's program describes ([scene:de6_the_program|Part Six]), as the destination. A test needs an inventory first. This section lists what exists as of October 2026, layer by layer, with the part of the book that documents each item, when it was built and under which administration. It adds no new machinery. It sets out on one page what the earlier parts established one at a time, so that the next three sections can ask what the parts could do together and what they still lack.

The inventory follows one rule throughout the book: capability is not intent. That a tool exists is a documented fact. That it has been used, or will be used, against a particular kind of person needs a document of its own. The list below records capability. The repurposing test in the next section looks at use.

The end state, in writing

The destination exists as text. Between 2007 and 2014 Yarvin, writing as Mencius Moldbug, and the philosopher Nick Land set out a state run like a company, with a chief executive answerable to owners rather than voters, and an exit for anyone who objected ([scene:de2_core_texts|Part Two]). Yarvin's 2008 answer for residents his sovereign corporation found unproductive was confinement, which he called "a humane alternative to genocide." [cite:de6_tnr_duran] His check on a bad chief executive was a board able to recall him and holding keys to disable the state's weapons, a design [scene:de8_who_enforces_the_charter|Part Eight] found unanswered on who would check the board. [cite:de8_kofman_ny] These are the theorists' own words. The machinery below was not built from them; no document traces any of it to their texts.

Records, sensors, brokers and the integration layer

The first layer is the records the state holds because people must give them: tax returns, Medicaid and welfare files, Social Security numbers, visa and student records, travel manifests, and the fingerprints, faces and irises in DHS's biometric systems. Since 2025 several of these have been opened to immigration enforcement. The IRS agreed in April 2025 to check addresses for ICE. Social Security data was linked to DHS's citizenship-check system, SAVE, in May 2025. Food-aid records from at least 27 states were run through SAVE. A notice opening cash-aid (TANF) records to DHS took effect on 30 September 2026 after a federal judge declined to block it. [cite:de30_ap_krause] [cite:de24_npr_save] [cite:de24b_npr_snap27] [cite:de24b_bl_tanf] The travel archive is older and deeper. CBP's targeting system keeps booking and manifest records for fifteen years, and TSA passed records on more than 31,000 travellers to ICE between January 2025 and February 2026 ([scene:de25b_who_knows_you_travel|Part Twenty-Seven]). [cite:de25b_pia_ats] [cite:de25b_reuters_tsaice]

The second layer is surveillance the government runs itself. ICE's Mobile Fortify app matches faces in the street against about 200 million images; the person scanned cannot decline, and photographs of citizens are kept for fifteen years. CBP has photographed every non-citizen at entry and exit since 26 December 2025 ([scene:de26_faces_drones_satellites|Part Twenty-Eight]). [cite:de26_404_fortify] [cite:de26_reason_fortify] [cite:de26_bu_entryexit] ICE also holds phone-extraction tools ([scene:de25_spyware_and_phones|Part Twenty-Six]). Its contract with the spyware maker Paragon was closed out in January 2026, and in May DHS would neither confirm nor deny that ICE still had the capability. [cite:de28_npr_paragon] The third layer is state and local systems that federal agents can search, chief among them licence-plate cameras. The fourth and fifth are the vendors and data brokers that sell to ICE directly: location trails, people-search profiles, face galleries and social-media monitoring ([scene:de24_data_and_fusion|Part Twenty-Four], [scene:de28_ice_contracts|Part Thirty]). By the Brennan Center's count, which it calls a minimum, DHS committed at least $2.9 billion to surveillance, data and analytics tools from January 2021 to July 2026. [cite:de28_brennan_tools]

The sixth layer joins the rest. Palantir does not collect data; it joins whatever data a customer is authorised to give it ([scene:de27_capability_not_intent|Part Twenty-Nine]). Its work for ICE dates from 2014. In 2025 and 2026 it added ImmigrationOS, the ELITE targeting tool that gives addresses a "confidence score," and an $86.3 million enforcement-operations order dated 5 May 2026. [cite:ow9_mj_icm2019] [cite:ow9_fortune_elite] [cite:de27_hg_ero] Outside ICE it holds an Army order to maintain the Personnel Data Environment, a system of Army personnel records, until 15 November 2026, and it is building a "common API layer" inside the IRS's modernisation. [cite:de24b_govly_pde] [cite:de27_treasury_api] Part Thirty's map grades each of these joins, and the end-to-end map in this part reuses those grades exactly.

Gates: who can be stopped, denied or cut off

A second set of tools does not watch people. It decides what they may do. Many of these gates long predate 2025. E-Verify, which checks a new hire's papers against SSA and DHS records, was created in 1996. Since October 2021 more than 3.8 million clearance holders have been under continuous vetting, with automated checks of criminal and financial records. [cite:de24b_ev_manual] [cite:de24b_bu_cv] A passport can be denied for tax debt over $66,000 in 2026 or for child-support arrears over $2,500. The government's terrorist screening dataset held about two million entries at the end of 2023, most of them people who are neither citizens nor permanent residents ([scene:de25b_who_can_stop_you|Part Twenty-Seven]). [cite:de25b_irs_passport] [cite:de25b_crs_childsupport] [cite:de25b_cbs_watchlist] Banks file millions of confidential suspicious-activity reports a year; DHS ran about 290,000 queries of that reporting system in a year. Since 2025 the GENIUS Act has required every regulated stablecoin issuer to be able to "block, freeze, and reject" transactions on a lawful order ([scene:de26_money_rails|Part Twenty-Eight]). [cite:de26_aba_fincen] [cite:de26_crec_genius] Sanctions under the International Criminal Court order of February 2025 have frozen the assets of court officials and of a United Nations rapporteur. [cite:ow11_wiki_eo14203] [cite:de34_jurist_albanese]

Law, command and force

The legal layer moved in 2025 and 2026 ([scene:de30_rage_to_schedule|Part Thirty-Two], [scene:de31_five_patterns|Part Thirty-Three]). On 29 June 2026, in Trump v. Slaughter, the Supreme Court held that a president may remove members of the Federal Trade Commission at will. An order of 3 June 2026 made about 8,000 senior career officials removable by notice. On 24 January 2025 about 17 inspectors general were removed in one night. [cite:de0_slaughter] [cite:de30_npr_8000] [cite:de31_npr_igs] A presidential memorandum of September 2025, NSPM-7, names ideas as indicators of domestic terrorism, and the Attorney General acknowledged in February 2026 that a list of groups compiled under it exists but is not public ([scene:de30_nspm7_and_detachment|Part Thirty-Two]). [cite:de30_wh_nspm7] [cite:de34_int_list]

The force is larger than at any time in ICE's history ([scene:de32_ice_as_template|Part Thirty-Four]). The 2025 budget law gave ICE about $75 billion over four years, about $45 billion of it for detention, with funds available to 30 September 2029. [cite:us6_brennan_obbba] [cite:de32_aic_hr1] On DHS's own figures ICE grew from about 10,000 to about 22,000 officers and agents. Agreements deputising local officers rose to 1,372 by January 2026. ICE held nearly 66,000 people on 11 July 2026, after a record of 70,766 in late January; in early 2025 about nine in ten detainees were held in privately run facilities. [cite:de32_kwtx_hiring] [cite:de32_fwd_287g] [cite:de34_trac_66k] [cite:de32_trac_private] GEO's subsidiary monitored about 184,000 people in the alternatives-to-detention programme in August 2026, nearly 54,000 of them on ankle monitors. [cite:de32_lt_isap] National Guard quick reaction forces totalling more than 23,000 were stood up in every state. [cite:de32_mt_qrf]

What the inventory shows, and what it does not

Three things stand out. First, most of the machinery was not built for this purpose or by these people. The ICE–Palantir relationship began under Obama. E-Verify, continuous vetting, the travel archive, private detention and the fusion centres were built and kept by Democratic and Republican administrations alike. [cite:ow9_mj_icm2019] [cite:de32_brennan_14006] Second, what changed in 2025 and 2026 is mainly direction and scale, not invention: records collected to check eligibility began moving toward enforcement, and the force that acts on them doubled. Third, some pieces were switched off. DOGE's temporary organisation ended on 4 July 2026. Quiet Skies, a programme that sent air marshals to watch travellers who were not on any watch list, was ended in June 2025. The Guard deployments to Los Angeles, Chicago and Portland ended on 31 December 2025 after the Supreme Court refused, at an interim stage, to allow the Illinois one. [cite:de21_ee_end] [cite:de25b_cnn_quietskies] [cite:de33_pbs_guard]

What the inventory does not contain is as important as what it does. It contains no single identity file joining a person's records across all these layers, no statute letting the government designate a domestic group as terrorist, no exit permit and no court order upholding the use of any of these tools against citizens for their speech. [cite:de30_dla_nspm7] [cite:de25b_kent] [scene:de34_missing_pieces_and_triggers|The last section] returns to those gaps. First comes the question the inventory raises: whether tools built for one group could be pointed at another.
