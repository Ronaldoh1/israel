Gaza

This ledger begins in Gaza because the war that began on 7 October 2023 began there. That morning the Qassam Brigades, Hamas's armed wing, led at least four other armed groups through the fence into southern Israel. They attacked at least 19 kibbutzim, five moshavim, the towns of Sderot and Ofakim and two music festivals. By AFP's cross-referenced count, cited by Human Rights Watch, 1,195 people were killed, 815 of them civilians, and 251 civilians and members of the security forces were taken into Gaza as hostages. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7] The Hamas volume tells the day in detail. [scene:gz6_attack|Gaza, Documented: the attack] Israel's government called what followed, in a statement of May 2024, a "defensive and just war to eliminate Hamas and to secure the release of our hostages." [cite:gz_toi_rafah] What follows sets out what that war did to Gaza, by source and counting method, with Israel's stated reasons alongside.

The earlier rounds

The war of 2023 was the fifth large Israeli operation in Gaza since Hamas took control of the Strip in 2007. In Operation Cast Lead, from December 2008 to January 2009, Palestinian deaths were counted at between 1,391 (B'Tselem) and 1,440 (the Gaza health ministry); the Israeli army's own count was 1,166. Thirteen Israelis were killed, ten of them soldiers, four of those by friendly fire. [cite:gz_wiki_castlead] In the war of July and August 2014, OCHA counted 2,220 Palestinians killed, 1,492 of them civilians; Israel's foreign ministry counted 2,125, about 36 per cent of them civilians. Sixty-seven Israeli soldiers and six civilians in Israel were killed, more than 7,000 homes were razed, and up to 520,000 people were displaced at the peak. [cite:gz_wiki_2014] In May 2021 at least 256 Palestinians were killed in Gaza, 66 of them children by the UN's count, and at least 13 people in Israel; about 4,360 rockets were fired from Gaza. [cite:gz_wiki_2021] Part Five of the Gaza volume compares these wars. [scene:gz5_castlead|Gaza, Documented: Cast Lead] [scene:gz5_2012_2014|2012 and 2014] [scene:gz5_march_2021|2018 to 2021] The 2023 war killed many times more people than all of them together.

The dead and the wounded

The figures below come from three sources, which count in different ways, and they are not combined. The first is the Gaza health ministry, which counts deaths registered through hospitals, morgues and family reports and does not separate fighters from civilians. On 30 September 2026 the UN's humanitarian office (OCHA), citing the ministry, gave 74,032 Palestinians killed and 175,142 injured since 7 October 2023. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] By 12 October the ministry's count had reached 74,382, of whom 1,493 had been killed since the ceasefire of 10 October 2025. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] The total kept rising for two reasons: people were still being killed, and bodies recovered from the rubble were added to the register: between 12 August and 12 October the count of those killed since the truce rose by 234, and the overall total by about a thousand. [cite:gi14_olj_toll_aug26] [cite:gz_aj_year2026] Israel cast doubt on the ministry's figures for more than two years. In late January 2026 Haaretz reported that the army had accepted its estimate of about 71,000 dead; on 30 January the army spokesman Nadav Shoshani said such reports did not reflect official data. [cite:gz_yahoo_idf71k] [cite:gi14a_na_idfdeny]

The ministry gives a breakdown only for the dead it has identified by name. Of 71,444 identified by 31 December 2025, 21,283 were children, 10,983 women, 5,100 elderly people and 34,078 men. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] The UN Commission of Inquiry, working from the same records to 7 October 2025, put the number of children killed at no fewer than 20,179 and the number injured at 44,143. It found that children were 30 per cent of the dead, against about 24 per cent in the wars of 2008–09 and 2014. [cite:pr_nw_coi2026]

The second source is a peer-reviewed estimate. A study in The Lancet used three independent sources of names and estimated 64,260 deaths from traumatic injury up to the end of June 2024. The ministry had counted 37,877 by then, an undercount of about 41 per cent. [cite:gz_aj_lancet] The third is Israel's own data. A classified Israeli military database, reported in August 2025, listed 8,900 named fighters of Hamas and Islamic Jihad as dead or probably dead by May 2025, when the ministry's count was about 53,000. That is about 17 per cent. The army called the figures "incorrect." [cite:gz_aj_83pct] On Israel's side, OCHA, citing the Israeli military's website, gives 474 soldiers killed in the Gaza ground operation. Israeli media citing official sources put Israel's total dead, including 7 October and its immediate aftermath, at over 1,200. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap]

Some of the people who died were there to help others or to report. OCHA counts 597 aid workers killed, among them 394 staff of UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestinian refugees, 55 Palestine Red Crescent workers and four of the International Committee of the Red Cross. The health ministry counted more than 1,700 health workers killed to October 2025, and Gaza's civil defence lost 145 staff on duty. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] The Committee to Protect Journalists counted 189 Palestinian journalists killed in Gaza by 24 September 2026. The Palestinian Journalists Syndicate, which counts media workers more widely, gave 262 to 30 June 2026. [cite:gz_cpj_2026] [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] Foreign journalists were still barred from entering independently in October 2026. [scene:gz12_access|Gaza, Documented: why the record is thin]

Displacement and destruction

The damage was surveyed from satellite imagery and on the ground. Images taken by the UN's satellite centre on 11 October 2025, the day after the ceasefire, showed about 81 per cent of all structures in the Strip damaged or destroyed. [cite:gz_unosat2025] The World Bank, UN and EU damage assessment counted 371,888 of Gaza's 485,361 housing units destroyed or damaged, 76.6 per cent, and found that the economy had contracted by 84 per cent. It put recovery needs at $71.4 billion over a decade. [cite:gz_rdna2026] [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] By the Education Cluster's count, 526 of 564 school buildings needed full reconstruction or major repair. Eighty-eight per cent of above-ground water and sanitation assets and 76 per cent of underground networks were damaged. Only 3 per cent of cropland was undamaged and accessible. Of Gaza's 670 health facilities, 355 were not functioning at the end of September 2026, 273 were functioning only in part and 42 fully. [cite:gi14a_ocha_gazasnap]

Almost the whole population was displaced at some point. In mid-2026, 1.98 million people, 94 per cent, needed shelter assistance and 65 per cent of households surveyed lived in tents or makeshift shelters. In August 2026, 57 per cent of households surveyed had less than the six litres of drinking water a person needs each day. [cite:gi14_ocha_gazasnap] On 22 August 2025 the IPC, the UN-backed system that classifies hunger, found that "famine is now occurring in Gaza City and its surrounding area." Israel's foreign ministry answered: "There is no famine in Gaza." [cite:gz_aj_ipc] [cite:gz_olj_ipc] After the ceasefire the IPC found no area in famine, but at least 1.6 million people still faced high levels of acute food insecurity. [cite:pr_un_ipc_dec2025] Part Seven of the Gaza volume sets out the siege, the aid blockade and the dispute over the counts. [scene:gz7_toll|Gaza, Documented: the dead, the ruins and the famine]

Israel's stated reasons

Israel's government has given two answers to the charge that it fought without regard for civilians. It accused Hamas of operating from hospitals and civilian buildings, which Hamas denies; and it said it went to great lengths to avoid civilian deaths. [cite:gz_aj_lancet] It also said in May 2024 that it "has not and will not" create conditions of life that could bring about the population's physical destruction. [cite:gz_toi_rafah] The rulings of the International Court of Justice, the warrants of the International Criminal Court and Israel's answers to them belong to Part Sixteen and the Gaza volume. [scene:gz7_courts|Gaza, Documented: the courts] [scene:gi17_courts|Part Sixteen: the courts] Hamas held living hostages until 13 October 2025, when it released the last twenty in exchange for more than 1,900 Palestinian prisoners and detainees; the remains of 28 dead hostages were still in Gaza that day and were returned in stages. [cite:gz_kt_hostages] [cite:gz_wiki_timeline2025]

Destabilisation, and where things stand

The ceasefire left Gaza without a functioning government, economy or security force of its own. A year on, aid entered at about a third of the agreed rate and no international stabilisation force had deployed. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] ACLED documented, as of April 2026, at least five anti-Hamas armed groups operating from Israeli-held areas, and assessed that Israel supplies them with weapons, logistics, intelligence and training; they had been involved in more than 40 violent events and nearly 80 deaths since the ceasefire. [cite:hz11_acled_gaza] Netanyahu had confirmed in June 2025 that Israel had "activated clans in Gaza who oppose Hamas," saying it saved soldiers' lives. [cite:hz11_ynet_clans] As of October 2026, OCHA reported restrictions on 65 per cent of the Strip, and Israel had told the UN in June that it controlled about 70 per cent. About 2.1 million people were left in roughly 35 per cent of the territory. [cite:gi9_aj_maps] Part Nine traces that line. [scene:gi9_gaza|Part Nine: the Gaza perimeter]

Whether the war served territorial expansion is an argument, and both sides of it rest on statements. On one side, Netanyahu said in May 2026 that his "directive is to go to 70" per cent of the Strip, and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich said in January 2025 that he was developing an "operational plan" for what he calls the "voluntary emigration" of Gazans. [cite:gi9_olj_net70] [cite:gz_jc_smotrich] On the other, the US plan Israel accepted states that "Israel will not occupy or annex Gaza," and Netanyahu said in August 2025: "We don't want to govern it." [cite:gz_almon_plantext] [cite:gz_mm_netanyahu_aug25] No Israeli law or government decision claims Gaza as sovereign territory. This book's finding of documented expansion programmes is limited to the West Bank and the Golan. [scene:gi13_record|Part Eighteen: the record]
