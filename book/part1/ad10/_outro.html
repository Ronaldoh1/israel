Part Ten established that the private pension did not end by law but by choice: the active members of private defined-benefit plans fell from 71 per cent of the total in 1975 to about 10 per cent in 2023, so that by March 2026 only 14 per cent of private workers had a pension available while 70 per cent had a 401(k)-type account, which carries investment and longevity risk on the worker. [cite:ad10_dol_hist] [cite:ad10_bls_ebs26] Those accounts reach the low-paid least, hold a median of $44,115 against an average of $167,970, and leave 45 per cent of families with no retirement account at all, though automatic enrolment and falling fees show the model can be repaired. [cite:us22_bls_ebs25] [cite:ad10_vanguard_has26] [cite:ad10_scf26] [cite:ad10_ici_fees25] Social Security is the part of the promise that has held: old-age poverty fell from 35.2 per cent in 1959 to 9.9 per cent in 2024, and the programme moved 28.8 million people out of poverty in 2025; its combined reserves are projected to run out in 2034, after which about 83 per cent of scheduled benefits would still be paid unless Congress acts, while the cap now leaves about a fifth of covered wages untaxed. [cite:ad10_census_a3] [cite:us18_census_p60290] [cite:ad10_crs_tr26] [cite:ad10_crs_base] Measured against what old age costs, the record is thinner: 15.4 per cent of older Americans were poor on the supplemental measure in 2025, half of older people living alone fell below the Elder Index in 2019, and nearly one in five people over 65 is still working. [cite:ad10_aarp_spm65] [cite:ad10_umb_2019] [cite:ad10_pew_older] These figures stand as of October 2026. Who pays for all of this, through taxes, fees and fines, is the subject of Part Eleven.
