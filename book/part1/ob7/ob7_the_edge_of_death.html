The Edge of Death

The AWARE study, led by Dr Sam Parnia and published in 2014, followed 2,060 patients who had cardiac arrests in 15 hospitals in the United Kingdom, the United States and Austria. [cite:ob_soton_aware] Among survivors well enough to be interviewed in detail, 39 percent described some awareness during the arrest without an explicit memory of events. Of those who went on to longer interviews, 46 percent described recollections that did not fit the usual picture of a near-death experience, including fearful and persecutory ones; 9 percent had experiences that did fit it; and 2 percent described full awareness of the kind reported in out-of-body experiences, with explicit recall of seeing and hearing. [cite:ob_soton_aware]

One case could be checked. A patient's recollection was matched to sounds made during the resuscitation, which placed his awareness in a period of about three minutes when he had no heartbeat. [cite:ob_soton_aware] Parnia's summary was that "death is not a specific moment but a potentially reversible process". [cite:ob_soton_aware]

AWARE II

The second study enrolled 567 patients in 25 hospitals, mostly in the United States and Britain, between May 2017 and March 2020, and was published in the journal Resuscitation on 14 September 2023. [cite:ob_elsevier_aware2] Fewer than one in ten patients survived to leave hospital, and four in ten of the survivors recalled some degree of consciousness during resuscitation that standard measures had not captured. [cite:ob_elsevier_aware2] In 85 patients whose brain activity was monitored, nearly 40 percent showed activity that returned to normal or near normal, at points up to an hour into resuscitation. [cite:ob_elsevier_aware2]

These studies support two claims and not a third. They show that some people recall awareness during resuscitation, and that the brain can show signs of electrical recovery long after the heart has stopped. [cite:ob_elsevier_aware2] They do not show that awareness continues without a brain: the second study's own measurements found the brain more active than expected, which points towards a brain still working, not one left behind. The single timed case is a real data point and remains a single case.
