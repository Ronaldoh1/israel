Part One covers the three decades in which Israel and Iran were allies in all but name. It begins with Iran's vote against the partition of Palestine and its quiet recognition of Israel in 1950, and with the coup of 1953, which the CIA has since acknowledged directing and which restored a Shah who made the alliance possible. It follows the alliance through Israel's search for non-Arab friends on the region's edge, the intelligence partnership between Mossad and the Shah's security service, SAVAK, the route by which Iraqi Jews reached Israel through Iran, the trade, advisers and arms, and a secret missile project signed in 1977. It sets the Shah's public distance from Israel beside the private record, and corrects a widely repeated claim that Iran joined the 1973 oil embargo. It ends with the pipeline the two governments built together, whose ownership is still being argued over in Swiss arbitration nearly half a century after the alliance ended.
