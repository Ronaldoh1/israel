Part One established the frame. A canon closes revelation, names its authorised readers and, through them, the rites and revenues that depend on the scriptures. Irenaeus tied the four gospels to a line of twelve Roman bishops around 180. [cite:cn_iren_3_3] Neither popular story holds: the canon was not a quiet consensus, since Trent's binding vote of 1546 went 24 to 15, and it was not chosen at Nicaea, which never discussed it. [cite:vt14_wiki_trentcanon] [cite:vt22_canons] What remains to be traced is the documented link between the closing of the list and the growth of the office that held it. Part Two begins where the question was first forced: with a rich man's list and the church that returned his money.
