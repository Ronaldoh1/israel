Part Six established that the two states draw their membership lines on different principles. Israel grants immediate citizenship to Jews and their relatives under the Law of Return and gives citizenship at birth only to children of citizens; the United States grants it to almost everyone born on its soil, a rule the Supreme Court upheld on 30 June 2026, and naturalises permanent residents after five years. [cite:rs_ch01_lor_text] [cite:us6_refworld_nationality] [cite:us6_fd_barbara] Immigration to Israel came in waves, 199,516 arrivals in 1990 and 74,400 in 2022 but 22,522 in 2025, while American naturalisations rose from about 210,000 a year in the 1980s to 878,460 in 2023. [cite:us6_jvl_aliyah] [cite:us6_ohss_nat2023] Each system leaves people out: in Israel, Palestinian spouses of citizens under the 2003 law re-enacted in 2022, most of East Jerusalem's Palestinians, and those whose citizenship can be revoked under the 2023 law; in the United States, about 14 million unauthorised residents with no route to naturalise. [cite:rs_ch01_toi_citizenship2022] [cite:us6_aje_revocation] [cite:us6_pew_immig2025] Israel recognised fewer than 1 per cent of its African asylum seekers and held more than 13,000 in Holot before closing it in 2018, but about 25,000 remain; the United States cut refugee admissions to the lowest ceiling in its history, carried about 3.8 million cases in its immigration courts, and funded enforcement at several times its earlier level, while courts in both countries struck down parts of each government's programme.
