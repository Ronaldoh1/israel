How a Removal Is Weighed

Taking a node off the map is simple. Deciding what else falls with it is not, and the map follows a short list of rules, the same for every node. Each comes from how the record itself worked.

What no longer connects is lost. A tie counts only if it still connects, through the ties that remain, to the bodies that founded the state: the Zionist Organization, the Jewish Agency, the Haganah, the provisional government, Resolution 181 and the Declaration of Independence. Money given to an organization that no longer reaches any of them builds nothing.

Someone has to pursue the goal. If the Zionist Organization, the Jewish Agency and the Jewish National Council (the Va'ad Leumi) are all removed, every channel counts as empty. Outside support remains on the map, but on this record none of it set out to build the state on its own.

Documents need the bodies that issued them. A declaration, treaty or resolution loses its force when every body that issued or signed it is removed. A document that wrote another into its text falls with it: the Mandate carried the Balfour Declaration. The Mandate also needs its Mandatory power, Britain. It was a framework, not a single act. Under it the Jewish population grew from 83,794 in 1922 to about 630,000 in 1947, some 21,900 a year, against about 2,200 a year under Ottoman rule (24,000 in 1882 to 94,000 in 1914). [cite:f_jvl_pop] [cite:f_pq_mandate] Without the Mandate, immigration and settlement from 1920 to 1948 go at the Ottoman pace. That credits to the Mandate some of the immigration that persecution in Europe drove in the 1930s, and the map says so.

The UN vote needs two-thirds. Resolution 181 passed on 29 November 1947 by 33 votes to 13, with 10 abstentions, and it needed two-thirds of the votes cast. [cite:u_jvl_181] A removed state that voted yes counts as abstaining (or, in a harsher reading the map offers, as voting no); if the yes votes fall below twice the no votes, the resolution fails.

Blocs follow their leaders. In 1947 and 1948 most governments moved with the power they depended on. The map takes the blocs from the treaties of the time: the Americas under the Rio Treaty, signed on 2 September 1947 [cite:m_wp_rio]; Western Europe under the Convention for European Economic Cooperation, the Marshall Plan accord signed in Paris on 16 April 1948 [cite:m_cvce_oeec]; and the Soviet republics and Moscow's allies. The partition vote measures how closely each bloc followed its leader: two-thirds of the Rio states and nearly three-quarters of the Marshall Plan states voted as the United States did, and four of the five on the Soviet side voted as Moscow did. [cite:u_jvl_181] If a leader is out, each member keeps its vote, and its 1948 recognition, only at its bloc's measured independence. The vote then passes with a probability, not a certainty.

A decision's chance of still happening is the documented push over push plus resistance, measured against the full record, where it happened. Push aimed at the man himself (his staff, his friends, his correspondents) does not pass to a successor unless the successor had his own documented tie to that person. The resistance stays. A successor's own documented position counts for or against: Henry Wallace had met Ben-Gurion in Palestine in October 1947 and in 1948 attacked Truman from the pro-Israel side. [cite:p_aish_wallace] [cite:p_wp_wallace] A successor with no documented position adds one unit to each side, an even chance. The overall chance is then averaged over every combination of decisions happening or not. Each push and each resistance counts as one; that is the map's simplest assumption, and it is disclosed with every reading.

Five readings, one verdict. Each removal is read five ways: the chance of a founding on time, in 1948; by 1958; by 1978; ever; and whether the movement itself survives, keeping its money and organization whatever happens to the state. A later founding waits while the lost capacity is rebuilt. The network that is left rebuilds at the pace each channel was first built, scaled to how much of it remains: half a channel rebuilds at half the speed. Each year of waiting costs 2 percent of the chance, because the window of 1947 and 1948 was not going to stay open: the end of the Mandate, the UN vote, the sympathy that followed the Holocaust, and American and Soviet support at the same time. The verdict reads the on-time chance first, then the chance ever: "happens on time" at 85 percent on time or more; "still likely, but delayed" at 70 on time or 75 ever; "possible, with great difficulty" at 55 ever; "unlikely" at 30 ever; below that, "not possible on this record".
