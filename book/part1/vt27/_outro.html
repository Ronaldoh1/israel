Part Twenty-Seven established that the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, founded through Frumentius, whom Athanasius consecrated, holds the Nicene faith and counts 81 books of scripture, reached either as 54 plus 27 (the narrower canon of the imperial Bibles) or as 46 plus 35 (the broader canon of the commentary tradition and of the church's own website), with the Fetha Negest's heading of 81 standing over a list of 73. [cite:vt27_wiki_frumentius] [cite:vt27_eotc_chalcedon] [cite:vt27_cowley1974] [cite:vt27_eotc_books] Its unique books are old and new: 1 Enoch and Jubilees from Judea before the Christian era, quoted in Jude and copied at Qumran, and the Meqabyan books written in Ethiopia in the Middle Ages. [cite:vt27_wiki_enoch] [cite:vt27_wiki_jubilees] [cite:vt27_wiki_meqabyan] Enoch left Western use through the judgments of Jerome and Augustine, not by any papal order found here, and returned through James Bruce's copies in 1773 and Laurence's translation in 1821; Rome's documented intervention in Ethiopia, the Latin patriarchate of 1625–1634, targeted the church's rite and Sabbath and was reversed in 1632.
