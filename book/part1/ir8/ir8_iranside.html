Iran's Side: Plots Abroad, Ships, Water and Detainees

The shadow war was fought by both sides. Iran's government and the bodies it directs, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and its Quds Force above all, were accused of attacks on Israeli diplomats and travellers abroad, of attacks on ships linked to Israeli owners, of a cyberattack on Israel's water system and of holding foreign and dual nationals as bargaining chips. The evidence for these accusations varies in strength. A few rest on court judgments. Others rest on the accusing government's word, and some on anonymous officials quoted in newspapers. Iran denied almost all of them. This section sets the evidence out case by case.

The clearest cluster came in February 2012, a month after Mostafa Ahmadi Roshan was killed in Tehran. On 13 February a motorcyclist attached a magnetic bomb to an Israeli embassy car in New Delhi. The explosion wounded the diplomat inside, her driver and two passers-by. The same day police in Tbilisi defused a bomb fixed to another Israeli embassy car. Netanyahu called Iran "the biggest exporter of terror in the world." Iran's foreign ministry spokesman, Ramin Mehmanparast, replied: "It seems that these suspicious incidents are designed by the Zionist regime." [cite:ir8_aj_delhi2012] In March 2012 Indian police named three Iranian men, who had entered India on tourist visas and left after the bombing, as the main suspects and issued warrants for them. They also arrested an Indian journalist who had worked part-time for Iran's state news agency. [cite:ir8_brec_delhi]

The day after the Delhi bombing, on 14 February, explosives went off by accident in a rented house in Bangkok. One of the occupants, Saeid Moradi, threw a bomb at police and lost his legs when it went off beside him. An expert witness told the court that five C-4 charges had been hidden in radios inside the house. On 22 August 2013 a Thai court sentenced Moradi to life imprisonment and a second Iranian, Mohammad Khazaei, to 15 years. Both had denied the charges. Two other suspects were believed to have returned to Iran. Israel's ambassador, Simon Roded, said the sentence proved "once again that Iran is engaged and in proliferation of terror all around the world." [cite:ir8_afp_bangkok] The Bangkok judgment is a court finding against two Iranian nationals for an explosives plot. The verdict as reported did not establish the intended target, and it did not make a finding about who in Iran directed the plot. On 18 July 2012 a bus carrying Israeli tourists was bombed at Burgas in Bulgaria, killing five Israelis and the Bulgarian driver. A Bulgarian court later convicted two men in absentia for helping the bomber. That attack was attributed to Hezbollah, which denied it, and the Hezbollah volume covers it. [cite:hz4_aje_burgas] [scene:hz4_abroad|Attacks abroad] The highest court finding on Iran's own role in attacks abroad is the ruling of Argentina's Federal Criminal Cassation Court of 11 April 2024, which held that Iran ordered the 1992 Israeli embassy and 1994 AMIA bombings in Buenos Aires. [cite:hz4_bat_cassation] Part Nine returns to Iran's record abroad. [scene:ir9_record|Iran's record]

From 2019 the fight moved to the sea, and here the accusations ran both ways. On 11 March 2021 the Wall Street Journal reported that since late 2019 Israel had attacked at least 12 vessels bound for Syria, most of them carrying Iranian oil to the Syrian government in breach of US sanctions, using water mines and limpet mines. According to the report, some of the attacks hit weapons cargoes. No ship was sunk and no casualties were reported. Israeli and Iranian officials declined to comment. [cite:ir8_twz_tankers] [cite:ir8_almon_wsj] On 25 February 2021 the Israeli-owned car carrier Helios Ray was holed by an explosion in the Gulf of Oman. Israeli officials blamed the IRGC. Iran denied involvement and called the incident a "false flag." On 10 March Iran's state shipping line said one of its container ships, the Shahr-e Kord, had been hit by a "terrorist" attack in the Mediterranean. [cite:ir8_twz_tankers] The campaign turned lethal on 29 July 2021. Drones struck the tanker Mercer Street off Oman, killing a British and a Romanian crew member. The ship was managed by Zodiac Maritime, part of the Israeli businessman Eyal Ofer's group. Secretary of State Antony Blinken said the United States was "confident" that Iran had carried out the attack. The British foreign secretary said it was "highly likely," and Prime Minister Naftali Bennett said "Iran is the one who carried out the attack." Iran's foreign ministry called the accusation "baseless." None of the three governments publicly produced physical evidence. [cite:ir8_ap_mercer]

The water attack was reported in the same way, through officials speaking anonymously. On 24 April 2020, according to the Washington Post, hackers tried to break into the computers that run rural water systems in Israel. The attempt failed and caused no damage. Officials attributed it to Iran, and Iran denied it. On 9 May computer systems at the Shahid Rajaee port terminal near Bandar Abbas, on the Strait of Hormuz, failed, leaving trucks queued for kilometres and ships waiting offshore. Officials told the Post that Israel had carried out the attack in retaliation. One of them called it "highly accurate." Iran's port authority said the hackers "failed to penetrate" its systems. Israel did not comment. [cite:ir8_aj_port2020]

The detention of foreigners is documented more directly, because the releases were negotiated in public. On 18 September 2023 five Americans whom the US government described as "unjustly detained" in Iran, among them Siamak Namazi, Emad Shargi and Morad Tahbaz, were flown out. In return the United States released five Iranians and allowed $6 billion of frozen Iranian funds to be moved from South Korea to Qatar, for use only on humanitarian purchases. US officials warned that Iran might go on using detained Americans as "bargaining chips." The same day Washington imposed sanctions on Iran's intelligence ministry over the 2007 disappearance in Iran of Robert Levinson. The comparison shows a pattern that runs through the whole record. Most acts on both sides were neither confirmed nor denied by the state responsible, and most attributions rest on unnamed officials. The two kinds of evidence point in different directions. Court findings exist almost only against Iranian nationals and Iranian-directed operations abroad, because those acts took place in countries with open courts. The killings inside Iran were never tried anywhere outside Iran. Each government described the other's operations as terrorism. Netanyahu called Iran "the biggest exporter of terror in the world," and Iran called the Natanz blackout "nuclear terrorism."
