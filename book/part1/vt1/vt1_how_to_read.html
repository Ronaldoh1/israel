How to Read This Volume

A book about the Vatican has to settle its words before it can settle anything else, because the same word is used in the press for three different things and many of the errors this volume corrects begin with that confusion. This section sets out the vocabulary, the labels the volume puts on its evidence, the few places where it allows itself an interpretation, and the route through its nineteen parts.

Three subjects, not one

The Holy See is the sovereign authority of the pope as head of the Catholic Church, and it is the subject of international law: it makes treaties, sends and receives ambassadors and has been a permanent observer at the United Nations since 6 April 1964. [cite:dv1_wiki_holysee_un] The Lateran Treaty of 11 February 1929 recorded Italy's recognition of "the sovereignty of the Holy See in the international field" as an attribute inherent to its nature. [cite:vt1_va_lateran] As of January 2026 it had full diplomatic relations with 184 states, the European Union and the Sovereign Order of Malta. [cite:dv1_dfat_holysee] When this volume describes a recognition, a refusal or a treaty, the actor is the Holy See, and usually its Secretariat of State.

Vatican City State is the territory: the small enclave in Rome created by the same treaty, in which Italy recognized the Holy See's "full ownership and exclusive and absolute power and sovereign jurisdiction." [cite:vt1_va_lateran] It is an elective absolute monarchy in which the pope holds legislative, executive and judicial power, and it had 882 residents in 2024. [cite:dv1_wiki_vatican] Its government, courts, police, post office and museums belong to the City State. The Holy See existed for centuries before 1929 and does not depend on the City State for its legal personality; the City State exists to give the Holy See a territory it does not have to share with any other sovereign. [scene:dv1_sovereignty|The Divine 3+1] applies the standard tests of statehood to it, and [scene:vt4_holy_see_vs_city|Part Four] sets out how the two are run.

The Catholic Church is a worldwide communion of believers organized in dioceses, religious orders, parishes, schools, hospitals and charities, most of which are separate legal persons under the law of the countries where they stand. A diocese in Pennsylvania or an order's university in Spain is not the Holy See, and its property and its debts are not the Holy See's. This volume writes "the Church" only where the subject really is the whole Church, as in the acts of a council. Elsewhere it names the body that acted: the Secretariat of State, the Institute for the Works of Religion, a named nuncio, a named bishop, a named order. "The Vatican" is used only for the place, or where a quoted source uses it.

The same care applies to the other side of the central question. Zionism is a political movement; the State of Israel is a state; Judaism is a religion, and Jews are a people with every range of view on all three. The Holy See's own documents have come to separate them. Its 1985 Notes asked Catholics to understand the Jewish religious attachment to the land "without however making their own any particular religious interpretation of this relationship", and placed the State of Israel under "the common principles of international law." [cite:ts1_crrj_notes1985] This volume follows that separation. A pope's teaching about the Jewish people and the Holy See's policy toward a state are different subjects, and the volume never writes "the Jews" where a document speaks of the Zionist Organization, the State of Israel or a named person.

Three labels for evidence

Every claim in the volume is cited at the end of its sentence or paragraph, and the sources carry one of three strengths. A documented claim rests on the document itself: a treaty text, an encyclical, a court judgment, an annual report, or a person's own dated words. A reported claim rests on a named outlet or a named researcher. A disputed claim is one where the record contains a denial or a conflicting account, or where it rests on one party's report of a private exchange. Herzl's diary shows the difference: it is documented evidence of what Herzl wrote on 26 January 1904, and the words it puts in Pius X's mouth are one party's report of a private conversation, since the account of the audience rests on his diary alone. [cite:dv1_ej_piusx] [cite:vt1_ccjr_herzl]

Three further rules follow from those labels. Living people are presumed innocent, and every claim about a living person that touches a court case carries its status as of October 2026. Abuse figures are never added across countries, because the national reports use different methods, periods and scopes; each figure is given with its own method. And beside every critical finding the volume gives the stated position of the Holy See or of the person concerned, in their own words where they have given them.

Myths, and where interpretation is allowed

Much of what is written about the Vatican online, including some earlier material on this platform, repeats claims that have no documentary basis: a "Grey Pope" said to rule both the pope and the head of the Jesuits, an unbroken chain of Rothschild control over papal finance from 1831 to the present, a single family descended from antiquity standing behind the throne. This volume does not ignore such claims. It names each one, dates its first appearance, traces the chain of sources it rests on and says where the chain breaks, in [scene:vt6_grey_pope_myth|Part Six] and [scene:vt11_chain_tested|Part Eleven]. Where a claim belongs to an antisemitic tradition, as the claim that Jewish bankers control the papacy does, the volume says so.

A small number of interpretive readings appear because they are part of how the subject has been discussed on this platform: the wordplay that reads the bank's initials, IOR, backwards; the description of the Holy See as an operating system written in code; the line of imperial succession some writers draw from Babylon to Rome; the "loud silence" said to link papal reticence in different eras; and the philosophical argument that an institution offering a promise no one can verify must protect itself. Each appears only under a subhead that begins "Interpretation, not finding", is attributed to whoever holds it, and is never used as evidence for a documentary claim.

The route through the volume

The nineteen parts fall into six groups. Parts Two to Five explain what kind of sovereign the Holy See is: why there is a Vatican at all, from the papal state of 754 to the pacts of 1929 ([scene:vt2_papal_states|Part Two]); what each side gained in 1929 and what the deal still costs Italy ([scene:vt3_what_each_got|Part Three]); how the City State is actually governed ([scene:vt4_fundamental_law|Part Four]); and who recognizes and checks its sovereignty ([scene:vt5_recognition|Part Five]). Parts Six to Eight turn to people and orders: popes, the Roman Curia and the myths of hidden popes ([scene:vt6_curia_2022|Part Six]); the old Roman aristocracy and the Orsini ([scene:vt7_black_nobility|Part Seven]); and the Jesuits, Opus Dei and the Order of Malta ([scene:vt8_jesuits|Part Eight]).

Parts Nine to Eleven follow the money: the Institute for the Works of Religion, the property administration and Peter's Pence ([scene:vt9_ior|Part Nine]); the scandals from Banco Ambrosiano to the trial over the London property and the retrial ordered in 2026 ([scene:vt10_ambrosiano_calvi|Part Ten]); and what the documents show about Rome and the Rothschilds ([scene:vt11_papal_loans|Part Eleven]). Parts Twelve to Fifteen test the Holy See where its public statements and its conduct have most often diverged: clergy sexual abuse and its cover-up ([scene:vt12_the_rules|Part Twelve]); its reach through schools, universities and the appointment of bishops ([scene:vt13_schools|Part Thirteen]); its texts and archives ([scene:vt14_apostolic_archive|Part Fourteen]); and the disappearances of Emanuela Orlandi and Mirella Gregori in 1983 ([scene:vt15_the_case|Part Fifteen]).

Parts Sixteen to Eighteen give the record on Zionism and Israel in order: from Herzl's audience to the end of the Second World War ([scene:vt16_herzl_1904|Part Sixteen]); from the Jerusalem campaign of 1947–49 to recognition in 1993 ([scene:vt17_jerusalem_1947_49|Part Seventeen]); and from 1994 to the Gaza war and the present ([scene:vt18_relations_since_1994|Part Eighteen]). [scene:vt19_predictions_tested|Part Nineteen] applies the five tests of the previous section to that record and gives the verdict, with what remains open.
