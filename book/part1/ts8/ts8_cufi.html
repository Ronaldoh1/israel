Christians United for Israel

The organisation that made Christian Zionism a standing lobby in Washington was founded by John Hagee, a pastor in San Antonio, Texas. Christians United for Israel (CUFI) had first been set up under that name in 1975 by David A. Lewis; Hagee relaunched it, and it was incorporated on 7 February 2006 with the backing of "over 400 pastors and ministry leaders." Its founding executive director was David Brog, who stepped down in April 2018; Diana Hagee and Shari Dollinger then became co-executive directors. [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] Brog's book Standing with Israel (2006) and Hagee's In Defense of Israel (2007) set out the case for Christian readers, and a student wing, CUFI on Campus, followed. [cite:ts8_jvl_cufi]

Members: a figure it counts itself

CUFI's best-known number is its membership, and every version of it is the organisation's own. It has never published a definition of a member or an audited count, and the dated figures do not always agree with one another. It reported passing one million members in March 2012 and two million in January 2015. [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] Vice President Mike Pence, addressing its summit on 17 July 2017, gave "more than 3.3 million believers." [cite:ts8_wh_pence2017] CUFI's own press release of 27 November 2018 gave "more than 4.5 million," while the encyclopaedic record puts "over 5.1 million" a month later. [cite:ts8_cufi_ky2018] [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] On 8 July 2019 Pence said CUFI represented "6 million," then, after a call from the audience, "She said 7 million." [cite:ts8_wh_pence2019] CUFI announced on 22 December 2020 that it had passed 10 million, without saying how members are counted, and later accounts repeat "over ten million." [cite:ts8_hfl_10m] [cite:ts8_jvl_cufi] The growth from a few hundred pastors to millions of names is real as a claim and is unchecked as a count; on that basis this volume does not compare CUFI's size with that of other lobbies.

The summit and the lobbying day

CUFI's main instrument is an annual summit in Washington, followed by a day on which delegates visit congressional offices. In 2013 the eighth summit heard Netanyahu by satellite, and its eastern regional director, Victor Styrsky, reported, "We took Capitol Hill by storm today!" [cite:ts8_charisma_2013] [cite:ts8a_charisma_2013] In July 2014, during the Gaza war, the summit drew "some 4,800" delegates by one reporter's count; Netanyahu sent a taped message, Senator Lindsey Graham told Israel to "go as far as you need to go," and the next day members lobbied Congress to stop funding the Palestinian Authority. [cite:ts8_jp_summit2014] [cite:ts8a_jp_summit2014] Netanyahu had addressed the summit by video in 2010, and on 8 July 2019, the day Pence spoke, he addressed it again from Jerusalem. [cite:gi11_wiki_cufi] [cite:ts8a_inn_netanyahu2019]

The vice president's two speeches are the clearest record of what the summit meant to a sitting administration. In 2017 Pence said that "my passion for Israel springs from my Christian faith" and, on moving the embassy to Jerusalem, "It is not a question of if, it is only when." [cite:ts8_wh_pence2017] In 2019 he cited the embassy move and the recognition of Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights as done. [cite:ts8_wh_pence2019] The speeches show the administration presenting those decisions to CUFI as delivered; whether CUFI's lobbying caused them is a separate question, examined in [scene:ts12_embassy|Part Twelve].

Lobbying also reached individual members of Congress in their districts. In November 2018 CUFI and its legislative arm, the CUFI Action Fund, put $100,000 into two weeks of digital and cable advertising in Kentucky and bought full-page newspaper advertisements to press Senator Rand Paul to drop his hold on a bill codifying US security aid to Israel; Hagee said Paul was "way outside the mainstream on this." [cite:ts8_cufi_ky2018] Before the Republican primary of 19 May 2026 the Action Fund spent "six figures" on billboards in Representative Thomas Massie's district, reading "Tell Massie: Support Peace Through Strength" beside an image of Massie over the Iranian flag; Ari Morgenstern, a senior director of the Action Fund, said it was securing "every available billboard in the district." [cite:ts8_ji_massie] [cite:ts8a_ji_massie] Massie lost to the Trump-endorsed Ed Gallrein in a race that cost about $37 million in all, in which other pro-Israel committees spent about $8 million and the Associated Press judged Trump's opposition the key factor. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways] The race is set out in [scene:pm5_massie|the Follow the Money volume].

What CUFI says it wants

CUFI's stated policy agenda, as summarised from its own materials, is support for US aid to Israel and its military edge, opposition to international pressure on Israel to make concessions, sanctions on Iran, adoption of the IHRA definition of antisemitism, opposition to the boycott movement through federal and state law, and support for recognising Israeli sovereignty over the Golan. [cite:ts8_jvl_cufi] On territory, Hagee's theology and his policy line are distinct. In a January 2011 webcast he said that Jerusalem "should never be divided for any reason." [cite:gi11_cp_hagee2011] He has also said that "Israelis – and they alone – have the right to make existential decisions about land and peace," and described CUFI's action as asking the White House not to press concessions Israel does not wish to make. [cite:gi11_jpost_hagee] The documented position is thus that Israel's government decides, and Washington should not push it; it is not a CUFI programme for territorial expansion. [scene:gi11_christian|Greater Israel volume, Part Eleven]

On conversion, CUFI states an official policy against proselytising Jews, and Hagee has said that Christian Zionists support Israel out of gratitude to the Jewish people, not to convert them. [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] [cite:ts8_jvl_cufi] Jewish leaders and interfaith organisations have nonetheless raised concern, by the encyclopaedic record's account, that Hagee's teaching that salvation is through Christ alone may link the support to proselytising. [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi]

Controversy and disclosure

Hagee's preaching became a national issue in May 2008, when a 1990s sermon surfaced in which he said that God "sent a hunter" and that "Hitler was a hunter," reading the Holocaust as driving Jews towards their land. Senator John McCain, whom Hagee had endorsed for president, called the remarks "deeply offensive and indefensible" and repudiated them. Hagee called the charge that he condoned the Holocaust "the worst of lies" and withdrew his endorsement; earlier that month he had apologised for remarks critics considered anti-Catholic. [cite:ts8_cnn_hagee] [cite:ts8_ncr_hagee] He later apologised for saying Hitler had been fulfilling God's will. [cite:gi11a_wiki_cufi] The Middle East Council of Churches and Anglican and Catholic theologians have criticised CUFI's end-times theology. [cite:gi11a_wiki_cufi] Some accounts date the sermon to 2005; the 2008 reporting places it in the 1990s.

CUFI's finances are only partly public. The CUFI Action Fund, a 501(c)(4) in San Antonio, files returns: its revenue ranged from about $654,000 in 2015 to $1.8 million in 2017 and $1.7 million in 2021, and fell to $89,000 in 2024, when it spent $716,000; revenue in the intervening years was uneven, with $57,000 in 2019. [cite:ts8_pp_cufiaf] [cite:ts8a_pp_cufiaf] No return for the main CUFI organisation was found in the public database, and its budget and donors are not disclosed. A circulating claim that CUFI is registered with the IRS as a church could not be confirmed, nor could the claim that its returns show budgets in the tens of millions. The reference entry that lists CUFI as a 501(c)(4) gives the Action Fund's tax number. [cite:ts8_wiki_cufi] [cite:ts8_pp_cufiaf]
