Part Ten sorts the evidence people cite for a "Greater Israel" plan into three classes, and says plainly which is which. Some items are authentic but narrower than their use: the verse in Genesis, a note in Herzl's diary from 1898 that he never acted on, the boundary the Zionist Organization asked for at Paris in 1919, and a rabbi's answer to a United Nations committee in 1947. Some are real documents or objects that are misread: Oded Yinon's essay of 1982, the 1996 paper "A Clean Break" and Israel's 10-agora coin. Two are myths: the inscription said to hang over the Knesset and the claim that the flag's blue stripes stand for the Nile and the Euphrates. The part corrects an error carried in earlier volumes of this library, which called the Herzl note a fabrication, and ends with a ledger of all nine items.
