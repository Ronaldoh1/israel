This volume widens the map. The other books on this shelf follow Israel, the Palestinians and their nearest neighbours; this one follows the states further out whose choices have shaped the conflict: Saudi Arabia, Turkey, the Emirates, Bahrain, Morocco, Sudan, Azerbaijan and Pakistan, and the Jews of Ethiopia who were carried to Israel in secret airlifts. It opens with the lay of the land, the four centuries of Ottoman rule and the decade in which Britain and France drew the borders that every later war has fought over.

The record here is uneven. Some relationships are public and signed, like the Abraham Accords; some were kept secret for decades and are known from leaked cables, defectors and later admissions; some rest on single accounts that are given here as single accounts. Where a state's own statements and its conduct point in different directions, both are set down.
