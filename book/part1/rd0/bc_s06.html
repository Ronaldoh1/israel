Three Enclaves, One Trope

Three capital jurisdictions are regularly bundled into a single claim: the City of London, Vatican City and the District of Columbia. In its popular form, the claim holds that these are three sovereign city-states that together secretly govern the world's finance, religion and military, one apiece. Each of the three is a genuine legal anomaly. Taken one at a time, the documents show three different kinds of gap in ordinary accountability. Taken together, they show nothing that links them.

The City of London, as the previous section described, is a local authority. It has privileges preserved by express exception in Acts of Parliament, a business electorate, and an official lobbyist seated in the House of Commons. It is not a state. Parliament legislates for it, and the Crown Dependencies enjoy more autonomy than it does. [cite:wiki_city_of_london_corporation] [cite:metabunk_city_sovereign_debunk]

Vatican City is sovereign, but only recently and only by treaty. It became a state on 11 February 1929 under the Lateran Treaty with Mussolini's Italy. The treaty recognized papal sovereignty over the 44-hectare enclave and paid the Holy See compensation for the Papal States lost in 1870. The Vatican's financial bodies, the IOR, APSA and the Governorate, publish no statements equivalent to those of a comparable secular institution. They operate under canonical secrecy, the confidentiality of church law, and sit outside European Union banking regulation. [cite:wiki_lateran_treaty] Its statehood before 1929 is not documented; its opacity since then is.

The District of Columbia is in some ways the reverse of a sovereign enclave. Under Article I, Section 8 of the United States Constitution, it is a federal district under the exclusive legislative authority of Congress. Its residents have elected a mayor and council only since the District of Columbia Home Rule Act of 1973. Congress retains a veto over the District's legislation, and the roughly 700,000 residents have no voting member in either house. [cite:wiki_dc_home_rule] Far from standing above the nation, Washington is a capital with less self-government than any state.

Each of the three, then, is a real carve-out built into a capital: a business franchise inside a legislature, a sovereign bank outside banking regulation, a seat of government whose people lack a vote. That is a good deal to examine, and each deserves examination on its own terms.

Here the popular version and the documents part company. The record contains no treaty, charter, shared governing body or documented coordination among the three. The "empire of three cities" narrative has no primary source behind it. In circulation it tends to move in a predictable direction: from three real jurisdictional oddities, to a claim about hidden rulers, and from there into older ethnic and religious conspiracy tropes about who secretly controls the world. [cite:metabunk_city_sovereign_debunk] Bundling the three enclaves into one hidden empire adds nothing the documents support and brings in a great deal that they do not.

For that reason this book treats them separately. The City's role in the story of British finance, and the Vatican's later dealings with banking houses and with the state of Israel, appear in their own places, each held to the size the evidence allows.
