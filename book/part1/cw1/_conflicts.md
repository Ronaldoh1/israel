# cw1: source contradictions presented in the prose

- Israel's reception of the Tripartite Declaration: the US progress report of 7 August 1950 says Israel welcomed it as recognizing Israel as "a permanent element in the Near East" vs Ben-Gurion's diary of 30 May 1950 (via Shlaim) accusing it of arming the Arab states while denying arms to Israel (cw1_tripartite; research cw1_tripartite_declaration_1950).
- Length of Ben-Gurion's first US visit as prime minister: the State Department listing dates it 3–8 May 1951 and also says he left the United States on 31 May (cw1_korea; research cw1_non_identification_korea_1948_1951).
- Why Israel tilted West: Brecher (arms and economic aid) and Shlaim (falling East European immigration, American Jewry, reparations) vs the Israeli establishment's view, in Sharett's phrase, that Soviet "developmental logic" pushed Israel away; Bialer: Soviet attitudes shifted further after the Korea vote (cw1_korea, cw1_soviet_break).
- The ICJ's 22 July 1952 judgment in the Anglo-Iranian case: "in Iran's favour" (Library of Congress country study) vs the Court's own ruling that it had no jurisdiction, with four dissents (me_s06; research cw1_iran_oil_crisis_1949_1952).
- Israel's FY1952 US aid: $65 million won by Kenen (JTA obituary, 1988) vs about $68.8 million at the Senate authorization stage (Kenen's 1951 letter to Silver, partly illegible); Barda's figures ($50M each for Israel and the Arab states, $53M technical cooperation, $50M for refugees) do not match the Administration's May 1951 recommendation ($23.5M Israel, $23.5M Arab states, $50M refugees) (cw1_grant_aid; research cw1_us_grant_aid_1951_1952).
- Founding of the lobby: Kenen set up the American Zionist Committee in 1951, which became AIPAC in 1954 (JTA obituary) vs beginnings inside the American Zionist Council in 1951, AZCPA separated in 1954, renamed AIPAC in 1959 (JVL) vs AZCPA 1953–54, AIPAC 1959 (cjh_kenen_papers, britannica_aipac); reason for separation: non-profit limits on lobbying (JVL) vs fear of a foreign-agent order (Volume II's sources) (cw1_lobby, s07g, s11; research cw1_lobby_cold_war_frame_1950_1954).
- Start of the CIA–Israel liaison: formal liaison in 1951, managed by counterintelligence chief Angleton, with a 1951 no-spying agreement (df_s04; wrmea, michaelcronin) vs Robarge (CIA historian): Angleton headed the Counterintelligence Staff only from 1954, his control of the Israel account "an administrative anomaly" vs McConnell: "how early is a matter of dispute", the no-covert-operations understanding a mid-1950s effort (cw1_cia_liaison, df_s04; research cw1_cia_israel_liaison_origins).
- The no-spying understanding has three dates in circulation: 1951 (df_s04), "since the 1950s" (Blitzer via McConnell), and the mid-1950s (McConnell) (cw1_cia_liaison, df_s04).
- Year of the National Fraternal Congress resolution of 24 September: 1952 (Congressional Record, 2002) vs 1953 (Janikowski, Knights of Columbus SC State Council) (cw1_pledge; research cw1_pledge_under_god_1951_1954).
- Motive for "under God": Cold War contrast (1954 House and Senate reports, via the Newdow brief) vs Kruse's business-funded campaign against the New Deal, in which the Knights' proposal at first "doesn't go anywhere" (cw1_pledge).
- Who drove the Soviet rupture: Soviet internal "developmental logic" (Sharett, Israeli establishment) vs Soviet goodwill tied to Israel's alignment (Bialer via Shlaim) (cw1_soviet_break).
- Date of US approval of the Iran coup: 25 June 1953 (Roosevelt's memoir; Library of Congress study, "June 1953") vs 11 July 1953 (CIA March 1954 memorandum, CIA chronology, handwritten note); "No written authorization from the President has been found" (s11, ira_s00; research cw1_tpajax_1953).
- Cost of the coup: about $5 million (s11; wiki_1953_iranian_coup, globalsecurity) vs a $1,000,000 Tehran Station budget approved 4 April 1953 (Wilber) and a $5,330,000 grand total across three phases (CIA memorandum); Risen: $5 million funnelled to Zahedi's government after the coup; Gasiorowski: at least $50,000 beforehand (s11, ira_s00).
- Roosevelt after the failed attempt of 14–15 August 1953: headquarters advised him to evacuate (Byrne, 2017 FRUS) vs headquarters directed the operation to stop (roosevelt_nl, npr) (s11, ira_s00).
- Who carried out the coup and why: Anglo-American planning and direction (CIA internal history, per the National Security Archive) vs Iranian forces and divisions as decisive (Byrne: "heavily disputed"); oil (British concession, US condition of British flexibility) vs Soviet fear dominating the 2017 volume (s11, ira_s00, ira_src_s19).
- Mossadeq's punishment: three years' imprisonment, then house arrest until 1967 (Library of Congress) vs a sentence of house arrest (kathryn_palmer) (s11, ira_s00).
- Deaths on 19 August 1953: "some 300" in Tehran (Britannica) vs "at least 300" in gun battles (RFE/RL) (s11).
- Mossad and SAVAK: "Mossad-trained from 1957" and Trident from 1958 (s11 entity detail; mag972, globalsecurity_savak) vs the sources for this chapter, which document only Bakhtiar's September 1957 offer to exchange intelligence with Ambassador Zur (Iranica) (s11, sq_s01; research cw1_iran_israel_1947_1953).
- Founding of IRIS: after Mossadeq's fall, as the first sign of a reversal (sq_s01; iranica_israel_iran, mossadegh.org) vs dated only to 1953 in the Iranica entry, which places the 11 June 1953 bank credit line before the coup (sq_s01).
- Founding of the Arab Nationalist Movement: early 1950s at the American University of Beirut (Encyclopedia) vs December 1956, announced by Habash at a congress in Amman (PalQuest) (cw1_refugees; research cw1_refugees_paris_unrwa_1950_1953).
- The Arab League and resettlement: Truman (27 July 1951) says the League endorsed a large resettlement program in Arab states vs the Arab delegations at Paris that autumn: "no limitations on the return of the refugees" (cw1_refugees).
- Sovereignty in the Syrian demilitarized zone: Israel claims sovereignty; Syria claims a right to be consulted as the former occupier; the UN Chief of Staff (June 1951) finds neither party sovereign (cw1_huleh; research cw1_huleh_res93_1951).
- Tel el Mutilla: Syria denied its army's involvement (S/2360) vs the UN report of 23 September 1951 (S/2359) finding Syrian army participation "proved" from the Syrian Official Gazette (cw1_huleh).
- Suez restrictions: Egypt's case that belligerency "still exists" and that the Constantinople Convention and the Council's competence do not apply (Gross) vs Resolution 95's findings that the armistice is "of a permanent character" and no party is "actively a belligerent" (cw1_suez_blockade; research cw1_suez_blockade_res95_1951).
- Prague trial counts: 14 tried, 11 sentenced, most of the condemned Jewish (Britannica) vs 12 of 14 defendants Jewish (JTA); different measures, presented as consistent (cw1_soviet_break).
