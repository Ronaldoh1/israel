Part Eleven takes the claim that the Rothschild family has stood behind the papacy's money since the 1830s and tests it against the documents. It sets out what is on record: the papal bankers of the Torlonia family, the loans that the Rothschild houses made to the Papal States from 1831 to the 1850s, what those loans paid for and how they ended. It traces the alleged "exclusivity clause" of 1831 to its single source and explains how contemporary remarks about a Jewish banker and the pope grew into an antisemitic trope. It then follows the "unbroken chain" said to run from 1831 to 2026 link by link, through the 1929 money, Bernardino Nogara's Special Administration and the IOR, and shows where the documents stop. It ends with the one recent fact that gave the chain new life: the appointment in 2026 of François Pauly, a former Edmond de Rothschild executive, to chair the IOR's board, and a Paris inquiry of the same year that touched the bank but not the Vatican.
