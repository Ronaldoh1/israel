Year by Year

Seen in the dollars of each year, the aid looks like a line that rises slowly and then levels off at three to four billion dollars a year. Seen in today's dollars, it has a different shape: a great wave in the 1970s and 1980s, a long decline as inflation shrank the value of a fixed annual sum, and one new peak in 2024. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:rf_bls_cpi_oct2026]

1949–1972: loans and food

For its first quarter-century the aid was small. From FY1949 to FY1972 it came to about $2.7 billion in the dollars of the time, about $27 billion in today's dollars, most of it economic aid, Export-Import Bank loans and food aid, and from 1959 a growing stream of military loans. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:tal_crs2006] The first year, FY1949, was a single $100 million Export-Import Bank loan, worth about $1.4 billion today. Military aid passed $500 million for the first time in FY1971, when $545 million was lent; that year's aid is worth about $5.3 billion today. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:tal_crs2006]

1973–1984: the wave

The war of October 1973 changed the scale, as Part One describes. FY1974 brought $2.65 billion, about $18.6 billion in today's dollars, and from then on the aid rarely fell below a billion dollars a year. Because prices rose fast in those years, the multipliers are large: a dollar of the 1970s is worth between about five and nine of today's, depending on the year. From FY1974 to FY1984 the United States gave and lent Israel about $24.8 billion in the dollars of the time, about $116 billion in today's. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:aa_crs_2023] [cite:rf_bls_cpi_oct2026] The largest single year of the whole record, in today's dollars, falls in this period: FY1980, with $5.1 billion, about $21.7 billion today, $4 billion of it military aid. The Research Service's table of 2006 places that $4 billion package in FY1979 instead; the difference moves the money by a year but changes the total by little, because the two years' prices are close. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:tal_crs2006]

1985–1998: three billion a year, all grants

From FY1985 the aid became grants, and it settled near $3 billion a year: about $1.8 billion in military aid and $1.2 billion in economic aid, with economic aid of about $1.9 billion in each of FY1985 and FY1986. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:aa_crs_2023] These fourteen years come to about $45.9 billion in the dollars of the time and about $117 billion in today's, the largest of any period. Together, 1974 to 1998 account for more than half of the whole in today's dollars. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:rf_bls_cpi_oct2026]

1999–2023: the agreements

From 1999 the aid was set by ten-year agreements, which phased out economic aid and raised military financing, as Part One sets out. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] The sums written into those agreements rose in the dollars of each year, from about $3 billion to $3.8 billion, but prices rose faster, so in today's dollars the aid fell: about $57 billion for FY1999 to FY2008, about $51 billion for FY2009 to FY2018, and about $25 billion for FY2019 to FY2023. A year of aid under the current agreement, $3.8 billion, is worth less in today's money than a year of aid in the 1980s. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:rf_bls_cpi_oct2026] Missile defence grew within those totals: from $52 million in FY1990 to $500 million a year under the current agreement, with $1 billion more in FY2022 to replenish Iron Dome. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable]

2024–2026: the war years

The war that began in October 2023 produced the second-largest year of the whole record. FY2024 carried the regular $3.8 billion and the April 2024 supplemental's $3.5 billion in military financing, $5.2 billion for missile defence, $4.4 billion to replace American weapons sent to Israel and $801.4 million for ammunition, with smaller cooperative programmes: about $17.8 billion in all, about $19.3 billion in today's dollars. [cite:us1_jvl_aidtable] [cite:aa_hartung_2025] [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] FY2025 returned to the regular level, about $3.9 billion. For FY2026 Congress passed, on 3 February 2026, $3.3 billion in military financing and $500 million for missile defence, with $80 million for anti-tunnel cooperation, $75 million for counter-drone and directed-energy work and $47.5 million for cooperation on emerging technology; by AIPAC's own summary of the bills, $6.5 million more went to resettling refugees in Israel. In all, about $4.0 billion. [cite:tal_wh_hr7148] [cite:tal_dbr_fy26] [cite:tal_sac_fy26def] [cite:tal_aipac_fy26]

Status as of 27 November 2026: the FY2026 money is counted as appropriated, as every year in the table is. President Trump said on 3 November that the United States would stop paying Israel by the end of the year, and the next agreement is under negotiation, as Part Nine describes; whether all of the FY2026 money has been paid out has not been confirmed. A request sent to Congress in June 2026 for $87.6 billion after the war with Iran was reported to contain no line for Israel, and it is not counted. [cite:aa_abc_trump] [cite:tal_bd_supp2026]
