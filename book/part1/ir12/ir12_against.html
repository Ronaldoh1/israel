The Case Against, and What the Record Supports

Avoiding all-out war is not the same as peaceful intent. The evidence against Ron's thesis does not show an Iranian government seeking a full war with the United States. It shows something narrower and better documented: for more than four decades, Iran's government and forces it armed and directed have killed and held Americans, plotted killings on American soil, attacked shipping and oil in ways Washington attributed to it, and declared hostility in ritual form. Each item below is given with its source and with Iran's stated response.

Americans killed by Iran's allies

On 23 October 1983 a truck bomb destroyed the US Marine barracks in Beirut, killing 241 American servicemen. In Peterson v. Islamic Republic of Iran (30 May 2003), Judge Royce Lamberth of the US District Court in Washington found that Hezbollah carried out the attack with "massive material and technical support" from Iran, and a later ruling in the same line of cases found that Iran and its Ministry of Intelligence played "crucial and necessary roles in planning and ordering" it. Iran did not defend the suit; Hezbollah, Iran and Syria have denied involvement. [cite:hz12_dcd_anderson] [cite:hz2_anderson] Part Two of the Hezbollah volume examines the evidence in detail ([scene:hz2_1983|the 1983 bombings]). These are civil judgments in undefended cases, and their findings rest on the evidence the plaintiffs presented; they are the strongest legal findings that exist on the question.

In Iraq after 2003, militias trained and supplied by the Quds Force fought American troops. In April 2019 a Pentagon spokesman said that "at least 603 U.S. personnel deaths in Iraq were the result of Iran-backed militants" during the war, about 17 per cent of all American personnel deaths there from 2003 to 2011, up from about 500 in earlier official estimates. The causes listed included explosively formed penetrators, armour-piercing roadside bombs which the United States says Iran made and supplied. This is a US government figure: the Pentagon did not release the underlying study, and Military Times could not assess it independently. [cite:ir12_mt_603] The pattern continued after the war. A rocket attack in late December 2019 killed an American contractor; the United States blamed Kata'ib Hezbollah, an Iraqi militia, and American air strikes on the militia, its supporters' assault on the US embassy in Baghdad and the killing of Soleimani followed. [cite:hz10_fox_pentagon2020] On 28 January 2024 a drone struck Tower 22, a US outpost in north-eastern Jordan, killing Sergeant William Jerome Rivers, Specialist Kennedy Ladon Sanders and Specialist Breonna Alexsondria Moffett; President Biden blamed Iran-backed militias. [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] Washington named Kata'ib Hezbollah, which then said it was "halting" operations under pressure from Iran and Iraq; Iran denied ordering the attack. [cite:hz10_at_farida] The Hezbollah volume's account of the Iraqi militias is at [scene:hz10_iraq|Iraq's militias].

Hostages and plots

The relationship between the Islamic Republic and the United States began with hostage-taking. On 4 November 1979 students seized the US embassy in Tehran and held 52 Americans for 444 days. [cite:ir12_usip_hostage] Khomeini took control of the hostages and rejected appeals to free them; they were released on 20 January 1981, minutes after Ronald Reagan's inauguration. [cite:ir12_histuk_release] The detention of Americans continued as a practice. On 18 September 2023 five Americans, among them Siamak Namazi, held since 2015, were freed in exchange for clemency for five Iranians and access to $6 billion of Iran's frozen oil revenue, moved to Qatar; the Biden administration said the money could be used only for humanitarian purchases, and President Ebrahim Raisi said it "belongs to the people" of Iran. [cite:ir12_almon_swap] Part Nine sets out Iran's record of detentions ([scene:ir9_record|Iran's own record]).

Three plots on American soil have been documented in court filings. Manssor Arbabsiar pleaded guilty on 17 October 2012 to conspiring with Quds Force members in Iran to kill Saudi Arabia's ambassador to the United States by bombing a restaurant he used; he wired about $100,000 as a down payment on a $1.5 million fee to an account that turned out to belong to the FBI, told agents that senior Quds Force officials had recruited, funded and directed him, and was sentenced to 25 years. [cite:ir12_doj_arbabsiar] On 10 August 2022 the Justice Department charged Shahram Poursafi, a member of the Revolutionary Guards, with offering $300,000 for the murder of the former national security adviser John Bolton, likely in revenge for Soleimani; the plot went no further. [cite:ir12_npr_bolton] On 8 November 2024 it charged Farhad Shakeri, believed to be in Iran, alleging that the Revolutionary Guards had tasked him on 7 October 2024 with planning the killing of Donald Trump; two New York men were charged over a related plot against an Iranian-American journalist, apparently Masih Alinejad. Iran's foreign ministry called the accusations "totally unfounded". The Poursafi and Shakeri cases are charges, not convictions. [cite:ir12_toi_shakeri]

Shipping, oil and the 2026 war

In 2019, during the maximum-pressure campaign, Washington attributed several attacks to Iran. After two tankers were struck in the Gulf of Oman on 13 June 2019, Secretary of State Mike Pompeo said "Iran is responsible", and US Central Command released video it said showed a Revolutionary Guards boat removing an unexploded limpet mine from one of them; the ship's Japanese owner said it had been hit by projectiles, and Iran denied involvement. [cite:ir12_usni_tankers] On 14 September 2019 drones and cruise missiles struck the Saudi Aramco plants at Abqaiq and Khurais, shutting more than 5 per cent of world oil supply. The Houthis claimed the attack; the United States, European governments and Saudi Arabia blamed Iran, which denied it; a UN sanctions panel concluded that "the Houthi forces did not launch the attacks", without naming who did. [cite:ir12_aj_abqaiq] Part Eight covers the wider shipping war ([scene:ir8_shadow|the shadow war]).

In 2026 Iran's strikes on American forces were lethal; they came after the United States and Israel had attacked it. AP itemised the dead in July: six soldiers killed by an Iranian drone at a port in Kuwait shortly after 28 February, one at Prince Sultan Air Base after an Iranian attack on 1 March, and two in Jordan on 18 July while defending against Iranian missiles and drones, alongside deaths in accidents. [cite:hz12_ktar_deaths] As of October 2026 the Pentagon's casualty system counted 21 American service members dead and 865 wounded in operations against Iran. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026] Part Eleven tells that war ([scene:ir11_war|the 2026 war]).

Words

Hostility is also declared. At the Quds Day marches the Iranian government has organised since 1979, crowds chant "Death to America" alongside "Death to Israel". [cite:ir2_wiki_quds] Khamenei explained the slogan on 8 February 2019: "It means death to America's rulers … we have nothing against the American people." [cite:ir12_aj_dta] His explanation limits the target; it does not withdraw the hostility.

What the record supports

The record supports one part of the thesis. On every occasion examined here when Iran's government struck American forces directly in reply to an American attack, before 2026, it gave warning and no American was killed; it negotiated with Washington in 2003 (by the disputed fax), 2013–2015, 2021–2022, 2025 and 2026, and in two of those cases the talks were still running when Israel, and then the United States, struck. [cite:ir12_aj_iraqwarn] [cite:ir12_ahram_udeid] [cite:ir12_too_sixth] [cite:ir12_euronews_feb26] Its leaders said they did not want war, and analysts who study its doctrine, from Vaez to Barzegar, read its use of allied forces abroad as a way of fighting without inviting a direct war at home. [cite:ir12_c4_vaez] [cite:ir12_na_forward]

The record does not support the stronger reading that Iran's government is not hostile to the United States or has not harmed Americans. Through Hezbollah in 1983, the Iraqi militias after 2003 and again in 2024, by hostage-taking from 1979 to 2023, by assassination plots on American soil in 2011, 2022 and 2024, and directly in 2026, the Iranian state or forces it armed have killed, held or sought to kill Americans. [cite:hz12_dcd_anderson] [cite:ir12_mt_603] [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] [cite:ir12_doj_arbabsiar] [cite:hz12_ktar_deaths] The fair statement of the evidence is therefore two-sided: Iran's government has repeatedly chosen to avoid a full war with the United States, and it has repeatedly harmed Americans while doing so. Analysts on both sides of the argument largely agree on the method, calibrated pressure through allies with deniability, and disagree on whether that method is defence or expansion. That is an argument about motive, and the documents do not settle it.

One further point bears on the second half of Ron's thesis. Trump's statement of 28 February 2026 justified the war partly by Iran's record, citing attacks on American citizens and soldiers and the 1979 hostage crisis. [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] The record above shows that those grievances were real. Whether they, or Iran's nuclear programme, or Israel's urging, decided the American choice of war is a separate question. Part Fourteen takes it up ([scene:ir14_bill|Dragged In: the American bill]).
