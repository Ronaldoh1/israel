Ansar Allah: From Saada to Sanaa

Of the armed groups that Israel and the United States describe as Iran's network, the Houthis are the one whose origins lie furthest from both Israel and Iran. The movement grew out of a religious revival in Saada, the mountainous governorate on Yemen's northern border with Saudi Arabia. A movement known as Believing Youth (al-Shabab al-Mumin) was founded there in 1992 to teach young people Zaidism, the branch of Shia Islam whose imams had ruled northern Yemen for centuries and whose revivalists, by the 1990s, saw it as pressed by the government of President Ali Abdullah Saleh and by Saudi-funded Salafi preaching in its own heartland. [cite:hz10_nh_saada] [cite:hz10_albab_houthi] Zaidis make up about a third of Yemen's roughly 34 million people. [cite:hz10_cfr_iranhouthis]

The movement's first leader, Hussein al-Houthi, was the son of a prominent Zaidi cleric, Badreddin al-Houthi, and sat in Yemen's parliament from 1993 to 1997 for the small al-Haqq party before breaking with it. [cite:hz10_wiki_hussein] [cite:hz10_albab_houthi] From January 2002 his followers adopted a slogan, first chanted at a school in Marran in Saada: "God is great, Death to America, Death to Israel, Curse on the Jews, Victory to Islam." [cite:hz10_wiki_sarkha] The slogan names the United States and Israel as enemies, and its fourth line is directed at Jews as such; it has been the movement's emblem ever since and appears on banners at its rallies. [cite:hz10_wiki_sarkha]

The state's war with the movement began on 18 June 2004. [cite:hz10_nh_saada] Hussein al-Houthi was killed on 10 September 2004 at Marran; Yemen's interior and defence ministries announced that he and 20 aides had died. His father briefly led the movement, and his brother Abdul-Malik al-Houthi then took command. [cite:hz10_wiki_hussein] [cite:hz10_nh_saada] Between 2004 and 2010 the government and the Houthis fought "a series of six intermittent wars" in Saada. [cite:hz10_albab_houthi] By mid-2008 a humanitarian news agency counted hundreds killed, thousands displaced and, according to local rights groups, about 3,000 arrests of supporters. [cite:hz10_nh_saada] The movement took the formal name Ansar Allah, "Supporters of God." [cite:hz10_albab_houthi] It was in these wars, the Council on Foreign Relations notes, that a political movement became an army. [cite:hz10_cfr_iranhouthis]

The Arab uprisings of 2011 forced Saleh from office, and the transition that followed gave the Houthis their opening. On 21 September 2014 Houthi fighters occupied the prime minister's compound, an army command centre and the state television complex in Sanaa after four days of clashes in which more than 100 people died; the prime minister resigned, and the parties, including the Houthis, signed a UN-brokered agreement at the presidential palace that same day. [cite:hz10_voa_sanaa2014] The agreement did not hold. By early 2015 the Houthis controlled the capital and much of the north, and as of April 2026, by the Council on Foreign Relations' account, they ruled about a third of Yemen's territory and about two-thirds of its people. [cite:hz10_crs_if12587] [cite:hz10_cfr_iranhouthis]

The Saudi-led war and the American role

In March 2015 Saudi Arabia formed a coalition and launched military operations in Yemen to restore the ousted government. On 25 March 2015 the United States announced that it would provide "logistical and intelligence support." According to the Congressional Research Service, that support grew to include in-flight refuelling of coalition aircraft, weapons transfers, intelligence and training, coordinated through a joint US–Saudi planning cell. [cite:hz10_crs_yemencongress] After a coalition airstrike on a funeral hall in Sanaa killed 140 people in October 2016, the Obama administration held back a sale of precision munitions and limited intelligence sharing, while keeping refuelling; the Trump administration went ahead with the deferred sales in May 2017. [cite:hz10_crs_yemencongress]

The refuelling ended on 9–10 November 2018, when the coalition announced it would refuel its own aircraft, a decision Defense Secretary Jim Mattis backed after having warned that US support was "not unconditional." [cite:hz10_aje_refuel2018] [cite:hz10_crs_yemencongress] In 2019 both chambers of Congress passed a War Powers resolution directing the removal of US forces from hostilities in Yemen, the Senate 54–46 in March and the House 247–175 on 4 April 2019. President Trump vetoed it on 16 April, and on 2 May the Senate failed to override, 53–45. In May 2019 the administration also declared an emergency to push through arms sales worth more than $8 billion; the Senate then passed 22 resolutions of disapproval, and the President vetoed the three that also cleared the House. [cite:hz10_crs_yemencongress]

The UN Development Programme projected that by the end of 2021 the war would have caused about 377,000 deaths, some 60 percent of them from indirect causes such as hunger and preventable disease rather than combat. [cite:hz10_aje_undp377k] That figure counts all parties' violence, including the Houthis' own, and the war's blockade and economic collapse; it is a projection by modelling, not a count of names. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 then froze the front lines for about four years. [cite:hz10_usni_crs2026] [cite:hz10_ahram_taiz]

Iran's role: supplier, partner or master

That Iran arms the Houthis is no longer seriously disputed. Some experts date Iranian military support to around 2009, and most agree Iran was supplying weapons by 2014; the Congressional Research Service says Iran "has increased its support" since 2014–2015, including ballistic and cruise missiles and drones. [cite:hz10_cfr_iranhouthis] [cite:hz10_crs_if12587] The UN Panel of Experts on Yemen, which monitors the Security Council's arms embargo, has traced the trail in detail. Its January 2020 report described engines that were unlicensed copies of foreign designs Iran produces, and a disassembled Quds-1 cruise missile seized at sea that carried a Farsi label recording a test at an Iranian facility in February 2019. [cite:hz10_wi_poe2020] Its October 2024 report found that "without foreign support, the entity does not have the capacity to develop and produce complex weapon systems," listed 53 items with strong similarities to Iranian materiel, and reported that a Quds Force general held a predominant advisory role in the Houthi command. [cite:hz10_un_poe2024] In July 2025 Yemeni government forces seized 750 tonnes of weapons and components bound for the Houthis, the largest such shipment yet found. [cite:hz10_cfr_iranhouthis] Iran's officials, for their part, told the UN Panel that certain small arms with Iranian-like markings did not match their production. [cite:hz10_un_poe2024]

Supplying weapons is not the same as giving orders, and here specialists divide. The same UN report found that Hezbollah, not only Iran, is "one of the most important supporters of the Houthis," and recorded a senior Houthi official saying that although the axis coordinated with Iran, the decision to seize the Galaxy Leader in November 2023 "was made by the Houthi leaders." [cite:hz10_un_poe2024] Two researchers writing in January 2024 called the Houthis "strategic allies" rather than "mere proxies," with their own local base and politics. [cite:hz10_conv_proxies] Norman Roule, a former CIA manager for Iran, ranked Hezbollah closest to Tehran and the Houthis the most distant, and argued that Iran "seeks the product of militia actions, not the process of controlling them." [cite:hz10_npr_roule] Others put more weight on Tehran: many experts quoted by the Council on Foreign Relations describe an "informal partner" whose claims of responsibility can give Iran deniability for attacks it ordered or helped carry out. [cite:hz10_cfr_iranhouthis] The record supports the supply relationship; it does not settle who decides. [scene:hz11_proxies|Part Eleven tests the proxy frame across the network.]
