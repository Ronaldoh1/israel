Part Twelve established that the Dream's income story hides a wider wealth story. In 2025 the median white family earned about 1.9 times the median Black family but held about 9.0 times its net worth, and the Black median fell 25 per cent between 2022 and 2025, a change the Federal Reserve did not test for significance. [cite:ad2_scf_t1] [cite:us8_scf2025] [cite:ad12_scf25_t2] In the second quarter of 2026 the top 1 per cent held a record 32.5 per cent of household net worth and 36.5 per cent of financial assets; the bottom half held 2.3 per cent of net worth. [cite:ad12_audit_fred_t1nw] [cite:ad12_fred_t1fin] [cite:ad12_audit_fred_b50nw] White Americans born 1949–72 were more than twice as likely as Black Americans to have received an inheritance, though earnings and pensions, not bequests, explain most of the gap; the estate tax reaches fewer than one death in a thousand, and the step-up in basis lets lifetime gains pass untaxed. [cite:ad12_richmond_inherit] [cite:ad12_bostonfed_inherit] [cite:ad12_crs_estate] In 2008 the record is specific: Black and Hispanic buyers with equal credit were far more likely to get high-cost loans, two of the largest lenders settled federal discrimination cases, and median Hispanic and Black wealth fell 66 and 53 per cent between 2005 and 2009, while the government's investments in banks and other financial institutions, apart from AIG, were repaid with a net gain.
