Part Thirty-Five asks whether the divine feminine was suppressed, and answers with three bodies of record. The first is the hill of Tepeyac outside Mexico City, where a Franciscan friar complained in 1576 that the Virgin of Guadalupe was being honoured under the name of a mother goddess, and where the documents of the 1531 apparition story appear only a century later, in 1648 and 1649. The second follows the mothers of other peoples, Pachamama in the Andes, Yemayá and Oshún in the Caribbean and Brazil, Brigid in Ireland, and the Black Madonnas of Europe, into the Virgins who carried them, and stops in Rome in October 2019, when female figures from the Amazon synod were thrown into the Tiber by Catholic critics and the pope apologised. The third tests the oldest claim of all, that Mary is Isis renamed, against the dated evidence for each borrowed image and title, sets out the feminine figures the institution did push down, Wisdom and Mary Magdalene, and weighs them against the Catholic Church's own elevation of Mary. It ends with a labelled interpretation and the strongest readings against it.
