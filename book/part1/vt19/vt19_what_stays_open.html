What Stays Open

A verdict built on documents is only as final as the documents. This section lists the questions the record left open as of October 2026, what each would take to settle, and where the answer is likely to come from. Some bear on the central question and could, in principle, change the verdict. Others concern the volume's surrounding questions and will change only the details. None is answered here in advance; each is stated with its status as of October 2026.

The private record after 1945

The single most important gap is the Holy See's own file on Palestine from 1945 to 1958. The papers of Pius XII's pontificate, opened on 2 March 2020, include the Secretariat of State's files for those years, the years of partition, Israel's independence and the encyclicals on Jerusalem. [cite:vt14_vn_open2020] The first study devoted to them on this subject, Maria Chiara Rioli's chapter "Archives in War: The Pius XII Papers and the Israeli–Palestinian Conflict" in a volume published by Berghahn in 2024, could not be checked for this book, and nothing has been attributed to it. [cite:vt17_berghahn_rioli] What would settle the question for those years is a body of published research from the Secretariat of State's files, by Rioli and others, showing whether instructions to nuncios and internal memoranda match the encyclicals. After October 1958 the archive is closed: no date had been announced, as of October 2026, for opening the papers of John XXIII, the next pontificate in line, and the papers that would show how Nostra Aetate and the Fundamental Agreement were negotiated lie further ahead still. [cite:vt14_vn_libarch2025]

The 1947 vote and the Sneh–Tardini meeting

Whether the Holy See pressed Catholic delegations at the United Nations in the autumn of 1947 remains open in both directions. The one account of a meeting between Moshe Sneh of the Jewish Agency and Domenico Tardini on 3 October 1947, arranged through the nuncio in Paris, Angelo Roncalli, rests on later recollections and cites no archive; it says Tardini "did not make any promises." [cite:vt17_tenembaum_sneh] What would settle it: the Paris nunciature's papers for 1947 and the Secretariat of State's files now open in Rome, the Jewish Agency's own records of the meeting, and the American diplomatic record of that autumn. Until then, the only firm fact is the vote itself, in which most Catholic-majority delegations supported partition.

The 1944 note

The handwritten note in which Pius XII is said to have written "The Jews need a land of their own" has been announced but not shown. The Pave the Way Foundation said in 2012 that it was held in "the closed section of the Vatican Library", and gave no image, transcription or reference. [cite:vt16_ptwf_zenit] What would settle it is publication of the document with its archival signature, most likely from the Apostolic Archive or the Secretariat of State's historical archive rather than the Library. If it exists and says what is claimed, it would show a private view of the pope in one year at odds with the letters his Secretariat sent in 1943; it would not by itself show money, favours or diplomatic help.

The Becciu proceedings

The conviction of Cardinal Giovanni Angelo Becciu and eight others on 16 December 2023 was set aside on 17 March 2026, when the Court of Appeal found the summons defective and ordered the hearings renewed. [cite:vt10_vn_mistrial2026] As of 22 October 2026 the court had received defence briefs on 21 September and fixed the next hearing for 29 October 2026. [cite:vt10_us_oct2026] Becciu and every other defendant in the renewed proceedings are presumed innocent. What would settle it is a judgment, and then the exhaustion of appeals; the case also tests the court reforms described in [scene:vt4_citizens_courts_prisons|Part Four].

The Orlandi and Gregori inquiries

Three inquiries opened in 2023 and none had reported as of October 2026. The parliamentary commission set up by Law no. 202 of 4 December 2023 runs for the rest of the legislature and must report to both chambers; it had held at least 77 sittings by February 2026. [cite:vt15_law_202] [cite:vt15_sen_feb2026] Rome's prosecutors were following three lines at the end of 2025, and the Vatican's Promoter of Justice had sent his material to them. [cite:vt15_agi_dec2025] [cite:vt15_cwr_senate] What would settle the case is evidence of what happened to Emanuela Orlandi and Mirella Gregori; what would settle the narrower question of the Vatican's role is the commission's report and the release of the papers the family has asked for since 2017. The family holds that the Vatican knows more than it has said; the Holy See's stated position is that the search for the truth is in its interest. [cite:vt15_qn_aug2025] [cite:vt15_vn_2019]

The ICI recovery

Italy's procedure for recovering the property tax not paid for 2006 to 2011 set 30 September 2026 for declarations and 30 October 2026 for payment, with sums over 100,000 euros payable in four quarterly instalments. [cite:vt3_ff_sept2026] As of 21 October 2026 no figure had been published for declarations filed or sums recovered. [cite:vt3_io_ici2026] The only widely quoted estimate, 4 to 5 billion euros attributed to Italy's association of municipalities, is not official, and the 2025 decree's threshold and exclusions mean the amount due may be far lower. [cite:vt3_money_anci] [cite:vt3_dpcm_2025] What would settle it is a published account by the Italian government or the European Commission of what was declared and paid, and by whom.

The economic agreement with Israel

The agreement on property, tax and economic matters promised in Article 10 of the 1993 treaty has been under negotiation since 11 March 1999. The last joint communiqué found is dated 14 June 2017 and hoped for a "rapid conclusion." [cite:vt18_press_2017] [cite:vt1_va_fa1993] What would settle it is a signature, or a published statement that the talks have ended. Israel votes on 27 October 2026, and any further round will be conducted with the government that election produces. [cite:gi6_aje_dissolve]

Leo XIV's next steps

As of October 2026 Leo XIV had kept relations with Israel, appointed a new nuncio, Archbishop Giorgio Lingua, on 22 January 2026, said in September 2025 that the Holy See could make no declaration on genocide "at this time", and, through Cardinal Parolin at the United Nations in September 2026, restated the two-state position and the call for an internationally guaranteed statute for Jerusalem. [cite:vt1_aleteia_lingua] [cite:vt18_ansa_genocide2025] [cite:vt18_ncr_unga2026] His published travel list for 2026 includes no visit to Israel or Palestine; his next announced journey, to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, runs from 6 to 17 November 2026. [cite:vt19_va_travels2026] What would change the picture is a diplomatic act the volume's record does not yet contain: a visit, a signed economic agreement, a change in the Holy See's language on the war, or a change in its relations with either state.

Of these, only the first three bear directly on the central question, and each could in principle change the verdict: a document showing the Holy See encouraging in private what it opposed in public, in 1944, 1947 or the years of the encyclicals, would reopen the test of the private record. The rest will change the volume's details rather than its answer.
