The Refugees, 1948–1964

In the war of 1947–49 roughly 700,000 to 750,000 Palestinians were displaced. [cite:wiki_nakba] On 11 December 1948 the UN General Assembly resolved in Resolution 194 that refugees "wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date." [cite:gi18_jvl_res194] The conciliation commission the resolution set up later found that neither side would "recede," naming Israel's refusal on the return paragraph and the Arab governments' on another. [cite:rs_cw1_unccp_a1985]

The refugees stayed where the war had left them. UNRWA began operating on 1 May 1950 and took over the camps. [cite:gz_palquest_refugees] Inside Israel, the Absentees' Property Law of 14 March 1950 gave the refugees' property to the state, including that of Arabs who had stayed inside Israel but were away from their homes; the Arabs who remained lived under military government until 1966. [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] [cite:wiki_present_absentee] [cite:wiki_kafr_qasim_massacre]

The national movement that followed formed among them. Fatah's core group was most likely founded in Kuwait in 1957 by Palestinian expatriates, most of them from Gaza, among them Yasir Arafat; the Palestine Liberation Organization was founded in 1964, and Fatah became its dominant faction by 1969. [cite:rs_cw4_palquest_fatah] [cite:npr_hamashistory] Three generations later, more than two-thirds of Gaza's people on the eve of the war of 2023 were registered refugees of 1948 and their descendants. [cite:gz_afp_camps] The Cold War Architecture and Gaza, Documented tell this in full.
