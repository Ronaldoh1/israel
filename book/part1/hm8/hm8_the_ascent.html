The Ascent Through the Spheres

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The Poimandres describes what happens to the soul on its way back to God. First, Mind tells the narrator, "thou surrenderest the body by itself unto the work of change". Then the soul rises through the seven zones of the heavens, and at each it gives something back: "To the first zone he gives the Energy of Growth and Waning", to the second the "Device of Evils", and so on, until "to the seventh zone, Ensnaring Falsehood". [cite:hm_mead_poim] Stripped of all of them, the soul comes "to that Nature which belongs unto the Eighth", and "becoming Powers they are in God". The text concludes: "This the good end for those who have gained Gnosis - to be made one with God." [cite:hm_mead_poim]

The same picture appears in the Gnostic texts read earlier on this shelf, where the soul passes the archons at their gates, and in the Merkabah ascent through seven palaces. [cite:vt24_origen_cc6] [cite:et_wiki_merkabah] The Hermetic version adds something distinctive: at each level the soul does not fight a guard but gives back a part of itself. What holds the soul down is not an enemy outside but energies it has taken on: growth and decline, devices of evil, ensnaring falsehood.

The inner reading takes the ascent as a practice of this life, not only a journey after death. Each zone names a compulsion: the drive to grow and the fear of waning, the habit of scheming, the hold of falsehood. To rise is to give each back, one by one, until nothing borrowed remains. The last thing surrendered is falsehood itself, and what is left is what the text calls gnosis. Read this way, liberation is subtraction: not acquiring higher states, but returning what was never the self.

The record limits the reading. The Poimandres describes a cosmos of real planetary spheres and an ascent after death, and its authors drew on the astronomy and philosophy of their time. The translation quoted is G. R. S. Mead's, and its wording is old-fashioned. The application to a practice in this life is the reader's, though the text itself says the end of gnosis is union with God.
