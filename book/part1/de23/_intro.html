This part closes Book Four with the one exit project its central theorist built himself. Urbit is a computer network that Curtis Yarvin began writing in 2002, and its names are cut into a fixed hierarchy of 256 "galaxies," 65,280 "stars" and about four billion "planets," owned, traded and, at the top, given a vote. The first section reads the design and its author's own descriptions of it, from "digital feudalism" in 2010 to "Urbit is land" in 2016, and sets them against who actually came to own the map and how many people use it. The second follows Yarvin's return in August 2024 as a self-described "wartime CEO," the board fight of 2025 and the vote by which the galaxy owners settled it, and then tests the book's worst-case reading against Book Four as a whole: exit as an escape for anyone, or an enclave for the few.
