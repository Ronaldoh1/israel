The Maps at the UN

The phrase "Greater Israel" is usually argued over in words. Between September 2023 and September 2024 it was argued over in pictures, because Benjamin Netanyahu, as prime minister, held up maps of the region at the United Nations and at a televised press conference, and on several of them no Palestinian territory was marked. [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023] [cite:gi7_aje_phil2024] This section sets out what those maps showed, what Netanyahu said they meant, and how governments and critics answered. It keeps three things apart: what the maps documentably showed, what he stated as their purpose, and what others argued they revealed.

22 September 2023: "a new Middle East"

Netanyahu addressed the 78th session of the UN General Assembly in New York on Friday 22 September 2023, two weeks before the Hamas attack of 7 October. His subject was a possible peace with Saudi Arabia. He first showed what he called "a map of the Middle East in 1948, the year Israel was established," saying: "Here is Israel in 1948. It's a tiny country, isolated, surrounded by a hostile Arab world." He then took a red marker "to show a great blessing. The blessing of a new Middle East," and promised "a new corridor of peace and prosperity that connects Asia through the UAE" onward across the region. "Peace between Israel and Saudi Arabia," he said, "will truly create a new Middle East." [cite:gi7_toi_un2023]

The verbatim text settles one point on which accounts disagree: the first map was presented as the region in 1948, not "before the Abraham Accords." According to the Jerusalem Post, the 1948 map showed Israel alone in green, and the second, headed "The New Middle East," showed seven countries in green, Israel and Saudi Arabia among them, to illustrate the normalisation agreements signed since 2020 and the one he hoped for. On the map he held up, the West Bank, Gaza and East Jerusalem appeared inside Israel's colour, with no boundary and no Palestinian territory marked. [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023] Whether the 1948 map also coloured the West Bank and Gaza as Israel, which would be a factual error (Jordan held the West Bank and Egypt held Gaza from 1948 to 1967), could not be settled from the written reports found; the video record of the session is the primary source for it.

Netanyahu's stated purpose is on the record in the same speech. He said he believed "we must not give the Palestinians a veto over new peace treaties with Arab states," and also that peace with Saudi Arabia "will enhance the prospects of peace with the Palestinians" and that "a real path towards a genuine peace with our Palestinian neighbors can finally be achieved." [cite:gi7_toi_un2023] On his own account, then, the map was about normalisation without a Palestinian veto, not about borders. Critics read the map itself as the message. Laith Arafeh, the Palestinian Authority's representative in Germany, called it an insult to "every foundational principle of the UN"; Hadar Susskind of Americans for Peace Now wrote that "His map of Greater Israel is perhaps the only honest part of his speech today." [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023]

Two Western governments answered. In the days that followed, Germany's Foreign Ministry spokesman, Sebastian Fischer, said that showing a map "that does not depict territories that are occupied or annexed" was "of no help" to efforts toward a negotiated two-state solution. The US State Department spokesman, Matthew Miller, declined to "get into any discussion about the map that the prime minister chose to use" and restated American support for two states. Ynet asked Netanyahu's office to respond and had no answer when it published. [cite:gi7_ynet_germany] No Israeli government statement explaining or withdrawing the map was found.

2 September 2024: the Philadelphi press conference

Eleven months later, in the Gaza war, Netanyahu gave a televised press conference on 2 September 2024 to explain why Israel would keep forces on the Philadelphi Corridor along Gaza's border with Egypt. He used a pointer on a large map of Gaza captioned, in Hebrew, "Gaza after the disengagement, oxygen pipe of Hamas," and a separate map on which the West Bank appeared as part of Israel. [cite:gi7_aje_phil2024] The stated subject was security: the caption called the border Hamas's lifeline, and he framed control of the corridor as essential to Israel's security and to the war's aims. Critics again took the map as the finding. Mairav Zonszein of the International Crisis Group wrote that the speech would "go down in history as Netanyahu's open admission" that Israel meant to stay between the river and the sea; the Palestinian ambassador in London, Husam Zomlot, said it showed an aim "to erase the Palestinian people and take the rest of our land"; the UN special rapporteur Francesca Albanese also criticised it; and the Israeli group Peace Now called the government "messianic." The opposition leader, Yair Lapid, called the speech "political spin." No clarification from the Israeli government about the map was reported. [cite:gi7_na_phil2024] [cite:gi7_aje_phil2024]

27 September 2024: "The Blessing" and "The Curse"

At the 79th General Assembly on 27 September 2024 Netanyahu held two maps. One, labelled "The Blessing," showed a corridor of normalisation through the Gulf linking Asia to Europe with, in his words, rail lines and fibre-optic cables; the other, "The Curse," showed Iran and its allies, which he called "Iran's arc of terror." "The line separating the blessing and the curse," he said, "could not be more clear." Many delegates left the hall as he began. [cite:gi7_ji_un2024] [cite:gi7_gzero_un2024] Middle East Monitor, citing Turkey's Anadolu Agency, reported that the maps "did not identify the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, but showed all the territory as part of Israel." [cite:gi7_memo_un2024] The Western reports of the speech found describe the two maps without addressing that point, so it rests on Turkish and advocacy reporting and on the video. [cite:gi7_ji_un2024] [cite:gi7_gzero_un2024] The speech's stated theme was a choice of alliances, not borders. The same blessing-and-curse language had already appeared in the 2023 speech. [cite:gi7_toi_un2023]

What the maps do and do not show

The documented fact is narrow and firm: on at least two occasions, in September 2023 at the UN and in September 2024 at a press conference, a sitting Israeli prime minister displayed maps on which the territories occupied in 1967 were shown inside Israel and no Palestinian territory appeared. [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023] [cite:gi7_aje_phil2024] A third case, the 2024 UN maps, is reported but less firmly sourced. [cite:gi7_memo_un2024] The pattern that can be shown is repetition without correction: in none of the cases was a government clarification found, and in 2023 Netanyahu's office did not answer a request for comment. [cite:gi7_ynet_germany] [cite:gi7_na_phil2024]

What the maps mean is an argument, and it has two sides. Critics, from Americans for Peace Now in 2023 to Israel's Peace Now in 2024, read them as a statement that the West Bank and Gaza are Israel's, and Germany's Foreign Ministry called the omission of occupied territory "of no help"; on that reading the maps are consistent with the 2022 coalition guidelines and the annexation steps described in Part Six. [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023] [cite:gi7_ynet_germany] [cite:gi7_na_phil2024] [scene:gi6_plans|Part Six: the annexation plans] [scene:wb9_annex|The West Bank, Documented: annexation] Netanyahu's own words at the time point the other way in one respect: none of the maps extended Israel's colour into Jordan, Egypt, Lebanon, Syria or Iraq, and the 2023 map placed Saudi Arabia and other Arab states on Israel's side of a peace, not inside its borders. [cite:gi7_jpost_map2023] [cite:gi7_toi_un2023] The maps are therefore evidence about the West Bank and Gaza, the territory the Likud platform of 1977 already claimed, not about the Nile-to-Euphrates map of the popular image. [scene:gi5_likud|Part Five: the Likud platform] [scene:gi10_myths|Part Ten: the myths] It was a television interview in August 2025, not a map, that brought the wider phrase back into a prime minister's mouth.
