The Warnings, and the Inquiries

"Jericho Wall." More than a year before the attack, according to documents, emails and interviews reported by the New York Times, Israeli officials had obtained a Hamas battle plan of about forty pages, which they code-named "Jericho Wall." It set out, step by step, an invasion to overwhelm the border defences, seize Israeli towns and storm military bases, with rocket barrages, drones to knock out the border cameras and machine guns, and gunmen crossing on paragliders, on motorcycles and on foot. The attack of 7 October followed it closely. Israeli military and intelligence officials judged the plan "aspirational", an attack on that scale being, in their view, beyond Hamas's capabilities. [cite:gz_nyt_jericho]

In July 2023 a veteran analyst in Unit 8200, Israel's signals intelligence unit, warned that Hamas had held a day-long training exercise resembling the plan. "I utterly refute that the scenario is imaginary," she wrote. A colonel in the Gaza division called the scenario "totally imaginative", and answered: "In short, let's wait patiently." [cite:gz_nyt_jericho]

The army's inquiry. In February 2025 the Israeli army published the findings of its own investigation. It concluded that Israel had "underestimated its capabilities" and had misread Hamas's aims, working from the belief that Hamas "was more interested in governing the territory than fighting Israel." The chief of staff, Herzi Halevi, said: "I was the commander of the military on October 7th, and I have my own responsibility," and he had announced his resignation the month before. [cite:gz_pbs_idfprobe]

The inquiry that has not been held. Israel's law provides for a state commission of inquiry, whose members are appointed by the president of the Supreme Court. Benjamin Netanyahu said in October 2023 that the failures would be investigated and that everyone, himself included, would have to answer, after the war. As of October 2026 no state commission has been established. In the summer of 2025 the government told the Supreme Court it did not intend to set one up; Netanyahu argues that a commission appointed under the existing law would lack public trust, and prefers one with equal representation for the coalition and the opposition. He rejected a compromise proposed by President Isaac Herzog. A bill for a different kind of commission passed a first reading in July 2026 but was not completed before the Knesset dissolved. The State Comptroller has conducted a separate examination. [cite:gz_ynet_inquiry2026] [cite:gz_pbs_idfprobe]
