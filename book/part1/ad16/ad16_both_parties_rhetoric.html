Both Parties' Dream: The Words and the Laws

[scene:ad1_who_sells_it|Part One] showed that presidents of both parties promise the same Dream: a job that pays enough, a home, savings, a secure old age and children who do better. This section looks at the phrases each party has used to carry that promise, sets them side by side in the same register, and then asks what each party's enacted laws did to the mechanisms traced in Parts Four to Fifteen. The aim is not to score the parties against each other. It is to show that the rhetoric is shared, that the record of each party includes changes that strengthened some mechanisms of the Dream and weakened others, and that the gap between word and law is not the property of one side.

A phrase that began as a joke

The most durable figure of self-made success began as its opposite. The earliest uses of lifting oneself "by the straps of his boots," found in an 1834 Chicago newspaper and in print through the 1860s, mocked people who claimed to do the impossible, such as a man selling a perpetual-motion machine. The newer sense, of rising by one's own effort, is first recorded by the Oxford English Dictionary in James Joyce's Ulysses (1922), and became standard in the twentieth century. [cite:ad16_curzan_boot] The shift matters for this book's subject: a phrase that once named a physical impossibility now names the core of the taught Dream.

The phrases, both parties

Ronald Reagan accepted the Republican nomination in Detroit on 17 July 1980 by inviting voters into "a great national crusade to make America great again." [cite:ad16_reagan_1980] Donald Trump used the same words to close his announcement on 16 June 2015, after declaring "Sadly, the American dream is dead." [cite:ad16_trump_maga] [cite:ad1_trump_2015] George W. Bush, accepting renomination in New York on 2 September 2004, promised to "build an ownership society, because ownership brings security, and dignity, and independence," in which "more people will own their health care plans" and own "a piece of their retirement." [cite:ad16_bush_2004] Speaker John Boehner, arguing against tax increases in September 2011, said "Job creators in America are essentially on strike." [cite:ad16_abc_boehner]

On the Democratic side, Bill Clinton signed the welfare law on 22 August 1996 saying "Today we are ending welfare as we know it," so that welfare would be "a second chance, not a way of life." [cite:ad16_clinton_1996] Barack Obama, campaigning in Roanoke, Virginia, on 13 July 2012, told business owners "If you were successful, somebody along the line gave you some help," and continued: "Somebody invested in roads and bridges. If you've got a business, you didn't build that. Somebody else made that happen." [cite:ad16_obama_roanoke] Opponents quoted "you didn't build that" on its own, and the fuller passage is given here so that readers can judge what "that" refers to, as each phrase in this section is given with its occasion. Obama's 2014 State of the Union proposed to "build new ladders of opportunity into the middle class." [cite:ad1_obama_2014]

The DREAM Act took the Dream's name for a bill offering legal status to people brought to the United States as children who completed college or military service. The House passed it 216 to 198 in December 2010; on 18 December 2010 the Senate fell five votes short of the 60 needed to end debate, 55 to 41, with 36 Republicans and five Democrats voting no. [cite:ad16_mpi_dream] The Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals policy (DACA), created later by executive action, covered many of the same people with temporary protection and work permits. In January 2025 the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals held DACA unlawful but limited the effect to Texas, leaving the programme "largely intact" for about half a million recipients. [cite:ad16_abc17_daca] As of late April 2026, renewals were accepted nationwide, new applications were accepted but not processed, and recipients in Texas kept protection from deportation while awaiting a district court order on when their work permits end. [cite:ad16_fwd_daca] Status as of October 2026: Judge Andrew Hanen of the federal district court in Texas had still not issued that order (none had been issued as of 1 July 2026, and none was reported by early October), so Texas recipients' work permits remained valid; the Justice Department had proposed in September 2025 that they end; new applications were still accepted but not processed, and renewals were processed slowly, with about 125,000 pending, a record. No law has replaced the DREAM Act. [cite:ad16_audit_daca_forum]

Read together, the phrases divide the Dream's work between the individual and the public. "Bootstraps," "job creators" and "ownership society" place the engine in the person or the firm; "you didn't build that" and "ladders of opportunity" place part of it in shared investment. Both sets of phrases promise the same outcome, and each states a theory of how to reach it. The record shows how each theory fared when enacted.

What the laws did

The test of a promise is the law that follows it. Taking each administration since 1978 in turn, in the same register, the record of the mechanisms covered earlier in this book is mixed for both parties.

Under Jimmy Carter, a Democrat, the Revenue Act of 1978 added section 401(k) to the tax code; it was "never intended (nor designed)" to replace pensions, but private workers moved from guaranteed pensions to individual accounts over the following four decades, shifting investment and longevity risk onto the worker. [cite:ad10_napa_1978] [cite:ad10_dol_hist] Under Ronald Reagan, a Republican, the firing of more than 11,000 striking air-traffic controllers in August 1981, under a 1978 law barring federal strikes, marked the start of a long fall in large strikes; private-sector union membership fell from 16.8 per cent in 1983 to 5.9 per cent in 2025. [cite:ad4_reagan_patco] [cite:ad4_bls_spotlight2016] [cite:us13_bls_union2025]

Under Bill Clinton, the 1996 welfare law replaced open-ended cash aid with Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF), a fixed block grant to states with time limits and work requirements. Its defenders point to the results: adult recipients fell from 4.38 million in 1995 to 570,000 in 2024, and work among the most disadvantaged mothers rose by almost 40 per cent in the four years from 1996, in a strong economy and alongside an expanded Earned Income Tax Credit. [cite:ad16_aei_welfare] Its critics point to reach: for every 100 families in poverty, 68 received cash aid in 1996 and 21 in 2023. [cite:ad16_cbpp_tanf] Both figures are accurate; they measure different things, and which matters more is a judgement the record cannot make. The Higher Education Amendments of 1998, also signed by Clinton, removed the last time-based route for discharging federal student loans in bankruptcy. [cite:ad7_crs_rs22699]

Under George W. Bush, during the homeownership push documented in [scene:ad1_who_sells_it|Part One], the ownership rate reached its peak of 69.4 per cent (second quarter of 2004, seasonally adjusted); it fell back to 63.1 per cent by 2016, below its 1994 level. [cite:ad1_fred_hor] The push was one of several forces behind the rise, alongside low interest rates and looser lending, and the record does not separate their shares. The 2005 bankruptcy law extended the bar on discharge to private student loans. [cite:ad7_crs_rs22699] The last increase in the federal minimum wage took effect in three steps from July 2007 to July 2009, reaching $7.25, where it has stayed through Congresses controlled by each party in turn. [cite:us13_dol_minwage]

Under Barack Obama, the Affordable Care Act of 2010 helped take the uninsured rate from nearly 17 per cent in 2009 to 8.8 per cent in 2016. [cite:us16_khn_uninsured] The DREAM Act failed in the Senate, as above. Under Joe Biden, the 2021 American Rescue Plan temporarily enlarged the child tax credit and made it fully available to families with little or no earnings; the Census Bureau's supplemental measure of child poverty fell to 5.2 per cent in 2021, a record low, and rose to 12.4 per cent in 2022 after the expansion expired without renewal. [cite:us18_scripps_ctc] Several rules aimed at other mechanisms in this book were set aside before they took full effect: the 2024 overtime salary threshold was vacated by a federal court in November 2024, the federal rule keeping medical debt off credit reports was vacated in July 2025, and the SAVE student-loan repayment plan was ended through a settlement announced in December 2025. [cite:ad5_hunton_ot2024] [cite:ad8_ap_vacatur] [cite:ad7_npr_save]

Under Donald Trump, the 2017 tax law doubled the maximum child tax credit, to $2,000, and the 2025 law raised it permanently to $2,200 per child, indexed to inflation, though families with very low earnings receive only part of it or none. [cite:ad16_audit_crs_ctc] The 2017 law also doubled the estate-tax exemption, and the 2025 reconciliation law set it at $15 million from 2026, indexed to inflation. [cite:ad12_crs_estate] The Congressional Budget Office found that the 2025 law lowers resources for households toward the bottom of the income distribution and raises them for the middle and top, with in-kind benefits, mainly Medicaid and food aid, cut by about $900 billion over 2026 to 2034, and that it will add about 10 million people to the uninsured by 2034. [cite:ad11_cbo_pl11921] [cite:us16_kff_obbba] In September 2025 the Federal Trade Commission dropped its appeals in defence of the nationwide ban on non-compete clauses. [cite:ad5_ftc_accede]

Where the words and the laws agreed

The record is not only one of gaps. Clinton's 1993 promise that a full-time worker with a child would not be poor was backed by an expansion of the Earned Income Tax Credit, and by 2025 the official poverty rate among full-time, year-round workers was 1.6 per cent. [cite:ad1_clinton_1993] [cite:us18_crs_pov2025] The Affordable Care Act did what its sponsors said it would do to the uninsured rate. Social Security, expanded under presidents of both parties, moved 28.8 million people out of poverty in 2025, and old-age poverty fell from 35.2 per cent in 1959 to 9.9 per cent in 2024. [cite:us18_census_p60290] [cite:ad10_census_a3] These are cases in which a promise was matched by a mechanism built to keep it, which is the standard the rest of the record can be held to.

What the side-by-side reading shows is that the rhetoric of the Dream is bipartisan and the enacted record is bipartisan too. Each party has passed laws that narrowed a mechanism of the Dream (pensions, bargaining power, bankruptcy relief, the reach of cash aid, health coverage) and laws that widened one. Neither party has raised the federal minimum wage since 2009. A reader who concludes that one party alone keeps or breaks the promise will find counter-examples in the record of each. Motive is a separate question: the record shows what each law did, not why each legislator voted for it, and the reasons stated at the time are given above in the speakers' own words.
