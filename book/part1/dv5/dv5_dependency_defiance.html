Dependency and Defiance, 1948–2026

This volume's subtitle speaks of parents, and its working notes describe the fourth sovereign with a blunter family word: a dependent child that defies the parent who keeps it. The phrase is shorthand, and this section translates it into the documented record. The record shows two things at once. Israel depends on the United States more heavily than on any other state for its weapons, its aid and its protection at the Security Council. And Israeli governments have repeatedly acted against the stated policy of the American government, sometimes in public and sometimes in Washington itself, without losing that support. The question is how both can be true.

The dependency. Part Four documented it: about $175 billion in American aid through 2025, a ten-year agreement worth $3.8 billion a year, a stockpile of American munitions on Israeli soil, a statutory guarantee of Israel's military edge, and more than fifty vetoes at the Security Council. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025] [cite:dv4_jvl_vetoes] Israel's own defence spending is far larger than the aid: its Defence Ministry's budget for 2025 was a record 110 billion shekels, about $29 billion. [cite:dv4_toi_rubio_nov2026] The dependency is therefore not mainly about money. It is about what no one else will supply: the most advanced American aircraft and munitions, replenishment in a long war, and the veto.

The defiance, documented. Four episodes show Israeli governments acting against American policy.

  • Settlements. Successive American administrations opposed settlement growth, and in 2016 the United States let the Security Council declare the settlements without legal validity; settlement activity continued, and in February 2026 Israel's security cabinet approved measures its own finance minister said would "bury the idea of a Palestinian state." [cite:deccan_unsc_2334] [cite:dv5_wt_ap_wb_feb2026]
  • The 2015 speech. In March 2015 Netanyahu addressed a joint session of Congress, at the invitation of the Speaker, arranged without consulting the White House, to argue against the nuclear agreement President Obama was negotiating with Iran; at least fifty-eight Democratic members and the Vice President stayed away, and the agreement was concluded that July. [cite:yahoo_congress_invites_netanyahu] [cite:rollcall_netanyahu_speech_controversial] [cite:wiki_netanyahu_2015_congress_address]
  • The Gaza war. American officials demanded at least 350 aid trucks a day in October 2024; aid groups counted about 42. [cite:aljazeera_ultimatum_outcome] The Biden administration paused a shipment of large bombs over Rafah in May 2024; the operation went ahead. [cite:dv4_national_bombs_2025]
  • Palestinian statehood. The Holy See and 157 of the 193 UN members have backed a Palestinian state or two states; [cite:wiki_recognition_palestine] the Knesset voted 99 to 9 against unilateral recognition, and the prime minister said there will be no Palestinian state. [cite:dv3_ewtn_herzog_2025] [cite:toi_knesset_99] [cite:globalsecurity_two_state]

The times the guardian prevailed. Defiance has limits, and the record marks them. When an American President attached a firm condition to something Israel needed, Israel usually gave way: on the Sinai in 1956, on the Lavi fighter in 1987, on settlement building in exchange for loan guarantees in 1992, and on the Phalcon sale to China in 2000. [cite:dv4_jvl_special_alliance] [cite:migflug_lavi_cancelled] [cite:fmep_loan_guarantees_1992] [cite:ynet_phalcon_2000] In each case the American interest at stake was concrete and the President was willing to pay a political price. Where Washington's objections stayed at the level of statements, as on settlements for most of the period since 1967, Israeli governments acted on their own judgment.

Why the support continues. The record suggests three reasons, each documented in this or other volumes. The first is the American government's own strategic view, stated by its supporters as an alliance with a capable democracy that shares intelligence and builds missile defence jointly. [cite:crs_aid_israel_2023] The second is industrial: the aid is designed to be spent on American weapons. [cite:israeled_mou_text] The third is domestic politics, which the Secretary of State named as the motive for recognition in 1948 and which later volumes trace through Congress, campaign finance and Christian Zionist voters. [cite:dv4_frus_may12_1948] Together they explain why the levers documented above were pulled so rarely: each pull had a cost at home, and the gain had to be large and American.

November 2026. The relationship is now changing shape from both ends. Israel's prime minister has proposed ending American military aid within a decade and turning it into a partnership; the American President has said the money should end sooner; the two governments are negotiating; and Israel itself is between governments, with the man asked to form the next one, Gadi Eisenkot, still counting coalition votes. [cite:dv4_wiki_next_mou] [cite:dv4_toi_rubio_nov2026] [cite:dv5_israeled_watch] If the grants end, one part of the dependency ends with them. The weapons, the replenishment and the veto would remain matters of American choice, which is where the record of the last fifty years places them.
