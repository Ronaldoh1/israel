The Worst-Case Scorecard

The preface set out a hypothesis from the Sophia platform so that the record could answer it. In its strongest form it holds that immigration enforcement and the campaign against government waste were the occasions, and that a sovereign executive without a veto, the end state the Dark Enlightenment texts describe, is the destination: that the tools built to find, track and remove immigrants, and the powers taken to cut and purge the civil service, add up to the machinery such a state would need, and could be turned on anyone who criticises those in charge. Forty-two parts have tested pieces of that reading. This section gathers every grade in one place. It adds no new evidence; each row below rests on a part that sets out the record in full, and the reader can follow the link to check it.

The method, disclosed

A hypothesis this large cannot be true or false as a single sentence, so the scorecard breaks it into the sub-claims it needs, in the order the argument runs: the destination as written, the people and money that might carry it, the occasions, the machinery, the removal of brakes, the force, the turn of the machinery on critics, and the destination reached. Each sub-claim keeps the grade the grounding part gave it, in the book's four registers. Documented means a primary record or independent named reporting shows it. Probable means several documented facts point the same way and none points against, but the link itself is not on paper. Possible is allowed only for joins between systems, never for facts about people: technically feasible, with no document. Not supported means the record does not show it, or shows the opposite. A fourth column says where the record runs against the hypothesis even when a row is documented, because a fact can be true and still be checked.

Where the hypothesis turns on people who are not citizens and people who are, the scorecard gives them separate rows. The law treats them differently, the machinery has been used on them differently, and merging them would make the record look either stronger or weaker than it is.

What the rows show

The shape of the table matters more than any one line in it. The documented rows cluster at the two ends that need no plan: the texts' own words and the machinery of the state. The texts do describe an owned state run by a chief executive with "no voice" for residents, and Yarvin said in 2025 that power should be concentrated "in a single individual." [cite:de8_land_de] [cite:de8_cnn_yarvin] The state does hold, buy and join records at a scale no earlier civilian agency reached, with a Medicaid file sent on to Palantir's software over a chat channel in January 2026 and travel records passed from TSA to ICE more than 31,000 times. [cite:de27_npr_medicaid] [cite:de25b_reuters_tsaice] And the brakes did thin: the Supreme Court let presidents remove commissioners at will on 29 June 2026, and about 8,000 senior officials became removable by an order of 3 June. [cite:de0_slaughter] [cite:de30_wh_eo14410]

The rows graded not supported cluster in the middle, where the hypothesis needs a connection: a plan, a hand-off from the texts to an office, money to the theorists, a programme against citizens. Those are the places the record would show such a thing if it existed, in filings, sworn statements, contracts and court findings, and it does not. [cite:de17_li_oped] [cite:de16_natcon_signers] [cite:de30_dla_nspm7] The probable rows sit between, and each names what would settle it.

Non-citizens and citizens

For people who are not citizens, almost the whole chain has been run. A face can be matched in the street, records joined, a visa revoked for speech, a person detained and a $1-a-day work programme offered, and a federal court found that a policy of targeting non-citizen academics for their speech violated the First Amendment before vacating it on 22 January 2026. [cite:de26_404_fortify] [cite:de30_audit_aaup] [cite:de32_pogo_dollar] For citizens the record holds cases, not a system: subpoenas to unmask critics that DHS withdrew when challenged, a Global Entry card revoked three days after an agent named an observer, a link alleged in her pending suit and denied by CBP, revoked clearances, and orders against law firms that four judges struck down. The aggregate, labelled as editorial synthesis

Any single verdict on a hypothesis this broad is a judgment, not a finding, and what follows is the Sophia platform's editorial synthesis, with its method stated so that a reader who weighs the rows differently can recompute it. The first method is a plain tally, every row counting once and no row weighted: of the 25 sub-claims, 10 are documented, 5 probable, 1 possible as a join and 9 not supported. The second method treats the hypothesis as the chain it is, occasion to machinery to the turn on critics to the destination, and grades the whole by its weakest necessary link. For non-citizens, the occasion, the machinery and the turn are documented and the destination, a ruler no court or legislature can stop, is not supported. For citizens, the turn as a programme and the destination are both not supported.

Read together, the two methods say one thing. The occasion and the means are documented; the plan and the destination are not. That is neither the comfort of "nothing is happening" nor the alarm of "it has already happened." It is a narrower and more useful finding: the parts of a machine exist, several are joined, its brakes are thinner than they were, and what has stood between the parts and the worst case, as of October 2026, has been agreements, rules, judges, legislators and voters. Each of those can change, which is why [scene:de40_watch_list|the next section] lists the dated events that would move these grades in either direction.
