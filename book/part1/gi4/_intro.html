Part Four covers the decade in which "the whole Land of Israel" stopped being a question about maps on a wall and became a question about land Israel actually held. In six days of June 1967 Israel took Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan Heights. Within weeks its government had offered to give most of it back for peace, while a new movement of writers, Labor veterans, Revisionists and one prominent rabbi declared that no government had the right to give any of it up. The part sets out what was taken, what the government first decided, what is and is not available of that movement's 1967 manifesto, the Allon Plan with which Labor governments answered it, and the religious settlement movement, Gush Emunim, that took its cause over from 1974.
