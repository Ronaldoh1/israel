Part One sets out what the Ethiopian Bible is and where it came from. It explains the eighty-one books and the two ways the church counts them, and the liturgical language, Ge'ez, that held the scriptures as firmly as Latin held the West's. It then follows Christianity's arrival at Aksum through two shipwrecked captives, a bishop consecrated by Athanasius, a king who put the cross on his coins, and a Roman emperor who tried to replace the bishop within a generation.
