Who Owns the Textbook Houses Now

The Maxwell case is often told as a strange episode from a vanished era of press barons. The structure that made it possible did not vanish. The American school-book market is still dominated by a few large firms, and those firms are still bought and sold like any other business, with no public review of who the buyer is or what the buyer wants.

Three transactions of the last decade show how ownership now works. In February 2019 Pearson, the British education group, sold its American school courseware business to Nexus Capital Management, a private investment firm, for $250 million; the business was renamed Savvas Learning. [cite:pen_edsurge_pearson2019] On 2 August 2021 Platinum Equity, a private-equity firm, completed a $4.5 billion purchase of McGraw Hill, the company that had been Maxwell's partner in the 1989 school venture. [cite:pen_platinum_mh2021] On 7 April 2022 Veritas Capital, another private-equity firm, completed a $2.8 billion purchase of Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, at $21 a share, and took it off the stock market. HMH describes itself as serving "more than 50 million students." [cite:pen_journal_hmh2022]

Nothing in these purchases is improper, and nothing in the public record suggests that any of these owners has tried to shape what the books say about Israel, Palestine or anything else. That is not the point. The point is what the law asks of a buyer, which is the same as it asked of Maxwell: nothing. A private-equity fund owes no public account of its investors. A firm taken off the stock market stops publishing the reports a listed company must file. The four publishers that a peer-reviewed study found reach most American world-history students in grades 6 to 12 (see the section on how the story is taught) are, for the most part, now owned by funds whose investors the public cannot see.

The section that follows sets out who, in the American system, does review what those publishers print. The answer is a patchwork of state boards and volunteer panels, and in some states no one.
