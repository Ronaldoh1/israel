Part Two followed the West Bank under Jordan. Proclaimed in union with Transjordan at Jericho in 1948 and annexed in April 1950, it was recognized as Jordanian by almost no one, and the Arab League treated Jordan as holding it in trust. [cite:rs_rd6_kramer_annexation] [cite:wb_wiki_annex] Its people, residents and refugees alike, became Jordanian citizens with half the seats in parliament, [cite:wb_wiki_annex] [cite:wb_palquest_nationality] while Israelis were barred from the holy places of the Old City. Across the Green Line, Israel's free-fire policy against infiltrators, most of them unarmed, killed thousands, and infiltrators killed Israelis; and reprisal raids on Qibya in 1953 and Samu in 1966 drew UN censure; Samu set the West Bank rioting against Hussein months before he lost it. What was left open is whose territory it was, a question Jordan's annexation did not settle and that Israel's occupation, the subject of Part Three, reopened.
