Part Nineteen closes the volume. It takes the five tests fixed in Part One, the private record, money and favours, the timing of shifts, treaty behaviour and the cost of opposition, and scores each against what Parts Two to Eighteen documented, adding no new evidence. It then gives the volume's answer to its central question, whether the Holy See's public refusal of Zionism from 1904 to 1993 was a front, stated plainly and graded by strength into what is settled, what is probable and what is not established, while keeping the Holy See's position on a movement and a state separate from its teaching on the Jewish people. It sums up the answers to the volume's other questions, from the papal state's origins to the Orlandi case, sets out in one labelled passage the interpretive lenses the volume declined to treat as findings, and ends with the questions that stay open as of October 2026 and the dates on which some of them may move.
