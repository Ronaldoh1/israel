Part Six established that the housing Dream was a federal design that partly worked. The long, fixed-rate, amortising mortgage built by HOLC and the FHA helped lift homeownership from 43.6 per cent of households in 1940 to 64 per cent by 1980, and it remains the standard loan. [cite:ad6_greenwachter] The same agencies wrote racial exclusion into their underwriting, and the Black–white ownership gap, 27 points in 1960, was 29.1 points in mid-2026. [cite:ad6_fha_manual] [cite:ad6_stateline_gap] [cite:us15_census_hvs_q226] A typical home now costs about 5.0 years of median household income against 3.2 in the 1990s, the 30-year mortgage rate stood at 7.40 per cent in October 2026, first-time buyers in the realtors' survey had a record median age of 40, and 49 per cent of renters spent more than 30 per cent of their income on housing. [cite:us14_jchs_pti] [cite:ad6_pmms] [cite:ad6_nar_2025] [cite:ad6_nlihc_son26] Large investors own a small share of single-family rentals nationally but a large one in some Sunbelt metros, and a federal ban on further purchases by the largest takes effect in January 2027. [cite:ad6_crs_inst] [cite:ad6_mayer_road] Where housing extracts, the record is specific: about 3.6 million eviction filings a year before the pandemic, records of which about a fifth are ambiguous or wrong, contract sales that cost Chicago's Black buyers an estimated $3.2–4.0 billion, and a pricing-software case settled without admission of wrongdoing.
