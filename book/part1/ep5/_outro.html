Three lives, set side by side, show the same thing from three directions. Each man had built his authority long before he met Epstein: a fortune and a company, a party and a cabinet seat, an army and a premiership. Each kept up the contact after the 2008 conviction, when the record of what Epstein had done was public. And in each case the documents show what the relationship was used for: money placed and tax advice welcomed, government information passed on, a channel for selling surveillance systems to a foreign government.

The consequences were not the same. The British minister was arrested, under a law on misconduct in public office that covers what he is accused of doing. The investor and the former prime minister have faced no investigation, because nothing in the record so far describes a crime, and none of their own statements was given under oath. That difference is the subject of Part Eight. Before that, Part Six turns to how men like these, and the institutions around them, kept Epstein's reputation intact.
