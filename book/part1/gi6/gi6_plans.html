Pledges, a Plan and a Pause, 2019–2024

Until 2019, formal annexation of West Bank land was a position held by parties and ministers on the right of Israel's governments, not a policy any prime minister had promised to carry out by a date. The Likud platform of 1977 and the coalition guidelines of 2022, read in Part Five, spoke of rights and sovereignty in principle. [scene:gi5_likud|Part Five: the Likud platform] This part follows the years in which the idea became a timetable: who pledged what, when, on what terms, and what stopped it. The settlement record underneath these years, the approvals, outposts and budgets, is set out in The West Bank, Documented and is not repeated here. [scene:wb9_annex|The West Bank, Part Nine: the fastest years of settlement]

The Jordan Valley pledge, September 2019

On 10 September 2019, a week before a closely fought general election on 17 September, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stood before a map and pledged that, if re-elected, he would extend Israeli sovereignty over the Jordan Valley soon after forming a government, and later over the settlements. He called it "a one-time opportunity, to extend Israeli sovereignty on our settlements in Judea and Samaria," and tied the timing to the peace plan President Donald Trump was expected to release after the vote. The Jordan Valley makes up nearly a quarter of the West Bank; annexing it would have left the Palestinian areas surrounded by Israeli territory. [cite:gi6_ap_jv2019] [cite:gi6_npr_jv2019]

The responses were immediate. The Palestinian president, Mahmoud Abbas, said all agreements with Israel would be cancelled if Netanyahu went ahead. Jordan's foreign minister, Ayman Safadi, called the pledge "a serious escalation that undermines all peace efforts," and the United Nations called it "devastating" to the prospects of two states. Inside Israel, the opposition politician Yair Lapid called it "an election stunt." American officials said they had been briefed in advance and raised no objection. [cite:gi6_ap_jv2019]

The Trump plan, January 2020

The plan arrived on 28 January 2020. Referred to in later official documents as the President's "Vision for Peace", it allotted Israel sovereignty over about 30 per cent of the West Bank, including the settlements and most of the Jordan Valley. The Palestinians were offered a demilitarized state in the remaining areas, which would be non-contiguous and joined by roads, bridges and tunnels, with a capital in Abu Dis or elsewhere on the edge of East Jerusalem. Statehood was conditional: among the criteria were the disarming of Hamas and recognition of Israel as "the nation state of the Jewish people," with four years to meet them. Secretary of State Mike Pompeo said the United States would support annexation consistent with the plan's maps, and Netanyahu said his government would begin considering measures that, in the Congressional Research Service's words, would "effectively unilaterally annex" the settlements and the Jordan Valley. [cite:gi6_crs_plan2020]

Earlier Israeli schemes for keeping part of the West Bank, beginning with the Allon Plan of 1967, are read in Part Four, and the offers that went the other way in Part Twelve. [scene:gi4_allon|Part Four: the Allon Plan] [scene:gi12_withdrawals|Part Twelve: the withdrawals] What was new in 2020 was an American president's map, and a secretary of state's statement that Washington would support Israel applying its law to the land the map assigned it, before any agreement with the Palestinians. [cite:gi6_crs_plan2020]

The Israeli coalition formed in April 2020 wrote the step into its founding agreement. Clause 29 of the Netanyahu–Gantz agreement, signed on 20 April, allowed Netanyahu to bring "the agreement reached with the US on the application of sovereignty" to the cabinet or the Knesset "starting July 1, 2020," in full agreement with the United States, including on maps, and while "maintaining regional stability" and existing peace agreements. Settler leaders welcomed it. The Palestinian negotiator Saeb Erekat said: "Annexation means the end of any possibility for a negotiated solution." [cite:gi6_toi_unity2020] The date passed without a vote. Jordan's response, and its treaty of 1994, are followed in Part Eight. [scene:gi8_jordan|Part Eight: Jordan]

The pause, August 2020

What stopped the step was a trade. On 13 August 2020 the United States, Israel and the United Arab Emirates issued a joint statement announcing full normalization between Israel and the UAE. Its operative sentence on the West Bank read: "Israel will suspend declaring sovereignty over areas outlined in the President's Vision for Peace." [cite:gi6_dcpd_uae2020] The parties read the word differently from the first day. Netanyahu said annexation "remains on the table" and that there was "no change" in his plan to apply sovereignty in coordination with Washington. Trump said: "It's off the table," adding that he could not speak about the future. The American ambassador, David Friedman, said "suspend" had been "chosen carefully" and meant a "temporary halt": off the table now, "but it's not off the table permanently." [cite:gi6_jta_table2020] Emirati officials later described Israel's ruling out of annexation as the condition on which they had signed. [cite:gi6_axios_uae2025] The suspension, not a renunciation, is the line every later stage of this part pushes against.

Sovereignty in the coalition agreement, 2022

The government formed after the November 2022 election put the commitment in writing again, in different form. The coalition agreement of 1 December 2022 between Likud and Religious Zionism stated that the prime minister "will work towards the formulation and promotion of a policy whereby sovereignty is applied" to Judea and Samaria, the Hebrew biblical names the government uses for the West Bank. The same agreement gave Bezalel Smotrich, the Religious Zionism leader and finance minister, a second ministerial post inside the Defense Ministry, overseeing civil matters in the West Bank. [cite:gi6_wiki_37gov] The government's general guidelines, which asserted the Jewish people's "exclusive and inalienable right to all parts of the Land of Israel," are read in Part Five. [scene:gi5_nationstate|Part Five: the Nation-State Law and the 2022 guidelines]

The civil powers, 2023

The administrative transfer followed. On 23 February 2023 Defense Minister Yoav Gallant signed an agreement assigning governmental powers in the West Bank to a body headed by Smotrich. [cite:gi6_wiki_37gov] In June 2023 the cabinet moved the approval of settlement planning to him, and civil powers in Area C that the army had held passed to a civilian deputy reporting to his Settlements Administration; in March 2023 the Knesset had already repealed the part of the 2005 disengagement law that barred Israelis from the evacuated sites in the northern West Bank. [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis] The West Bank volume documents how those powers have since been used. [scene:wb9_annex|The West Bank, Part Nine: who runs it]

What the transfer meant was described by Smotrich himself. On 9 June 2024, at a gathering of settlers in the West Bank, he was recorded saying, of moving authority from the army to civilians under him: "We created a separate civilian system." He called the change "mega-dramatic" and said: "Such changes change a system's DNA." The Defense Ministry had been kept formally involved, he explained, because "it will be easier to swallow in the international and legal context," "so that they won't say that we are doing annexation here." He said Netanyahu knew the details and was "with us full on." Smotrich's spokesman confirmed the speech. The prime minister's office replied that the West Bank's final status "will be determined by the parties in direct negotiations" and that "this policy has not changed." [cite:gi6_bg_nyt_smotrich]

Two things are established at this point, and one is not. It is documented that a sitting prime minister promised sovereignty over a defined area in 2019; that the United States endorsed it on a map in 2020; that it was suspended, in words both sides read as temporary, as the price of normalization with the UAE; and that the coalition of 2022 committed itself again in principle and moved West Bank civil government into a minister's hands. It is documented, in his own recorded words, that the minister who received those powers described their purpose as a change that would not look like annexation. Whether the prime minister shared that purpose is not settled by the recording: his office's stated position was that nothing had changed.
