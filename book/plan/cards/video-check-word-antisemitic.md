# Video checked: "the word antisemitic was invented to silence critics" (20 Nov 2026)

Ron brought in a 34-second video (VID 20261120 171720 010). Each claim below is checked against sources, and against the library's own series on the word ("Who Named It?", Parts 1–5, and The Baseline scenes s00b and s00b0).

**Verdict key:** ✅ confirmed · 📰 reported by a named reference · ⚠️ disputed · ❌ wrong · ❓ not found

| Claim in the video | Verdict | What the record shows |
|---|---|---|
| Moritz Steinschneider, a Jewish writer, invented "antisemitic" in 1860 | ✅ | He wrote *antisemitische Vorurteile* ("antisemitic prejudices") in 1860, criticizing Ernest Renan's claim that Semitic peoples, Arabs as well as Jews, were inferior to Aryans (Avner Falk, cited in Wikipedia, "Antisemite"). He coined the adjective, not the movement. |
| The term was made to silence people who exposed "Jewish crimes" | ❌ | His target was a race theory, not critics of Jews. The word became a political label in 1879, when Wilhelm Marr, who wasn't Jewish, published *Der Sieg des Judenthums über das Germanenthum* and founded the League of Antisemites (Antisemiten-Liga). It was the haters' own name for their movement, a scientific-sounding substitute for *Judenhass*, "Jew-hatred." It first appeared widely in print in 1881. |
| The regime's first law was an "Antisemitism Act" in 1917 | ❌ | The first decrees of Soviet power, on 8 November 1917, were on peace, on land and on setting up the government. Dozens more followed in 1917 (press, eight-hour day, banks, courts), and none was on antisemitism. The decree "On the struggle against antisemitism and anti-Jewish pogroms" came in late July 1918: sources give 25, 26 or 27 July, the last being its publication date. It was signed by Lenin, Bonch-Bruevich and Gorbunov. A Cambridge study notes the Bolsheviks "did not broach the question of antisemitism even once" in their first nine months. |
| Antisemitism was made a capital crime | ❌ | The decree's operative line orders that "pogromists and those who carry out pogrom agitation" be declared outlaws. It names violent pogroms and incitement to them, not antisemitism in general, and states no death penalty. |
| Anti-communists were treated as antisemites | ❓ | Not found in the decree or in the sources checked. |
| "The predominantly Jewish character of the regime" | ⚠️ / ❌ as framed | Several leading Bolsheviks were of Jewish origin, Trotsky among them. Describing the regime as Jewish is the "Judeo-Bolshevism" claim. The same state later shut down Jewish religious and Zionist life, and under Stalin it ran campaigns against Jews, such as the 1953 Doctors' Plot. |
| Trotsky started using "racist" to silence dissent | ❌ | The French *raciste* was in print by 1895–1897 (Maurras; Méry in *La Libre Parole*). The OED's first English "racism" is Richard Henry Pratt in 1902, denouncing segregation (NPR). Trotsky used the word in 1930 and 1933, decades later. |
| These words are used today to silence criticism | ⚠️ (narrower version documented) | The documented version is about Israel, not "Jewish actions." The IHRA working definition (2016, not legally binding) lists examples that tie some criticism of Israel to antisemitism, such as calling the state's existence "a racist endeavor" and applying double standards. It also says "criticism of Israel similar to that leveled against any other country cannot be regarded as antisemitic" (European Commission). In March 2021 more than 200 scholars signed the Jerusalem Declaration, which calls the IHRA definition unclear and says boycott, divestment and sanctions are "not, in and of themselves, antisemitic" (Jerusalem Post). |

## Does it support the library's findings?

No. It reverses them. The library's finding is that the word was named by the people doing the hating (Marr, 1879), and that its first written use was a Jewish scholar's criticism of race theory against all "Semites." The video keeps one true fact, the 1860 date, and builds three false claims around it: the 1917 law, the capital crime and Trotsky's coinage. It then blames "Jews" as a people, which the platform's rule forbids.

The part of its complaint that holds up is already in the library, in documented form: the dispute between the IHRA examples and the Jerusalem Declaration over whether criticism of Israel is labelled antisemitic.
