Benjamin Netanyahu has been Israel's prime minister for longer than anyone else. This volume is about the man and about the machine around him: the doctrine he inherited, the craft of persuasion he learned in America, the governments he formed and the partners he brought into them, the criminal cases that have run alongside his rule since 2019, and the structure of a state that let one leader and a handful of small parties hold so much power for so long.

It ends where the record stands at the time of writing, in late November 2026. Netanyahu's bloc lost its majority in the election of 27 October; the president has asked his rival, Gadi Eisenkot, to form a government; and on 25 November the judges in his trial signalled that two of the three cases against him do not hold. [cite:ny_wiki_election2026] [cite:ny_toi_vindicated] Whether his long reign is ending is, on that date, an open question, and the last part says so.

Three rules hold throughout. What Netanyahu did is kept apart from what he said he did and from what others say he intended. A court's finding, a police recommendation and a critic's charge are different things, and each is named for what it is. And other volumes on this shelf carry parts of the story in full: his decades of warnings about Iran in The Iran Architecture, the money to Hamas in The Hamas Architecture, the war and the warrants in Gaza, Documented, and the settlements in The West Bank, Documented. Here they are summarized and linked.
