Part Eleven examines the legal protections that surround Israel, or those who deal with it, in American law and policy: the federal anti-boycott law of 1977 and the state anti-BDS laws that followed it decades later; the IHRA definition of antisemitism in civil-rights enforcement, from Executive Order 13899 to the stalled Antisemitism Awareness Act; the sanctions imposed on the International Criminal Court's officials; and three general laws or powers applied to Israel in a particular way: the Leahy Law, the Security Council veto and the non-proliferation amendments. The foreign-agent question is treated in [scene:ow2_lobby_law|Part Two]. For each shield the part gives the government's stated reason beside the critics' case.
