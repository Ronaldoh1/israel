The taught Dream promises that a steady job brings health insurance and that insurance keeps a family safe from ruinous bills. This part tests that promise in three steps. The first section traces how health coverage came to be tied to the job, through wartime wage controls and a tax exclusion written into law in 1954, and sets national spending beside health outcomes, the price a family pays for coverage at work, the uninsured and the underinsured, and the changes made by the 2025 budget law. The second follows a claim and a prescription through the businesses that stand between patient and doctor: insurers that deny claims and require prior authorization, pharmacy benefit managers, and hospital prices. The third measures medical debt, explains why its published totals differ, follows a denied claim to a debt on a credit report, and sets out the disputed link to bankruptcy and the state of the credit-reporting rules as of October 2026.
