Before any of the three cities can be tested as a parent of Israel, the reader needs to know what each of them is. The popular story calls all three "sovereign city-states" and moves on. The record does not allow that shortcut. One of the three is a sovereign state, one is a privileged local authority, and one is a federal district with less self-government than an ordinary American city. Part One sets out the tests by which lawyers and political scientists decide what counts as sovereignty, applies them to the three cities and to Israel, compares the cities side by side on the points that bear on power, and then defines the test that each of the following parts will apply: what a "parent" is, which function each city is said to have performed, and what kind of document would prove or disprove it.
