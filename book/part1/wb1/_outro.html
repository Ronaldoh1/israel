Part One followed the making of the West Bank. The 1947 plan gave the proposed Arab state the hill country of Samaria and Judea and gave the Jewish state, about 56 percent of the land, most of the Negev desert, the Jezreel Valley and most of the coastal plain, when Jews owned about 6.6 percent of Palestine. [cite:wb_ej_partition] [cite:rs_rd5_yusuf_2002] [cite:wb_wiki_landpurchase] The war that followed left Israel holding about 78 percent of the Mandate, including towns such as Lydda and Ramle that the plan had assigned to the Arab state, whose people were driven east. [cite:rs_rd6_mfa_armistice_map] [cite:rs_rd5_palquest_lydda] Jordan's Arab Legion held the hills, as its king had told the Zionist leaders he would, and the armistice of April 1949 drew the Green Line around them. [cite:rs_rd5_cie_golda] [cite:rs_rd6_arm_jordan_text] What is settled is the plan's terms and the line. What is contested is the plan's fairness, argued from the same map, and the causes of the exodus. What was left open was the fate of the territory the plan had meant for an Arab state, and Part Two follows Jordan's answer.
