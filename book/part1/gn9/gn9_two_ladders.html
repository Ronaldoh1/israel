Two Ladders: the Aeons and the Tree of Life

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The Gnostic texts describe creation as a descent. From the unknown God, which the Apocryphon of John calls a Monad "with nothing above it", come Barbelo, the first emanation, and then a series of aeons, paired powers that fill the divine world, until Sophia's unconsented act produces Yaldabaoth and the lower world. [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch] Other texts from the jar describe the way back up. Zostrianos describes an ascent with six baptisms, and Allogenes an ascent to the Unknowable. [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch]

Jewish mysticism built a ladder of the same shape. In the Kabbalah, the sefirot are the ten emanations or powers through which God the Creator becomes manifest. The idea first appears in the Sefer Yetzira, the "Book of Creation", where they are "ideal numbers", and later Kabbalists made them ten stages of emanation from En Sof, the unknowable Infinite. [cite:gn_brit_sefira] Their names run from keter, the supreme crown, through wisdom, intelligence, love, might, beauty, eternity, majesty and foundation, to malkhut, kingship. They are often grouped in threes to form right, left and middle pillars, and Kabbalists used them as the central subject of mystical contemplation, though critics thought such speculation close to heresy. [cite:gn_brit_sefira]

The inner reading treats both ladders as maps of consciousness. The descent is the way a single awareness becomes many: thought, feeling, body and finally the world of things, each step further from the source. The ascent is the same road travelled back, by attention turning inward step by step. In this reading the two traditions describe one territory in two vocabularies, and the Tree of Life is not a diagram of heaven but a diagram of the person reading it. "As above, so below": the order by which the world came out of God is the order in which the self can return.

The record does not show that one ladder came from the other. The Apocryphon of John was composed before about 180, and its surviving copies come from the jar; the sefirot first appear in the Sefer Yetzira, and their full system was built by later Kabbalists. [cite:vt25_wiki_hyparch] [cite:gn_brit_sefira] How, or whether, the two are historically related is debated, and this book does not try to settle it. The resemblance is a reader's observation. What the record does show is that two traditions, both kept outside the institutions that defined orthodoxy in their communities, both pictured the divine as a ladder that a person could climb, and both were treated by their critics as dangerous for that reason.
