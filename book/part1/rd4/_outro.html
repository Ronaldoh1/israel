By the end of 1945 the Mandate's structure still stood, but the weight resting on it had moved. Britain still held the Mandate and still governed by the White Paper of 1939. The argument over what should replace it was now being made as loudly in Washington as in Jerusalem or London.

The legal layer had been altered without being resolved. Cmd 6019 of 17 May 1939 ruled out a Jewish state, promised an independent Palestine state within ten years, allowed some 75,000 Jewish immigrants over five years, and made any further immigration depend on Arab acquiescence; Parliament approved it on 23 May. [cite:rs_rd4_wp_cojs_text] [cite:rs_rd4_wp_frus_d841] Whether Britain had the right to adopt it was put to the League and never decided. The Permanent Mandates Commission held unanimously that the policy departed from the interpretation it had always placed on the Mandate, and found by four votes to three that it could not say the policy conformed; the war came before the League Council could act. [cite:rs_rd4_wp_jvl_league] [cite:rs_rd4_wp_wiki_pmc] The Jewish Agency's strongest case was that an Arab veto on immigration could not be squared with the Mandate's duty to "facilitate Jewish immigration." [cite:rs_rd4_wp_bjpa_jacase] The strongest case for it was the Commission minority's, that existing circumstances could justify the policy provided the League Council did not object, together with Britain's own stated objective of an independent state within ten years. [cite:rs_rd4_wp_jewishlink] The record leaves the question where the war left it.

The administrative layer was coercive. To end the revolt Britain sent about 20,000 troops, demolished houses, imposed collective fines on whole neighbourhoods, detained and deported, and hanged between 108 and 122 Palestinian Arabs and two Jews. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_origins_yehudai] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_shindler_hughes] The White Paper then placed the remaining levers with the High Commissioner: when 25,000 refugees could enter, general powers over land transfers, and a rule deducting every unauthorized arrival from the quota, which became the ground for suspending the whole six-month quota in July 1939. [cite:rs_rd4_wp_cojs_text] [cite:rs_rd4_wp_frus_d841] At sea the same rule was enforced against particular ships, and the Patria and the Struma together cost more than 1,000 lives. [cite:wiki_patria_disaster] [cite:wiki_struma_disaster] The wartime High Commissioners and Colonial Secretaries are not named in the sources for this stage.

The institutional and financial layer belonged largely to the Yishuv. On the figures in this library's funding ledger, the Jewish Agency's 1939 budget of £720,000 carried £80,000 for defense, and the Kofer HaYishuv levy of 1938 funded the Haganah. Britain trained parts of that force when it needed it, at Ein Harod in 1938, in the Palmach in 1941 and in the Jewish Brigade Group of 1944–45, and jailed members who trained outside its control. [cite:wiki_special_night_squads] [cite:archiveshub_wingate] [cite:wiki_zvi_brenner] The Mossad LeAliyah Bet was created as a branch of the Haganah in 1938, and the Haganah founded the Shai, its intelligence arm, in 1940. [cite:ushmm_aliyah_bet] [cite:shai_wiki] In October 1942 the Zionist General Council made the Biltmore demand for a Jewish Commonwealth binding policy. [cite:biltmore_enc] The movement's fringe stood outside this discipline: Lehi offered in 1941 to fight on Germany's side and killed Lord Moyne in 1944, and the Zionist Executive called its members "traitors." [cite:academickids_lehi] [cite:daat_moyne] In America, by the same ledger's reckoning from Marc Lee Raphael's history, the United Jewish Appeal raised more than $124 million between 1939 and 1945, split among European relief, the Palestine programme and resettlement in the United States in shares the record here does not give; the Emergency Council's own files document a method, not a budget.

The Palestinian Arab side ended the stage with the heaviest losses and the weakest institutions. The Arab Higher Committee of April 1936 had joined rival parties under one leadership; by the autumn of 1937 it was outlawed, its members arrested or deported to the Seychelles, its president in exile. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_wiki_ahc] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_jvl_andrews] Walid Khalidi's count of 5,032 Arab dead, against official British figures that are lower and higher estimates that reach 8,000, measures the revolt's cost; the sources fetched put Jewish deaths at about 400 to 415 and British deaths at about 100 to 200. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_shindler_hughes] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_unpacked] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_origins_yehudai] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_jvl_riots] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_ebsco_strickert] The rivalry between the Mufti's followers and the Nashashibi peace bands produced hundreds of killings of Palestinians by Palestinians. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_origins_yehudai] [cite:rs_rd4_ar_hughes_peacebands_full] The leadership rejected Peel outright and rejected the White Paper, though a US consul reported "very obvious relief" among Arabs at its terms. [cite:wiki_peel_commission] [cite:rs_rd4_wp_frus_d826] Its strongest case was a majority's claim to an independent government and to a halt to immigration it had never consented to. The strongest case against its choices is the record of rebel violence against other Palestinians and of al-Husseini's own road to Berlin, which remains one man's documented choice, not the record of a people. [cite:wiki_amin_al_husseini]

The regional layer widened in every direction. Arab rulers mediated the end of the 1936 strike; Egypt, Iraq and Saudi Arabia sat at St James's Palace; Iraq's pro-Axis coup collapsed in 1941 into the Farhud, the pogrom whose death toll the sources give as 150 to 180 or higher. [cite:rs_rd4_ar_hcc] [cite:rs_rd4_pw_wiki_london1939] [cite:ushmm_farhud] [cite:wiki_farhud] By 1945 the Saudi king, whose kingdom was already tied to the United States through ARAMCO, held a written presidential undertaking on Palestine. [cite:loc_aramco_oil_pamphlet] [cite:fdr_letter_ibnsaud]

The American layer was the stage's new construction, raised under a 1938 law that for the first time required agents of foreign principals to register. [cite:doj_fara] The Emergency Council, founded in August 1943, ran letter and telegram campaigns from 342 Madison Avenue; the Bergson committees bought pages and filled Madison Square Garden; both parties wrote Palestine into their platforms in 1944, "the first time in American history." [cite:azec_powerbase] [cite:aja_azec_local_1944] [cite:w_wwnd] [cite:aja_azec_appraisal_1944] Against them stood the War and State Departments, which twice asked Congress to wait, and a president who approved silence on Palestine for the war while assuring Arab leaders in writing that he would consult them. [cite:djn_1944_marshall] [cite:brandeis_center_fdr_zionism] [cite:state_dept_oct1945_release] Congress's resolution of December 1945 spoke not of a Jewish commonwealth but of equal rights "regardless of race or creed." [cite:frus1945_concres]

What was settled, then, was narrower than either side's hopes: Britain's partition plan was dead, its White Paper stood, and the Palestinian leadership that might have negotiated its terms was scattered. What was contested ran through the whole stage: whether the White Paper broke the Mandate, whether the Zionist Congress accepted Peel, what Ben-Gurion's letter to his son meant, how many died in the revolt, whether Roosevelt's two voices were statecraft or duplicity. What the record leaves open is the question the stage handed on. A Labour government pledged against the White Paper took office in July 1945, and the State Department forecast that it would modify the immigration limits and not act on its conference's call for Arabs to "move out." [cite:rs_rd4_lab_frus_yale] Roosevelt had died on 12 April 1945, and his promise of consultation passed to a successor who had not been told how ill he was. [cite:arabnews_fdr_ibnsaud] [cite:globalsecurity_fdr_veiled_decline] How those two inheritances would meet the survivors of the Holocaust is the subject of the next stage.
