The Board Before the Game, 1789–1815

A common way of telling Israel's story begins with the Holocaust, as though nothing before it mattered. The record begins much earlier. The dynasties, states and religious institutions that would later fund the Zionist project, permit it or shape the ground it worked on were all in place by 1789, a full century before the political movement was founded. Between 1789 and 1815 the Napoleonic wars redrew the map of Europe. In those same years one Frankfurt family placed five sons in five capitals and, by 1815, had become the most reliable creditor to regimes that kept collapsing. This section sets out that board: the family first, then the states around it.

It is a subject on which two errors are common, and this section avoids both. The Rothschild dynasty is real, and its banking history is one of the best-studied cases of dynastic finance in modern European history. That same history has served for two centuries as the raw material for antisemitic conspiracy theory. Both facts are on the record, and the work of this section is to keep them apart.

Mayer Amschel Rothschild. The founder, Mayer Amschel Rothschild (1744–1812), was born in the Frankfurt Judengasse, the walled Jewish quarter where the law of the Holy Roman Empire required Frankfurt's Jews to live. The family name came from the red shield on the house sign of an earlier ancestor. In the 1760s he trained as a numismatist, a dealer in rare coins for aristocratic collectors. That trade was his entry into the economy of the German courts; he was not yet a banker. In 1769 he was appointed Hoffaktor, court factor, to Landgrave Wilhelm IX of Hesse-Kassel. The post was a regulated intermediary role: Jews were barred by law from most professions but could serve as financial agents to particular nobles. In 1770 he married Gutle Schnapper. They had ten surviving children, five sons and five daughters, and under German succession law only the sons could inherit and manage the business.

The decisive moment came in 1806. In the Napoleonic Wars, Landgrave Wilhelm fled the French and entrusted his fortune to Mayer Amschel. On the account this book draws on, that custody was the operational moment of the family's rise. Mayer Amschel died in 1812. His will, held in the Rothschild Archive in London, instructed his five sons to work only among themselves, to share information across borders, and to arrange marriages within the extended family so as to hold the capital together. At his death he was not the wealthiest man in Europe. He left a family firm about the size of a mid-tier private bank: a man rising, not yet risen.

Five sons, five capitals. Between 1798 and 1812 Mayer Amschel placed his sons in five European cities. Amschel stayed in Frankfurt and ran the original house. Salomon went to Vienna and the Austrian court, Nathan to London, Carl to Naples and the Italian states, and James to Paris. [cite:schama_two_rothschilds] The sources behind this volume do not agree on when the two western houses were formally founded. One dates N M Rothschild & Sons in London to 1811 and another to 1809; the Paris house, de Rothschild Frères, is dated to 1817 in one account and to 1812 in others. The structure itself is not in doubt.

The dispersion was the innovation, and the mechanism is clear. Five branches in five capitals let information move across borders faster than through the state postal systems. They let capital move between currencies and interest rates. They spread political risk, since trouble in one country did not threaten the whole. And they let the family underwrite loans to sovereigns who were at war with each other. This five-city structure meant the family could move capital across borders faster than any state. [cite:schama_two_rothschilds] The family's coat of arms, five arrows held in a fist, commemorates the five branches.

The model was distinctive in its scale but not unique in kind. The Barings in Britain, the Fuggers in an earlier Germany, the Warburgs in Hamburg, the Bleichröders in Berlin and later the Morgans in America all worked on similar dynastic, cross-border lines. This is the documented history of a kind of finance, not a conspiracy.

The London house and the wars. Nathan Mayer Rothschild (1777–1836) established the London house. Through the nineteenth century it was a principal underwriter of British government bonds, and in 1875 it financed Britain's purchase of shares in the Suez Canal. [cite:schama_two_rothschilds] In the last campaign against Napoleon, Nathan financed the payment of British subsidies to Britain's allies, and Wellington's army was partly paid through Rothschild couriers carrying gold to Spain. On this book's reading, this is where the family's business met the state borrowing that the Bank of England had organized a century earlier: a government at war needed lenders who could move money quickly across a continent, and the five houses could.

The most famous story about Nathan is the one the sources handle least consistently. The account of the Frankfurt dynasty used for this section, drawing on Niall Ferguson's The House of Rothschild (volume 1, 1998), holds that the tale of Nathan learning of Wellington's victory at Waterloo a day early, by carrier pigeon, and making a fortune on the London Stock Exchange is partly documented and partly embellished. On that account the information advantage was real, the market timing was more complicated than the legend, and the version in which Nathan "cornered the market" is a nineteenth-century antisemitic caricature by Édouard Drumont, later amplified. The sources behind the later section of this volume on the London house tell it differently. They trace the story to an 1846 pamphlet by Georges Dairnvaell, published under the name "Satan," and quote the Rothschild Archive as saying that nothing in the record suggests Nathan was first in London to know of the victory, and that the legend "is just that: a legend." The two accounts disagree on whether Nathan had any real advantage and on where the story began. Both accounts cite Ferguson, and the London-house account adds his estimate that any quick gain was at most about £7,000. They agree that the legend's popular form is antisemitic in origin. The full account belongs to the section on the London house, which also notes that the archive's words reach this book only through a secondary source.

The Paris house. James Mayer Rothschild (1792–1868), the youngest son, was sent to Paris at about nineteen. Between 1817 and 1848 he built de Rothschild Frères into the largest private bank in France, underwriting French government loans under both the Bourbon Restoration and the July Monarchy. From the 1830s to the 1850s he financed the Chemin de Fer du Nord, the northern French railway and one of the largest infrastructure projects of the age, where much of the Paris fortune came to be concentrated. When he died in 1868 his was the largest single Rothschild branch by capital. It was mainly an investment bank, lending to states and building infrastructure. The house passed to his sons, principally Alphonse and Gustave. His youngest son, Edmond, was 23.

The peak, 1815–1875. Between 1815 and 1875 the Rothschild banks were arguably the largest private-capital institution in the world. They underwrote sovereign loans for Britain, France, Austria and several Italian states. Their business was to make such loans pay for both lender and borrower, which is what an investment bank still does. The Vienna, Paris and Italian branches financed early railway building across Europe. From the 1830s, under Pope Gregory XVI, the papacy borrowed from the Rothschilds more than once; the account used here says this is documented in Vatican financial archives, and the papal loans are told in full in a later section. How large was all this? On Ferguson's figures, as the same account reports them, the family's combined capital around 1875 was roughly £8–10 million, about £1 billion in modern terms. That is substantial, but nothing like the "half of the world's wealth" of the legend. The Rothschilds were the largest private bankers of the period, not the only ones, and significant rather than dominant.

The decline after 1901. The founding house did not last. The Frankfurt house, the mother house of the dynasty, closed in 1901, because Amschel's line had produced no male heirs to carry it on. By the time of the Balfour Declaration in 1917, the original branch had been closed for sixteen years. The account used here traces the decline to four causes: the spreading of capital among dozens of cousins of uneven talent by the third and fourth generations; the rise of joint-stock banking, which outcompeted private family banks; the rise of American banking, which moved the centre of world finance to New York; and the two world wars. In 1938 the Nazis expropriated the property of the Austrian branch, which the same account says is documented in exhibits at the Nuremberg trials. In the words of that account, the Rothschilds were victims of the Holocaust, not its orchestrators. By 1945, the dynasty as a coherent cross-border banking force had come to an end. Descendants today run separate businesses, among them Rothschild & Co in Paris, RIT Capital in London and various family offices, none of them near the dynasty's nineteenth-century scale.

Two branches, two positions. The family's later role in the history of Palestine runs through particular people, and the record keeps them distinct. The Paris branch produced Edmond de Rothschild (1845–1934), who from the 1880s used a large personal fortune to fund agricultural settlements of the First Aliyah in Ottoman Palestine, among them Rishon LeZion, Zichron Yaakov and Rosh Pina, while personally opposing Theodor Herzl and the political Zionist Congress. [cite:schama_two_rothschilds] His colonies, his administrators and his refusal of Herzl are told in [scene:bc_s04|the section on Edmond's colonies]. The London branch later produced Walter Rothschild (1868–1937), a zoologist who founded a natural-history museum at Tring and in 1917 was chosen as the addressee of the Balfour Declaration. He did not draft it: the letter was drafted by Weizmann, Sokolow and Sykes and signed by Balfour, the Foreign Secretary, as [scene:s06|the section on the Declaration] sets out. [cite:schama_two_rothschilds] One Rothschild funded settlements and opposed Herzl; another received a letter he had not written. Antisemitic conspiracy writing merges the two into a single "Rothschilds." The historical record keeps them apart.

The states. Around the family stood the powers whose decisions would matter far more. Britain, financed at scale by Nathan's house through the nineteenth century, would issue the Balfour Declaration in November 1917. It did so as an imperial and strategic move that served three British interests at once: securing the approaches to Suez, keeping Russia in the First World War, and bringing America into it. [cite:cohen_palestine_great_powers] A century separates the bond-financing from the Declaration, and the record does not show that the first caused the second; the decision of 1917 had its own, later, strategic logic.

France, post-revolutionary and then Napoleonic, was financed at scale by James Mayer's Paris house. It was also, in 1791, the first European state to grant Jews full legal citizenship, the model of nineteenth-century emancipation that Zionism would later argue had failed. In 1894 the same France produced the Dreyfus Affair, the false conviction of a Jewish army captain, Alfred Dreyfus, which persuaded the Viennese journalist Theodor Herzl that legal emancipation had not made Jews safe even in the most enlightened states of Europe. [cite:herzl_diaries] These are two separate facts about one country, decades apart. The record does not show any causal link between the Paris bank and the Dreyfus Affair, which had its own military and political causes, or that French antisemitism in 1894 was a reaction to Jewish banking. The affair is told in its own section later in this volume.

Imperial Russia was home to the largest Jewish population in Europe. The Pale of Settlement, created by Catherine the Great in 1791, confined most Russian Jews to the empire's western provinces. After Tsar Alexander II was assassinated in March 1881, waves of pogroms swept Ukraine and southern Russia. About two million Jewish emigrants left the Russian Empire between 1881 and 1914, and the great majority went to America, not Palestine. [cite:cohen_palestine_great_powers] Some did go to Palestine, and their settlements are part of this volume's story. But the main current of the emigration ran elsewhere, which complicates any account that treats the flight from the pogroms and Zionist settlement as one movement.

Palestine itself was Ottoman, as it had been since 1516 and would remain until 1917, more than four centuries. Under the millet system, Christian, Muslim and Jewish communities had substantial internal self-government. [cite:wiki_ottoman_palestine] The source behind this point adds that the Ottoman parliamentary reforms of 1876 brought Palestinian representation, and that Palestinian delegates sat in the Ottoman parliament. The parliamentary record gathered for a later section supports one such delegate: Yusuf Diya al-Khalidi, elected in 1877 as the sole deputy for Jerusalem. [cite:rs_rd0_khalidi_palquest] The plural is not supported beyond that. Even on the narrowest reading, a land under four centuries of continuous rule, with a working system of communal self-government, was not a blank space. How that order worked in Palestine from 1799 is told in [scene:rd0_ottoman|the section on Ottoman Palestine].

The last of the powers was the papacy. The Papal States were lost in 1870, and Vatican City became a sovereign state only in 1929, as an earlier section showed. The papacy's part in the later story of Israel arrives through absence: the Vatican did not recognize the State of Israel from its founding in 1948 until 30 December 1993, when the Vatican-Israel Fundamental Agreement was signed. [cite:jcpa_vatican_israel_recognition] As of 2026 that recognition remains in force, and it has survived public criticism from Rome, including the description of Gaza as an "ongoing massacre" by the Vatican Secretary of State, Cardinal Parolin, reported by the Times of Israel in October 2025. [cite:jcpa_vatican_israel_recognition] Why the Vatican waited 45 years is a question for later volumes.

A reading, not a finding. The account of the dynasty this section draws on goes on to offer an interpretation of what the family's history means for Palestine, for the region and for today. It is set apart here because it is a reading of the record, not part of the record. On that reading, the dynasty's direct role in Palestine ran through one branch only: one man, Edmond, in Paris, over four decades, 1882 to 1934. The Frankfurt house had no role in Palestine, and the London house had no part in funding it; Walter received a letter. The settlements Edmond paid for became part of the physical base of the Jewish community before the state, but the political order of the modern Middle East, from the Mandate to partition and the wars that followed, was made by states, not banking families. Ferguson, on this account, is explicit that the Rothschilds took part in some financial episodes and did not design the geopolitical order. To read the region as "Rothschild designed" is, on this reading, a category error that hides the documented actors: the British and American empires, the Israeli state, Palestinian resistance, the Arab states and Iran, intelligence services and arms makers.

The same account draws a lesson for the present. Dynastic finance still exists: it cites a 2024 Deloitte report counting about 15,000 family offices in the United States managing more than $6 trillion. Some of the families behind them are of Jewish origin and most are not. The "Rothschild" of modern conspiracy content, it argues, is an imagined dynasty that ceased to exist in coherent form between 1901 and 1945, not the businesses that carry the name today. The name survives in that content because it performs a substitution: it replaces an impersonal, documented pattern, concentrated capital passed down the generations, with a single Jewish family name. That turns a critique of a class into an ethnic one, and it lets the real pattern go unexamined. That is an interpretation, and it is labeled as one here.

What is documented, and what is refused. Stripped to the record, the history is this. Mayer Amschel Rothschild founded a banking family in Frankfurt in the late eighteenth century. His five sons went to five capitals. The family reached its peak in mid-nineteenth-century Europe as one of the largest private holders of capital in the world. The Frankfurt house closed in 1901, the Austrian branch's property was seized by the Nazis in 1938, and by 1945 the dynasty as a cross-border force was gone. Two framings are refused. The first holds that "the Rothschilds," as one undifferentiated collective, run world finance, started the world wars or engineered Israel. That claim is antisemitic in structure, because it puts a Jewish family name in place of the documented forces at work, and it is false on the evidence, since the family's businesses today are not large enough to do what the legend assigns them. The second is the defensive reply that the Rothschilds were "just another banking family" with nothing to examine. That evades a dynastic pattern that did shape European capital in the nineteenth century. The discipline is to name the family branch by branch and date each activity precisely. The account used here points readers to Ferguson's two-volume The House of Rothschild (1998–1999), to the Rothschild Archive, and to Simon Schama's Two Rothschilds and the Land of Israel (1978) for the distinction between the branches. [cite:schama_two_rothschilds]

Some of this remains unsettled. The founding dates of the London and Paris houses differ across the sources, as noted above. The Waterloo story is told two ways, and the account that credits Nathan with a real information advantage and the account that calls the whole thing a legend cannot both be fully right. Several figures in this section, among them the capital estimate for 1875, the four causes of decline and the Nuremberg exhibits, reach this book through a single account summarizing Ferguson and the archive rather than through those works directly, and they are given here as that account gives them. What is not in doubt is the shape of the board in 1815: a Frankfurt family lending across five capitals, a British state borrowing on a scale the Bank of England had made possible, a France that had emancipated its Jews, a Russia that confined them, an Ottoman Empire that ruled Palestine, and a papacy that still held its own territory.
