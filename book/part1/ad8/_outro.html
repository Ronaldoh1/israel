Health coverage in the United States is tied to the job by design: wage controls from 1942 exempted benefits, and a 1943 ruling and the 1954 tax code made employer coverage untaxed, a break worth $299 billion in 2022. [cite:ad8_thomasson2017] [cite:ad8_tpc_esi] The country spent $15,474 a person in 2024, nearly twice the OECD average share of its economy, yet its life expectancy was two years below the OECD average. [cite:us16_cms_nhe2024] [cite:ad8_cmwf_global26] A family plan at work cost $28,303 in 2026, and 23 per cent of working-age adults were insured all year but underinsured; the uninsured rate, 7.9 per cent in 2025, was less than half its 2009 level, but the 2025 law is expected by the CBO to add about 10 million uninsured by 2034, with Medicaid work requirements from 1 January 2027. [cite:ad8_kff_ehbs2026] [cite:ad8_cmwf_underins24] [cite:us16_census_2025] [cite:us16_kff_obbba] Marketplace insurers denied 19 per cent of in-network claims in 2024 and fewer than 1 per cent were appealed; the three largest PBMs settled the FTC's insulin case in 2026 without admitting wrongdoing, and a 2026 law rewrote how they are paid.
