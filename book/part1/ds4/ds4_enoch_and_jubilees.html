Enoch and Jubilees in the Caves

Before 1947 the Book of Enoch was known as a whole only in Ge'ez, the language of the Ethiopian church, with parts in Greek and quotations in the early Christian writers. [cite:ds_bas_enoch] [cite:ds_loc_enoch] Cave 4 at Qumran "finally provided parts of the Aramaic original". [cite:ds_loc_enoch] It held fragments of eleven Aramaic manuscripts of 1 Enoch, covering "perhaps one fifth of the Ethiopic text". [cite:ds_bas_enoch] One of them, known as 4Q201, was copied around 200 to 150 before Christ. [cite:ds_loc_enoch] The Polish scholar J. T. Milik published the Aramaic fragments in 1976. [cite:ds_loc_enoch]

Two findings stand out. The first is what is missing. The Book of Parables, chapters 37 to 71 of the Ethiopian Enoch, the section about a heavenly Son of Man, is absent from Qumran. [cite:ds_bas_enoch] The second is what is extra. Nine further Aramaic manuscripts contain a work called the Book of Giants, which is not in 1 Enoch at all and tells more of the story of the giants born to the Watchers; with it, the caves held twenty Aramaic manuscripts of the Enoch tradition. [cite:ds_bas_enoch] The Enoch the Ethiopian church kept is one edition of a larger tradition that the caves show was once broader.

Jubilees fared even better. The caves held fourteen or fifteen copies, in Hebrew. [cite:ds_vq_nature] Jubilees insists on a solar year of 364 days, and so do other texts in the caves: the Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice, a liturgy for the first thirteen Sabbaths of the year, presume a solar calendar, and the community's commentary on Habakkuk implies that its enemy, the Wicked Priest, kept a different one. [cite:ds_vq_nature] [cite:ds_enc_dss] The calendar that Part Nine of The Ethiopian Bible read as an image of heaven's order was, at Qumran, a line that divided a community from the priests in Jerusalem.

The caves and the Ethiopian church kept the same books by opposite means. At Qumran they survived because they were hidden, in jars in caves in a desert, and forgotten for nearly two thousand years. In Ethiopia they survived because they were never hidden at all, but read aloud in church in an unbroken tradition. Both were outside the reach of the councils and lists that set them aside elsewhere. [cite:ds_bas_enoch] [cite:vt27_hessayon]

The fragments also answer a question that critics of the Ethiopian canon used to raise: whether Enoch was a late invention. The Aramaic manuscripts show the book in Jewish hands two centuries before Christ, and the collection as a whole was composed, according to the Biblical Archaeology Society, "between about 350 B.C.E. and the turn of the era." [cite:ds_bas_enoch] What the Ethiopian church called scripture was, in the caves, among the most copied books of a Jewish community before Christianity began.
