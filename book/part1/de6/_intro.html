This part sets out what Curtis Yarvin actually proposes and the passages for which he is most often condemned. It begins with the program in his own words: formalism, the idea that real power should be made official property; the Cathedral, his name for the universities, press and civil service he believes really govern; neocameralism, a state run as a company that owns its country, under a chief executive answerable to a board; Patchwork, a world of small sovereign corporations that residents may only leave; and RAGE, his 2012 proposal to "Retire All Government Employees." It then reads five hard posts, on white nationalism, on the surplus residents of a company-run city, on biodiesel and virtual-reality confinement, on slavery and on the Breivik massacre, each dated, quoted briefly and set beside his own later account. The last section grades ten of his public claims about himself against his texts and the record, using the four flags the book applies to everyone.
