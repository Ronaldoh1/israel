# The Names: Biographies Rebuilt from the Record
### A new part for *The Epstein Architecture* (Part 5) · plan, roster and three sample entries · 31 October 2026

Ron's request: *rebuild the biographies of everyone in the files from the documented record, because the news versions aren't accurate. Give it its own section. Call it "The Names".*

**What this part is.** A biographical dictionary of every person in the book, written from documents rather than headlines. Each entry answers four questions:
1. Who was this person before and apart from Epstein?
2. What, exactly, does the record show between them, dated?
3. What has the person said, and does it match the documents?
4. What did the news get wrong, in either direction?

**Where it sits.** It becomes **Part 5**, straight after *Who Stayed* (Part 4). Part 4 stays the analysis: tiers, clusters, loops. Part 5 is the reference that readers return to. Every name anywhere in the book links to its entry here.

---

## On free speech, and why the method is the protection
Ron's point is right: reporting on public people is protected speech. The protection has conditions, though, and the method of this part is built around them. *(This is a summary of how U.S. law generally works, not legal advice. A media lawyer should review the finished part.)*
- **Truth is a complete defence.** Every statement of fact in an entry is tied to a document. That is what makes the part safe to publish.
- **Public figures** (officials, billionaires, celebrities) must prove a false statement was made with "actual malice", meaning knowledge that it was false or reckless disregard for the truth (*New York Times v. Sullivan*, 1964). Documented sourcing and a response request are the opposite of reckless disregard.
- **Private people** (staff, pilots, the four lineup men, private women in emails) get much stronger protection. So the part names them only where they are already on the public record in that role, and says no more than the record does.
- **Opinion is protected; implied false facts are not.** The entries use plain verbs ("emailed", "flew", "paid") and never imply an act the record doesn't show.
- **A response request is sent to every living person** before publication, and logged with the date (Appendix G). Their answer, or their silence, goes in the entry.

## The template (every entry, the same order)

**Name.** Dates. Role at the time of contact → role today.

1. **Before and apart from Epstein.** Career, wealth, public offices, from primary records (filings, official biographies, court records). Two to five paragraphs. This is what the news usually skips, and it is what tells the reader why it mattered that this person stayed close.
2. **The record, dated.** A table, one row per documented contact:

   | Date | What | Kind (email, flight, visit, payment, photo, meeting, tip) | Document (EFTA number, court record, filing) | Tier |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
3. **What they said vs. what the files show.** Their statements, dated, with the venue and whether they were **sworn**, set beside the conflicting or confirming documents.
4. **Allegations.** Only if the person is in `ep_ALLEGATIONS_REGISTER`. Copied at the same tier, with their response and the outcome. If they are not in it: *"No survivor has accused [name] on the record."*
5. **Consequences.** Charged, arrested, resigned, sued, sanctioned, or none. Dated.
6. **What the news got wrong.** Corrections in **both directions**: claims that overstated the record, and things the record shows that the coverage missed.
7. **What is not established.** The open questions, stated plainly.
8. **Response.** The date it was requested, and the reply.
9. **Sources.**

**Length by depth:**
- **A, full entry:** 2,000–3,500 words. People with power, money or a central role.
- **B, standard entry:** 700–1,500 words.
- **C, short entry:** 150–400 words. Staff, investigators and witnesses: only what the record shows of their role.
