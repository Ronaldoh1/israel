Part Twenty-One set every measure side by side and added no total. This part adds one, in the open. It first reproduces the scorecard of the simulation's chapter on the two systems, which scored eleven citizen services at 3.70 of 5 for Israel and 2.49 for the United States, and shows what that scorecard could and could not support. It then follows three families, lower-income, middle and upper-middle, through each country's household survey, grades both systems again on 43 measures in twelve areas, from health and housing to a family-budget area built on the Sophia platform's American Dream module, liberty, the economy and the gaps inside each society, against goalposts printed beside every row, and scores Israel twice: for its citizens, and for everyone it governs. Its last section adds the scores up, tests the totals under other choices, sets the household score beside what people who have lived in both countries describe, answers the questions readers ask most often, from who carries more debt and who works longer to which state is the more oppressive, and gives the volume's final assessment.
