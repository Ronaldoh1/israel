Israel did not found Hamas, but it did register and tolerate the network Hamas grew out of. In 1979 Israeli authorities officially registered Mujama al-Islamiya, the Islamist charity led by Sheikh Ahmed Yassin, as an association. Avner Cohen, who handled religious affairs in Gaza, later said "Hamas, to my great regret, is Israel's creation," and David Hacham, an adviser to seven defense ministers, called the 1979 recognition Israel's "original sin." [cite:portside_higgins_hamas]

Hamas itself was founded by Yassin and six co-founders in December 1987, at the start of the first intifada, twenty years into the occupation of Gaza. [cite:npr_hamashistory] Its ideology draws on the Muslim Brotherhood, founded in Egypt in 1928, before Israel existed. Its 1988 charter called for Israel's destruction and included antisemitic content, and its later choices, from suicide bombings during the Oslo years to October 7, were its own. [cite:hamas_charter1988] Israel helped make room for Hamas; it did not write its program or make its decisions.
