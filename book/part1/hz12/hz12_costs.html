The American Bill, 1983–2026

The United States has paid for the wars in this book in two different ways, and they need to be kept apart. Some Americans were killed by the groups themselves: by the bombers of Beirut in 1983, by hijackers and kidnappers in Lebanon in the 1980s, and by an Iraqi militia's drone in 2024. Other costs came from decisions taken in Washington: to send warships, to shoot down missiles aimed at Israel, to bomb Yemen, to pay for Israel's air defences. This section sets out both, with the stated reasons of each government and group beside them.

Beirut, 1983–1985

American troops were in Beirut because of the Israeli invasion of 1982. A Multinational Force of American, French and Italian troops arrived on 24 August 1982 to oversee the PLO's evacuation; the Marines left on 10 September and were sent back on 29 September, after the massacre at Sabra and Shatila. [cite:hz2_an_1983] [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks] Part Two follows how the force came to be seen as a party to Lebanon's war. [scene:hz2_1983|The founding years]

On 18 April 1983 a suicide truck bomb destroyed the American embassy in West Beirut, killing 63 people, 17 of them Americans and 8 of those CIA officers. The Islamic Jihad Organization, which the CIA says later became part of Hezbollah, claimed it. [cite:hz12_cia_embassy] On 23 October 1983 a second truck bomb destroyed the Marine barracks at Beirut airport, killing 241 American servicemen; a near-simultaneous attack on the French contingent killed 58 French soldiers. [cite:hz12_wiki_barracks]

Responsibility was settled in American courts, not by any admission. In Peterson v. Islamic Republic of Iran (30 May 2003), Judge Royce Lamberth found that Hezbollah carried out the barracks attack, that it had been "formed under the auspices of the government of Iran", and that "it is beyond question that Hezbollah and its agents received massive material and technical support from the Iranian government." He found that Iran and its intelligence ministry "played crucial and necessary roles in planning and ordering" the bombing. [cite:hz12_dcd_anderson] Iran was served but did not answer the families' suit, and the court entered damages of about $2.66 billion in 2007; in 2016 the Supreme Court allowed frozen Iranian central-bank assets to be used to pay. Hezbollah officially denied any involvement, and Hezbollah, Iran and Syria have continued to deny it. [cite:hz12_wiki_barracks] President Reagan ordered the Marines to begin withdrawing on 7 February 1984; the last left Beirut airport on 26 February. [cite:hz12_wiki_barracks]

The kidnappings followed. William Buckley, the CIA's Beirut station chief, was seized on 16 March 1984; Islamic Jihad announced on 4 October 1985 that it had executed him, while the National Security Council concluded he had probably died in captivity on 3 June 1985. His remains were found beside a road near Beirut airport in December 1991. [cite:hz12_wiki_buckley] On 14 June 1985 TWA Flight 847, flying from Athens to Rome, was hijacked; the next day the hijackers killed Robert Stethem, a 23-year-old US Navy diver, and threw his body onto the tarmac in Beirut. The United States indicted Imad Mughniyeh, Hassan Izz-al-Din and Ali Atwa, men it linked to Hezbollah, and offered $5 million for information on them; Hezbollah denied any link. [cite:hz12_nbc_twa847] The effort to free the hostages held in Lebanon fed directly into the Reagan administration's secret arms sales to Iran. [cite:hz12_wiki_buckley]

Iraq, Jordan and the Red Sea, 2023–2025

After 7 October 2023 the United States sent the USS Gerald R. Ford carrier strike group to the eastern Mediterranean, saying the aim was to deter a wider war. [cite:ow7_ap_ford2023] Iran-aligned militias in Iraq and Syria then attacked American bases repeatedly: Military Times, tracking incidents announced by Central Command, counted at least 217 involving US forces between October 2023 and the autumn of 2024. [cite:hz12_cow_2024] On 28 January 2024 a drone struck Tower 22, a small US outpost in north-eastern Jordan, killing Sergeant William Jerome Rivers, Specialist Kennedy Ladon Sanders and Specialist Breonna Alexsondria Moffett; President Biden blamed Iran-backed militia groups. [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] Washington named Kata'ib Hezbollah, part of the umbrella calling itself the Islamic Resistance in Iraq; Kata'ib Hezbollah said it was halting its operations, and Iran denied ordering the attacks and said the factions acted on their own. [cite:hz10_at_farida] Part Ten sets out the Iraqi militias' history. [scene:hz10_iraq|The Iraqi militias]

The same month two American service members were lost at sea during an operation that seized Iranian-made missiles from a vessel bound for Yemen. [cite:hz12_cow_2024] In the Red Sea, Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin announced Operation Prosperity Guardian on 18 December 2023, and American and British aircraft began striking Houthi targets on 12 January 2024; Biden said he would "not hesitate" to order further measures. [cite:hz10_dn_opg] [cite:hz10_mm_biden2024] By June 2024 the Navy had fired about $1 billion of munitions there. [cite:hz12_cow_2024] Operation Rough Rider, the Trump administration's campaign from 15 March 2025, cost more than $1 billion in its first weeks by NBC's reporting, used about 2,000 bombs and missiles and lost two fighter jets at sea; it ended on 6 May 2025 in an Omani-brokered deal under which the Houthis stopped attacking American ships, a deal the State Department said did not cover Israel. [cite:hz10_nbc_rrcost] [cite:hz10_aje_truce] The Houthis' stated position throughout was that their attacks supported Gaza. [cite:hz10_aje_truce] The campaign itself is told in Part Ten. [scene:hz10_redsea|The Red Sea war]

Defending Israel

From April 2024 the costs changed in kind. When Iran fired more than 300 drones and missiles at Israel on 13–14 April 2024, American ships and aircraft destroyed more than 80 drones and at least six ballistic missiles; on 1 October 2024 two American destroyers fired about a dozen interceptors; on 13 October the United States sent a THAAD battery, which needs about 95 soldiers, to Israel. [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] [cite:ow7_usni_oct2024] [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024] [cite:ow7_ap_thaad2024] In the twelve-day war of June 2025 American batteries fired about 150 THAAD interceptors, roughly a quarter of the stock, which the Costs of War project valued at $1.8 billion to $1.95 billion. [cite:ow7_cnn_thaad2025] [cite:hz12_cow_2025] The One-Way Mirror sets these operations out in full. [scene:ow7_iran|Defending Israel, fighting Iran] The Pentagon's stated purpose each time was to protect Israel and American personnel from Iranian attack. [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] [cite:ow7_dod_thaad2024]

The money

Congress paid for much of this in the Israel Security Supplemental Appropriations Act, which the House passed 366–58 on 20 April 2024 and the Senate 79–18 on 23 April; it became law on 24 April as Public Law 118-50. [cite:ow1_clerk_roll152] [cite:ow1_lobby_hr815] The Costs of War project at Brown University counted $14.1 billion in it for Israel's military, including $4 billion for Iron Dome and David's Sling, $1.2 billion for the Iron Beam laser, $3.5 billion in Foreign Military Financing and $4.4 billion to replenish American stocks sent to Israel, and a further $2.4 billion for American operations, force protection and deterrence in Central Command's region. [cite:hz12_cow_2024] Its later accounting put the cost of American military operations in the wider Middle East from October 2023 to September 2025 at $9.65 billion to $12.07 billion, excluding aid to Israel, and at $31.35 billion to $33.77 billion including the $21.7 billion of military aid. [cite:hz12_cow_2025] [cite:hm_cow_hartung] In 2025 Central Command's region took 41 per cent of the Navy's carrier steaming days, against 8 per cent the year before. [cite:hz12_cow_2025] The war with Iran that began in 2026, described in the next section, cost a further $43.6 billion by 3 September 2026 on Central Command's estimate. [cite:ow7_ap_cost2026]

What the record shows

The two kinds of cost point in different directions for the thesis. The deaths of 1983–85 and of Tower 22 were caused by the groups themselves, and the court findings and the record of claims and denials are as set out above; they are the strongest evidence that these groups have been a real danger to Americans, not only a pretext. But they also came where American forces had been placed inside a war: in Beirut after the 1982 invasion, and in Iraq, Syria and Jordan during the Gaza war, when the militias said they were acting over Gaza. [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks] [cite:hz10_at_farida] The larger costs, the interceptors, the carriers, the supplementals, were choices made in Washington for reasons Washington stated: deterrence, freedom of navigation, the defence of an ally and of American troops. [cite:ow7_ap_ford2023] [cite:hz10_mm_biden2024] [cite:ow7_dod_apr2024] Whether those reasons were sufficient, or whether the groups' existence was what made the choices easy to justify, is the argument Part Eleven set out; this book's finding is the bill itself: most of the American deaths before 2026 were caused by attack, but most of the money was spent by decision.
