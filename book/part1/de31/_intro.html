Book Six turns from the tools of the state to the limits on it. A brake, here, is a veto point: a commissioner who cannot be fired for disagreeing, an inspector general who reports to Congress, a sum Congress has ordered spent, a court that can halt an order, a law that keeps tax or health records apart. This part traces five patterns in the orders, rulings and notices of 2025 and 2026 that weakened those brakes, sets each beside the times earlier presidents of both parties used the same powers, follows who gained and who paid, and records which brakes held when the courts, Congress and the states pressed on them, as of October 2026.
