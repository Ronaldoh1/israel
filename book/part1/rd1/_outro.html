By the end of this stage, the movement launched at Basel had become a working structure. The choice of territory was settled. The Uganda offer was rejected in 1905, and Herzl had explained as early as 1896 why Palestine carried a weight Argentina could not. In Jaffa, the Palestine Office, opened in 1908, coordinated land purchase and settlement with the Jewish National Fund. The Palestine Land Development Company had handled roughly 50,000 dunams by the outbreak of war. Named agents closed the deals.

Across the Atlantic, two separate bases of support had taken shape in the United States. One was Christian, rooted in the Scofield Reference Bible of 1909, and would grow into a large Christian Zionist constituency. The other was Jewish, built through Hadassah, Louis Brandeis and the Zionist Organization of America. By 1918 it had more than 147,000 members and a direct line to President Wilson. During the war, NILI's intelligence and the Jewish battalions in British uniform gave the movement standing in London just as the cabinet weighed the Balfour Declaration.

Opposition was in place too. From 1911, Filastin was reporting Zionist land purchases to every village in the Jaffa district. And one popular claim, that the Federal Reserve's founding was tied to Zionist finance, finds no support in the record.
