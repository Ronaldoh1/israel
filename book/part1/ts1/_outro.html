Part One established that Christian Zionism is a belief carried by named organisations, not a church, and that it is older and wider than the end-times system most often tied to it: Britannica defines it by prophecy, while Goldman traces it from the Puritans through covenant, prophecy and cultural affinity. [cite:ts1_brit_cz] [cite:ts1_penn_goldman] Its premise is held by large shares of white evangelical respondents (82 per cent in Pew's 2013 survey said God gave the land to the Jewish people), but the premise does not fix a policy: in LifeWay's 2017 poll only 23 per cent backed a Palestinian state and 46 per cent were unsure. [cite:ts1_pew_2013] [cite:gi11_ct_lifeway] The volume's working term, the theology shield, joins Book Three's sense (a promise substituted for an argument) and Book Four's (criticism recast as religious hostility), and is to be tested, not assumed; the case Book Four built on, the removal of a commissioner in February 2026, carries both the chair's stated reason and the commissioner's own remarks. [cite:ts1_hill_boller] [cite:ts1_ji_boller] Genesis 12:3 is read in two ways: Scofield's 1917 note, CUFI and Senator Cruz apply the blessing clause to "the Jew" and later to the state, while Paul's reading in Galatians, the Catholic Church, the Jerusalem church heads of 2006, a Church of Scotland report of 2013 and the Presbyterian Church (USA) in 2024 locate its fulfilment in Christ and decline a religious warrant for the state.
