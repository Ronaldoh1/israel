Ehud Barak

Born 1942, Kibbutz Mishmar Hasharon. At the time of contact: former chief of staff and prime minister of Israel, later defence minister, then a private investor in security technology. Today: no office; not under investigation, as far as the record shows.

Before Epstein

Ehud Barak's public authority came from more than three decades in the army, ending at its head. He joined the army in 1959 and commanded an elite unit; in April 1983 he became head of military intelligence, and in April 1991 the fourteenth chief of the general staff. He entered politics in 1995 as interior and then foreign minister, became Labour's leader in 1996, and was elected prime minister on 17 May 1999, taking office in July as both prime minister and defence minister. [cite:ep5_jvl_barak]

His premiership was short and consequential. Talks with Syria reached a draft at Shepherdstown in January 2000 and collapsed in March. [cite:w_isr_syr_rel] Israeli forces withdrew from southern Lebanon that year; in 2006 Barak said of the occupation that "it was our presence there that created Hezbollah". [cite:wiki_hezbollah_mirror] He led Israel's side at Camp David in July 2000, a summit that has three documented accounts of what was offered and why it failed. [cite:modeldiplomat_camp_david] He resigned in December 2000 and lost the February 2001 election to Ariel Sharon. [cite:ep5_jvl_barak]

He returned as Labour leader and defence minister in June 2007, served on as defence minister under Benjamin Netanyahu from 2009, broke away to form his own party in 2011 and left politics after the January 2013 election. [cite:ep5_jvl_barak] As defence minister he oversaw the 2012 Gaza operation that began with the killing of the Hamas commander Ahmed Jabari; the Israeli mediator Gershon Baskin has documented that Jabari had received a draft long-term ceasefire hours before the strike. [cite:war2012_baskin] After leaving office he became a private investor in security technology, including as chairman of Carbyne, an emergency-call data company. [cite:dropsite_epstein_cote_divoire]

The record with Epstein

The released files show Barak and his wife, Nili, in frequent contact with Epstein for years after the 2008 plea deal. A 2013 email from Epstein's assistant concerns a dinner with the Baraks, several business people and Woody Allen; the files contain plans for a 2017 stay at Epstein's New York residence; in 2018 Epstein connected Barak with Steve Bannon, who wanted a larger role in Israeli politics; in June 2019 Nili Barak emailed Epstein that their flight to New York had been delayed by a week, and about a week before Epstein's arrest that July an exchange referred to Epstein "dealing with Ehud in Israel". Barak visited the Manhattan house several times and, with his wife and security guards, made one three-hour visit to the island, where he says they saw only Epstein and maintenance staff. [cite:ep5_euronews_barak2026]

The business record comes from two sources: Barak's own emails, leaked from his account, and documents released by the House Oversight Committee. From 2012 Epstein and Barak acted as intermediaries between Israeli officials and the government of Côte d'Ivoire; Epstein introduced Barak to the president's chief of staff in September 2013, and Barak proposed surveillance and monitoring centres for Abidjan, to be built by the French-Israeli MF Group to a plan prepared by two former Israeli generals, Aharon Ze'evi-Farkash and Amnon Unger; a formal Israel–Côte d'Ivoire security agreement followed in 2014. The firms marketed such systems as "field-proven", a phrase the reporting traces to their use against Palestinians under occupation. In a 2014 email about unrest in Syria, Libya and Somalia, Epstein wrote to Barak, "isn't this perfect for you"; Barak replied, "You're right in a way. But not simple to transform it into a cash flow." [cite:dropsite_epstein_cote_divoire] [cite:aljazeera_epstein_ivory_coast_2026] Epstein's money went into Carbyne through Barak's investment partnership [cite:aljazeera_epstein_ivory_coast_2026], and in 2018 Peter Thiel's Founders Fund led a funding round for the same company. [cite:bylinetimes_thiel_epstein_valar]

A third thread predates both. Reporting in 2019 found that Barak had received about $2 million in grants from the Wexner Foundation for unspecified "research" over the previous decade, while Epstein was a trustee of the foundation. [cite:ep5_euronews_barak2026]

What he said, and what the files show

In 2019 Barak said Epstein "didn't support me or pay me". [cite:ep5_euronews_barak2026] That is literally consistent with the files, since the Wexner grants came from a foundation rather than from Epstein, but it leaves them out. In an interview with Israel's Channel 12 in February 2026, he said he regretted ever knowing Epstein, apologised to those who "feel deeply uncomfortable", said he had never seen or taken part in anything inappropriate, that he had known of the earlier case but assumed Epstein had paid his debt to society, that he cut off relations in 2019, and that "nothing will be discovered, because there is nothing". [cite:ep5_euronews_barak2026] The files show the contact continuing into the weeks before Epstein's arrest, which fits "cut off in 2019" only if the break came afterwards. None of these statements was made under oath.

An allegation, kept apart

Virginia Giuffre's posthumous memoir says that a "well-known prime minister" assaulted her on Epstein's island in 2002, when she was eighteen. The memoir does not name him; several outlets have identified the reference as Barak. He denies it. [cite:aljazeera_barak_nigeria_spytech_2026] It is an allegation, published and denied and never tested in court, and the book keeps it separate from the business record, never using one to support the other.

What the news got wrong

The commonest error is to make Barak the proof that Epstein worked for Israeli intelligence. The documents show a private business channel run after Barak left office, through private firms; even the reporters who broke the Côte d'Ivoire story describe it as "intelligence-adjacent". [cite:dropsite_epstein_cote_divoire] Prime Minister Netanyahu, Barak's long-time rival, has argued the opposite, that Barak's ties prove Epstein did not work for Israel. [cite:ep5_toi_netanyahu_barak] Both are political arguments, not evidence. And both slide easily from a named man to a country, and from a country to a people, which is the step this book never takes.

What the coverage missed was the documented business: a convicted sex offender and a former prime minister brokering surveillance systems to an African government, sold as proven in the field. That was in the record all along, and drew far less attention than the speculation.

What remains unestablished: when and how the two men first met; how much of Epstein's money went into Barak-linked ventures; what the Wexner Foundation grants paid for; and whether any Israeli state body knew of the brokering.
