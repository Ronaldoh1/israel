Arms and Fighters

Israel. The International Institute for Strategic Studies counts 169,500 active personnel in Israel's forces and 465,000 reservists; in January 2024, 295,000 reservists had been called up. Israel's air force has 316 combat aircraft, 175 of them able to fly more than 1,000 kilometres; its navy has five submarines. The Stockholm International Peace Research Institute estimates that Israel holds about 90 nuclear warheads. [cite:hm_almon_idf]

Hamas. Israel's Institute for National Security Studies estimated that at the start of the war Hamas had 18,000 to 30,000 rockets and inaccurate missiles, most of them short-range; 20,000 to 25,000 fighters organized in battalions; 500 to 700 kilometres of tunnels; anti-tank missiles; and an unknown number of drones. Unlike Israel, it has no air force and no navy. [cite:hm_inss_arsenal] [cite:hm_almon_idf]

What the Gap Does Not Mean

Asymmetry of resources is not the same as an inability to do harm. With its tunnels, rockets and fighters, Hamas carried out the attack of 7 October 2023, which killed about 1,200 people, most of them civilians, and took 251 hostages; Part Seven sets it out. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7] [scene:hm7_attack|October 7] Nor does the asymmetry settle the questions of law: the laws of war bind both sides regardless of strength. What the numbers do show is the scale of the two forces that met in Gaza after 7 October: a state ranked twelfth in the world for military spending in 2024, supplied by the United States and Germany, against an armed movement with no air force, ruling a territory whose whole economy was smaller than the American aid its enemy received each year. Part Nine follows what the war did to it.
