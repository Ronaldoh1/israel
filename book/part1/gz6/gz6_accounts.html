The Accounts of the Day

Several accounts of 7 October now exist, given by the attackers, by UN and human rights investigators and by Israel's own army and press. They agree on the scale of the killing and on the hostages; they disagree, or remain incomplete, on intent, on sexual violence and on how some of the Israeli dead were killed. This section sets them side by side.

Hamas's account. In January 2024 Hamas published a sixteen-page document titled "Our Narrative." It called the attack, which it named Operation Al-Aqsa Flood, "a necessary step and a normal response to confront all Israeli conspiracies against the Palestinian people," and cited the Palestinians held in Israeli prisons, settlement construction and what it called the "Judaization" of the West Bank and Jerusalem. It said its fighters had targeted only soldiers and people carrying weapons, and that "maybe some faults happened" because of "the rapid collapse of the Israeli security and military system." It also said that many Israelis "were killed by the Israeli army and police due to their confusion." [cite:gz_aj_narrative] Human Rights Watch's investigation found that the killing of civilians was a central aim of the attack, not a fault. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7]

Sexual violence. A UN team led by Pramila Patten, the Secretary-General's special representative on sexual violence in conflict, visited Israel in early 2024 and reported on 4 March that there were "reasonable grounds to believe" that sexual violence, including rape and gang rape, had occurred during the attack, among other places on Road 232 by the festival and at Kibbutz Re'im, and "clear and convincing information" that some hostages in Gaza had been subjected to it. It also found at least two widely repeated allegations from Be'eri to be "unfounded", said it had been unable to meet any victims of sexual violence, despite efforts to encourage them to come forward, and said that a "fully-fledged investigation" would be needed to establish the scale. Hamas has denied the allegations. [cite:gz_aj_patten]

The Hannibal reports. In July 2024 the Israeli newspaper Haaretz reported that army commands had, on 7 October, invoked the "Hannibal" procedure, a directive meant to prevent soldiers being taken captive even at risk to their lives, which had been officially repealed in 2016. It reported an order of "Hannibal at Erez" with an attack drone, similar orders at the Re'im base and the Nahal Oz post, and an instruction that "not a single vehicle could return to Gaza." A Southern Command source told it that no vehicle known to carry hostages was attacked, but that "you couldn't really know if there were any such people in a vehicle." [cite:gz_toi_hannibal] Israeli inquiries have found individual cases: an air force probe found that Efrat Katz of Nir Oz was likely killed by helicopter fire, and the army's Be'eri probe found that one hostage was highly likely killed by tank shrapnel. The army has said friendly-fire deaths that day were "not many" but has published no total, and no published inquiry has established an overall number. [cite:gz_toi_hannibal] [cite:gz_idf_beeri_probe] [cite:gz_pbs_idfprobe]

The first response. On 9 October Israel's defence minister, Yoav Gallant, announced: "We are putting a complete siege on Gaza … No electricity, no food, no water, no gas – it's all closed." [cite:gz_aj_siege] The war that followed is the subject of Part Seven.
