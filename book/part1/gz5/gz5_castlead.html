Cast Lead, 2008–2009

The lull and its end. On 19 June 2008 a six-month truce between Israel and Hamas, brokered by Egypt, took effect. On 4 November Israeli forces crossed into Gaza near Deir al-Balah to destroy a tunnel, which Israel described as a preemptive strike against a plan to abduct its soldiers; Hamas called the raid a violation of the truce and answered with rocket fire. [cite:gz_wiki_castlead] The exchanges continued as the truce's six months ran out.

The war. On 27 December 2008 Israel launched Operation Cast Lead, with the stated aim of stopping the rocket fire. Its ground invasion began on 3 January 2009. On 18 January Israel declared a unilateral ceasefire, Hamas declared a one-week ceasefire twelve hours later, and Israel completed its withdrawal on 21 January. [cite:gz_wiki_castlead]

The dead. Thirteen Israelis were killed: ten soldiers, four of them by fire from their own side, and three civilians. [cite:gz_wiki_castlead] The Palestinian toll was counted by several bodies, and the counts differ most on who among the dead had been fighting. The Palestinian Centre for Human Rights counted 1,417 dead and the Gaza health ministry 1,440. The Israeli human rights group B'Tselem counted 1,391, and found that 773 of those killed by the army had not taken part in hostilities, among them 320 minors and 109 women; it counted 330 combatants and 245 police, most killed in the bombing of police stations. The Israeli army's own figures, released in March 2009, counted 1,166 dead, of whom it classed 709 as involved in hostilities and 295 as unarmed civilians. [cite:gz_wiki_castlead] [cite:gz_ynet_btselem_cl]

The Goldstone report, and its author's second thoughts. In September 2009 a UN fact-finding mission led by the South African judge Richard Goldstone accused both the Israeli army and Palestinian armed groups of war crimes and possible crimes against humanity, and recommended that those responsible be brought to justice. [cite:gz_wiki_castlead] On 1 April 2011 Goldstone wrote in the Washington Post that "if I had known then what I know now, the Goldstone Report would have been a different document." He now concluded that Israel had not targeted civilians as a matter of policy, while the crimes of Hamas, he wrote, were intentional. [cite:gz_jvl_goldstone] The mission's other three members, Hina Jilani, Christine Chinkin and Desmond Travers, replied on 14 April that "there is no justification for any demand or expectation for reconsideration of the report", since nothing of substance had appeared to change its findings. [cite:gz_aj_goldstone_coauthors] The report was never withdrawn; its chief author's reconsideration and his colleagues' refusal stand side by side in the record.
