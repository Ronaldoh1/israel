No such law exists. The first decrees of Soviet power, on 8 November 1917, concerned peace, land and the new government, and none of the decrees of 1917 dealt with antisemitism [cite:rs_ch01_wiki_soviet_decrees]. The decree that did came in late July 1918, during the pogroms of the civil war: signed by Lenin, it ordered that pogromists and those who stirred up pogroms be declared outlaws. It named violent pogroms, not antisemitism in general, stated no death penalty and did not mention anti-communists [cite:rs_ch01_decree1918] [cite:rs_ch01_cup_soviet1918].

The claim usually comes with "Jewish Bolshevism," a conspiracy theory spread by the White armies and central to Nazi belief [cite:rs_ch01_wiki_jewish_bolshevism]. The same Soviet state later dismantled Jewish religious and Zionist life and staged the Doctors' Plot of 1953 [cite:rs_ch01_wiki_soviet_antisem].
