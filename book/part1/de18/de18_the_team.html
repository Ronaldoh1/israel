The Team

DOGE was staffed by a few hundred people, most of whom never became public figures. Congress's auditor counted at least 206 in posts in the Executive Office of the President by January 2026, 27 of them special government employees. [cite:de17_gao_108403] This section names only those whom major outlets named, gives their ages and backgrounds only as those outlets published them, and types each person's tie to Elon Musk's or Peter Thiel's companies by kind and date. Where no wrongdoing is documented, none is implied. Where a record is disputed, the person's or the government's answer is given beside it.

The leaders

Steve Davis ran DOGE day to day. He joined SpaceX in 2003 as roughly its fourteenth hire, led the Boring Company from 2018 and ran cost-cutting at X after Musk bought it in 2022. [cite:de18_wiki_davis] At DOGE he directed operations, including hiring and firing, as a special government employee who kept his job as head of the Boring Company; the New York Times described him as "effectively the leader of DOGE." [cite:de18_aol_davis] [cite:de18_wiki_davis] His departure was announced at the end of May 2025, the day after Musk's. [cite:de18_aol_davis] The legal head, the acting administrator Amy Gleason, was a health-technology executive who had served in the same office from 2018 to 2021; her role is set out in [scene:de17_what_doge_was_legally|Part Seventeen]. [cite:de17_ap_gleason]

Others came from Musk's companies or his investors. Tom Krause, chief executive of Cloud Software Group, took over the duties of the Treasury's fiscal assistant secretary, the official responsible for federal payments, after the senior career official, David Lebryk, retired following a clash with Musk's allies over access to the payment systems; Krause kept his company post as a special government employee. [cite:de18_reuters_krause] At the Office of Personnel Management (OPM), Reuters reported in January 2025, the chief of staff was Amanda Scales, a former Musk employee, and the senior advisers included Brian Bjelde, at SpaceX since 2003 and its vice-president for human resources, and Riccardo Biasini, a former Tesla engineer and Boring Company director; career managers there lost access to personnel databases holding Social Security numbers and home addresses. [cite:de18_reuters_opm] Antonio Gracias of Valor Equity Partners, a former Tesla director and early SpaceX investor, was sent to the Social Security Administration in March 2025 with two other finance executives. [cite:de18_bbg_ssa] Thomas Shedd, a Tesla engineer for about eight years, became director of the General Services Administration's Technology Transformation Services in January 2025, told staff the unit would be "AI-first," and later served as chief information officer at the Labor Department. [cite:de18_wiki_shedd]

The engineers

On 2 February 2025 Wired named six engineers aged 19 to 24 working with DOGE at OPM and the General Services Administration: Akash Bobba, a Berkeley graduate with internships at Bridgewater, Meta and Palantir; Edward Coristine, a recent high-school graduate who had studied at Northeastern and worked briefly at Neuralink; Luke Farritor, a former SpaceX intern; Gautier Cole Killian, formerly of the trading firm Jump Trading; Gavin Kliger, formerly of the AI company Databricks; and Ethan Shaotran, a Harvard student who had founded an AI start-up. [cite:de18_fox_wired] Farritor, 23, had shared a $700,000 prize in 2024 for using AI to read a carbonised scroll from Herculaneum, and was a fellow of the Thiel Foundation. [cite:de18_hyper_farritor] Musk wrote that reports that DOGE had "some of world's best software engineers" were "in fact true"; Trump said: "They're very smart, though, Peter. They're like you." A White House spokesman said the work was being done "in full compliance with federal law." [cite:de18_fox_wired]

Three records examined

Marko Elez, 25, worked on the Treasury's payment systems. On 6 February 2025 he resigned after the Wall Street Journal linked him to an account, which said its owner had worked at SpaceX and Starlink, that had posted "Just for the record, I was racist before it was cool" and "Normalize Indian hate." Vice President J.D. Vance urged that he be rehired ("So I say bring him back"), Musk agreed ("To err is human, to forgive divine"), and Trump said: "I'm with the vice president." [cite:de18_nbc_elez] [cite:de12_ap_elez] He was rehired, at the Social Security Administration. Treasury later told a court that he had briefly held read-write access to a payment system although the court had been told it was read-only, that its forensic review found no changes to the data, and that he had emailed an unencrypted spreadsheet of names and payment amounts to two officials at another agency in breach of its rules; Treasury's privacy officer called the names "low-risk." [cite:de18_bbg_elez] In April 2026 Congress's auditor confirmed the inadvertent write access and the undetected transfer, and found that the bureau had not ensured the employee signed its security rules before getting a laptop. [cite:de18_gao_108131]

Edward Coristine was 19. Bloomberg reported that a data-security firm, Path Network, ended his internship in June 2022 "for leaking internal information to the competitors"; he wrote at the time that he had done "nothing contractually wrong." [cite:de18_mediaite_coristine] Reuters reported in March 2025 that a group an FBI agent said had been investigated in connection with "swatting" had publicly thanked a web-protection company Coristine ran for its services in 2023; Coristine did not reply. He was listed as a senior adviser at the State Department and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. [cite:de18_fortune_coristine] These reports concern an internship and a company's customer; no court or agency finding against him is part of this record. On 3 August 2025 he was the victim of an attempted carjacking in Washington, and Trump cited the attack in threatening federal control of the city. [cite:de18_ap_assault] By 2026 he worked at the National Design Studio. [cite:de17_ee_end]

Gavin Kliger, 25, was at OPM, then at the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the IRS. ProPublica reported that his public disclosure listed Tesla shares worth $100,000 to $250,000, Apple shares and cryptocurrency, holdings barred for bureau staff, while a sworn declaration said he "managed" the firing of about 90 per cent of its employees. The White House called that account "an outright lie," said he "did not even manage" the layoffs, and pointed to a divestment deadline. [cite:de18_pp_kliger] In April 2025 he and Sam Corcos, chief executive of the health start-up Levels, planned an IRS hackathon to build "one new API to rule them all" over taxpayer data, with Palantir "consistently" named as a candidate vendor. [cite:de18_tc_irs] What happened inside those systems is the subject of [scene:de19_the_access|Part Nineteen].

Recruiting and vetting

Recruitment ran partly through Musk's and Thiel's networks. Marc Andreessen interviewed candidates in late 2024, as [scene:de16_the_valley|Part Sixteen] records. [cite:de16_fortune_doge] Anthony Jancso, a former Palantir employee brought on by Davis, wrote in a chat group of Palantir alumni: "I'm helping Elon's team find tech talent for the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) in the new admin." [cite:de18_indep_jancso] These are ties of employment and recruitment, not evidence that Thiel or Palantir directed anyone. Some staff arrived with no government experience at all: two who reviewed humanities grants, Justin Fox and Nate Cavanaugh, used ChatGPT to flag "DEI" grants worth more than $100 million for cancellation, and in 2026 Cavanaugh, asked under oath whether they had reduced the deficit, answered: "No, we didn't." [cite:de18_abc_depos] When Congress's auditor asked three agencies for proof that their DOGE staff had passed background investigations, only one supplied any. [cite:de18_gao_108192] No documented waiver of a security clearance for a named operator has been published.

Where they went

The team dispersed before the office ended. Davis left with Musk at the end of May 2025, and DOGE's spokeswoman, Katie Miller, reportedly went to work for Musk. [cite:de18_aol_davis] By November 2025, Reuters reported, the Airbnb co-founder Joe Gebbia, who had worked with DOGE, led a new National Design Studio; Zachary Terrell had become chief technology officer at Health and Human Services; Rachel Riley led the Office of Naval Research; and Jeremy Lewin oversaw foreign assistance at the State Department. Gleason had become an adviser to the health secretary. [cite:de17_reuters_noexist] These are ordinary appointments, documented as such; whether DOGE's methods went with them is examined in [scene:de21_the_ending|Part Twenty-One].

The worst-case reading, tested

The worst-case version of this part is that unvetted loyalists with ties to Musk's companies were given the keys to federal systems. Documented: the ages and inexperience of several named staff; direct ties of employment, investment or board service to Musk's companies for most of those named; a Thiel Foundation fellowship and Palantir backgrounds for some; a staffer rehired after racist posts who mishandled payment data; and agencies unable to show Congress's auditor that DOGE staff had been vetted. [cite:de18_nbc_elez] [cite:de18_gao_108131] [cite:de18_gao_108192] Probable: that vetting fell below normal federal standards. Not supported: that any named operator acted for Musk's companies, that Thiel chose or directed staff, or that any operator acted from a reading of Yarvin or Land; no operator's own words to that effect are on record. Against the reading: Treasury's review found no payment data altered, the White House disputed the conflict account, and the legal head, Gleason, was a veteran of the same office. [cite:de18_bbg_elez] [cite:de18_pp_kliger] [cite:de17_ap_gleason] Who paid for the cuts these staff carried out is the subject of [scene:de20_the_cuts|Part Twenty].
