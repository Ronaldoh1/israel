Two Economies

Israel. By the World Bank's figures, Israel's economy produced $513 billion in 2023, the year the war began, $542 billion in 2024 and $611 billion in 2025. [cite:hm_fred_ilgdp] In 2024 it spent $46.5 billion on its military, by the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute's count: 8.8 percent of its economy, a rise of 65 percent in a year and the second-highest share in the world after Ukraine. [cite:hm_globes_sipri]

Gaza. In the three years before the war, Gaza's economy produced an average of about $670 million of value a quarter, by UNCTAD's estimate: roughly $2.7 billion a year, for about two million people. By the first quarter of 2024 its activity was 86 percent below that level. [cite:hm_aspi_gazagdp] By 2025, Palestinian official statistics put Gaza's real output at 84 percent below 2023, and its unemployment at 78 percent. [cite:hm_pcbs_2025]

UNCTAD's later figures make the gap concrete per person. In 2024 Gaza's economy shrank by 83 percent to $362 million, and its output per person fell to $161 for the year, less than 50 cents a day; UNCTAD put the cost of rebuilding at more than $70 billion and said recovery to pre-war conditions would take decades. [cite:hm_unctad_2025] Israel's economy that year was worth about $542 billion, and its arms industry alone exported a record $14.8 billion of weapons and systems, about forty times Gaza's whole economy. [cite:hm_fred_ilgdp] [cite:hm_globes_exports]

The Aid

From the United States. Under a memorandum signed in September 2016, the United States committed $38 billion in military aid to Israel for the ten years from 2019 to 2028: $3.3 billion a year in grants to buy weapons and $500 million a year for missile defence, $3.8 billion in all. [cite:hm_dn_mou] On top of that, Brown University's Costs of War project and the Quincy Institute counted at least $21.7 billion in American military aid to Israel in the two years after 7 October 2023, $17.9 billion of it in the first year, and between $9.65 and $12 billion more spent on American military operations in the region. [cite:hm_an_costsofwar]

From elsewhere. In 2023, the United States supplied 69 percent of Israel's arms imports and Germany 30 percent. [cite:hm_dp_sipri_imports] Israel is also a major exporter: its defence exports reached $14.8 billion in 2024, more than half of them to Europe and about half of them air-defence systems. [cite:hm_globes_exports]

Hamas's money. Hamas does not publish a budget, and the figures are estimates. Israeli and Palestinian sources cited by NBC News put its military spending at $100 million to $350 million a year. A former US Treasury official, Matthew Levitt, estimated its income from taxes, fees and smuggling in Gaza at $300 million to $450 million a year. The US State Department has put Iran's support at about $100 million a year. Qatar gave $1.49 billion in aid to Gaza between 2012 and 2021, for salaries, fuel and poor families, and denies funding Hamas. The US Treasury has described a Hamas investment portfolio of about $500 million in companies and property abroad. Hamas says it keeps its civil and military funds separate; Western governments and former Israeli officials say it does not. [cite:hm_nbc_money]
