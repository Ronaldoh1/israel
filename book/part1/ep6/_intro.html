Epstein left jail in July 2009 as a registered sex offender. Within a decade he was again hosting scientists, advising bankers, dining with television anchors and being courted by a former White House strategist. That did not happen by accident. Part Six shows, largely in the emails of the people who did the work, how a convicted man's reputation was rebuilt: through search engines, publicists, the press, universities and friends.

The part asks one question of every piece of that work: who benefited from his return? Epstein benefited most. But the record shows others gaining too: fees for the people who cleaned his name, money for the laboratories and salons that took his gifts, access for the people he introduced. And it shows the cost falling where it had always fallen, on the women whose accounts were kept out of print.

The part also gives the strongest version of the other side. Some of the people here say they did not know the conviction involved a child, or believed the matter was closed; some later apologised, some did not. Their own words are reported alongside the documents.
