Part Two established the sequence in which Hezbollah formed. Israel invaded Lebanon on 6 June 1982 after the shooting of its ambassador in London, with a stated aim of 40 kilometres, and its army reached Beirut within a week. The PLO left by sea, and Kataeb militiamen then killed hundreds, by some counts thousands, in Sabra and Shatila, camps that Israeli troops had surrounded. [cite:hz2_idf_lw1] [cite:hz2_wiki_1982il] In the same year Iran's Revolutionary Guards set up a base in the Bekaa. There they brought together Islamic Amal and Lebanese clerics, and a US court later found the resulting organization "formed under the auspices of the government of Iran", in a case Iran did not contest. [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks] [cite:hz2_anderson] The bombings of 1983 killed 63 at the US embassy, then 241 American and 58 French service members. They were claimed by the "Islamic Jihad Organization". US courts later attributed them to Hezbollah acting with Iranian direction, while Hezbollah and Iran have denied any part. The Marines left Lebanon in February 1984. [cite:hz2_an_1983] [cite:hz2_wiki_barracks] [cite:hz2_peterson07] [cite:hz2_aj_theft] In February 1985 the movement announced itself with a letter that named Israel and the United States as enemies and Khomeini as its authority. In 2009 it revised its programme for Lebanon but not its armed stance toward Israel, and in 1985 Israel itself pulled back to an occupied zone in the south. [cite:hz2_cep_letter] [cite:hz2_belfer_cohen] [cite:hz4_crs_sla] Part Three asks whether Hezbollah would have existed without the invasion and the occupation, and treats the answers as argument.
