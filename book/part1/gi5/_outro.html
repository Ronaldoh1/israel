Part Five established that the claim to the land west of the Jordan entered Israeli government with the Likud in 1977, in documents that can be quoted. The 1977 platform declared that "between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty"; Begin called Judea and Samaria a land that "cannot be given up in any part", and his government made settlement in the hill country state policy. [cite:gi5_jvl_likud1977] [cite:gi5_djn_begin77] [cite:wb_ej_gush] It also established the limits of that record. Begin's autonomy plan kept the sovereignty claim but proposed to leave it "open"; Israeli law was applied to East Jerusalem and, in 1981, to the Golan, both declared null and void by the Security Council; and Sinai was returned in 1982. [cite:gi5_ecf_selfrule] [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex] [cite:gi5_wiki_golanlaw] [cite:gi1_csm_sinai1982] Likud's Central Committee voted in 2002 against any Palestinian state west of the Jordan and in 2017 for sovereignty over the settlements, while Likud prime ministers removed the Gaza settlements in 2005 and, in 2009, accepted a demilitarised Palestinian state on conditions. [cite:gi5_ap_likud2002] [cite:gi5_toi_likud2017] [cite:gz_wiki_disengagement] [cite:gi5_aje_barilan] By December 2022 a Basic Law named Jewish settlement a national value without defining where, the government's guidelines claimed "an exclusive and inalienable right to all parts of the Land of Israel", and the coalition agreement committed the prime minister to a sovereignty policy at a time of his choosing. [cite:gi5_toi_nslaw] [cite:gi5_adalah_hcj] [cite:gi5_jl_guidelines] [cite:gi5_cgtn_sov] In every one of these texts the claim stops at the Jordan. Part Six follows what the government did with those commitments between 2019 and October 2026, from the annexation pledges and their suspension to the land-registration decision and the Knesset votes.
