What the Map Cannot Know

It counts only what is documented. A person who did work for the operation but left no tie, or a payment no record states, cannot be weighed. The map is a floor under what happened, not a full account of it.

It measures 1991 to 2019. The earliest allegations, from about 1985 and 1990, come before the year the map starts, so it cannot weigh those years.

A tie is not wrongdoing. A bank account, a property sale, a donation or a dinner is an act the record documents; whether the other party knew of the crimes is a separate question that most ties do not answer. Claims that a named person took part in the crimes are carried only where court records, sworn testimony or case-file logs support them.

Its weights are choices. The ranges in the tests show how far each result moves when they change. The map was also run forty more times, each time leaving out one of the most-used sources. The largest shift is 12 points, in the test that removes the houses and the island: one FBI interview with a pilot (2006) is the only source for which company owned two of the aircraft. Next is the test that removes every bank, which moves 10 points without one newspaper's account of his banks. A second source for those ties would steady both.

Comparison cases are comparisons, not ties. The other networks the map holds for comparison, from the R. Kelly enterprise to the Pennsylvania dioceses, show that each pattern the book names has documented precedents. Three of them, the Dutroux inquiry, the Kincora inquiry and Operation Midland, are kept because a claimed protection network was tested and not found.
