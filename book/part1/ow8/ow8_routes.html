Two Routes Out: Foreign Military Sales and Commercial Sales

American weapons reach a foreign army by one of two legal routes, both governed by the Arms Export Control Act. In a Foreign Military Sale, the buyer is a government and so is the seller: the foreign state buys from the US government, which in turn buys from the manufacturer or takes the item from Pentagon stocks. In a Direct Commercial Sale, the foreign government buys straight from an American company, which needs an export licence from the State Department. [cite:ow8_crs_fms2026] Most of what the United States pays for on Israel's behalf moves by the first route, because Israel's Foreign Military Financing grant is spent through the Foreign Military Sales system. [cite:ow7_js_wrsa]

The money. The 2016 memorandum of understanding commits the United States to $3.8 billion a year for fiscal years 2019 to 2028: $3.3 billion in Foreign Military Financing and $500 million for missile defence. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] Israel's financing is treated differently from other recipients' in one important respect. Since a 1990 appropriations law, it has been paid out as a lump sum in the first month of the fiscal year and held in an interest-bearing account at the Federal Reserve, rather than drawn down as bills come due. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] In fiscal 2025, Foreign Military Financing funded $17.9 billion of the $104.4 billion that passed through the whole Foreign Military Sales system worldwide; most sales are paid for by the buyers themselves. [cite:ow8_crs_fms2026]

The process. A sale begins with consultations and a formal Letter of Request from the buyer to the Pentagon agency that manages the item. That agency prices it and drafts a Letter of Offer and Acceptance; the Defense Security Cooperation Agency reviews it, and the State Department, which sets arms-transfer policy, approves the case. If the sale is large enough, Congress must be notified, and the offer cannot be issued until the review period has run. Once the buyer signs and pays a deposit into the Foreign Military Sales Trust Fund, the Pentagon receives authority to spend it, places contracts with manufacturers or takes the items from its own stocks, and delivers them; the sale is then subject to the Pentagon's "Golden Sentry" end-use monitoring. [cite:ow8_crs_fms2026]

Israel's shorter review. Congress is told of a sale formally only above certain values, and the thresholds and waiting periods depend on the buyer. For most countries, notification is required for major defence equipment worth $14 million or more, or other articles and services worth $50 million, and Congress has 30 days to act. For NATO, its members, Japan, Australia, South Korea, Israel and New Zealand, the thresholds are higher ($25 million and $100 million) and the period is 15 days. Before formal notification, the State Department informally consults the two foreign-affairs committees, under a "tiered review" of 20 to 40 days. [cite:ow8_crs_review2021] Congress can block a sale only by passing a joint resolution of disapproval in both chambers, which the President can veto; overriding a veto takes two-thirds of each chamber, and Congress has never blocked a sale this way. [cite:ow8_crs_review2021]

The emergency door. The Act also allows the review to be skipped. If the President states in the notification that "an emergency exists" requiring the immediate sale in the national security interests of the United States, with a detailed justification, the sale may go ahead at once. [cite:ow8_crs_review2021] This power, rarely used, has been used for Israel repeatedly since December 2023; the next section sets out when.

What the routes mean. The official purpose of the system, in both governments' account, is to keep Israel able to defend itself against better-armed neighbours: the 2008 law that defines Israel's "qualitative military edge" requires every other arms sale to the region to be checked against it. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] The American side of the bargain is also built in: because Israel's aid is spent through Foreign Military Sales, the money is paid to American manufacturers, and from fiscal 2028 the share Israel may spend in Israel falls to zero, as the section on co-production explains. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] The asymmetry lies in the last step. Every stage up to delivery runs through American hands and American records; the use of the weapon does not, and the United States has said, in its own report, that it could not verify how its weapons were used. [cite:ow8_js_nsm20report]
