Part Twenty-Nine established three things. The Templars were arrested by Philip IV on 13 October 1307, tortured into confessions and, in fifty-four cases in May 1310, burned for retracting them; the Chinon parchment of August 1308 records the leaders' absolution after confession, not a finding of innocence, and Vox in excelso of 22 March 1312 suppressed the order "by apostolic provision", not by a heresy sentence, while the Friday-the-13th origin story is a twentieth-century invention. [cite:vt29_barber_cup2] [cite:vt29_barber_cup1] [cite:vt29_wiki_chinon] [cite:vt29_ewtn_vienne] [cite:vt29_natgeo_fri13] Teachers of the inner way inside the Church were condemned in named documents: Porete burned on 1 June 1310, Eckhart's propositions condemned on 27 March 1329, the alumbrados' forty-eight propositions listed in 1525, and Molinos's sixty-eight in Coelestis Pastor on 20 November 1687, while others who taught union with God under obedience were warned, cleared or published. [cite:vt29_wiki_porete] [cite:vt29_mckay_inagro] [cite:vt29_wiki_alumbrados] [cite:vt29_wiki_molinos] Bruno was burned on 17 February 1600 for theological heresies with cosmology and magic among the charges, and Galileo abjured on 22 June 1633 and lived under house arrest, with the 1992 papal address stopping short of annulling the sentence.
