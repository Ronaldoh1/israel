Hezbollah was founded in 1982, but the conditions it grew out of are forty years older. Part One follows them from Lebanon's independence in 1943 to the eve of Israel's 1982 invasion: a state built on a confessional bargain that left its Shia community on the margins; the cleric Musa al-Sadr, who organised that community and gave it a militia, Amal, before he vanished in Libya in 1978; the Palestinian refugees of 1948 and the PLO, which made southern Lebanon its base after 1970 under an agreement the Lebanese state signed in secret; the attacks and reprisals across the border and the civil war of 1975; Israel's invasion of 1978, the UN force it produced, and the Lebanese militia Israel armed and paid; and Iran's revolution of 1979, whose constitution committed it to carrying the revolution abroad.
