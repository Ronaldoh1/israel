Part Five established what the Scofield Reference Bible was, what it said and who paid for it. Oxford University Press published it in 1909, through Henry Frowde and the press's New York branch, with dispensational notes printed beside the King James text; Scofield revised it in 1917, adding Ussher's dates, and a committee of nine revised it again in 1967 while keeping its seven dispensations and its Israel/Church distinction. [cite:ts5_dunlap] [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] [cite:ts5_ct_1967] No source supports the claim that the 1917 edition referred to the Balfour Declaration, and the citable sales figure is about two million copies by 1945. [cite:ts5_wiki_srb] The 1917 notes say that nations which persecuted "the Jew" fared ill, that Christians do not inherit "the distinctive Jewish promises", and that Russia was Ezekiel's Gog; they place Israel's restoration to the land after "The return of the Lord", not before it. [cite:ts1_sac_gen15] [cite:ts5_sac_rom11] [cite:ts5_sac_ezek38] [cite:ts5_sac_deut30] The work was paid for by Brethren-linked businessmen, Alwyn Ball, John Pirie, John Buss and Francis Fitch, with a reported gift from the oilman Lyman Stewart; the story that Samuel Untermyer or the Rothschilds paid for it rests on Canfield's "we assume" of 1988 and Piper's unsourced claim of 2004, and fails on the record.
