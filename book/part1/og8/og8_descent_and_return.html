Descent and Return

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The Mysteries of Eleusis were built on a story: a daughter taken down into the underworld, a mother who searches for her, and a return. [cite:ts13_brit_eleusis] The initiates who went through the rite said they came away with "a better hope" at death. [cite:vt33_wh_eleusis] The gold tablets in the graves gave the dead a password for the guardians of the underworld: "I am the son of Earth and Starry Heaven. But my race is heavenly." [cite:vt33_getty_goldtablets]

The inner reading takes the story as a map of a life. Every person, in this reading, is Persephone: taken down into the world of the body and its forgetting, searched for by something that does not stop looking, and able to return. The tablets name the danger as forgetting and the remedy as memory: drink from the Lake of Memory, and say who you are. The rite at Eleusis, whatever it was, seems to have given its initiates an experience of that return in advance, so that death was no longer the end of the story.

The same shape appears across this shelf: the Gnostic soul that rises past the powers that held it, the Hermetic soul that gives back what it took on, the Sufi who passes away from himself. [cite:vt26_mattison_gmary] [cite:sf_brit_fana] Eleusis is the oldest documented rite on the shelf to stage it, and it kept its secret.

The record limits the reading. The Hymn to Demeter is a myth of the seasons and of a mother's grief, and many scholars read the Mysteries as an agricultural and civic cult; the gold tablets come from initiations associated with Orpheus and Dionysus, not from Eleusis. [cite:vt33_getty_goldtablets] What happened in the hall is unknown. The reading is the reader's.
