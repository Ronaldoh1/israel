"That which is below is like that which is above." Isaac Newton's translation of a line in the Emerald Tablet, a short text whose oldest version is Arabic, from the late eighth or early ninth century, gave the Western world its most quoted esoteric rule, now usually paraphrased as "as above, so below". [cite:de39_wiki_emerald] The tablet was attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, "Thrice-Great Hermes", the legendary Egyptian sage behind a body of Greek writings called the Hermetica. [cite:ht_enc_hermeticism]

This book follows those writings and the practices that grew around them: alchemy, astrology and the ritual magic called theurgy. It follows them as this shelf follows everything, by asking who held them. A Florentine banker had them translated before Plato. A scholar proved in 1614 that they were not as old as everyone believed. An English king made alchemy a crime because gold could be counterfeited, and a later king licensed alchemists to pay for his wars. An emperor kept as many as two hundred alchemists in Prague. A queen's adviser talked with angels. A philosopher was burned in Rome. And the greatest scientist of his age wrote more than a million words on alchemy and kept them hidden.
