Four Truces

November 2023. The first truce began on 24 November as a four-day pause and was extended twice. [cite:hm_aj_truce_ext1] [cite:hm_aj_truce_ext2] Ben-Gvir's ministers voted against the deal, and he urged others to do the same. [cite:hm_toi_bg_nov2023] It ended on 1 December when talks on a further extension failed: Hamas did not hand over a list of ten more hostages for another day of calm and fired rockets, and Israel resumed its campaign, saying it was "committed to achieving the goals of the war." About 137 hostages were still in Gaza. Hamas said it had offered to release more hostages, including older people, and called the resumption "a prior decision to resume the criminal aggression." [cite:hm_jw_truce2023] [cite:hm_salon_truce]

2024: the deals that did not happen. Through 2024 a hostage deal was repeatedly negotiated and not concluded. In September 2024 Israel recovered the bodies of six hostages, among them Hersh Goldberg-Polin, whom its army said Hamas had killed shortly before troops arrived. The Hostages Families Forum said that "were it not for the delays, sabotage, and excuses" they would likely still be alive; Netanyahu said: "whoever murders hostages doesn't want a deal." [cite:hm_katv_six] In January 2025 Ben-Gvir described his own role in that year, in a post on X: "In the past year, through our political strength, we succeeded in preventing such a deal from being made, time and again." The opposition leader, Yair Lapid, said this showed Netanyahu had blocked releases for political reasons; Netanyahu had denied rejecting deals for political reasons, and Secretary of State Blinken said Hamas had repeatedly failed to conclude deals it should have. [cite:hm_ynet_bg_foiled]

January–March 2025. The second ceasefire, in three phases, took effect on 19 January after a delay of about three hours, which Israel blamed on Hamas's failure to provide the hostages' names; Israeli strikes killed 19 people in those hours. Ben-Gvir quit the government in protest. On 1 March Hamas rejected an Israeli proposal to extend the first phase and demanded the second phase as agreed, which required Israel's withdrawal and a permanent calm; Israel then endorsed an American plan to extend the truce through Ramadan and Passover with half the remaining hostages released at once. On 2 March Israel stopped all aid entering Gaza, which Egypt, Qatar and the UN called a violation, and on 9 March it cut the electricity supply. On 18 March it resumed the war, citing Hamas's refusal to release hostages; by then reports submitted to the UN listed about 1,000 ceasefire violations attributed to Israel. [cite:hm_wiki_jan2025cf] Ben-Gvir announced his return the same day, strengthening the coalition before its budget vote. [cite:hm_gp_bengvir]

October 2025 onward. The third ceasefire, under President Trump's plan, brought the last 20 living hostages home on 13 October. Ben-Gvir voted against its first phase and threatened to quit if Hamas was not "dismantled politically and militarily," saying his party "will not be part of a national defeat and eternal disgrace." [cite:hm_na_bg_oct2025] The ceasefire held in name only: by February 2026 Gaza's health ministry counted more than 530 Palestinians killed since it began, and Israel's army four of its soldiers; Israel described its strikes as responses to Hamas violations. [cite:hm_npr_feb2026] Part Nine follows the disarmament deadlock that followed. [scene:hm9_after|After the war]

The record does not show that Ben-Gvir alone broke these ceasefires; Hamas's own conduct, the government as a whole and the prime minister bear on each. It does show, in his own words, that he counted the prevention of hostage deals as an achievement of his political strength, and that in 2025 he left the government when a ceasefire began and returned when the bombing resumed.
