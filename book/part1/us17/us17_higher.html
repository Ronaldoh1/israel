Universities, Cost and Debt

Higher education is where the two systems differ most in who pays. Israel's universities and most of its colleges are funded through a national budget and charge a single regulated tuition fee. American higher education is a market of public, private non-profit and for-profit institutions whose prices are set by states and by the institutions themselves, financed largely by federal student loans. This section compares access, price and debt, and states where a comparable figure does not exist. [scene:us17_schools|Schools: streams, money and scores]

Israel: a regulated public system

Israel's Council for Higher Education accredits institutions and, through its planning and budgeting committee, allocates the state's money to them. It also sets tuition, which is linked to the consumer price index rather than left to each university: for 2025–26 a bachelor's degree at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem cost Israeli citizens NIS 12,017 a year. [cite:us14_huji_tuition] The system has grown and widened. The council counted 337,230 students in 2021–22, 256,170 of them on bachelor's degrees, and expected about 342,000 the following year; women were 60 per cent of students, and about a third of bachelor's students came from the four lowest of the ten socio-economic clusters. [cite:us17_che_2022]

The largest change has been among Arab citizens. Their share of bachelor's students rose from 10 per cent in 2009–10 to 18 per cent in 2020–21, and of master's students from 6.5 per cent in 2011–12 to 16 per cent; the number of Arab students more than doubled, from 25,951 to 57,552, after the council funded preparatory and scholarship programmes. Arab citizens remained under-represented at the doctoral level, at 8 per cent of PhD students. [cite:us17_che_arab] By 2022–23 Arab students numbered more than 60,000, 18 per cent of all students against 21 per cent of the population. [cite:us17_che_2022] Haredi students rose from 5,500 in 2010–11 to 16,575 in 2021–22, but were still 4.9 per cent of students against 13 per cent of the population, and only 8.5 per cent of them studied at universities rather than colleges, against 33.5 per cent of other Jewish students. [cite:us17_che_2022] [cite:us17_idi_haredi2022] The school figures in the previous section, where 14 per cent of Haredi pupils earned a matriculation certificate in 2018–19 against 83 per cent in the state streams, point to where that gap begins. [cite:us17_idi_haredi2022]

The weakness of the Israeli system is money per student. In the OECD's 2026 Education at a Glance, Israel spent $7,885 per university student in 2023, in purchasing-power dollars, against an OECD average of $16,405, fifth from the bottom of 33 countries; the government paid 38 per cent of higher-education spending against an average of 68 per cent. [cite:us17_ynet_eag2026h] Attainment is split by sex. Among 25- to 34-year-olds, 57 per cent of Israeli women held a tertiary degree in the 2023 edition, above the OECD average of 54 per cent, but only 36 per cent of men, below the average of 41 per cent; the men's figure had not moved since 2015 while the OECD average for men rose to 43 per cent. [cite:us17_oecd_eag2023_isr] [cite:us17_ynet_eag2026h] Conscription, which for those who serve comes before university, is taken up in Part Nineteen; no source used here measures its effect on degree rates. [scene:us19_service|Part Nineteen: service]

The United States: price, grants and loans

The College Board's series shows how far American public tuition has risen. Average published tuition and fees at a public four-year college for in-state students were about $5,940 in 1995–96 in 2025 dollars; they peaked near $12,810 around 2015–16 and stood at $11,950 in 2025–26. Private non-profit four-year colleges averaged $45,000 and public two-year colleges $4,150. [cite:us14_ncan_cb] [cite:us14_cb_trends2025] Grants narrow the gap: after grant aid, the average in-state student at a public four-year college paid about $2,300 in net tuition in 2025–26, down from a peak of $4,450 in 2012–13. Neither figure includes living costs. [cite:us14_cb_trends2025] On the OECD's 2023 basis, 46 per cent of American men and 56 per cent of women aged 25 to 34 held a tertiary degree. [cite:us17_oecd_eag2023_usa]

What families cannot pay up front, they borrow. Student loan balances almost tripled between 2004 and 2012, when the number of borrowers grew by about 70 per cent, and reached about $994 billion in the second quarter of 2013; student debt was the only kind of household debt that kept rising through the Great Recession. [cite:us17_nyfed_2013] By the second quarter of 2026 it stood at $1.651 trillion, by the New York Fed's count. [cite:us14_nyfed_q2_2026] The federal portfolio, by the government's own data to 30 June 2026, covered 42.3 million borrowers and more than $1.7 trillion. Of these, 9.3 million borrowers owing $234 billion were in default, about 400,000 more than three months earlier. Defaulted borrowers can have tax refunds and part of their Social Security benefits taken through the Treasury offset programme, and up to 15 per cent of disposable pay garnished. [cite:us17_ci_defaults]

The law changed in 2025. The reconciliation act signed on 4 July 2025, known as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, [cite:us16_kff_obbba] ends Grad PLUS loans for new graduate borrowers from 1 July 2026; caps unsubsidised graduate loans at $20,500 a year and $100,000 in all, and professional-school loans at $50,000 a year and $200,000; limits Parent PLUS loans to $20,000 a year and $65,000 per student; and replaces the repayment options for new loans with a single income-based Repayment Assistance Plan and fixed-term standard plans. [cite:us17_unmc_obbba] The new plan launched in July 2026, and the government had not yet reported how many borrowers had joined it. [cite:us17_ci_defaults]

What the comparison shows

The two systems put the cost of a degree in different places. Israel holds tuition down by regulation and spends little per student by OECD standards; no national figure for Israeli student debt was found for this book, and none should be inferred from its absence. [cite:us17_ynet_eag2026h] [cite:us14_huji_tuition] The United States spends more and charges more, and has shifted much of the cost onto borrowers: about one in five borrowers in the federal portfolio was in default in mid-2026. [cite:us17_ci_defaults] Each system's access gap follows its school system. In Israel, the stream a pupil attends largely decides whether they reach university; in the United States, family income decides how much a degree will be borrowed against. [cite:us17_idi_haredi2022] [cite:us14_cb_trends2025] Israel's education minister, Yoav Kisch, ends his term with the election of 27 October 2026, and the American loan changes take full effect as Congress is chosen on 3 November; both records are stated as of October 2026.
