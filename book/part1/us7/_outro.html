Part Seven established that Israel leaves marriage and divorce to religious courts under arrangements dating from the Ottoman millet system, the 1947 status quo and the 1953 rabbinical-courts law, so that couples of mixed faith or the same sex, people the Rabbinate does not count as Jewish and wives refused a divorce have no full remedy inside the country; the state registers marriages made abroad, about 9,200 in 2022, rather than offering civil marriage.
