The Americans Killed, and the "Dragged In" Argument

This section lists every American service member whose death the record ties to the theatres of the wars since 7 October 2023, from Gaza and the Red Sea to the militia attacks and the war with Iran. It then lists American civilians killed in Israel, Gaza and the West Bank, with responsibility stated as the record states it, and closes with the argument that the United States was dragged into these wars by Israel, setting out both sides. Earlier American losses to Iran and its allies, from the Beirut barracks in 1983 to Ain al-Asad in 2020, are counted in the Hezbollah and Iran volumes. Service members, October 2023 to October 2026

The first deaths came at sea. On 11 January 2024, off Somalia, two Navy SEALs, Special Warfare Operators Christopher J. Chambers and Nathan G. Ingram, went into rough water while boarding a dhow that Central Command said was carrying Iranian weapons, including missile parts bound for the Houthis; one was knocked off by waves and the other went in after him. They were declared dead on 21 January. [cite:gi16_mt_seals] [cite:hz12_cow_2024] On 28 January 2024 a drone struck Tower 22, a small American outpost in north-eastern Jordan, killing three Army soldiers, Sergeant William Jerome Rivers and Specialists Kennedy Ladon Sanders and Breonna Alexsondria Moffett. President Biden blamed Iran-backed militia groups; the United States named Kata'ib Hezbollah, which then said it was halting operations under pressure from Iran and Iraq, and Iran denied ordering attacks. [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] [cite:hz10_at_farida] [scene:hz10_iraq|The Iraqi militias] On 20 March 2024 Aviation Machinist's Mate 2nd Class Oriola Michael Aregbesola, of a helicopter squadron aboard the destroyer USS Mason, which was then responding to Houthi attacks on shipping, died in the Red Sea region; the Pentagon called it a non-combat incident and opened an investigation. [cite:gi16a_tp_aregbesola] In May 2024 Army Sergeant Quandarius Stanley, 23, was critically injured when high winds and heavy seas damaged the temporary pier the United States had built to bring aid into Gaza; officials said the injury was not combat-related, and he died in late 2024. [cite:gi16_bdn_stanley] Not counted here: five Army special operations aviators killed on 10 November 2023 when their helicopter crashed in the eastern Mediterranean "during an air refueling mission as part of military training", which the reporting set in the build-up after 7 October but did not tie to the fighting. [cite:gi16a_nbc_med]

No American was reported killed in the June 2025 war with Iran. [cite:hz12_cow_2025] The war that began on 28 February 2026 was the deadliest for American forces. By the Pentagon's Defense Casualty Analysis System, 21 service members had died and 865 had been wounded by 9 October 2026, most of the wounded after 7 July. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026] [cite:gi16_tp_21] Eleven of the deaths were by hostile fire, by the official count The Intercept reported on 28 September, when it stood at 19; the two deaths added on 9 October were non-hostile. The Intercept calls the tally an undercount, and The Washington Post reported four more deaths than it showed. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth called the Post's report "DISGUSTING and FAKE", and an official said the additional deaths it reported "were not directly related to the war with Iran". [cite:hz12_intercept_cas] [cite:gi16_tp_21] From AP, Army, Navy, Newsweek and Task & Purpose reporting, the 21 were these. On 1 March an Iranian drone struck a tactical operations centre at Port Shuaiba, a civilian port in Kuwait, killing six Army Reserve soldiers of the 103rd Sustainment Command: Chief Warrant Officer 3 Robert M. Marzan, Major Jeffrey R. O'Brien, Captain Cody A. Khork, Master Sergeant Nicole M. Amor, Sergeant 1st Class Noah L. Tietjens and Sergeant Declan J. Coady. Sergeant Benjamin N. Pennington, wounded in an Iranian attack on Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia the same day, died on 8 March. [cite:gi16a_tp_kuwait] [cite:hz12_ktar_deaths] Major Sorffly Davius of the New York Army National Guard died of a medical emergency at Camp Buehring, Kuwait, on 6 March; he was added to the war count only on 9 October. [cite:gi16_tp_21] [cite:gi16a_inq_dcas] Six airmen, three of them from the Ohio Air National Guard, died when a KC-135 tanker crashed in western Iraq on 12 March; Central Command said the crash "was not due to hostile fire or friendly fire". [cite:gi16a_tp_kc135] Commander Gabriel Edwards, commanding officer of a Navy helicopter squadron, died when his MH-60S ditched in the Arabian Sea on 1 July; initial findings did not suggest hostile action. [cite:gi16a_usni_edwards] When fighting resumed in July, three soldiers were killed in an Iranian missile and drone attack on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan: Private Isabella Gonzales on 17 July, Sergeant Angel S. Rampersad, first listed as missing and believed to have died on 17 July, and First Lieutenant Tyler James Feehan on 18 July. [cite:gi16a_at_jordan] [cite:gi16_nw_19th] Army Sergeant Michael Emmanuel Swinton was killed in action at Erbil Air Base in Iraq on 19 July "during a controlled detonation of a downed One-Way Unmanned Aerial System", in the Army's words. [cite:gi16a_army_swinton] Captain Bianca C. Wilkerson of US Army Central died in a "non-combat related incident" in Qatar on 18 September, and a Navy sailor died in September of a cause listed as pending; both deaths were non-hostile. [cite:gi16_nw_19th] [cite:gi16_tp_21] One further death is outside the 21: Sergeant Devin A. Seibel, 26, died in a training accident in Iraq in May, which the military said was not tied to operations against Iran; it is recorded under Operation Inherent Resolve, the campaign against ISIS. [cite:gi16a_inq_dcas] The war itself is told in Part Eleven of the Iran volume. [scene:ir11_war|The 2026 war]

On those counts, 28 American service members died in these theatres between October 2023 and 9 October 2026: two SEALs at sea and a sailor in the Red Sea in 2024, three at Tower 22, one from the Gaza pier and 21 in the 2026 war. That total is this book's addition of the figures above, and it leaves out the Mediterranean training crash of November 2023 and the training death in Iraq in May 2026; 14 of the 28 deaths were hostile, and 21 of the 28 came in the war with Iran.

American civilians

The largest single loss of American civilian life came on 7 October 2023. On 24 October Secretary of State Antony Blinken told the United Nations that 33 Americans had been killed in the Hamas-led attack, and a State Department official said ten more were unaccounted for and might be held hostage. [cite:gi16_kshb_oct7] Hostages killed in captivity are described in the Hamas volume. [scene:hm12_ceasefires|The ceasefires and the hostages]

In the West Bank, ABC News counted five American citizens killed between 7 October 2023 and July 2025. [cite:gi16_abc_musallet] Tawfic Abdel Jabbar, 17, of Gretna, Louisiana, was shot dead on Highway 60 on 19 January 2024; Israeli police said an off-duty officer, a soldier and a settler had fired at the car, and police and the army said he had been throwing stones, which a passenger, his father and the Israeli rights group B'Tselem disputed. No findings or charges had been published by July 2025. [cite:gi16_wiki_abdeljabbar] Mohammad Alkhdour, 17, was killed on 10 February 2024; the reporting checked here does not set out who fired. [cite:gi16_abc_musallet] Aysenur Eygi, 26, an American and Turkish citizen, was shot in the head at a protest in Beita on 6 September 2024. The army found it "highly likely" she had been hit "indirectly and unintentionally" by fire aimed at a protest leader; President Biden called for "full accountability", and Blinken called the killing "unprovoked and unjustified". [cite:gi16_axios_eygi] Amer Rabee, 14, of Saddle Brook, New Jersey, was killed near Turmus Ayya in April 2025; the army said it had fired at "three terrorists who hurled rocks toward the highway", and the State Department said Israel was investigating. [cite:gi16_abc_rabee] Sayfollah Musallet, 20, of Tampa, Florida, was killed in Sinjil on 11 July 2025; his family and the Palestinian Health Ministry said settlers beat him to death and blocked an ambulance, and the army said a clash had followed stone-throwing at settlers and that the security service and police were examining it. [cite:gi16_abc_musallet] According to a compilation of the reporting, as of March 2026 no criminal charges had been brought in any killing of an American by Israelis since January 2022. [cite:gi16_wiki_abdeljabbar] Settler violence in this period is documented in The West Bank, Documented. [scene:wb8_settlers|Settler violence]

In Gaza, Jacob Flickinger, an American-Canadian, was one of seven World Central Kitchen workers killed by Israeli strikes on their convoy on 1 April 2024. The army's own inquiry called it "a grave mistake" caused by misidentification and breaches of its rules of engagement, and dismissed two officers and reprimanded three. [cite:gi16_dfat_wck] Two earlier cases frame these. Rachel Corrie, 23, was crushed by an Israeli military bulldozer in Rafah on 16 March 2003 while trying to stop a house demolition; the driver said he did not see her, and in February 2015 Israel's Supreme Court upheld a ruling that the state was not liable because the act was "wartime activity", a decision Human Rights Watch said implied "immunity even for deaths caused by alleged negligence". [cite:gi16_hrw_corrie] Shireen Abu Akleh, a Palestinian-American Al Jazeera journalist, was killed near Jenin in May 2022; the army said there was a "high possibility" a soldier had shot her, and no criminal investigation followed. [cite:pr_npr_abuakleh]

"Dragged in": the argument on both sides

The claim that Israel dragged the United States into these wars is an argument about cause, not a finding of fact, and the Iran volume tests it in detail. [scene:ir14_who|Who pushed, who decided] [scene:ir12_question|Iran and war with America] The case for it rests on documented links. The Houthis described their attacks on shipping as support for Gaza, and the Iraqi militias attacked American forces more than 160 times between 7 October 2023 and early February 2024. [cite:hz10_aje_truce] [cite:hz10_at_farida] In 2026 Secretary of State Rubio said, "We knew that there was going to be an Israeli action," and that it would bring attacks on American forces; Reuters reported, from anonymous sources, that Netanyahu urged President Trump by phone less than 48 hours before the strikes. [cite:ir14_aje_rubio] [cite:hz12b_reuters_call] Joe Kent, director of the National Counterterrorism Center, resigned on 17 March 2026, writing that the war began "due to pressure from Israel and its powerful American lobby". [cite:ir14_fox_kent]

The case against rests on documents too. Every American operation was ordered by an American president: Trump said, "I might have forced Israel's hand," and Under Secretary of Defense Elbridge Colby said, "The president made our decision." [cite:ir14_sn_forced] President Bush refused an Israeli request to strike Iran in 2008, and by Netanyahu's own memoir President Obama declined one in 2013, and by one analyst's account Israel called off strikes in June 2025 when Trump asked it to; the same analyst cites Rubio's later statement that the war "had to happen no matter what". [cite:hz12_cato_hoffman] [cite:ir14_fdd_goren] Trump called Kent "very weak on security"; Senator Mitch McConnell said his letter was tainted by "virulent anti-Semitism", and J Street's Ilan Goldenberg said the claim played on "the worst antisemitic tropes" and that responsibility lay with Trump. [cite:ir14_npr_kent] [cite:ir14_fox_kent] Iran and forces the United States says it armed had also attacked Americans long before 2023, by the Pentagon's own unpublished count killing at least 603 in Iraq. [cite:ir14_mt_603] [scene:hz12_costs|The American bill, 1983–2026] Netanyahu called the claim that he dragged the United States in "fake news". [cite:hz12b_mediaite_bibi]

The deaths listed above fit neither side cleanly. The 2024 deaths came in theatres the Gaza war activated, but in operations that American commanders chose: interdicting Iranian arms at sea, patrolling the Red Sea, holding an outpost in Jordan, and building a pier for aid to Gaza. [cite:gi16_mt_seals] [cite:gi16a_tp_aregbesola] [cite:hz10_abc_tower22] [cite:gi16_bdn_stanley] Most of the dead, 21 of 28, fell in the 2026 war, which an American president ordered on a timetable his Secretary of State tied to a planned Israeli action. [cite:ow7_inq_deaths2026] [cite:ir14_aje_rubio] [scene:hz12_wars|Congress and the 2026 war] What the record supports is that Israeli action and advocacy shaped when the United States fought; no document shows that an American president was compelled to decide that it would.
