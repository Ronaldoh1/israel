The Heavenly Tablets and the True Year

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

The Book of Jubilees presents itself as the history of "the division of the days of the law and of the testimony", dictated to Moses on Sinai by the angel of the presence, who is told to "Write for Moses from the beginning of creation". [cite:vt27_ccel_jub1] Its central claim is that events on earth are already written "on the tablets of heaven". [cite:vt27_je_jubilees] It also insists on a calendar: a year of 364 days, in four quarters of thirteen weeks, and it rejects the lunar calendar and its added months "in the strongest terms". [cite:vt27_je_jubilees] The astronomical chapters of Enoch, revealed by the angel Uriel, describe the same 364-day year. [cite:vt27_ccel_enoch1]

A 364-day year divides exactly into fifty-two weeks. In such a calendar every festival falls on the same day of the week every year, and the year itself is an image of heaven's order: unchanging, divisible, whole. Jubilees rejects the other calendar in the strongest terms, and it teaches that Israel keeps the Sabbath together with the highest angels: time on earth is kept in step with heaven. [cite:vt27_je_jubilees]

The inner reading takes the heavenly tablets as the rule "as above, so below" written as history. What is written above is lived below, and the calendar is how a life keeps in step with what is written. Keeping the right days is then less a matter of dates than of attention: a person whose days follow the pattern above lives in time with it, and one whose days follow some other rhythm does not.

The record shows that calendars are also instruments of power, and the Ethiopian church learned it directly. When the Jesuit patriarch Afonso Mendes took control of the church in the 1620s, part of what he imposed was the abolition of its Saturday Sabbath and its own fasts. [cite:vt27_wiki_mendes] Part Five of this book followed that attempt and its failure. Read alongside Jubilees, it shows the same pattern at two levels: whoever sets the rhythm of days sets the shape of a people's inner life, and a church that kept its own calendar was keeping its own time with heaven.

The record also limits the reading. Jubilees was written around 160 to 150 before Christ, and its insistence on the 364-day year was a position taken in its own time. [cite:vt27_je_jubilees] Fragments of about fifteen scrolls of Jubilees were found at Qumran. [cite:vt27_je_jubilees] Its calendar was a programme for a community, not only a symbol. The inner reading is offered beside it, not in its place.
