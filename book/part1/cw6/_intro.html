Stage Five ended in Dallas on 22 November 1963. Lyndon Johnson inherited a Middle East policy that had been built to avoid choices. [cite:wiki_warren_commission] Israel had received its first American military aid in 1962, as loans, and the Hawk missile, but no American tanks; its embassy was already pressing for them, on the strength of Eshkol's letter of 4 November. [cite:merip_us_aid_israel_1990] [cite:wiki_israel_us_military_relations] [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_d7] Eshkol had agreed in August to regular American visits to Dimona in principle, without committing to the twice-yearly visits Washington wanted. [cite:rs_cw5_nsa_letters] Washington fed Egypt under a multi-year food agreement signed in October 1962. [cite:rs_cw5_frus18_d70] About 30,000 Egyptian troops, by Bruce Riedel's account, were fighting in Yemen by March 1963, against royalists whom Saudi Arabia supplied. [cite:rs_cw5_riedel_yemen] [cite:rs_cw6_fp_yemen] The Ba'th had held power in Damascus since the coup of 8 March 1963. [cite:rs_cw6_locsyria] Ahmad al-Shuqairi had been chosen by the Arab League Council as Palestine's representative at the League, but the PLO did not yet exist. [cite:rs_cw5_palquest_shuqayri] Israel's project to draw water from the Jordan was under way by the end of the year. [cite:rs_cw6_palquest_chron] Inside Israel, military government still ruled Arab citizens in the areas it covered, though personal travel permits had been cancelled on 21 October. [cite:rs_cw6_akevot_gl17005] On the Egyptian side of the southern line stood the United Nations Emergency Force, and only there, because Israel had refused it on its side of the line. [cite:rs_cw6_a6730] The State Department's historians give Johnson's method a name: keeping the Arab-Israeli dispute "in the icebox." [cite:rs_cw6_frus18_sum_a]

This stage asks how that order gave way, in three and a half years, to the crisis of May 1967, and through whose decisions. The answer the documents give is not one escalation but several, running side by side and feeding one another: an arms race that turned the United States from a refuser of tanks into Israel's direct supplier; a quarrel over the Jordan's waters; a Palestinian armed campaign sponsored by one Arab government and launched from the territory of two others; Israeli reprisals; a radical government in Damascus and a defence pact that tied it to Cairo; and, in May 1967, a Soviet warning of an Israeli concentration against Syria that the United Nations' observers could not find. The stage asks with equal weight what each government believed it was doing, and what the record cannot show of their intentions.

The cast is wide. In Washington stood Johnson and his advisers Bundy, Komer and Rostow, Secretary of State Rusk, Secretary of Defense McNamara, and Ambassador Barbour in Tel Aviv. In Israel stood Levi Eshkol, Abba Eban, the chief of staff Yitzhak Rabin, and, outside the government until June 1967, Ben-Gurion's new list, Rafi, with Moshe Dayan and Shimon Peres. [cite:rs_cw6_idi_rafi] In Cairo stood Nasser, his chief of staff Muhammad Fawzi and his foreign minister Riad; in Amman, King Hussein; in Damascus, after February 1966, Salah Jadid, Nureddin Atassi and Hafiz al-Assad. [cite:rs_cw6_locsyria] Shuqairi's Palestine Liberation Organization and Fatah's military wing, al-Asifa, acted on their own account. Bonn acted through Ludwig Erhard, East Berlin through Walter Ulbricht, Moscow through Kosygin, and the United Nations through U Thant and his commanders in the field. Israel's Supreme Court, the Atomic Energy Commission in Washington and the Bank of Israel each left their own record.

The sections follow that story roughly as it unfolded. They open with [scene:cw6_johnson_eshkol|Johnson and Eshkol], and with the Arab summits of 1964 and the founding of the PLO. The Jews of Egypt and Morocco after Suez, the water war, and the al-Ard movement and the Yeredor case follow. Bonn's secret tanks lead to recognition, and Fatah's campaign begins as Washington makes its first direct tank sale. The shortage of uranium at a plant in Pennsylvania, Syria's radical turn, and the Skyhawks and Dimona visits of 1966 come next. The raid on Samu and the end of military government close 1966, and a section on Israel's politics and recession shows the country that entered the crisis. Egypt's war in Yemen, the air battle of 7 April 1967 and [scene:cw6_may_crisis|the crisis of May] bring the stage to [scene:cw6_tiran_june4|its last thirteen days], ending with Israel's cabinet decision of 4 June 1967.
