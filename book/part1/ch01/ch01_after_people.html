The People Since 1948

In the war of 1947–49 roughly 700,000 to 750,000 Palestinians were displaced. [cite:wiki_nakba] The UN resolved in December 1948 that those wishing to return should be permitted to; UNRWA began operating on 1 May 1950 and took over the camps. [cite:gi18_jvl_res194] [cite:gz_palquest_refugees] The Arabs who stayed inside Israel became citizens, but lived under military government until 1966, and the Absentees' Property Law of 1950 took the property even of those who had stayed in the country but were away from their homes. In 1956, at Kafr Qasim, border police shot dead 49 villagers returning from their fields. [cite:wiki_kafr_qasim_massacre] [cite:rs_rd6_fischbach_2003] [cite:wiki_present_absentee]

Jews came in the same years from the Arab world. After the Farhud of 1941 and an Iraqi law of March 1950 that let Jews leave if they gave up their citizenship, Operation Ezra and Nehemiah flew more than 120,000 of them to Israel. [cite:jl_wiki_iraq] [cite:jl_jvl_ezra] The chapter Two Displacements sets out why the two movements were not one even exchange.

Today about 14.8 million Palestinians live in four legal situations, from Israeli citizenship to refugee registration, and at the end of 2023 Arabs made up 21.3 percent of Israel's population. [cite:alquds_pcbs_refugees] [cite:rs_ch01_jiis_pop] On 19 July 2018 the Knesset passed the Nation-State Law by 62 votes to 55. [cite:asianews_nsl_passed] In Gaza, more than two-thirds of the people on the eve of the war of 2023 were registered refugees of 1948 and their descendants. [cite:gz_afp_camps]
