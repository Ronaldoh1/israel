Part Twelve established four things. The Holy See's own rules placed abuse trials under the secret of the Holy Office from 1922 and under the pontifical secret from 2001, said nothing to bishops about the police until the 2010 guide told them to follow civil law, and lifted the secret only on 17 December 2019, while still imposing no reporting duty where a state imposed none. [cite:vt12_va_crimen1962] [cite:vt12_va_ddg2001] [cite:vt12_va_cdfguide2010] [cite:vt12_vn_rescript2019] The national reports, each counted by its own method, found allegations against about 4 per cent of US priests (1950–2002), 4.4 per cent of German clerics in the files (1946–2014) and 7 per cent of Australian priests (1950–2010), while France's survey estimated 216,000 victims of clergy and religious since 1950, a figure that cannot be added to any other. [cite:vt12_am_johnjay2004] [cite:vt12_ba_mhg2018] [cite:vt12_tablet_rc7pct] [cite:vt12_cna_ciase2022] Rome disciplined Maciel in 2006 and McCarrick in 2019, published its own McCarrick report in 2020, and saw Francis retract his defence of a Chilean bishop in 2018, while compensation was paid by dioceses, 44 American Catholic bodies having sought bankruptcy protection by March 2026, and suits against the Holy See itself failed on immunity.
