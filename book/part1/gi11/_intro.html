Part Eleven follows the religious currents that gave the claim to the whole land its most uncompromising form, and the religious voices that rejected it. It traces the line from Rabbi Abraham Isaac Kook and his Mercaz HaRav yeshiva to his son Zvi Yehuda Kook, whose teaching that no government may give up any part of the land inspired Gush Emunim, and from there to the Religious Zionism party and Otzma Yehudit in Netanyahu's government, with their standing on the eve of the October 2026 election. It then turns to the Temple Mount: the arrangement Israel and Jordan have maintained there since 1967, the movements that seek a Third Temple, and the changes in practice between 2023 and 2026. Last, and briefly, it sets out what American Christian Zionist organisations and one US ambassador have said about the land. Throughout, the claims are attributed to named rabbis, movements, parties and officials, and set beside the many religious Jews and Christians, and their institutions, who held otherwise.
