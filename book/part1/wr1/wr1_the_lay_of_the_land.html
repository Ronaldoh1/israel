The Lay of the Land, 1516–1979

From 1516 to 1917 the Ottoman Empire ruled most of the Arab Middle East as provinces of one state, with no international borders between them. Under the millet system, recognised religious communities, Muslims, the Christian churches and Jews, ran their own religious law, courts and schools. [cite:w_ottoman_millet] The order had real costs, heavy taxes, conscription and second-class status for non-Muslims, but Syria, Iraq, Lebanon, Jordan and Israel did not yet exist. Iran, a separate Persian state, was never Ottoman.

The route to India, then oil

Britain bought Egypt's shares in the Suez Canal Company in 1875 and occupied Egypt in 1882, before oil was found anywhere in the region. [cite:w_suez_company] [cite:w_brit_occupation_egypt] Palestine had no commercial oil; British strategists valued it as a buffer for the canal and the route to India, one of the motives historians record behind the Balfour Declaration. [cite:w_balfour_decl] Oil raised the stakes: a concession over most of Iran's oil in 1901, the strike at Masjed Soleyman in 1908, a British government controlling stake in 1914 to fuel the navy, Kirkuk in Iraq in 1927 and Dammam in Saudi Arabia in 1938. [cite:w_darcy] [cite:w_apoc] [cite:w_kirkuk_1927] [cite:w_dammam7] By the mid-1930s a pipeline carried Kirkuk's oil to Haifa, in British-ruled Palestine. [cite:w_mosul_haifa]

The carve-up

In 1915 and 1916 the McMahon–Hussein letters promised British support for Arab independence in return for a revolt against the Ottomans. [cite:w_mcmahon] In May 1916 the secret Sykes–Picot agreement divided much of the same land into British and French zones. [cite:w_sykes_picot] At San Remo in 1920 Britain received Iraq and Palestine, with Transjordan, and France received Syria and Lebanon; the Arab kingdom proclaimed in Damascus was crushed by French forces at Maysalun that July. [cite:w_maysalun] Revolts followed in Iraq in 1920 and Syria from 1925 to 1927. [cite:w_iraq_revolt_1920] [cite:w_great_syrian_revolt] At Cairo in 1921 Britain made Faisal king of Iraq and Abdullah emir of Transjordan. [cite:w_cairo1921] In Iran a constitutional revolution produced a parliament in 1906, which Britain and Russia overrode when they divided the country into zones of influence in 1907. [cite:w_iran_constrev] [cite:w_anglo_russian_1907]

After 1948

The war of 1947–49 displaced about 700,000 Palestinians. In the decades after it, most of the Jewish communities of the Arab world and Iran, some 800,000 to 900,000 people by common estimates, left through emigration, pressure, expulsion and persecution. [cite:w_jewish_exodus_arab] Defeat discredited the governments that fought: Syria's fell to a coup in March 1949, Egypt's monarchy in 1952, Iraq's in 1958. [cite:w_1949_syria_coup] [cite:w_1952_egypt] [cite:w_1958_iraq] In Iran, unrelated to Israel, the CIA and MI6 removed Prime Minister Mosaddegh in 1953 after he nationalised the oil industry; the Shah's rule that followed ended in the revolution of 1979. [cite:w_iran_oil_nationalization] [cite:w_1953_coup] [cite:w_iran_revolution]

The record supports a layered answer to the question "why is the region unstable?" The borders were drawn from outside between 1916 and 1922; revolts against that order came before Israel existed; oil made the region a prize; 1948 then added a permanent refugee question, the departure of most Arab Jewry and a state of war. A single-cause story, in either direction, leaves out part of the record.
