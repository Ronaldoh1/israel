The Pipeline: From Firms and Think Tanks to Office

Ideas reach government through people, and people reach government through appointments that are announced, confirmed and recorded. This section follows the people from the network mapped in this book who took public posts in 2025 and 2026, and the conservative institutions that trained or published some of them. It then asks the question that matters most for the book's hypothesis: which of these officials has cited Curtis Yarvin or Nick Land, which has said he agrees with them, and which has neither. The appointees who came from PayPal and from Thiel's own firms are told in full in [scene:de13_where_they_sit_now|Part Thirteen]; they are summarised here only so that the whole pipeline can be seen at once.

The venture firms

On 22 December 2024 the president-elect announced a set of technology appointments in a single day. Two came from Andreessen Horowitz. Scott Kupor, the firm's managing partner and first employee, was nominated to direct the Office of Personnel Management, the agency that runs the federal civil service; Sriram Krishnan, a general partner, was named senior policy adviser for artificial intelligence in the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy. [cite:de16_cnbc_picks] The Senate confirmed Kupor on 9 July 2025, and he was sworn in on 15 July. At his hearing he had said that "protecting data privacy is incredibly important" and that restructuring should be "surgical." [cite:de16_fedsmith_kupor] By December 2025, as director, he reported that about 317,000 federal employees had left government in 2025, including about 144,000 deferred resignations and about 24,000 involuntary separations. [cite:us2_bloomberg_317k] What that meant for the civil service is told in [scene:us2_integrity|the US vs Israel volume] and in [scene:de20_the_cuts|Part Twenty].

The same announcement named Ken Howery, co-founder of PayPal and Founders Fund, as ambassador to Denmark, and Michael Kratsios, a former chief of staff at Thiel Capital, as director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy; David Sacks, PayPal's former chief operating officer, had been named the White House "AI and crypto czar" earlier that month. [cite:de16_cnbc_picks] [cite:de16_fortune_thielnet] Jim O'Neill, a former chief executive of the Thiel Foundation and managing director at Mithril Capital, was confirmed as deputy secretary of Health and Human Services on 5 June 2025, also served as acting director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention from 28 August 2025, left both posts on 13 February 2026, and was nominated to direct the National Science Foundation on 2 March 2026; no confirmation is recorded as of October 2026. [cite:de16_wiki_oneill] Sacks stepped down from the czar role on 26 March 2026, when his days as a special government employee ran out, and stayed on as co-chair of the president's science and technology council. [cite:de16_reuters_sacks] The vice president himself had worked for Thiel at Mithril, as [scene:de12_the_thiel_years|Part Twelve] records. [cite:de16_fortune_thielnet] Marc Andreessen took no post but helped recruit for the Department of Government Efficiency. [cite:de16_fortune_doge]

Claremont, Hillsdale and the national conservatives

The second channel ran through conservative institutions rather than venture firms, and its clearest case is Michael Anton. In September 2016, writing under the pseudonym Publius Decius Mus in the online edition of the Claremont Review of Books, published by the Claremont Institute, he wrote "The Flight 93 Election," which compared a Clinton victory to passengers failing to rush the cockpit of the hijacked plane. [cite:de16_wiki_anton] He served on the National Security Council staff from February 2017 to April 2018, then taught at Hillsdale College's Kirby Center in Washington and became a senior fellow of the Claremont Institute. [cite:de16_wiki_anton] His 2020 book, The Stakes, discussed a "red caesarism," one-man rule on the right, as a possibility for American politics. [cite:de16_wiki_anton] He served as the State Department's Director of Policy Planning from 20 January to 15 September 2025, and took part in that year's negotiations with Iran. [cite:de16_wiki_anton]

Anton's documented contacts with Yarvin are two. In May 2021 the two recorded a conversation for the Claremont Institute's podcast, The American Mind, ostensibly about Anton's book; the Guardian later reported that Yarvin used it to discuss a president declaring a state of emergency in his inaugural address, and Anton's defenders replied that the newspaper's account did not match the recording. [cite:de5_powerline] [cite:de5_brewminate] And in January 2025 Yarvin said he had recently had lunch with Anton "at a coffee shop across the street from the State Department." [cite:de5_politico_ward] Both are contacts, the first by recording and the second on Yarvin's word. Neither is a statement by Anton that he holds Yarvin's views, and none has been found.

The institutions also met in a document. In June 2022 the Edmund Burke Foundation, which organises the National Conservatism conferences, published a statement of principles; its signers included "Peter Thiel / Founders Fund," "Michael Anton / Hillsdale College Kirby Center" and the foundation's chairman, Yoram Hazony. [cite:de16_natcon_signers] The statement commits its signers to "the Constitution of 1787" and a state "subject to constitutional restraints and a division of powers," which is the opposite of the end state in Yarvin's texts. [cite:de16_tac_natcon] Neither Yarvin nor Land signed it. [cite:de16_natcon_signers] No Thiel money to the conferences is documented, as [scene:de14_network_map|Part Fourteen] records.

Citing Yarvin, and following him

A pipeline of people is not a pipeline of ideas, and the book's ladder of support exists to keep the two apart. Placed on it by their own words, the people in this section fall into three groups. One person in office has cited Yarvin by name: J.D. Vance, in 2021, before he held any office, when he said "there's this guy, Curtis Yarvin, who has written about some of these things" and called for firing the civil service. [cite:de5_wbur_vance] That is on-record engagement, not on-record agreement, and he has not repeated it. A second group has documented contact with Yarvin or his circle without any statement of agreement: Anton by conversation and lunch, Andreessen as investor and self-described friend, and Srinivasan by a 2013 email. [cite:de5_powerline] [cite:de5_politico_ward] [cite:de5_sfstandard] [cite:de16_bi_balaji] The third group, which includes Kupor, Krishnan, Kratsios, O'Neill, Howery and Sacks, has no documented statement about Yarvin or Land at all. To connect them to the texts requires adjacency, the rung the book calls inference only.

The last row is the one most often used to close the gap. Yarvin said in 2025 that his influence ran "more through the kids in the administration," and an anonymous adviser to the Department of Government Efficiency told the Washington Post that it was an open secret that everyone designing policy had read him. [cite:de6_cnn_2025] [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] That is a claim of influence from its beneficiary and from one unnamed source, and the book grades it as reported, not documented ([scene:de5_the_public_years|Part Five]). Having read Yarvin is also not the same as following him: the record of DOGE, set out in Book Three, will be tested on what was done, not on what its staff may have read.

The pipeline, then, is documented as a pipeline of people from a small number of firms and institutions into offices that matter for this book's worst case: the civil service's personnel office, the science office, health and the State Department's planning staff. [cite:de16_cnbc_picks] [cite:de16_wiki_anton] What is not documented is that these officials carried Yarvin's or Land's program with them. The one public citation is Vance's in 2021; everything else is contact, friendship, investment or employment, which the ladder records for what it is.
