The March at the Fence, and May 2021

The Great March of Return, 2018–2019. From 30 March 2018 Gazans gathered, week after week, at protest camps along the fence with Israel, in protests that took their name from the demand for refugees' return. A UN commission of inquiry examined the protests through 31 December 2018. It counted 189 Palestinians killed at the protest sites, 183 of them by live ammunition, including 35 children, two clearly marked journalists and three clearly marked paramedics, and 6,106 wounded by live fire. One Israeli soldier was killed on a protest day, outside the protest sites, and four were wounded at them. [cite:gz_un_coi2019]

The commission found that the demonstrations "were civilian in nature, with clearly stated political aims," and that there were reasonable grounds to believe Israeli snipers had shot journalists, health workers, children and people with disabilities "knowing they were clearly recognizable as such"; the violations, it said, "may constitute war crimes or crimes against humanity." It acknowledged "some acts of significant violence" by demonstrators and that some were members of armed groups, but found that those killed and injured had not been taking a direct part in hostilities or posing an imminent threat. It also found that Hamas, as the authority in Gaza, had "failed to prevent" the incendiary kites and balloons launched from the strip, which burned land and frightened civilians in southern Israel. [cite:gz_un_coi2019] Israel refused to take part in the inquiry. Its acting foreign minister, Yisrael Katz, said that "Israel totally rejects the report", called it "hostile, false and incorrect," and placed the responsibility on Hamas: "Whoever pushes the residents of Gaza to the fences, including women and children, is the Hamas organization." [cite:gz_cnn_katz2019]

May 2021. The last of these wars began in Jerusalem. Palestinians in the Sheikh Jarrah neighbourhood had been protesting the expected eviction of six families, and on 7 and again on 10 May Israeli police raided the al-Aqsa compound; the second raid left about 300 Palestinians and 21 police officers injured; police said protesters had thrown stones and fireworks at them. Hamas demanded that Israel withdraw its police from the compound and from Sheikh Jarrah by six that evening, and when the deadline passed it and Islamic Jihad fired rockets at Israel. [cite:gz_wiki_2021] In eleven days of fighting, according to the UN, at least 256 Palestinians were killed in Gaza, 66 of them children, while Israel said it had killed 200 militants; at least 13 people were killed in Israel, two of them children. About 4,360 rockets were fired from Gaza, some 680 of which fell short inside the strip, and Israel's Iron Dome intercepted about 95 percent of those heading toward populated areas. On 15 May an Israeli strike destroyed the al-Jalaa tower, which housed the offices of Al Jazeera and the Associated Press; the army said Hamas military intelligence was based there, a claim the AP asked it to prove. An Egyptian-mediated ceasefire took effect on 21 May, and both sides claimed victory. [cite:gz_wiki_2021]

Between the wars the closure stayed in place, and after each one the reconstruction of what had been destroyed passed through the same crossings and permits that Part Four described; Part Eight follows that money. The war that began on 7 October 2023 was of a different order, and Parts Six and Seven are given to it.
