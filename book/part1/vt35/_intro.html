Part Thirty-Four asks whether the powers allied with institutional Christianity suppressed the sun gods of the peoples they conquered, and answers place by place, naming who acted each time. The first section follows Inti, the sun of the Inca: the gold stripped from his temple in Cusco in 1533, the Dominican convent built on its walls, the festival of Inti Raymi suppressed under Spanish rule and rebuilt in 1944, the seizure of the sacred sun image in 1572, the suppression of the Taki Onqoy movement, and the sun that Andean Catholics still greet at Qoyllurit'i. The second turns to Mexico and the Maya: the Mexica sun religion and the sacrifice it demanded, the razing of the Templo Mayor and the cathedral raised from its stones, the Sun Stone buried by an archbishop and later displayed on the cathedral, the Maya astronomical books burned and the four that survive, and the communities that joined the sun to Christ. The third tests the claim worldwide, in Rome, Egypt, Portuguese Goa, the American Plains and Japan, where the actors included emperors, crowns, the United States government and a shogunate that suppressed Christians, and grades the claim that every saviour is a sun god.
