## PASS 1 — SURFACE ENUMERATION

### Founding & funding
- Founded 2003, post-9/11, by Thiel + 4 co-founders. Origin story (company's own telling, corroborated
  by multiple outlets): Thiel wanted to apply PayPal's fraud-detection pattern-recognition to
  counter-terrorism finance tracking.
- Thiel personally funded initial launch. Silicon Valley VCs (incl. Sequoia/Michael Moritz) reportedly
  passed. **In-Q-Tel** (CIA's venture capital arm, est. 1999) invested ~$2M starting 2004-2005 — this
  is CONFIRMED across Britannica, Built In, Fast Company, The Intercept, multiple independent sources.
- The Intercept (2017), citing internal Palantir documents obtained via reporting: intelligence-
  community analysts didn't just fund Palantir, they iteratively collaborated on building the software
  over ~3 years. This is a stronger claim than "just an investor" — CONFIRMED via document-based
  reporting, not just inference.
- Product line: **Gotham** (government/intelligence/law enforcement), **Foundry** (commercial),
  historically also Metropolis (finance/quant).

### The Epstein/Valar thread — SOURCE HYGIENE FLAG
- Multiple low-quality sources (Substack blogs, "darkside.report", "ask the q") assert flatly that
  "Palantir was financed by Epstein" or that Epstein "co-owned" Palantir. **This is not what the
  documented record shows and should not be written this way.**
- What IS documented (NYT original reporting, corroborated by Forbes, CNBC, Washington Blade,
  Byline Times, Gizmodo — convergent independent reporting, high confidence):
  - Epstein invested $40M in 2015-2016 into **Valar Ventures**, a fund co-founded by Thiel but
    legally distinct from Palantir. Now worth ~$170M, the largest asset in Epstein's estate.
  - Thiel advised Epstein *against* investing in Palantir directly, telling him the price was
    "below market" (per DOJ email files cited by Forbes).
  - Nov 2025 House Oversight document release: correspondence shows Epstein invited Thiel to
    "the Caribbean"; Thiel's rep says he never went to the island. No evidence of Thiel
    involvement in Epstein's crimes (Washington Blade, explicitly).
  - Feb 2026: a former Israeli PM (Ehud Barak) reportedly characterized Epstein and Thiel as
    "owners" of the fund together; Thiel's spokesman denies this, confirms Epstein was a limited
    partner (Byline Times). This is a live dispute over characterization, not settled fact either
    way — CONTESTED.
  - Separately documented (Byline Times, corroborated): Palantir hired Peter Mandelson's lobbying
    firm Global Counsel in 2018 to help secure UK government contracts. Mandelson separately has
    documented Epstein ties (correspondence 2009-2010, per the same reporting). This is a real,
    citable network node — UK lobbying access via a figure with independent Epstein exposure —
    distinct from and stronger than the vague "Epstein financed Palantir" claim.
- **VERDICT:** "Epstein had a $40M stake in a Thiel-controlled venture fund, later revealed via
  document releases, run through a trust set up two days before his death" — Documentary 3,
  CONFIRMED, multiple independent outlets. "Epstein financed/owns Palantir" — Documentary 0
  against (Palantir has denied it, and the underlying investment is in a different legal entity)
  — do not write as fact. The Mandelson/Global Counsel lobbying connection is a separate,
  well-documented node worth keeping for the network map.

## PASS 3 — RECURSIVE DESCENT ON HIGH-PRIORITY NODES

### Node: New Orleans predictive policing (2012-2018)
- CONFIRMED, high documentary support: multiple independent investigations (The Verge's original
  2018 exposé, ACLU, Privacy International, VentureBeat, nola.com, The Lens) converge on the same
  facts.
- Structured as a "philanthropic donation" (no cost to the city), which let it bypass the normal
  public procurement process and city council oversight — even city council members didn't know
  the program existed for six years (2012-2018).
- Mechanism: social network analysis combining NOPD field interview cards (a program itself
  described as "possibly unconstitutional"), gang databases, social media, criminal history, and
  70,000+ police field interviews to build a target list of ~3,900-4,000 people.
- A defendant's trial (Evans "Easy" Lewis) involved 60,000+ pages of Palantir-generated documents
  with no disclosure of the Palantir partnership to the defense — a genuine due-process concern,
  not a speculative one.
- Ended February 2018 after press exposure; Mayor Landrieu declined to renew; successor Mayor
  Cantrell confirmed she would not revive it.
- This same pattern — access via "philanthropic" relationships with police departments — is
  documented as predating New Orleans, with prior LAPD and NYPD relationships also built through
  charitable-giving structures (Harvard CRCL journal), which is a real precedent worth checking
  further before generalizing into a "pattern" claim (currently Structural axis: 1 — one
  documented recurrence beyond New Orleans; needs a second independent case before it clears the
  ≥2-precedent bar for a pattern claim).
- **VERDICT:** CONFIRMED at the New Orleans level. The "recurring playbook" claim (philanthropy as
  a procurement-bypass mechanism used repeatedly) is currently STRUCTURAL INFERENCE, not yet
  CONFIRMED — LAPD/NYPD claims need their own documentary check before this generalizes.

### Node: ICE / DHS (ImmigrationOS, ICM)
- Already logged in cross-cutting supply-chain-adjacent research: $30M April 2025 ICE contract for
  ImmigrationOS, folded into a pre-existing sole-source Investigative Case Management (ICM) contract
  running since 2014, now ballooned past $145M, through Sept 2027 (ACLU).
- ACLU documentation is specific and citable — not vague "surveillance" claims but named contract
  values, dates, and stated purposes (tracking self-deportations, arrest targeting).
- Illinois/Chicago lawsuit (Jan 2026) against ICE's Mobile Fortify app — targets ICE's use, not
  Palantir directly; Palantir is the platform vendor, not the named defendant in that suit. Keep
  these distinct in the writeup.
- Palantir's own public position (Karp, Reuters interview, Feb 2026): defended the technology,
  claimed unspecified "safeguards," declined to discuss Minnesota ICE operations specifically amid
  protests following two U.S. citizen shootings.
- **VERDICT:** CONFIRMED — contract values, dates, and stated scope are well-documented via ACLU
  and USAspending.gov figures, not merely alleged.

### Node: UK — NHS Federated Data Platform
- Nov 2023: NHS England signs £330M contract (some reporting says up to £480M joint-contract
  figure with partners Accenture/PwC/NECS/Carnall Farrar) with Palantir for the Federated Data
  Platform.
- Origin predates this: March 2020, Palantir got its first NHS foothold via a £1 COVID-19 Data
  Store contract, repeatedly extended through Jan 2023 while the FDP procurement was finalized —
  a documented pattern of incremental access-widening from a nominal/loss-leader entry point.
  This is citable and specific (not just "mission creep" as vague rhetoric — there's an actual
  contract-extension paper trail via The Lowdown/NHS reporting).
- Opposition is broad and organizationally diverse: Amnesty International, Medact (health workers'
  org), Good Law Project, Privacy International, Corporate Watch — a genuine coalition, not a
  single advocacy group, which strengthens the CONTESTED/documented-opposition framing.
- Good Law Project pre-action legal challenge (2024) over redacted contract terms — the public
  cannot see how patient data handling is specified. This transparency gap is itself a documented,
  citable fact (a redacted public contract), independent of the human-rights allegations.
- Jan 2026: 22 MPs spoke against the contract in a Westminster debate after ~7,000 constituent
  contacts — a documented political fact (parliamentary record), not an activist claim.
- **VERDICT:** CONFIRMED — contract existence, value, redaction dispute, and parliamentary
  opposition are all documentary, not inferential.

### Node: Israel — IDF contract and the "genocide" framing
- January 2024: Palantir signed a contract with the Israeli military for what it called
  "war-related missions" — CONFIRMED, reported across multiple outlets, not disputed by Palantir.
- Amnesty International and, per Medact's briefing, the UN Special Rapporteur on the Occupied
  Palestinian Territories have both characterized Israel's Gaza campaign as genocide and named
  Palantir as a contributing enabler of "unlawful use of force."
- **This is a live, contested legal/political characterization, not a settled documentary fact.**
  Genocide is a specific legal term under international law; its application to the Gaza campaign
  is asserted by Amnesty, the UN Special Rapporteur, and others, and disputed by Israel and allied
  governments. The correct register here is CONTESTED: name that Amnesty/the UN Special Rapporteur
  have made this characterization, name that it is disputed, and do not write it as flat authorial
  fact ("Palantir enables genocide") — that would be an overclaim not supported by an
  international legal finding, however credible the accusing bodies are.
- What IS cleanly documentary: the contract exists, its stated purpose ("war-related missions") is
  Palantir's own language, and named human rights organizations have formally objected on that
  basis. That's enough to carry the chapter without reaching for the contested legal label.
- **VERDICT:** Contract — CONFIRMED. "Genocide"/"enabler of unlawful force" — CONTESTED, attributed
  to named bodies, not asserted as fact.

### Node: Palantir's own rebuttals (fairness check — the axiom cuts both ways)
- Palantir has published detailed, specific rebuttals to both the EFF's Jan 2026 report and a May
  2025 NYT article, denying: (a) any "master database" project unifying federal agency data, (b)
  proposing any government-wide surveillance "master list," (c) several predictive-policing and
  transparency allegations it calls "debunked."
- These rebuttals are on Palantir's own blog — primary-source but not independent, and they
  selectively characterize critics' claims as "previously rebutted" without always specifying what
  in the rebuttal actually resolves the underlying factual dispute (e.g., the New Orleans and NHS
  facts above are not seriously disputed by Palantir; the "master database" claim is one Palantir
  specifically and directly denies with more force).
- **For fairness/CONTESTED-handling:** any claim in the eventual chapter that overlaps with what
  Palantir has specifically and directly denied (the "master list"/unified federal database claim)
  should be flagged as CONTESTED with Palantir's denial included, not asserted flatly — this is
  different from the New Orleans/NHS contract facts, which Palantir has not factually disputed.
