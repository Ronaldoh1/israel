Part Five covers the war that began with Hezbollah's cross-border raid of 12 July 2006 and ended with a ceasefire 34 days later. It sets out what each side said it was doing and what it did: Israel's strikes on Lebanon's roads, bridges, airport and Beirut's southern suburbs, and Hezbollah's rockets on northern Israel. It gives the dead on both sides with the source and counting method of each figure, the Qana strike of 30 July, and the cluster munitions fired in the war's last three days, beside the findings of Human Rights Watch and the responses of the parties. It then turns to what came after: Security Council Resolution 1701 and the record of its terms, Israel's own Winograd inquiry, and the "Dahiya doctrine" that Israel's northern commander announced in 2008, which made the destruction of 2006 an explicit model for future wars.
