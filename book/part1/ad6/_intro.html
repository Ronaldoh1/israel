Part Six follows the asset at the centre of the American Dream: the home. It begins with the federal design that made ownership reachable on a wage, the long fixed-rate mortgage of the New Deal, and with the rules written into the same programmes that kept many families out of it: the HOLC maps and the FHA's underwriting manual, the local administration of the GI Bill, the covenants of Levittown and the zoning that still governs what can be built. It then measures what a home costs today in years of income, in monthly payments and in the age at which buyers first manage it, sets out what renters pay, and tests the claim that Wall Street is buying up the houses. It ends with the mechanisms through which housing takes from those who cannot own: eviction filings and the records they leave, tenant screening, the contract for deed and shared pricing software, and the count of those who end up with no home at all.
