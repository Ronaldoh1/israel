The Annexation

From Jericho to Amman. In September 1948 Egypt sponsored an All-Palestine Government in Gaza; on 30 September 1948, the day a Palestinian National Council met in Gaza, a rival congress in Amman pledged loyalty to King Abdullah, and on 1 December 1948 a congress of notables at Jericho proclaimed the union of Palestine and Transjordan. [cite:rs_rd6_shlaim_apg_1990] Abdullah renamed his country the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan in April 1949, held elections on both banks of the river in April 1950, and then formally annexed the West Bank. [cite:rs_rd6_loc_jordan] On 24 April 1950 the Jordanian parliament resolved in favour of "complete unity between the two banks of the Jordan, the eastern and western, and their union in one single state." [cite:rs_rd6_kramer_annexation]

Recognition. Almost no one recognized it. Britain recognized the union while excluding Jerusalem, and Iraq recognized it; whether Pakistan did is disputed, and the Library of Congress study names Britain and Pakistan as the only two. [cite:wb_wiki_annex] [cite:rs_rd6_loc_jordan] In May 1950 the Arab League's Political Committee found the annexation illegal, and Saudi Arabia, Lebanon, Syria and Egypt sought Jordan's expulsion; in June the League settled on treating Jordan as holding the territory in "trust" until a final settlement. [cite:wb_wiki_annex]

Citizens of Jordan. Alone among the Arab states that took in large numbers of refugees, Jordan gave them citizenship as a class, along with the West Bank's own people. A 1949 amendment to the nationality law effectively imposed Jordanian citizenship on local Palestinians and refugees alike, [cite:wb_wiki_annex] and the West and East Banks received equal representation in parliament, twenty seats each in 1950. [cite:wb_wiki_jo1950] The Nationality Law of 1954 granted citizenship to "any person who, not being Jewish, possessed Palestinian nationality before 15 May 1948" and lived in the kingdom. [cite:wb_palquest_nationality] The refugees of 1948 living in the kingdom thus became Jordanian citizens, while their claim to return, set out in the UN's Resolution 194, remained. [cite:rs_rd6_refworld_194]

East Jerusalem. Jordan held the Old City. King Hussein, who came to the throne in 1952 after his grandfather Abdullah was assassinated at al-Aqsa in 1951 and his father Talal abdicated, called East Jerusalem in 1953 the "alternative capital" of the kingdom. [cite:wb_wiki_annex] [cite:rs_rd6_loc_jordan] [cite:wb_loc_jordan] Jordan expelled the Jewish residents of East Jerusalem, much of the Old City's Jewish Quarter, including the Hurva Synagogue, was destroyed, and the committee the armistice had set up to arrange access to the holy places never met, so Israelis could not reach the Western Wall. [cite:wb_wiki_annex] [cite:rs_rd6_bpb_jerusalem]

The PLO, born in Jerusalem. It was in Jordanian Jerusalem, on 28 May 1964, under King Hussein's sponsorship, that the first Palestinian National Congress met, and on 2 June it announced the Palestine Liberation Organization. [cite:rs_cw6_palquest_chron] Its charter stated that the organization did "not exercise any regional sovereignty over the West Bank in the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan." [cite:rs_cw6_charter_1964] Two years later, after friction over the PLO's taxing of Palestinians and its distribution of arms in the West Bank and the camps, Hussein withdrew his support. [cite:rs_cw6_gs_jordan_a]
