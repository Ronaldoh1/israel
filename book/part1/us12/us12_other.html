Gas, Food, Medicines and Banks

Beyond tech and arms, four industries show how each state shapes markets that households pay into every day: energy, food, medicines and banking. In each the comparison runs on concentration, how few firms supply the market, and on what the state does about it. Israel's markets are small and were for decades dominated by a handful of business groups, a pattern its 2013 concentration law set out to break; America's are large but have consolidated in several of the same fields. [cite:us11_toi_concentration] [cite:us11_gl_concentration] The economy-wide picture is in the previous part. [scene:us11_concentration|Concentration] This section takes the industries one by one, with the series that exist and the snapshots where they do not.

Natural gas: two fields and an export trade

Israel's offshore gas fields came into production within a decade of each other. The Tamar field has produced gas since 2013, and the larger Leviathan field was to be developed by 2019 by the American company Noble Energy and Israel's Delek Group. [cite:us12_toi_gasframework] The two companies' hold on both fields became the central dispute. Israel's antitrust commissioner, David Gilo, resigned in May 2015 in opposition to their dominant position, and the government's gas framework of August 2015 required Delek to sell its holdings in Tamar and in the smaller Karish and Tanin fields within six years, and Noble to cut its share of Tamar to no more than 25 per cent, in return for regulated prices for six years and a ten-year commitment from the government. [cite:us12_toi_gasframework] Prime Minister Netanyahu said the deal would bring hundreds of billions of shekels to citizens; critics in the coalition, among them Finance Minister Moshe Kahlon, held out for stricter price controls. [cite:us12_toi_gasframework]

Production rose to 25.28 billion cubic metres (bcm) in 2023 and 27.38 bcm in 2024, by the Ministry of Energy's figures: 11.33 bcm from Leviathan, 10.09 bcm from Tamar and 5.96 bcm from Karish. Exports to Egypt and Jordan were 13.11 bcm in 2024, up from 11.56 bcm, so that about 48 per cent of output left the country. [cite:us12_enerdata_gas2024] In August 2025 the Leviathan partners signed a deal to sell Egypt about 130 bcm through 2040 for $35 billion, tripling previous export volumes; the largest partner, NewMed Energy, formerly Delek Drilling, held 45.3 per cent. [cite:us12_tml_leviathan] Netanyahu approved it on 17 December 2025 after holding it in September over a dispute with Egypt. [cite:gi15_aje_gas] Who gains from the gas depends on how its rent is split between the state and the partners, and how much is kept for domestic power at regulated prices; both were contested in 2015 and this book has not measured the outcome.

The American system runs at another scale. The Energy Information Administration put dry gas production at 103.2 billion cubic feet a day in 2024 and forecast 107.1 for 2025, record levels driven by export demand. [cite:us12_boe_eia] That is about 1,070 bcm a year, roughly 39 times Israel's output, by this book's conversion. In both countries private firms drill under state licence; the difference is that Israel's market had two dominant producers at the start, which made the terms of the licence a national political question.

Food: few suppliers in both

Israel's food prices are among the highest in the developed world, and its State Comptroller has tied them partly to concentration. In a January 2024 audit the comptroller found that Israel's overall price level was 27 per cent above the OECD average in purchasing-power terms and that food prices were 37 per cent above the OECD's and 51 per cent above the European Union's. The ten largest food suppliers held more than half the market for food and consumer products, and the comptroller found "abnormal profit" among the largest food and pharmaceutical importers. When butter was taken off tight price supervision in November 2021, the average price of imported butter rose by about 60 per cent. [cite:us12_comptroller_food] OECD data reported in July 2024 put Israel's food and drink prices 52 per cent above the developed-country average. [cite:us14_toi_food52] The comptroller recommended more imports as the structural remedy and price supervision only as an interim one. The 2011 cottage-cheese boycott, when a container fell by about a quarter after a two-week consumer revolt, is told in the part on the cost of living. [cite:us14_ap_cottage] [scene:us14_protests|The 2011 protests]

The American food system is concentrated at a different link, between farmers and processors. The four largest beef packers bought 36 per cent of the country's fed cattle in 1980, 81 per cent in 1995 and about 85 per cent in the latest data, according to the Department of Agriculture's Economic Research Service. Its review found that from 1980 to 2015 larger plants cut costs enough to offset packers' market power, but that after 2015 the gap between cattle and wholesale beef prices widened sharply. [cite:us12_ers_meatpacking] Concentration in Israel shows up mainly in what shoppers pay; in the American beef chain it shows up at least as much in what ranchers are paid.

Medicines: one company and one price system

Israel's pharmaceutical industry was for years dominated by one company, Teva, among the country's largest employers, with about 6,800 staff in Israel in 2017. Its purchase of Actavis Generics for about $40 billion left it with heavy debt, and on 14 December 2017 it announced about 14,000 job cuts, more than a quarter of its global workforce, with about 1,700 in Israel and the closure of its Jerusalem plant; about $10 billion of debt fell due within a year. The Histadrut labour federation called a nationwide general strike in response. [cite:us12_toi_teva2017] The episode showed how much of the Israeli economy can rest on a single firm. Medicines' prices are set very differently in the two countries. Israel's Ministry of Health fixes maximum prices from the three lowest of seven reference countries. [cite:us16_cwf_israel] In the United States, by RAND's 2022 comparison, drug prices were 278 per cent of those in 33 other high-income countries, though generics were cheaper in America than in most of them. [cite:us16_rand_drugs] The health part sets out what each system pays. [scene:us16_cost|What it costs]

Banks: five in Israel, thousands in America

Banking shows the starkest difference in structure. In Israel five banking groups, Leumi, Hapoalim, Discount, Mizrahi Tefahot and First International, held 98 per cent of the banking system's assets at the end of 2022, and the two largest 57 per cent. On 26 March 2024 the Competition Authority told them it planned to declare them a "concentration group" in retail banking, saying that barriers to entry and to switching banks "facilitate the stabilization of a coordinated equilibrium" among them. [cite:us12_globes_banks] The United States has thousands of banks, but far fewer than before: 14,483 federally insured commercial banks in 1984, 7,061 in 2008 and 4,027 in 2023, a fall of 72 per cent. [cite:us12_farmdoc_banks] One system is concentrated by size and history, the other is consolidating from a base of many small lenders.

Trade between them

The two economies have traded under a free trade agreement since 1 September 1985, the first the United States ever signed. In 2025 goods trade between them was $34.4 billion, with American exports of $13.7 billion and imports from Israel of $20.7 billion, and services trade added $19.7 billion, by the US Trade Representative's count. [cite:us11_ustr_israel] The flows traced in this part, Intel's exports from Kiryat Gat, Nvidia's and Google's purchases of Israeli firms, Noble's gas, Elbit's American subsidiary and Israel's purchases of American weapons, sit inside that trade. What each state does with the money its industries earn, and who pays the costs of its wars, is the subject of later parts. [scene:us20_flows|The money flow]
