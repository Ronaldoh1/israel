Part Seventeen compares how the two states educate their children and their students. It begins with the schools: Israel's single ministry and its separate streams, state, state-religious, Haredi and Arab, each funded and supervised on different terms, beside the American system of local districts paid for largely by states and property taxes. It sets out what each spends on one international definition, who is left behind inside each, and how 15-year-olds in both countries have scored on the OECD's PISA tests from the first rounds to the results published in September 2026, which recorded the steepest fall in Israel's history of the test. It then turns to universities: Israel's regulated public tuition and widening access for Arab and Haredi students, and the American mix of rising prices, grants and $1.65 trillion in student debt, with the federal loan changes that took effect in July 2026. Figures are given as of October 2026, ahead of Israel's election on 27 October and the American midterms on 3 November.
