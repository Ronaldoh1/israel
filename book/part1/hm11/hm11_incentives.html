Why the War Lasted

Israel's government stated its war aims from the start: to destroy Hamas's military and governing capabilities and to bring every hostage home. [cite:hm_ynet_hagari] The record also documents incentives its prime minister had to keep the war going that had little to do with Hamas.

The coalition. Netanyahu's government depended on parties that opposed ending the war. Itamar Ben-Gvir quit in January 2025 over the ceasefire and returned in March 2025, the day after Israel resumed its bombardment with strikes that killed more than 400 people, which strengthened the coalition before a budget vote it needed to survive; Netanyahu called the strikes "only the beginning." [cite:hm_gp_bengvir] In July 2025 a New York Times investigation, based on more than 110 officials and government records, reported that Netanyahu had shelved a truce that would have freed at least 30 hostages after Bezalel Smotrich threatened to bring down the government, and had derailed Saudi normalization, which required ending the war, in the face of Ben-Gvir's opposition. His office said the report "defames Israel"; the Times said the statement "does not refute the facts." [cite:hm_toi_nyt_prolonged]

The courts at home. Netanyahu has been on trial for bribery, fraud and breach of trust since 2020. His testimony was repeatedly postponed for security and diplomatic reasons, most recently in April 2026, and in November 2025 he asked President Isaac Herzog for a pardon, citing the need for national unity after two years of war. [cite:hm_toi_trialdelay] [cite:hm_npr_pardon] Three years after 7 October, his government had not set up a state commission of inquiry into the failure. [cite:gz_ynet_inquiry2026]

The International Criminal Court. The court issued a warrant for Netanyahu in November 2024 and rejected Israel's appeal in October 2025. [cite:pr_bc_icc2024] [cite:gz_toi_icc2025] A continuing war does not suspend that warrant, and Israel does not accept the court's jurisdiction. What the war does supply is the case Israel makes to the court and to the world: that its conduct is self-defence against Hamas, which hides among civilians. That argument depends on Hamas remaining the reason.

Two Readings

The first reading: Hamas became the permanent justification. On this reading, a government whose survival depended on continuing the war kept its aim, total victory over an enemy its own army spokesman said could not be destroyed, deliberately out of reach; it shelved deals under coalition threat, resumed the war when its coalition needed it, and used Hamas's name to justify strikes and policies the evidence did not support, while its prime minister's trial waited and no inquiry into 7 October was held. [cite:hm_toi_nyt_prolonged] [cite:hm_ynet_hagari] [cite:hm_gp_bengvir] [cite:hm_aj_83pct]

The second reading: Hamas was the reason. On this reading, Hamas carried out an attack that killed about 1,200 people in a day, held hostages for two years, fought from tunnels beneath a dense civilian population, and replaced its losses, as American officials acknowledged; no army has quickly destroyed an armed movement in those conditions, and Israel's government denies that politics drove its decisions.
