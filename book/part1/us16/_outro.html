Part Sixteen established that Israel has covered every resident by law since 1995, through four funds financed by an earmarked health tax and the state budget, while the United States still has no universal coverage law: 26.7 million people, 7.9 per cent, were uninsured throughout 2025, and the Congressional Budget Office expects the 2025 reconciliation law to add about 10 million by 2034. [cite:us16_wiki_ilhealth] [cite:us16_census_2025] [cite:us16_kff_obbba] Israel's law has its own exclusions, asylum seekers, undocumented migrants and foreign workers among them, and a large private layer on top. [cite:us16_cwf_israel] On the World Health Organization's series the United States spent about 3.3 times as much per person as Israel in 2023, $13,473 against $4,033 in purchasing-power dollars, while households in Israel paid a larger share, but a smaller amount, out of pocket. [cite:us16_wb_chepc] [cite:us16_wb_oop] American drug prices stood at 278 per cent of those in 33 peer countries in 2022, and Medicare's first negotiated prices took effect only on 1 January 2026. [cite:us16_rand_drugs] [cite:us16_kff_negotiation] The two countries had the same life expectancy in 1980; by 2024 Israel's was about four years higher, and its infant mortality rate, once above America's, was half of it, though infant mortality among Arab citizens in Israel and among babies born to Black mothers in the United States was markedly above the national averages. [cite:us16_wb_le] [cite:us16_wb_imr] [cite:us16_toi_moh_gaps] [cite:us16_cdc_imr2023] In mental health, Israel's public system had waits of about six and a half months in 2024 as demand rose after 7 October 2023, and in the United States about half of adults with a mental illness received treatment.
