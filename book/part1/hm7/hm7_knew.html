What Netanyahu Knew

The question this section answers is narrow and grave: what did Israel's prime minister know before 7 October, and what has he said about it since? The Israel Architecture simulation's October 7 chapter set out the warnings; here they are traced to the record, with Netanyahu's own account beside each.

What the state held. More than a year before the attack, Israeli intelligence held Hamas's battle plan, "Jericho Wall," and judged it "aspirational"; in July 2023 a Unit 8200 analyst warned that Hamas was training to carry it out and was told to "wait patiently." [cite:gz_nyt_jericho] Those warnings were dismissed inside the military and intelligence services. The record does not show that the plan itself was put before Netanyahu as a warning of imminent attack.

What reached Netanyahu. Several warnings did reach him, and they were general rather than specific:

Between March and July 2023, Military Intelligence sent him four letters on how Israel's enemies saw the damage to its national cohesion during the fight over the judicial overhaul; the army confirmed them in May 2024 after a freedom-of-information suit. [cite:hm_ih_4letters] In July 2023 the Shin Bet chief, Ronen Bar, told him he was delivering "a warning of war," without a date or a place. [cite:hm_ynet_bar_records] Egypt's intelligence chief, Abbas Kamel, urged a return to talks with Hamas in June 2023 and warned in September that Gaza could explode; according to The Wall Street Journal, citing Arab officials, he called Netanyahu directly days before the attack, though none of the warnings specified timing or scale. Netanyahu's office had denied any contact with Kamel before 7 October and did not answer the Journal's questions. [cite:hm_ih_egypt2026] According to Haaretz, drawing on a 2026 book by Shlomi Eldar and Ruth Yuval and on three senior foreign officials, the UAE's president, Mohammed bin Zayed, warned Netanyahu in a call in late September 2023 that Sinwar was planning a major operation, and Netanyahu answered that any Hamas action would likely come in the West Bank; his office called the report "a total lie" and said the two had not spoken in that period. [cite:hm_wlos_mbz] On 1 October 2023, by Channel 12's account of the Shin Bet's internal inquiry, Bar asked Netanyahu to approve killing Yahya Sinwar, and Netanyahu preferred "quiet in return for quiet"; Netanyahu's office called that "a complete lie" and said it was Bar who recommended civilian benefits for Hamas in return for calm. [cite:hm_toi_bar_sinwar]

The night. At 3:03 a.m. on 7 October the Shin Bet issued an alert on "unusual preparations and the possibility of offensive intentions by Hamas"; Israeli SIM cards were being activated in Gaza and Hamas commanders were entering tunnels. Bar says he ordered the prime minister's military secretary to be updated at 5:15; Netanyahu was first called at 6:29, as the attack began. Bar has called the night's misjudgement "a Shin Bet failure"; who was responsible for the gap before Netanyahu was called is not answered by the public record. [cite:hm_ynet_bar_records] [cite:hm_toi_bar_sinwar]

What Netanyahu has said. On 28 October 2023 he wrote: "At no time and no stage was a warning given to Prime Minister Netanyahu regarding war intentions of Hamas," and blamed the security chiefs, who he said had judged Hamas deterred; after an outcry he deleted the post and apologized. [cite:hm_aj_deletedpost] In February 2026 his office published a 55-page document that blames Bar for not alerting him on the night and says he received no warning of "a war in Gaza"; the full transcripts of the meetings it quotes have not been released, and Bar has said he will sue him for defamation. [cite:hm_ynet_bar_records] Three years on, Netanyahu has not established the state commission of inquiry that could settle these questions under oath. [cite:gz_ynet_inquiry2026]

Knew, and Chose Not to Act?

The claim that Netanyahu knew the attack was coming and chose to let it happen is the strongest form of the charge, and the record does not establish it: the warnings that reached him did not give a date, a place or a scale, and the plan that did was dismissed by the professionals below him. The simulation's own October 7 chapter reaches the same verdict: that warnings were dismissed is confirmed; that the attack was deliberately allowed is contested and not established.

What the record does establish is serious enough. A government that held its enemy's battle plan, received repeated strategic warnings that its enemies saw it as divided and weak, was told by its security chief that war was coming, and pursued a policy of "quiet" financed by Qatari cash that, by Yedioth's account, he had been warned was reaching Hamas's military wing, was caught unprepared by an attack that killed about 1,200 people. [cite:gz_nyt_jericho] [cite:hm_ih_4letters] [cite:hm_fdd_shinbet] [cite:hm_jc_warned2026] Its prime minister first said no warning had been given at any stage, retracted that, and has since blamed his security chiefs while declining the one inquiry that could test his account under oath. Whether those people had to die is the question a state commission exists to answer; it has not been allowed to.
