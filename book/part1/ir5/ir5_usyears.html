The American Years

The warnings tested in this part came, for more than three decades, from one man. Benjamin Netanyahu built much of his early public career in the United States, and the subjects he first warned Americans about were terrorism, the states that sponsored it and the Soviet bloc behind them. Iran was on that list from the start, as a sponsor of terrorism. A dated warning that Iran was a set number of years from a nuclear bomb came later, in 1992. This section sets out what the record shows of the years before that, so that the predictions in the next section can be read against where they began. [cite:ir5_jvl_bio] [cite:ir5_nyrb_1986]

After his military service Netanyahu studied at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he took a bachelor's degree in architecture and a master's in management studies; reference works date the master's to 1976. He then worked as a consultant at the Boston Consulting Group, from 1976 to 1978 by one directory's account. [cite:ir5_jvl_bio] [cite:ir5_enc_bio] Accounts of the next step differ in their dates. He became executive director of the Jonathan Institute, a foundation named for his brother Yonatan, who was killed in 1976 commanding the rescue of hostages held at Entebbe after an airliner was hijacked; reference works give its founding as 1976, 1977 or 1980, and one lists him as its director from 1978 to 1980. Between 1980 and 1982 he was a manager at Rim, a furniture maker in Jerusalem. [cite:ir5_enc_bio] [cite:ir5_lobelog_ji]

The institute's work set the frame of his early career. In 1979 it held an international conference on terrorism in Jerusalem, and in 1984 a second one in Washington, D.C.; Netanyahu organised both. [cite:ir5_jvl_bio] The future President George H. W. Bush attended the Jerusalem conference, as did people from the American Enterprise Institute, the Center for Strategic and International Studies and Commentary magazine; the proceedings were published in 1981 as International Terrorism: Challenge and Response. The Washington meeting, by one later account, gave weight to "the relationship between Islam and terror." Netanyahu's own framing was broad: he wrote that the battle against terrorism was part of a larger struggle "between the forces of civilization and the forces of barbarism." [cite:ir5_lobelog_ji] [cite:ir5_enc_bio]

In 1982 Netanyahu joined Israel's embassy in Washington as deputy chief of mission, the second post in the embassy, serving under Ambassador Moshe Arens until 1984. He was then Israel's ambassador to the United Nations in New York for four years, from 1984 to 1988. [cite:ir5_jvl_bio] [cite:ir5_enc_bio] In 1988 he returned to Israel, was elected to the Knesset for the Likud on 1 November and became deputy foreign minister. [cite:ir5_enc_bio] [cite:ir5_jvl_bio]

What He Warned About Then

The fullest statement of his views from those years is Terrorism: How the West Can Win, a collection of papers from the 1984 conference that he edited, published in 1986. Reviewing it in The New York Review of Books in August 1986, the historian Shaul Bakhash summarised its case: international terrorism "poses a mortal danger to civilized society," it is aimed mainly at Western democracies, and it is sustained by an international network of groups backed by "terrorist states," "most notably Syria, Libya, Iran, and South Yemen," with the Soviet Union and its satellites as the presence behind them. The remedy it proposed was Western cooperation: shared intelligence, sanctions and, if necessary, force. [cite:ir5_nyrb_1986] The review does not mention nuclear weapons. Iran appears in the book as one state sponsor among four, beside Libya, Syria and South Yemen. [cite:ir5_nyrb_1986] The Palestine Liberation Organization appears as a group that, by one contributor's account cited in the review, trained fighters from about a dozen countries. [cite:ir5_nyrb_1986]

One remark from those years does join Iran's leadership to the bomb. An undated video excerpt, published by the media-clip service Grabien under a headline dating it to 1986, shows Netanyahu arguing that if such regimes came to believe they could act freely, "you may very well find yourself in a world in a few years from now with a Gaddafi or Khomeini with nuclear weapons." [cite:ir5_grabien_khomeini] Ruhollah Khomeini died in June 1989, so the remark must be earlier than that, but its exact date and venue are not given and this book could not establish them. It is also a different kind of statement from those that followed. It names no programme and no date; it is a conditional warning about what might happen if terrorism went unpunished, with Iran's leader named beside Libya's.

The record examined for this book therefore supports a plain statement. Before 1992 Netanyahu treated Iran as a sponsor of terrorism, one of several, and at least once raised the general danger of its leader gaining nuclear weapons; no estimate from him of how many years Iran was from a bomb has been found before his reported remark in the Knesset in 1992. Some later accounts go further. An earlier version of this library's Netanyahu module described the Vietnam years he lived through as a teenager in America as "the operational template" for the Iran warnings he would give from 1992; the same material concedes that this is "an analytical observation, not a documented intention," and that he has never said so. It is an argument, and the record does not decide it. The 1992 remark itself rests on retrospectives, not on a Knesset record found by this book, and the next section begins there.
