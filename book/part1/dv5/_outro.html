Part Five examined the fourth sovereign and found its anomaly inside its own constitution rather than in its borders. Israel has had Basic Laws instead of a constitution since 1950, and a simple majority can change them; a citizenship law open by descent to Jews and their families abroad; and, since 2018, a Basic Law that reserves national self-determination to one people. [cite:gov_legalclarity] [cite:dv5_jvl_return] [cite:jta_nsl_upheld] Its governments are always coalitions, and small parties have held its most consequential portfolios; its decisions for the occupied territories are taken by cabinet committees in which the people living there have no voice. [cite:smotrich_planning_authority] [cite:dv5_ynet_wb_feb2026] In November 2026 it is between governments: the election of 27 October left neither bloc a majority, the opposition leader holds the mandate until 20 December, and the prime minister governs as caretaker while his trial continues and his request for a pardon waits. [cite:dv5_israeled_watch] [cite:dv5_toi_pardon]

Measured against the rules other states follow, Israel stands outside the nuclear treaty, in open dispute with both courts in The Hague, and against the recognition of Palestine that most UN members have now given.
