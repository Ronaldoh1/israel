Campaign Money to an Office

Book Three turns from the men and their money to the office that one of them held. The Department of Government Efficiency, always called DOGE, was the most visible instrument of the 2025 government cuts, and in the book's worst-case reading it is the place where cuts become "the occasion": a reason, sold as thrift, to take hold of the machinery of the state. This section sets out how the office came to exist, step by step, from Elon Musk's endorsement of Donald Trump in July 2024 to the executive order of 20 January 2025. It reports a sequence. Money came first and an office followed; the record shows the order of those events and, as Part Eleven has already said, no document records an agreement between the two men linking one to the other. [scene:de11_money_and_influence|Part Eleven] gives Musk's political money in full; here it is the first link in a chain.

The endorsement and the idea

Musk endorsed Trump on the evening of 13 July 2024, within hours of the attempt on Trump's life at a rally in Butler, Pennsylvania: "I fully endorse President Trump and hope for his rapid recovery." [cite:de17_reuters_endorse] Through the summer the two men discussed a role for him. On a podcast on 19 August Musk said he had spoken with Trump about it and would be interested in serving. On 5 September, at the Economic Club of New York, Trump announced the idea in public for the first time: a government efficiency commission "tasked with conducting a complete financial and performance audit of the entire federal government" and "making recommendations for drastic reforms," with a plan to eliminate "fraud and improper payments" within six months. He said Musk had agreed to lead it. Musk wrote on X the same day: "I look forward to serving America if the opportunity arises." [cite:de17_reuters_econclub]

The money came alongside the idea, and most of it after. Musk's year-end filings with the Federal Election Commission showed more than $290 million spent on the 2024 election, about a quarter of a billion of it through America PAC, the super PAC he founded and funded, and more than $50 million on the $1 million daily giveaways to petition signers in the last weeks of the campaign. [cite:de11_cnn_290m] At the rally in Madison Square Garden on 27 October he said that the federal budget could be cut by "at least $2 trillion," against total spending of about $6.75 trillion in the 2024 fiscal year. [cite:de11_fortune_2t] The promise and its fate are graded in [scene:de11_claims_vs_record|Part Eleven's claims ledger] and audited in [scene:de21_the_ending|Part Twenty-One].

The announcement

Trump won on 5 November. A week later, on 12 November, he announced that Musk and Vivek Ramaswamy, the entrepreneur who had run against him in the Republican primaries, would lead a "Department of Government Efficiency." The two men, he wrote, "will pave the way for my Administration to dismantle Government Bureaucracy, slash excess regulations, cut wasteful expenditures, and restructure Federal Agencies." He compared the project to "The Manhattan Project" and set its work to end by 4 July 2026, the 250th anniversary of independence. The body, as described that day, would give advice and guidance from outside government. Musk added: "This will send shockwaves through the system, and anyone involved in Government waste, which is a lot of people!" [cite:de17_aj_announce]

On 20 November Musk and Ramaswamy set out their plan in the Wall Street Journal. "We will serve as outside volunteers, not federal officials or employees," they wrote. They did not intend to wait for Congress: "Instead of new laws, existing legislation will lead DOGE to make the changes," and the president "can, by executive action, immediately pause the enforcement of those regulations." They named two recent Supreme Court decisions as their legal tools: Loper Bright v. Raimondo (2024), which ended the rule that courts defer to agencies' reading of ambiguous statutes, and West Virginia v. EPA (2022), which bars agencies from deciding "major questions" without clear authority from Congress. DOGE would identify "thousands" of regulations made by "unelected bureaucrats," find "the minimum number of employees required at an agency," and challenge "$500 billion plus in annual federal expenditures that are unauthorized by Congress." [cite:de17_li_oped] [cite:de17_jurist_oped] Over the following weeks Marc Andreessen, without any formal title, interviewed candidates for the new body, a story told in [scene:de16_the_valley|Part Sixteen]. [cite:de16_fortune_doge]

From commission to order

What was announced and what was built were different things. On the day of the inauguration, 20 January 2025, Trump signed Executive Order 14158. It did not create an outside commission. It renamed an existing White House office, the United States Digital Service, as the United States DOGE Service, placed it in the Executive Office of the President, and set up inside it a temporary organization to end on 4 July 2026. [cite:de17_eo_text] [cite:de17_fnn_structure] Ramaswamy left the same day. A spokeswoman said he had "played a critical role in helping us create DOGE," that he intended "to run for elected office soon," and that "the structure that we announced today" required him to stay outside it; he was running for governor of Ohio. [cite:de17_wt_ramaswamy] Two weeks later the White House confirmed that Musk was not an outside volunteer but a special government employee, unpaid, with a government email account and an office in the White House complex. [cite:de17_ap_sge] What that structure was in law, and why it mattered, is the subject of [scene:de17_what_doge_was_legally|the next section].

What the sequence shows, and what it does not

The sequence is documented: the largest donor of the election was given, within three months of election day, a government role with a mandate across the whole executive branch. [cite:de11_cnn_290m] [cite:de17_eo_text] That is the core of the "bought office" reading, and it is a fair description of the order of events.

Three things in the record complicate it. First, the office was not a secret: the candidate proposed it from a public stage on 5 September 2024, two months before the vote, and named the man who would lead it, so voters were told before they voted. [cite:de17_reuters_econclub] Second, there is no statement from either man that ties the money to the role. The nearest thing on the record is Musk's own claim, during the public quarrel of 5 June 2025, that Trump would have lost the election without him; that is a boast about influence, not evidence of a bargain, and it came after he had left government. [cite:de11_npr_feud] Third, the money did not secure the relationship. Within a week of leaving office Musk was threatened by the president with the loss of his companies' contracts, as [scene:de11_money_and_influence|Part Eleven] records. [cite:de11_npr_feud]

Two other readings need a plain answer. The first is that DOGE was Curtis Yarvin's 2012 proposal, "Retire All Government Employees," put into practice. The resemblance between RAGE and a programme of mass federal job cuts is real, and [scene:de6_the_program|Part Six] sets out what Yarvin proposed. But no document shows a line from his talk to EO 14158, and the published plan for DOGE cited Supreme Court decisions and the Impoundment Control Act, not Yarvin. [cite:de17_li_oped] Rhyme is not provenance, as [scene:de1_spine_and_overlay|Part One] explains. The second is that DOGE was a joint project of Musk and Peter Thiel. No published record shows Thiel in its creation, its chain of command or its staffing decisions; the ties that do exist between DOGE's staff and Thiel's companies and foundation are set out, by type and date, in [scene:de18_the_team|Part Eighteen].
