Part Seven turns from the offices of the Holy See to the families most often said to stand behind them. It explains what the "black nobility" was: the Roman aristocratic houses that took the pope's side after Rome fell to Italy in 1870, with the court offices they held and the record of five of them, the Colonna, Massimo, Torlonia, Pacelli and Orsini. It then sets the documented history of the Orsini against the claims made about them: how many popes the family produced, what its cardinals did, what titles it still holds, and whether it controls the papacy, shares a bloodline with the Rothschilds or supplies a hidden "Grey Pope". It ends with the one modern office that tied the family to the pope, Prince Assistant to the Papal Throne, the removal of Prince Filippo Orsini by Pius XII in January 1958 and the end of hereditary court office under Paul VI in 1968, and asks what, if anything, these families hold today.
