Golda's Tour: The Money That "Made the State Possible"

A common account holds that the new state was built and defended primarily on its own resources. Its own founding leader described it differently. In the winter of 1948, between the partition vote and independence, the largest single pre-state fundraising effort took place not in Palestine but across the United States, and David Ben-Gurion credited it with making the state possible.

The effort began with a failure. After the November 1947 vote, the Jewish Agency's treasurer, Eliezer Kaplan, went to the United States to raise $7 million and did not succeed. Ben-Gurion proposed going himself. The Agency's executive sent Golda Meyerson instead, later known as Golda Meir, who was then acting head of the Jewish Agency's political department. She left in January 1948. [cite:jta_golda_1948] [cite:reformjudaism_golda]

Her opening came through an established network. Henry Montor, executive vice-chairman of the United Jewish Appeal, pressed the Council of Jewish Federations to give her a speaking slot at its assembly in Chicago. Montor was not a newcomer to this work. Three years earlier, in 1945, he had gathered 17 wealthy businessmen to meet Ben-Gurion at Rudolf Sonneborn's apartment, a meeting that earlier sections of this book connect to the arms network. [cite:jta_golda_1948] The same organizer thus connected the fundraising of 1948 to the arms network of 1945.

The response was rapid. Reports put the early total at about $25 million in the first month. Chicago's Jewish federation took out a $5 million loan and handed it to her. She went on to about twenty cities and returned on 19 March 1948 with a reported $50 million. [cite:reformjudaism_golda] [cite:jwa_golda_50m] [cite:jewishpress_golda_25m] By one comparison offered in the sources, that sum was several times Saudi Arabia's oil revenue for 1947. The figures are reported ones, and they should be read as such.

The money was spent largely on weapons, including purchases from Czechoslovakia, the supply line taken up later in this stage ([scene:cz_s01|the Czech arms]). [cite:jwa_golda_50m] That link matters. It ties a fundraising campaign among American federations and appeals directly to the rifles and aircraft that reached the new state in its first months.

Ben-Gurion's own assessment is on record. He wrote in his diary that her success was "the only ray of light," and he said that history would record that there was "a Jewish woman who got the money which made the state possible." [cite:jta_golda_1948] [cite:yu_golda_archives] His words credit the donors' money, raised through a federation-and-appeal network that had been built since the 1920s and that, through Montor, overlapped with the arms network.

The narrower point the sources support is this: in the decisive months before independence, the new state's ability to arm itself depended heavily on funds raised abroad through named American organizations, and its leaders said so at the time.
