Arab citizens of Israel vote, stand for the Knesset and hold citizenship, but the law has not treated them identically. From 1949 to 1966 most lived under a military administration requiring permits to travel, with curfews and closed zones. Under that regime, on 29 October 1956, a Border Police unit killed 49 villagers at Kafr Qasim returning home after a curfew they had not been told of; the brigade commander was fined one-hundredth of a lira. [cite:wiki_kafr_qasim_massacre]

The 1950 Absentees' Property Law took the property even of Arabs who never left Israel and became citizens; by 2001 these "present absentees" were estimated at about 200,000 people. [cite:wiki_present_absentee] The law remains in force. In 2018 the Knesset passed, 62 to 55, a Basic Law stating that national self-determination is "unique to the Jewish people," with no equality clause. [cite:jta_nsl_upheld] The Supreme Court upheld it 10 to 1 in 2021, saying individual rights are protected by other laws; supporters say it only states what Israel already is.
