The Holy See and the State of Palestine

The Holy See's treaty with Israel was one half of a pattern. Within weeks of the 1994 protocol with Israel, it opened official, non-diplomatic relations with the Palestine Liberation Organization on 26 October 1994, and it went on to sign two treaties with the Palestinian side: a Basic Agreement with the PLO in 2000 and a Comprehensive Agreement with the State of Palestine in 2015. [cite:ts11_wiki_hspal] The texts matter for this volume because they show what the Holy See committed itself to in writing on Jerusalem, on borders and on Palestinian statehood, in the same years that it was building relations with Israel.

The Basic Agreement: 15 February, not 15 January, 2000

Secondary sources disagree on the date of the Basic Agreement. The text itself settles it. The version published by the Secretariat of State closes "Signed in the Vatican, the fifteenth of February, 2000", and its Latin note gives the same date; it was signed by Mgr Celestino Migliore for the Holy See and Dr Emile Jarjoui for the PLO, and Article 12 put it into force on signature. [cite:vt18_va_plo2000] The Holy See Press Office used the same date, 15 February 2000, when it announced the 2015 treaty, and the Holy See's statement of November 2012 on Palestine's status at the United Nations also cites "the Basic Agreement of 15 February 2000". [cite:vt18_va_bull2015] [cite:vt18_rv_un2012] No primary source consulted for this volume gives 15 January; that date should be treated as an error.

Most of the Basic Agreement concerns the Church: freedom of religion and conscience (Article 1), equal civil rights regardless of religion (Article 3), the Status Quo regime in the Christian holy places where it applies (Article 4), and the legal personality and the economic, legal and fiscal rights of the Church and its institutions under Palestinian law (Articles 5 to 7). [cite:vt18_va_plo2000] Its political weight is in the preamble. It calls for "a special statute for Jerusalem, internationally guaranteed", and declares that "unilateral decisions and actions altering the specific character and status of Jerusalem" are "morally and legally unacceptable". [cite:vt18_va_plo2000] This is the line Pius XII had drawn in 1949, restated in a treaty six years after the Holy See recognised Israel; see [scene:vt17_jerusalem_1947_49|Part Seventeen] for its origin. The Fundamental Agreement with Israel contains no comparable clause on Jerusalem, which is one of the asymmetries between the two sets of texts. [cite:vt1_va_fa1993]

From the 2012 vote to recognition in 2015

On 29 November 2012 the United Nations General Assembly made Palestine a non-member observer state. The next day the Holy See said it welcomed the decision "with favour", repeated its call for an internationally guaranteed special statute for Jerusalem, and quoted Benedict XVI's words of 2009 that the two-state solution should "become a reality, not remain a dream". [cite:vt18_rv_un2012] From then on, the Holy See's counterpart in its own documents was the State of Palestine.

On 26 June 2015, at the Apostolic Palace, Archbishop Paul Richard Gallagher, Secretary for Relations with States, and Riad al-Malki, the Palestinian foreign minister, signed the Comprehensive Agreement between the Holy See and the State of Palestine: a preamble and 32 articles in eight chapters. [cite:vt18_va_bull2015] [cite:vt1_rv_palestine2015] Gallagher tied the change of signatory from the PLO to the State of Palestine directly to the General Assembly resolution of 29 November 2012 and said he hoped "the much desired two-State solution" would soon become a reality and that the agreement might be "a stimulus" towards ending the conflict. Al-Malki said the agreement included the Holy See's official recognition of Palestine as a state and tied it to two states on the 1967 borders. [cite:vt18_va_bull2015] The preamble refers to a State of Palestine consisting of the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Gaza Strip. [cite:ts11_wiki_hspal] On 2 January 2016 the Holy See announced that the parties had notified each other under Article 30 and that the agreement "has come into full force". [cite:vt18_rv_force2016] [cite:vt18_press_bull2016] One news agency later dated the signing to 13 May 2015; that was the date the negotiating commission concluded the text, and the signing was on 26 June. [cite:vt18_ewtn_abbas2017] [cite:vt18_va_bull2015]

Israel's foreign ministry called the agreement one-sided. Its spokesman said it ignored the rights of the Jewish people in the "Land of Israel" and the places in Jerusalem holy to Judaism, and that recognising a Palestinian state harmed the peace process by strengthening Palestinian refusal to negotiate. [cite:ts11_wiki_hspal] The protest did not become a rupture: Israel's ambassador to the Holy See, Zion Evrony, who had presented credentials in 2012, stayed in post until 2016. [cite:vt18_wiki_hsisrael]

Abbas at the Vatican, and Leo XIV's position

Mahmoud Abbas became a regular visitor. He accepted Francis's invitation of May 2014 to pray for peace in the Vatican gardens with Shimon Peres. [cite:vt18_jta_francis2014] On 14 January 2017 he met Francis for about 23 minutes, then Parolin and Gallagher, and inaugurated the Palestinian embassy to the Holy See, which he called a "sign that the Pope loves the Palestinian people". [cite:vt18_ewtn_abbas2017] On 6 November 2025 Leo XIV received him to mark the tenth anniversary of the Comprehensive Agreement; the Holy See's statement recorded agreement on the "urgent need to provide assistance to the civilian population in Gaza" and discussion of a two-state solution. [cite:vt18_press_abbas2025] Israel's president had been received two months earlier. [cite:vt1_vn_herzog2025]

Leo XIV has kept the two-state position at the centre of his statements. On the flight from Istanbul to Beirut on 30 November 2025 he said the Holy See had long backed that solution and added: "We all know that at present, Israel still does not accept this solution." In the same answer he said: "We are also friends with Israel, and we are trying to act as a mediating voice for both parties." [cite:vt18_ewtn_flight2025] On 9 January 2026 he told the diplomatic corps accredited to the Holy See that the two-state solution "remains the institutional perspective" for the aspirations of both peoples. [cite:vt18_vn_twostates2026] At the United Nations General Assembly in September 2026 Cardinal Parolin called it "the only viable and equitable path", said the Holy See "continues to uphold the inalienable rights of the Palestinian people", and repeated the call for an internationally guaranteed special statute for Jerusalem. [cite:vt18_ncr_unga2026] As of October 2026 the Holy See recognises both Israel and the State of Palestine, keeps an embassy-level relationship with each, and holds the Jerusalem position it has held since 1949.
