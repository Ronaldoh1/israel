The Eight per Thousand

The otto per mille is the largest public money flow that the 1929 settlement, as revised in 1984, still produces. It is also the most misdescribed. It is not a church tax added to Italian income tax, and it is not a payment to the Holy See or to Vatican City. It is a share of the revenue Italy already collects from personal income tax (IRPEF), which the law divides among the state and a list of religious bodies; the Catholic share goes to the Italian Bishops' Conference (CEI), the body of the Church in Italy. [cite:vt3_l222_text] [cite:vt3_cei_ripart2026]

How the share is allocated

Article 47 of Law 222 of 1985 provides that, from tax year 1990, "a share equal to eight per thousand of personal income tax" is allocated according to the choices taxpayers make on their returns. [cite:vt3_l222_text] Each taxpayer may sign one box: the state, the Catholic Church, or one of the other confessions that have since signed agreements with the state. Signing costs nothing; the taxpayer's own tax bill is the same whichever box is chosen or if none is.

The point that decides the money is what happens to the shares of taxpayers who sign nothing. The law answers in one sentence: "In the case of choices not expressed by taxpayers, the allocation is determined in proportion to the choices expressed." [cite:vt3_l222_text] The whole 0.8 per cent of IRPEF is therefore divided according to the choices of the minority who sign. Those who do not choose have not chosen the state, or any other recipient; their share follows the decision of those who did.

The figures show how much this matters. For tax year 2022, the year on which the 2026 payments are based, about 42 million taxpayers filed and just over 16.8 million, about 40.1 per cent, made a valid choice, according to figures released by the Ministry of Economy and Finance in July 2026. [cite:vt3_teleborsa_8x2026] Of those who chose, 11.35 million signed for the Catholic Church and 4.53 million for the state. [cite:vt3_teleborsa_8x2026] The Church's signers were about 27 per cent of all taxpayers; through the proportional rule, the Church received between 67.3 and 67.8 per cent of the total, about 1.06 billion euros out of roughly 1.6 billion. The state received 426 million euros. [cite:vt3_teleborsa_8x2026]

The Catholic share has been falling slowly. For tax year 2020 it was 71.00 per cent of the amount; for tax year 2021, 68.72 per cent, with 11,599,375 taxpayers choosing the Church out of 16,687,453 who made a choice and 41,497,318 who filed. [cite:vt3_open_8x2025] For tax year 2022 it fell below 68 per cent. [cite:vt3_teleborsa_8x2026] The other recipients are much smaller: in 2026 the Waldensian Evangelical Church received 46.4 million euros, the Italian Buddhist Union 17.4 million, the Soka Gakkai Buddhist Institute 8.5 million, the Union of Italian Jewish Communities 4.5 million, the Orthodox Archdiocese 3.68 million, the Italian Hindu Union 2.41 million, the Seventh-day Adventists 1.86 million and the Assemblies of God 1.58 million. [cite:vt3_teleborsa_8x2026]

What the CEI does with it

The law fixes the purposes in Article 48. The state's share is for extraordinary interventions against world hunger, natural disasters and similar public purposes; the Church's is for "the worship needs of the population, the support of the clergy" and charitable work in Italy and in developing countries. [cite:vt3_l222_text] Each year the CEI's General Assembly divides the money and publishes the result. The 82nd General Assembly, meeting in Rome from 25 to 28 May 2026, divided 1,086,752,778.01 euros: an advance of 1,063,069,466.83 euros for 2026 and an adjustment of 23,683,311.18 euros on the sums for 2023. [cite:vt3_cei_ripart2026]

Clergy support is the single largest use. In 2024, the latest year for which the clergy institute has published accounts, 389 million euros of the eight per thousand went to priests' pay, supporting 31,053 priests; most received an integration on top of their parish or other income, and 2,444 needed none. [cite:vt3_icsc_2024] A full-time priest's monthly pay ranged from 1,049.60 to 1,981.12 euros. [cite:vt3_icsc_2024] Charity took just over a quarter of the 2026 total, and worship and pastoral needs, including church building and the upkeep of historic churches, a little under two-fifths. [cite:vt3_cei_ripart2026] The allocation table has no line for the Holy See or for Vatican City; the money stays with the Church in Italy. [cite:vt3_cei_ripart2026]

The criticism and the reply

The proportional rule has been criticized from inside the Italian state. In 2015 the Corte dei conti, the national audit court, objected that the mechanism involves citizens "regardless of their own will": the minority who choose decide the destination of the whole sum. It also criticized the state's "scant interest" in promoting its own share and the churches' heavy spending on advertising. [cite:vt3_avv_cdc2015] The CEI's reply, sent to the court after hearings in October 2015, called the eight per thousand "a case of democracy in the direction of public spending," compared those who do not choose to citizens who do not vote, whose abstention does not stop seats being filled, and noted that it publishes its accounts every year. [cite:vt3_avv_cdc2015] The CEI's daily, Avvenire, reported the exchange under the headline that the court had gone "beyond measure." [cite:vt3_avv_cdc2015]

Both descriptions are accurate as far as they go. Those who do not sign have not "chosen" the Church, and it would be wrong to say they actively refused it; they made no choice, and the law assigns their share by the choices of others. [cite:vt3_l222_text] On the 2026 figures that rule roughly multiplied the effect of the Church's signers: about 27 per cent of taxpayers directed about two-thirds of the fund. [cite:vt3_teleborsa_8x2026] The cost to the Italian budget is not the whole of the fund, since the law dedicates the 0.8 per cent to the listed recipients whatever the choices; it is the difference between what the state would receive if unexpressed shares came to it and what it receives under the proportional rule. Neither the ministry nor the CEI publishes that counterfactual figure, and this volume does not estimate it.
