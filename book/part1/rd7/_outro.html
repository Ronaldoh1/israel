Read as a network, the record of the road to 1948 shows a founding that depended on a few things nothing else could replace: an organized movement to pursue it, armed groups to hold it, and two great sources of money at either end of the period, Edmond de Rothschild's in the first decades and the American appeals' in the last. Britain supplied the framework under which the settlers came; the United States, and the bloc that followed it, carried the vote and the first recognition. Most single leaders could be replaced, because the institutions they built carried on. Where land was plentiful elsewhere, no ruler would give sovereignty; where sovereignty was offered, the land was already someone else's.

The map is open. Every figure here can be checked against the ties behind it, and any reader can remove any other combination of nodes and see what follows.
