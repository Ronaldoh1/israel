The End-to-End Map

The worst-case reading describes a pipeline: a person is identified, their records are collected and joined, a system scores or flags them, an official decides, and the state denies them something, enforces against them, confines them and, at the far end, compels their labour. This section walks that pipeline one link at a time. For each link it names the systems that do the job today, citing the part of the book that documents them; grades the join to the next link; and says what stands in the way. The grades for joins between data systems are taken unchanged from the ecosystem map in [scene:de28_the_ecosystem_map|Part Thirty]: documented (it exists today, with a source), probable (a contract, interface or authority already points to it), reported (named reporting, not yet confirmed by a primary record), possible (technically feasible, no document) and not supported (the record runs against it). "Possible" grades a join between systems, never a fact about a person.

Every link has to be read twice, because the pipeline runs differently for the two populations the law separates. For non-citizens in immigration enforcement, almost every link is documented and in daily use. For citizens, the links exist but are mostly unjoined, and where they have touched citizens the record shows error or a specific case, not design.

Identify, collect, join

Identification is the most fully built link. ICE's Mobile Fortify app returns a name, date of birth and removal status from a face in the street, and CBP photographs every non-citizen at the border ([scene:de26_faces_drones_satellites|Part Twenty-Eight]). Plate cameras on local networks answer federal searches: one Texas school district's eight cameras received 620 immigration-related searches in a single month. [cite:de26_404_fortify] [cite:de26_bu_entryexit] [cite:de28_74_flock] DHS's next-generation biometric system, HART, is built to be shared across DHS, the Justice and Defense Departments, the intelligence community, police and foreign partners. In August 2026 it was scheduled to reach initial operation by September. [cite:de28_hart_pia] [cite:de24_bu_schutt] Part Thirty grades the join from these sensors to the integration layer as possible: no record shows a feed from Fortify, drones or Paragon into Palantir. The join from plate cameras is reported for federal agents and possible for Palantir. [cite:de28_npr_paragon]

Collection draws on the state's own records and on the market ([scene:de24b_benefits|Part Twenty-Five], [scene:de24_data_and_fusion|Part Twenty-Four]). The joins graded documented in Part Thirty are specific. A Medicaid dataset reached Palantir once, over a Teams chat in January 2026, beyond a court's limits. Student-visa and air-passenger data was searched through Palantir systems, and phone extractions were imported into them, in records from 2014 to 2022. A 2026 work statement requires LexisNexis's people-search data to connect to "the Palantir platform," documented as a requirement and probable as a flow. [cite:de27_npr_medicaid] [cite:de28_guardian_icm] [cite:de28_bu_lexis] The IRS, SSA and TSA links are documented as far as DHS or ICE; the onward path to Palantir is not shown. The new TANF disclosures are documented to DHS and possible to Palantir. Thomson Reuters's CLEAR feeding Palantir is not supported: a training record says it "is not connected to Palantir tools." [cite:de30_ap_krause] [cite:de24_npr_save] [cite:de25b_reuters_tsaice] [cite:de28_bl_tanf] [cite:de28_lawsites_tr]

Score, decide, deny

Scoring exists in narrow forms. ELITE gives addresses a "confidence score," drawing on Medicaid and other data. [cite:ow9_fortune_elite] SAVE flags people whose citizenship it cannot confirm. Its record shows the cost of error: the government said it had checked more than 65 million voters in 26 states and flagged 28,635, and in Texas about a fifth of those flagged were later found to be citizens. [cite:de19_npr_save] Continuous vetting runs automated checks on the criminal and financial records of more than 3.8 million clearance holders. [cite:de24b_bu_cv] No document shows a general score that rates a person's loyalty or speech. The one programme that flagged unaccused travellers by algorithm, Quiet Skies, was ended in June 2025 by Secretary Kristi Noem, who called it "a political rolodex of the Biden Administration." [cite:de25b_cnn_quietskies]

Every decision on the record was made by a named official or office: an immigration judge in the Öztürk and Khalil cases, the Secretary of State in visa revocations, the Director of National Intelligence on clearances, OFAC on sanctions ([scene:de34_the_repurposing_test|the previous section]). [cite:de34_ccr_khalil] [cite:de24b_kvia_gabbard] That matters for the worst case, because a human decision can be challenged, appealed and found unlawful, and in this record several were. Denial then draws on gates built long before 2025: passport denial for tax debt over $66,000 or child-support arrears over $2,500, the no-fly list, E-Verify, benefit eligibility checks and trusted-traveller revocation ([scene:de25b_who_can_stop_you|Part Twenty-Seven], [scene:de24b_work_and_vetting|Part Twenty-Five]). [cite:de25b_irs_passport] [cite:de25b_crs_childsupport] [cite:de24b_ev_manual] The join from "flagged" to "denied" is documented for non-citizens in visa and sanctions cases. For citizens it is documented only in specific contested cases, such as the clearance revocations; in the Global Entry case the step from a face-recognition match to the revocation is alleged and denied, and Part Thirty grades that join possible. [cite:de24b_kvia_gabbard] [cite:de25b_bu_cleland] Which denials US law already allows, and for what, is the subject of [scene:de35_the_consequence_engine|the consequence engine] in the next part.

Enforce, confine, compel

Enforcement and confinement are built at a scale without precedent for ICE, and they are pointed at one population ([scene:de32_ice_as_template|Part Thirty-Four]). ICE doubled its workforce on its own count and was funded to 2029. Local deputisation grew roughly tenfold in a year, and detention stood near 66,000 in July 2026, in a system where about nine in ten detainees were already held in privately run facilities in early 2025. [cite:de32_kwtx_hiring] [cite:us6_brennan_obbba] [cite:de32_fwd_287g] [cite:de34_trac_66k] [cite:de32_trac_private] The link touches citizens by error, and the record documents the error. ProPublica counted 506 US citizens detained by immigration agents, at least 70 of them minors, and about a dozen deported, most of them children. [cite:de32_dn_propublica] Against citizens as such, enforcement would need different authority: the criminal process, or troops under the Insurrection Act, which had not been invoked as of 28 October 2026. The Supreme Court's Illinois order found the government had "failed to identify a source of authority" for the military to execute the laws there. [cite:de32_hill_time] [cite:de32_lawdork_il]

The last link is the weakest on the record. Detainees in the "voluntary work program" are paid at least $1 a day, a rate Congress funded in 1950. A Washington jury ordered GEO to pay about $23.2 million in back wages and unjust enrichment, and the Supreme Court is weighing whether to hear GEO's challenge. Claims that detainees were made to clean without pay under threat of solitary confinement are being litigated as forced labour, and in GEO v. Menocal in February 2026 the Supreme Court held that GEO's defence as a government contractor could not be appealed before trial, so the case continues. [cite:de32_pogo_dollar] [cite:de32_wa_nwauzor] [cite:de32_scotus_25828] [cite:de32_lii_menocal] Compelled labour as a policy of the state is not supported. Nor does any of the theorists' texts prescribe forced labour: Yarvin's documented answer for people he deemed surplus was confinement ([scene:de6_the_hard_posts|Part Six]). [cite:de6_tnr_duran] The ladder from watched to compelled is set out rung by rung in [scene:de35_the_consequence_ladder|Part Thirty-Seven].

Reading the map

Read end to end, the map has a clear shape. For non-citizens the pipeline is complete in practice: each link is documented, and several joins between links are documented too. That is the occasion the worst-case reading names, and it is on the record. For citizens the pipeline is a set of separate pieces. The first two links are built and reach everyone, since faces, plates, travel, tax and benefit records do not sort by citizenship. The middle links are joined for citizens only by accident, as in the Medicaid dataset and the citizens flagged by SAVE, or in specific contested cases. The last links have no legal route for citizens without a criminal charge or military authority that has not been used.

What separates the pieces is mostly not technology. Part Thirty found that every join between adjacent links needs an interface, an agreement or a rule change, not a new law. [cite:de28_bu_lexis] [cite:de28_brennan_consol] The barriers that held were courts and statutes. Judges found 42,695 tax disclosures unlawful, limited Medicaid sharing to five fields and paused it after the January breach, vacated the speech-targeting policy, and refused the Illinois Guard deployment. [cite:us10_cpa_irs_sep] [cite:de30_bl_medicaid] [cite:de28_ch_aaup] [cite:de32_lawdork_il] Some barriers gave way: the Supreme Court let SAVE checks resume, and a judge declined to block the TANF notice. [cite:de19_npr_save] [cite:de24b_bl_tanf] [scene:de34_missing_pieces_and_triggers|The next section] asks what the pipeline still lacks to run as one machine, and which dated events would move its grades.
