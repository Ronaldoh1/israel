Built Together: Iron Dome, David's Sling and Arrow

Not everything in the pipeline flows one way. Israel's three layers of missile defence, Iron Dome against short-range rockets, David's Sling against medium-range missiles and Arrow against ballistic missiles, were designed by Israeli state companies and paid for in large part by the United States, and they are increasingly built in American factories by American partners. This is the part of the relationship where the claim that the United States gets something back is easiest to test.

Iron Dome's funding. Iron Dome was developed by Israel's Rafael Advanced Defense Systems and first funded by the United States in fiscal 2011, with $205 million. Congress added $70 million in 2012, $194 million in 2013 and $235.3 million in 2014, plus a $225 million emergency appropriation during the Gaza war in the summer of 2014: $929.3 million in four years. [cite:ow8_crs_irondome2014] Since 2019 missile-defence funding has been part of the memorandum of understanding, at $500 million a year, and in wartime Congress has added more. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] On 23 September 2021, after Democratic leaders removed $1 billion to replenish Iron Dome from a stopgap spending bill in the face of objections from some members, the House passed it as a separate bill by 420 votes to 9; the appropriations chair, Rosa DeLauro, called it money "limited to a system that is entirely defensive". [cite:ow8_ap_irondome2021] The April 2024 supplemental added $4 billion for missile defence and $1.2 billion for Iron Beam, a laser system. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] The House later rejected, by 422 votes to 6, an amendment by Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene to strip $500 million in annual cooperative missile-defence funding from the defence bill for fiscal 2026. [cite:pm_ji_mtg_amendment] [scene:pm6_greene|Part Six of Follow the Money]

The 2014 co-production deal. In March 2014 the two governments signed an agreement that allowed components of Iron Dome to be made in the United States and gave the US Missile Defense Agency full access to Iron Dome technology, which until then had been Israel's property. Raytheon became Rafael's American partner and received a $149 million contract for Tamir interceptor parts in September 2014; American officials expected modest cost savings. [cite:ow8_crs_irondome2014] The same model was applied to the upper layers. By 2016 American production funding for David's Sling, built by Rafael with Raytheon, and for Arrow 3, built by Israel Aerospace Industries with Boeing, was split equally between the Israeli prime contractor and its American partner; an Israeli missile-defence official described "true joint production" with about half the work done in the United States. [cite:ow8_dn_coprod2016]

A plant in Arkansas. The arrangement has since moved from components to whole interceptors. On 9 October 2026 Raytheon–Rafael Protection Systems, a joint venture, announced that it had delivered the first Tamir interceptors built at its plant in Camden, Arkansas, opened in late 2025 and the first facility outside Israel to make them. Its chief executive said: "For the first time, we can produce these interceptors in the United States." The plant is also to build the US variant for the Marine Corps' new air-defence system. [cite:ow8_rtx_camden] [cite:ow8_dn_mric]

The end of offshore procurement. The other half of the "buy American" story is in the aid memoranda. Israel had been permitted to spend part of its American military grant inside Israel, on its own defence industry, a practice called offshore procurement; under the 2007 memorandum that share could reach 26.3 per cent. The 2016 memorandum, signed on 14 September 2016, phases it out, so that by the end of the memorandum in fiscal 2028 the grant is to be spent in the United States; in fiscal 2025 the offshore share was $450.3 million. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] [cite:hm_dn_mou] As the share falls, more of each year's $3.3 billion is paid to American manufacturers, and less to Israeli ones. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025]

Who benefits. The record supports both readings of this relationship. The United States has paid for a large share of Israel's missile defences; and it has obtained, in return, access to the technology, a combat-tested interceptor for its own Marine Corps, an interceptor production line in Arkansas, and a guarantee that Israel's grant money will be spent with American firms. [cite:ow8_crs_irondome2014] [cite:ow8_dn_mric] [cite:ow8_rtx_camden] [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] The beneficiaries on the American side are named companies, Raytheon and Boeing above all, as well as the government. [cite:ow8_dn_coprod2016] The asymmetry is that the money is American public money, appropriated year after year with near-unanimous votes, while the systems' first purpose is the defence of Israel. [cite:ow8_ap_irondome2021] [cite:pm_ji_mtg_amendment] And, as Part Seven showed, when Israel's defences ran short in 2025, American interceptors were spent in their place. [cite:ow7_cnn_thaad2025]
