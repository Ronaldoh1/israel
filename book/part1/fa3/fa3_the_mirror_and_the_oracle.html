The Mirror and the Oracle

The most famous oracle of the ancient world sat at Delphi. The Pythia, the priestess of Apollo, was a woman over fifty; consultations were held on one day a month; she bathed in the Castalian spring, sat on a tripod in a basement chamber while chewing laurel leaves, and priests recorded her words, often as ambiguous verse. Her prestige peaked between about the eighth and fourth centuries BCE, when lawmakers and colonists sought her guidance on wars and politics. Her last prophecy was reportedly given around 393 CE, when Theodosius I enacted laws to end pagan activity. [cite:fa_brit_delphi]

John Dee, scientific adviser to Elizabeth I, sought angels through a medium, Edward Kelley, between 1583 and 1589; at the court of Rudolf II the angels "could only be seen and heard by Edward Kelley". [cite:ht_brit_dee] [cite:ht_quil_rudolf] Dee's polished black "spirit mirror" is in the British Museum. In 2021 a study led by Stuart Campbell matched its chemistry to obsidian from Pachuca, near Mexico City, a region under Aztec control. For the Aztecs, mirrors were gateways to other worlds, and the god Tezcatlipoca, "Smoking Mirror", is often shown wearing them. How the mirror reached Europe after the conquest is not known. [cite:fa_smith_deemirror] An Aztec tool of sight ended in the hands of an English magus.

In March 1848 Kate and Margaret Fox reported mysterious rapping sounds in their house at Hydesville, New York, which they presented as messages from a spirit. Their public demonstration at Rochester in 1849 was the first paid Spiritualist event, and they became famous mediums. On 21 October 1888, before about 2,000 people at the New York Academy of Music, Margaret admitted the raps were a hoax and produced them by cracking her toe joints. In November 1889 she retracted the confession, saying it had been forced by poverty and pressure. Both sisters died poor. [cite:fa_wiki_fox] The Catechism rejects "recourse to mediums" with the other forms of divination. [cite:vt30_va_ccc2116]

The most recent seers were paid by a government. Remote viewing, the claimed ability to perceive distant places by the mind, was researched from 1972 at SRI in California, and the work was funded over the following decades by the US Army, the Defense Intelligence Agency and the CIA under several code names, combined in 1991 as Stargate. In 1995 the CIA commissioned a review by the American Institutes for Research. One reviewer, the statistician Jessica Utts, argued the results suggested psychic functioning; the other, the psychologist Ray Hyman, called that premature and unreplicated. The review found that "no remote viewing report ever provided actionable information for any intelligence operation", and the CIA ended and declassified the program in 1995. [cite:fa_wiki_stargate]
