Settler Violence

The count. The UN's humanitarian office records settler attacks that cause casualties or damage. It counted 1,449 in 2024 and 1,828 in 2025, about five a day. In 2025 it recorded 240 Palestinians killed in the West Bank, 225 of them by Israeli forces and nine by settlers, and 17 Israelis killed by Palestinians. [cite:wb_ocha_snap2025] Settlers injured 772 Palestinians in 2025 up to early December, in attacks across more than 270 communities. [cite:wb_ocha_upd348]

Huwara, 26 February 2023

On 26 February 2023, a Palestinian gunman shot dead two Israeli brothers, Hillel and Yagel Yaniv, as they drove through Huwara, a town south of Nablus. That night hundreds of settlers entered Huwara and nearby villages and set fire to homes, shops and cars. One Palestinian, Sameh Aqtesh, was killed and about a hundred were injured. The army's regional commander, Major General Yehuda Fox, called it "a pogrom carried out by outlaws." Days later the finance minister, Bezalel Smotrich, said Huwara should be "wiped out"; he later said he had spoken in anger. A court ordered the arrested suspects released; the Defence Ministry placed two people in administrative detention. [cite:wb_wiki_huwara]

The Herding Outposts

The newest form of settlement is the herding farm: one family or a few young men, a flock, and a fence, set up on a hill beside Palestinian grazing land. Peace Now and Kerem Navot, two Israeli groups that track settlement, counted more than a hundred such outposts in 2025, and calculated that through grazing, settlers had taken control of at least 786,000 dunams, about 14 percent of the West Bank. The outposts are unauthorized under Israeli law, but the groups traced state money to them: NIS 54 million under a budget line for security needs of settlement points, about NIS 3 million in Agriculture Ministry grazing grants, and roughly 80,000 dunams allocated for grazing by the Settlement Division. [cite:wb_pn_badsamaritan]

Since October 2023. In the first weeks of the Gaza war, the UN counted 828 people from 15 herding communities displaced amid settler violence and new restrictions. In Khirbet Zanuta, south of Hebron, 24 families took down about fifty structures and left on 28 October 2023, after three attacks that month. [cite:wb_ocha_herders2023] By December 2024, Kerem Navot counted 57 communities displaced since October 2023, 50 of them entirely, and at least 41 new outposts and herding farms. Zanuta's families went back in August 2024 under a High Court ruling, and left again within weeks. [cite:wb_972_villages] In 2025 the UN reported that Ein Samiya, a Bedouin community near Ramallah, had been fully displaced. [cite:wb_ocha_upd348]

Who Is Charged

Yesh Din, an Israeli rights group, has followed police files on settler violence since 2005. Of the files it monitored through 2025, 93.6 percent were closed without an indictment, and about 3 percent ended in a full or partial conviction. [cite:wb_yeshdin_2025] Police investigations themselves fell, by Channel 12's count, from 235 in 2023 to 60 in 2025 up to November, under a national security minister, Itamar Ben-Gvir, who oversees the police. [cite:wb_toi_netanyahu2025] Some cases are charged: five settlers were indicted for an attack on a school at al-Muarrajat in 2024. [cite:wb_972_villages]

Israel's answer. In November 2025, after settlers torched factories and a mosque, Prime Minister Netanyahu promised "very forceful action against the riots against IDF soldiers," and said the extremists were a minority who did not represent "the large community of law-abiding, loyal settlers." The army's chief of staff, Eyal Zamir, strongly condemned the attacks, and President Herzog called them "shocking and grave." The army's own count of "nationalistic crime" incidents by Israelis had reached 704 in 2025 by mid-November, more than in all of 2024. [cite:wb_toi_netanyahu2025] The record holds both: leaders who condemn the violence, and figures showing that very little of it ends in court.
