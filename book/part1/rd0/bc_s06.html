Three Enclaves and One Trope

The City of London is often bundled with two other capitals into a single story. In its most common form, the City of London, Vatican City and Washington, DC are three sovereign city-states that together and in secret govern the world's finance, its religion and its military. Each of the three is a real jurisdictional oddity, and each contains a real gap in ordinary accountability. The claim that they form one coordinated power is a different matter. The way to test it is to set out what the legal record shows about each enclave on its own terms, and then to ask what, if anything, connects them.

The City. As the previous section showed, the City of London Corporation is a local authority whose privileges are preserved by express exception in Acts of Parliament. It has an electorate that includes businesses, and an official lobbyist, the Remembrancer, seated on the floor of the Commons. It is not a state. Parliament legislates for it, and the Crown Dependencies have more autonomy than it does. [cite:wiki_city_of_london_corporation] [cite:metabunk_city_sovereign_debunk] Its peculiarity is privilege without sovereignty.

Vatican City. The second enclave is the only one of the three that is in fact a sovereign state, and it became one late. Vatican City became sovereign on 11 February 1929, through the Lateran Treaty with Mussolini's Italy. The treaty recognized papal sovereignty over an enclave of 44 hectares, and Italy paid the Holy See compensation for the Papal States it had lost in 1870. [cite:wiki_lateran_treaty] Before 1929 there was no Vatican state in this sense, so the enclave cannot have been a sovereign partner in any arrangement reaching back into the period this volume covers. Its financial bodies, the IOR, APSA and the Governorate, publish no statements equivalent to those of a comparable secular institution, and they sit outside EU banking regulation. [cite:wiki_lateran_treaty] The treaty, the compensation and the financial bodies belong to later volumes of this library, which take them up in full. Here only the legal fact matters: sovereignty, from 1929, by treaty.

The District of Columbia. The third enclave is the reverse of a sovereign city. Washington, DC is a federal district under the exclusive legislative authority of Congress, under Article I, Section 8 of the United States Constitution. Its residents have elected a mayor and council only since the District of Columbia Home Rule Act of 1973, and Congress keeps a veto over the District's legislation. Its roughly 700,000 residents have no voting member in either house of Congress. [cite:wiki_dc_home_rule] Far from ruling anything, the capital has less self-government than any state of the Union. The 1973 Act, like the Lateran Treaty, belongs to a later period; it appears here only to fix what kind of place the District legally is.

Set side by side, the three show what is documented and what is not.

The last row is the one that decides the question. The "empire of three cities" assigns each enclave a portfolio: finance to London, religion to Rome, the military to Washington. For such an empire to exist, something would have to bind the three: a treaty, a charter, a shared council, or at the very least documented coordination between them. The record contains none of these. [cite:metabunk_city_sovereign_debunk] The three have different legal foundations, created centuries apart by different authorities for different reasons. One is a medieval corporation kept alive by Parliament's exceptions. One is a sovereign created by a twentieth-century treaty with Italy. One is a federal district that its own Congress governs. The only thing they share is that each is an anomaly inside a capital.

The way the claim travels matters as much as its lack of a source. In circulation, the story reliably slides. It begins with three real jurisdictional oddities, moves on to an assertion about hidden rulers behind them, and from there passes into older ethnic and religious tropes about who those hidden rulers supposedly are. [cite:metabunk_city_sovereign_debunk] That last step is where the claim does its real work, and it is the step this library refuses. The three enclaves give no support to it; they are simply a container into which it has been poured. Where a later section of this volume meets real antisemitic material, such as hostility to the papal loans framed around the lenders' Jewishness, it names it as what it is. The "three cities" story belongs in the same category whenever it takes that final step.

None of this makes the three enclaves uninteresting. Each is a genuine carve-out from ordinary accountability: a franchise for business inside a national legislature, a sovereign's financial bodies outside EU banking regulation, a capital whose residents have no vote in the body that governs them. That is more than enough to examine, and later volumes do. What bundling them into one hidden empire adds is nothing the documents support, and everything they do not.

The gap here is narrow, and it should be stated exactly. The sources used for this section establish the legal status of each enclave and the absence of any documented link among them. They do not, and could not, prove a negative about every private contact among officials of three governments across centuries. What they do show is that the claim has no primary source behind it, and the burden of producing one rests with those who make it. Until such a document appears, the record supports three separate accountability gaps, each mapped on its own terms, and that is how this library treats them.
