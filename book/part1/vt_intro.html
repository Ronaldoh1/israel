This volume is about the Holy See, the sovereign at the head of the Catholic Church, and one question about it: whether its public refusal of Zionism, from Pius X's "Non possumus" to Theodor Herzl in January 1904 until the Fundamental Agreement with Israel of 30 December 1993, was what it appeared to be or a front for private support. The question was first put in this library by The Divine 3+1, which promised a volume on Rome; this is that volume. Answering it fairly means answering the questions underneath it, and the book takes them in turn. Why is there a Vatican at all, who designed the settlement of 1929, and who paid for it? Why is the Holy See still treated as a state, and who checks it? Who holds power inside it, and what of the Jesuits' "Black Pope", the Orsini, the Order of Malta and the "Grey Pope"? What does it own, what does its bank do, and what is the truth of its Rothschild loans? What did it do about the sexual abuse of children by clergy? How far does it reach through schools, universities and bishops? What do its texts and archives show? And what happened to Emanuela Orlandi, the Vatican citizen who disappeared in Rome in 1983?

The record answers the central question more firmly than most of the others. It shows Herzl recording Pius X's refusal in his own diary, Benedict XV speaking in 1919 against a "position of privilege and preponderance" in Palestine, and, in private letters of 1943 that the Holy See later published, its Secretary of State, his officials and his delegate in Washington opposing a Jewish home there, the delegate proposing "a more fitting territory than Palestine." [cite:vt1_ccjr_herzl] [cite:vt16_va_1919] [cite:vt16_jta1976] [cite:vt16_cicognani1943] It shows the Holy See's one material commitment of 1949 going to Palestinian refugees, forty-five years without recognition, and a treaty in 1993 that secured the Church's institutions and committed the Holy See to remain "a stranger to all merely temporal conflicts" over "disputed territories and unsettled borders." [cite:vt17_cnewa_pmp] [cite:vt1_va_fa1993] It finds no transfer of money or favours from the Holy See to the Zionist movement or to Israel, and no document behind the "unbroken chain" of Rothschild control said to run from the papal loans of 1831 to 2026. [cite:vt11_wiki_papalloans] [cite:vt11_iv_pauly] Its verdict is that the refusal was real policy toward a movement and a state, kept apart after 1965 from the Church's teaching on the Jewish people, and that the verdict could be reopened only by the Holy See's own files on Palestine after 1945, which are open to 1958 but have barely been studied, and closed after that. The volume is built from primary documents wherever they exist: the Lateran Pacts and the 1993 treaty, papal encyclicals and allocutions, the Holy See's own wartime edition and archive releases, the IOR's and APSA's annual reports, court judgments, UN records, national abuse inquiries and Italian law, with named historians and outlets for the rest. Its rules are the library's. The Holy See, Vatican City State and the Catholic Church are kept apart, as are Zionism, the State of Israel and Judaism; acts are attributed to named popes, officials and offices, never to Catholics or Jews as a whole; abuse figures carry each report's method and period and are never added together; living people are described with their legal status and their own stated positions; myths such as the "Grey Pope" and the Rothschild chain are named, sourced and graded, with the antisemitic history of the "Rothschilds control the Pope" trope stated where it arises; and the interpretive lenses used on this platform before, from "IOR = ROI" to the "loud silence", appear only in passages labelled as interpretation.
