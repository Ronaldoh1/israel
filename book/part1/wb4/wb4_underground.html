The Underground, and the Uprising

The mayors. On 2 June 1980 car bombs planted by Israeli settlers exploded under the cars of West Bank mayors. Bassam Shakaa of Nablus lost both legs, and Karim Khalaf of Ramallah lost a leg; a bomb meant for the mayor of al-Bireh blinded the Druze police officer who tried to defuse it. [cite:wb_wiki_1980bomb]

The Islamic College, and the Dome. In July 1983 gunmen from the same network attacked the Islamic College in Hebron, killing three students and wounding 33. When the network, known as the Jewish Underground, was broken up in 1984, its members turned out to include men who had planned to blow up the Dome of the Rock, one of Islam's holiest sites. In July 1985 three settlers were convicted of murder for the college attack and twelve more of crimes ranging from weapons possession to manslaughter and conspiracy. [cite:wb_csm_underground]

The pardons. Settlement leaders campaigned for clemency, and after Israel released Palestinian prisoners in an exchange in May 1985, Foreign Minister Yitzhak Shamir and several other politicians called for the members to be freed. [cite:wb_csm_underground] One member, Ira Rappaport, convicted of membership in a terrorist organization and of aggravated assault, was pardoned by President Chaim Herzog and released in 1988. [cite:wb_wiki_1980bomb]

The uprising. In December 1987 the first intifada began in Gaza and spread across the West Bank, with strikes, boycotts, stone-throwing and mass arrests, and it lasted until the Oslo agreements. The Gaza volume sets out its course and its toll in both territories. [scene:gz3_uprising|The first intifada] The years of the intifada led to the talks between Israel and the PLO at Oslo, the subject of Part Five.
