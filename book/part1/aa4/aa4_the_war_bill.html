The War Bill, 2023–2025

A study by William Hartung for the Quincy Institute and Brown University's Costs of War project counted $21.7 billion in American military aid to Israel between October 2023 and September 2025: $17.9 billion in the first year and $3.8 billion in the second. [cite:aa_hartung_2025] The largest single act was the supplemental of April 2024, which provided $3.5 billion in military financing, $4 billion for missile defence and $1.2 billion for the Iron Beam laser system, among other sums. [cite:dv4_crs_aid_2025]

American operations in Yemen, against Iran and across the region in the same period cost a further $9.65 to $12.07 billion, by a companion estimate. [cite:aa_hartung_2025]

The sales

Sales are separate from aid. In December 2023 the State Department twice invoked an emergency to send tank and artillery ammunition without congressional review; of 85 sales notified worldwide that year, those two were the only emergency invocations. [cite:ow8_ap_tank2023] [cite:ow8_at_155] [cite:ow8_js_wrsa85] In May 2024 the Biden administration paused a shipment of 2,000-pound and 500-pound bombs over a planned assault on Rafah, the first restriction of the war; on 25 January 2025 President Trump released the heavy bombs. [cite:ow8_axios_pause] [cite:dv4_national_bombs_2025] Around the start of March 2025 Secretary of State Rubio waived congressional review to fast-track about $3 billion in sales and signed a declaration to expedite about $4 billion in military assistance, calling the previous administration's policy a "partial arms embargo". [cite:ow8_toi_rubio2025] By Hartung's count at least $10.1 billion in sales were notified to Congress between 20 January and September 2025, and a further package of about $6 billion, including 30 Apache helicopters, was proposed in September 2025. [cite:aa_hartung_2025]
