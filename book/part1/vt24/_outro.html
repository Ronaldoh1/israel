Part Twenty-Four established that the teachers of gnosis were a family of Christian schools, not one church, and that most of what was known about them until 1945 came from opponents. [cite:vt24_wiki_gnosticism] [cite:vt24_ah1_23] Their own texts do teach the kingdom within and the ascent of the soul past ruling powers, though the canonical Luke 17:21 was also read as "within" by orthodox Fathers; alchemy enters only later, through Zosimos of Panopolis. [cite:vt24_gthom] [cite:vt24_ah1_21] [cite:vt24_ramelli] [cite:vt24_fraser2007] No tithe, code of commandments or Sunday obligation is documented for any of these groups; the Apocryphon of John curses anyone who sells its teaching, while Ptolemy kept the Ten Commandments and the proto-orthodox churches themselves relied on voluntary gifts until the tithe canons of 567 and 585. [cite:vt24_apjohn] [cite:vt24_ect_flora] [cite:vt24_justin67] [cite:vt24_ce_tithes] They were excluded first by argument and the doctrine of apostolic succession, then by Constantine's edict, which named the Valentinians, seized their meeting houses and ordered a search for their books; no ancient order to burn Gnostic gospels by name, and no execution of their teachers, is documented. [cite:vt24_ah3_03] [cite:vt24_vc365] [cite:vt24_vc366] Part Twenty-Five opens the jar in which some of those books survived.
