Part Four established what is documented about Scofield and what is not. He enlisted in a Confederate regiment at seventeen, was discharged as an alien in September 1862 and crossed to Union lines; he sat in the Kansas legislature and held the post of US Attorney for about six months in 1873 before being, in the Justice Department's words, "forced to resign" over money. [cite:ts4_rushing] [cite:ts4_doj_ks] He was jailed in St. Louis from October 1878 on forgery charges that were dropped by nolle prosequi on 6 November 1879; he was never convicted. [cite:ts4_rushing] He deserted his wife and daughters, was divorced once, in 1883, was converted in 1879, ordained in October 1883 and built a Dallas congregation, and called himself a doctor of divinity without a degree. [cite:ts4_wiki_scofield] [cite:ts4_wiki_kellogg] [cite:ts4_dts] The story that Samuel Untermyer sponsored or financed him rests on shared membership of the Lotos Club and an author's stated assumption, and on no document. [cite:ts4_sfiles] [cite:ts4_cyf] None of this decides whether his notes read the Bible rightly.
