The Annihilated Soul

This section is a reading. It makes no claim that the record proves.

Marguerite Porete's Mirror of Simple Souls describes a soul that has "given up everything except God through Love" and so becomes united with God. She called it the "Annihilated Soul". [cite:hr_wiki_porete] The word is strong, and her judges heard it as dangerous: a soul that has given up everything might seem to have given up the Church, its rules and its sacraments too.

The inner reading hears the same teaching that this shelf found in the Sufi idea of fana, "the complete denial of self and the realization of God", in which the Sufi "'passed away' from his own existence". [cite:sf_brit_fana] It is also the teaching of the Hermetic ascent, in which the soul gives back at each sphere what it had taken on, and of Luria's withdrawal, in which God makes room by stepping back. [cite:ht_mead_poim] [cite:kb_brit_luria] A French-speaking Beguine, a Sufi in Baghdad and a Hermetic writer in Egypt described the same movement: the self is emptied, and what remains is God.

The table shows a pattern the reader can check against the record. In each tradition the teaching of the emptied self was the one that drew the authorities' fire, because a self emptied of everything but God has, by definition, emptied itself of the authorities too. [cite:hr_wiki_porete] [cite:sf_brit_hallaj] The inner reading does not say the authorities were wrong to fear it. It says what they feared: not a doctrine about God, but a person who no longer needed them.

The record limits the reading. Porete wrote within the Christian mystical tradition, in a dialogue between allegorical figures, and the parallels with Sufism and the Hermetica are a reader's comparison, not a historical connection. Her book was condemned for specific propositions, read by her judges in a specific way. [cite:hr_wiki_porete]
