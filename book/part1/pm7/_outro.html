Part Seven followed the trips. More than a quarter of the House's privately sponsored foreign trips since 2012 went to Israel, three-quarters of them paid for by AIPAC's foundation, at a cost of at least $10 million; about half of the House serving in 2024 had traveled with AIPAC. [cite:cnsmd_trips_study] The trips are allowed because the 2007 ethics law exempted charities, an exception AIPAC sought. [cite:intercept_aief_donors] Below Congress, about 250 state legislators traveled in 2025 at the Israeli government's expense, and police officials have traveled on exchange programs since 2002. [cite:pm_idcap_50states] [cite:jta_adl_police]

What is settled is who paid and who went. What is contested is what the trips are: education about an ally, or a curated introduction by the same network that funds campaigns. What the record leaves open is the full extent of travel below Congress, which no federal body records, and the identity of most of the foundation's donors.
