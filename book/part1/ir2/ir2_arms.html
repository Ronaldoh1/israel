Arming the Islamic Republic, 1980–1984

Iraq invaded Iran on 22 September 1980. The war lasted until a ceasefire on 20 August 1988 and killed, by most estimates, more than a million people, more of them Iranian than Iraqi. [cite:ir2_brit_war] [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] For the whole of that war Iranian officials denounced Israel as an illegitimate state; for much of it, Israeli weapons and spare parts reached the Iranian armed forces. This section sets out what the record shows about those sales before 1985, what Israeli officials said about them, and what Iran's leaders said. The section that follows covers the better-documented shipments of 1985 and 1986, which became part of the Iran-Contra affair. [scene:ir2_contra|Iran-Contra: the Israeli leg]

Tyres before the war

The first sales came before Iraq attacked. In early 1980, while Iran was holding American diplomats hostage in Tehran, Israel sold Iran tyres for its American-built F-4 Phantom jets; a shipment of 250 tyres was flown in on 24 October 1980, as The New York Times reported the following August. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] The Carter administration learned of the sales and pressed Israel to stop. Zbigniew Brzezinski, Carter's national security adviser, later wrote that "we learned, much to our dismay," that the Israelis had been secretly supplying spare parts. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] IranWire, the Iranian exile news outlet, reports that Prime Minister Menachem Begin confirmed the tyre shipment and that, in return for the military aid, Khomeini "permitted many Iranian Jews to emigrate to the United States or Israel." [cite:ir2_iranwire_oil] A direct exchange of arms for emigration is reported but not documented in any agreement this book found; a 1989 history of the war says only that the clandestine relationship "allowed thousands to emigrate." [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] Part Three examines the community's emigration on its own terms. [scene:ir3_facts|The Jews of Iran: the facts]

Operation Seashell and the scale of the trade

In 1981, according to Ronen Bergman's account, Israel sold Iran about $75 million worth of arms from its own stocks, including 150 M-40 anti-tank guns and 24,000 shells, in an operation code-named Seashell. The material was flown on aircraft chartered from an Argentine cargo airline until one of them crashed after colliding with a Soviet aircraft in July 1981; after that the shipments went by sea. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] [cite:ir2_hnn_bergman] Much of what Israel sold in these years, according to the historian Trita Parsi, was paid for with Iranian oil. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport]

How much Israel sold in total is not established. The estimates differ by more than a factor of twenty, and none rests on a published Israeli or Iranian government record. In March 1982 The New York Times reported that $100 million to $200 million in arms and ammunition had reached Iran from Western Europe over eighteen months, with Israel possibly involved in transferring nearly half. [cite:ir2_csm_1982] Israeli press reports cited in December 1986 put Israel's sales at $50 million to $100 million over several years. [cite:ir2_csm_relat86] Parsi gives an estimate from Tel Aviv University's Jaffee Center for Strategic Studies of about $500 million between 1981 and 1983; a 1987 book by Jonathan Marshall, Peter Dale Scott and Jane Hunter put it at more than $500 million a year; and in December 1991 Seymour Hersh reported in The New York Times that internal US government reports put Israel's sales close to $2 billion a year in the early 1980s. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] An Iranian arms dealer, Ahmad Haidari, told Parsi that about 80 percent of the weapons Tehran bought after the war began came from Israel. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] These are estimates by people with different access and different interests, and they are presented as a range.

Sharon's statements

Israel's government first acknowledged the trade in May 1982, when Defence Minister Ariel Sharon, on a visit to the United States, said he had "deliberately confirmed" reports of shipments in order to clear Israel of "the smear of being Iran's major arms supplier." He said that "no America-made equipment was involved and quantities shipped were very small," and that Israel had been telling American officials about the shipments for months. [cite:ir2_djn_sharon82] The same month, as quoted in a 1987 book, he told The Washington Post that "Iraq is Israel's enemy and we hope that diplomatic relations between us and Iran will be renewed as in the past." [cite:ir2_jc_sharon]

His later statements did not match the first. The same book quotes him at a press conference in Paris, four months after the Post interview, saying that "Israel has a vital interest in the continuing of the war in the Persian Gulf, and in Iran's victory." [cite:ir2_jc_sharon] Another account dates a Paris press conference at which Sharon said the sales had American consent to 28 September 1983, a year later; whether these are two events or one dated differently is not resolved in the sources this book could check. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] In 1982 Moshe Arens, then Israel's ambassador in Washington, told The Boston Globe that Israel had shipped American spare parts to Iran, coordinated with the US government "at almost the highest levels," which contradicted Sharon's assurance that no American equipment was involved. [cite:ir2_csm_relat86] The Washington Post later reported that Secretary of State Alexander Haig had secretly approved Israeli shipments of American arms in 1981; Haig denied it. [cite:ir2_csm_relat86] In December 1991 US officials told reporters that the Reagan administration had changed policy in 1981 to let Israel sell "several billion dollars' worth" of American-made arms to Iran; that account remains unconfirmed by any released US document this book found. [cite:ir2_tbt_1991]

Why Israel did it, in its officials' words

Israeli officials gave several reasons, at different times and mostly without their names. The reasons reported in 1986 were to keep Iraq, which Israel regarded as its most dangerous enemy, tied down in a long war; to keep intelligence contacts with Iranian officers and officials; and to earn foreign currency. [cite:ir2_csm_interests86] Officials quoted that November said the war "ties down Iraq" and kept Arab states distracted, and that Iran, "sitting astride the Persian Gulf," was too important to give up on after Khomeini. [cite:ir2_csm_plays86b] Senior officials quoted in December 1986 added the protection of Iran's Jewish community. [cite:ir2_djn_fallguy86] Bergman sums up three aims: to recover influence lost with the Shah, to prevent an Iraqi conquest of Iran, and to keep Israel's arms industry busy. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] These are the logic of the periphery doctrine described in Part One, applied to a government whose leader named Israel as an enemy of the revolution. [cite:hz1_merip_khomeini] [scene:ir1_periphery|The periphery doctrine]

The relationship also had a nuclear dimension. Iran's air force bombed Iraq's Osirak reactor in September 1980, damaging secondary buildings, and Israel destroyed it on 7 June 1981. A 2011 RAND study says Israel received Iranian intelligence for its strike; IranWire, reviewing the record in 2024, found "no credible evidence of direct collaboration." The claim is contested. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] [cite:ir2_iranwire_oil]

What Iran said

Iran's leaders denied the trade. In a speech on 24 August 1981 Khomeini called reports of Israeli arms deliveries false and the claims of cooperation "childish nonsense," according to Parsi. [cite:ir2_wiki_israelsupport] Later, Iranian officials acknowledged parts of the record: Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani, Speaker of parliament during the war and later president, described in an interview with the Tehran daily Hamshahri how Israeli HAWK missiles had arrived in 1985 and "had to be returned." [cite:ir2_iranwire_oil] The next section returns to that shipment.

The American tilt

Israeli and American policy diverged. In 1984 the government of Shimon Peres stopped arms supplies to Iran under American pressure. [cite:ir2_csm_plays86b] The United States moved closer to Iraq; by 1987 it was giving Iraq detailed intelligence on Iranian deployments, including satellite imagery, although it had firm evidence from 1983 onward that Iraq was using chemical weapons against Iranian troops. A retired US Air Force officer who served in Baghdad recalled President Reagan writing "An Iranian victory is unacceptable" in the margin of a memo. [cite:ir2_fp_saddam] For most of the war, then, Israel's stated interest was that Iraq should not win, and Washington's was that Iran should not.
