Part Eighteen set the whole volume in one ledger and found that the American Dream is a promise kept broadly for the generation born around 1940 and unevenly since: about half of children born in the early 1980s out-earned their parents, against nine in ten born in 1940, while the odds of climbing from the bottom fifth held near 8 or 9 in 100 and were about a quarter as high for Black children as for white. [cite:ad3_fading_paper] [cite:ad2_chetty_trends] [cite:ad13_oi_race_sum] It found the promise kept where federal law was built to keep it, with old-age poverty cut from 35.2 to 9.9 per cent and the uninsured rate less than half its 2009 level, and narrowed where costs outran incomes, with a home at 5.0 years of median income against 3.2 in the 1990s and the largest college grant covering about a quarter of a public college year against more than three-quarters in 1975–76. [cite:ad10_census_a3] [cite:us16_census_2025] [cite:us16_khn_uninsured] [cite:us14_jchs_pti] [cite:ad7_nasfaa_pell] On a scorecard whose every rule and weight is printed, the seven promises averaged 4.7 out of 10, between 4.6 and 5.7 under every test: health care and a home hold with modification, a secure job and a secure retirement hold for some, and fair rules, college as a ladder and a better life than one's parents fall short on these measures, a result that is this book's editorial synthesis, not a discovered fact. The levers with documented effects, from the 2021 child credit, after whose expiry child poverty on the supplemental measure rose from 5.2 to 12.4 per cent, to automatic enrolment and Medicaid expansion, carry documented critics and have been pulled and reversed by both parties. [cite:us18_scripps_ctc] [cite:ad18_nber_corinth] [cite:ad10_vanguard_has26] [cite:ad18_ucd_wherry] [cite:ad18_ohp_nejm] The Sophia platform's reading of the Dream as a story that keeps an extraction system running remains an interpretation, answered by a record in which belief fell as outcomes fell and in which the same laws that narrowed some promises kept others. [cite:ad16_gallup_opp24] [cite:ad16_forrt_brandt] The Dream, on this record, is neither an illusion nor a guarantee but a promise carried by machinery that can be read, measured and changed.
