Part Thirteen established that the Church's own statistics count 229,090 Catholic schools with about 64.6 million pupils, 4.47 million university students and 103,951 charitable and health institutions at the end of 2023, with Africa holding most primary pupils, the Americas most university students and Europe most homes for the elderly; under canon law these are directed by bishops, parishes and orders, not owned by the Holy See. [cite:vt13_pv_dossier] [cite:vt13_fides_pdf] [cite:vt13_cic_803] The Jesuit networks count 177 universities, not 228, and 27 in the United States plus one in Belize; their American members have been held since the late 1960s and early 1970s by boards with lay members, under norms from Rome that give the local bishop a defined say over the teaching of theology. [cite:vt13_em_map2024] [cite:vt13_ajcu_home] [cite:vt13_bc_jesuithist] [cite:vt13_usccb_excorde] Of the 33 universities ranked highest by U.S. News in September 2026, three were founded by Catholics, thirteen by Protestant churches or ministers, ten by private nonsectarian founders and seven by legislatures, among them the University of Virginia, whose founders "proposed no professor of Divinity". [cite:vt13_pq_usnews2027b] [cite:vt13_wiki_colonial] [cite:vt13_fo_rockfish] Rome's most direct reach is the appointment of bishops through 128 nunciatures, shared by negotiation with China since 2018, while its ties to Lutheran, Orthodox and Anglican churches rest on signed agreements, not authority.
