Part Eleven sets the two economies side by side from 1980 to 2026. It measures output per person in constant dollars and at purchasing-power parity, and growth decade by decade, and shows why total growth and growth per person tell different stories for a country whose population has more than doubled. It then sets Israel's 1985 stabilisation plan, which ended an inflation of several hundred per cent, beside the American turn of 1979 to 1986 under Paul Volcker's Federal Reserve and the tax laws of 1981 and 1986, and follows both central banks to their decisions of September 2026. It compares how much each state taxes, whom it taxes, and what each owes, and closes with concentration: Israel's business groups and the 2013 law that broke up their pyramids, set against the debate over corporate power in the United States, and the trade that runs between the two countries.
