Shireen Abu Akleh

Shireen Abu Akleh, a Palestinian American who had reported for Al Jazeera for a quarter of a century, was shot dead in May 2022 while covering an Israeli military operation near Jenin. After months of denials, the Israeli army concluded in September 2022 that there was a "high possibility" one of its soldiers had fired the fatal shot, believing, from a vehicle with poor visibility during heavy gunfire, that he was shooting at a gunman. It found no reason to open a criminal investigation. A US-supervised test of the bullet could not match it to a gun, but the State Department concluded that Israel was likely responsible. Her family said Israel was trying "to avoid responsibility" and asked for an International Criminal Court investigation. Video from the scene showed no gunfire in the minutes before she was killed. [cite:pr_npr_abuakleh]

Her killing came seventeen months before the war. It set the pattern this book has followed: a journalist killed, an army account that changed, and no one held to account.

Arrests

Since October 2023, the Committee to Protect Journalists has recorded 118 arrests of journalists and media workers in Israel, the West Bank, Gaza and Jerusalem: 112 by Israeli authorities and six by the Palestinian Authority. As of October 2026, 39 remained in custody. One journalist and one media worker died in Israeli custody. On 1 December 2025 Israel held 31 Palestinian journalists, most of them under administrative detention, without charge or trial. Of 58 journalists the Committee interviewed after their release, 48 had never been charged, and they described "physical assault, forced stress positions, sensory deprivation, sexual violence, and medical neglect." The army told the Committee it "does not arrest journalists simply for being journalists." [cite:pr_cpj_arrests] Part Seven of The West Bank, Documented sets out the system of administrative detention. [scene:wb7_courts|Two systems of law]

The Ramallah Bureau

On 22 September 2024, masked and armed Israeli soldiers entered Al Jazeera's bureau in Ramallah, in the part of the West Bank governed by the Palestinian Authority, while it was on air. "There is a court ruling for closing down Al Jazeera for 45 days," a soldier told the bureau chief, Walid al-Omari, on camera; the order, al-Omari said, accused the network of "incitement to and support of terrorism." The cameras were taken. [cite:pr_newarab_ramallah] Israel had already closed Al Jazeera's operations inside Israel that May, under the law described in Part Eight. [scene:pr8_gate|The gate]
