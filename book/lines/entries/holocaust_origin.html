The Holocaust changed the urgency, the politics and the world's willingness to act, but the plan for a Jewish state, and much of the machinery to build it, existed decades before Hitler took power in 1933. Theodor Herzl published The Jewish State in 1896, weighing Palestine against Argentina. [cite:herzl_judenstaat] The Zionist movement formally rejected a British offer in East Africa in 1905 and kept Palestine as its goal. [cite:wiki_seventh_zionist_congress] Britain pledged support for "a national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine in 1917, [cite:balfour_letter] the Allied powers ratified that at San Remo in 1920, [cite:san_remo_wiki] and the League of Nations Mandate approved in 1922 bound Britain to facilitate Jewish immigration. [cite:mandate_palestine_wiki]

By then the movement also had a bank (1899), a land fund (1901) and an organized American lobbying and fundraising base. What the war years did change is real: the Biltmore Program of May 1942 was the movement's first explicit, public demand for a "Jewish Commonwealth." [cite:biltmore_wiki] The Holocaust accelerated statehood; it did not originate it.
