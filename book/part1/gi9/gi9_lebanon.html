South Lebanon: From Five Hilltops to the Forward Defense Line

Israel's previous occupation of south Lebanon, the "security zone" it held from 1985 until May 2000, ended in a withdrawal that the UN Secretary-General certified on 16 June 2000 as compliance with resolution 425, leaving only the disputed Shebaa Farms. [cite:hz4_crs_shebaa] [cite:hz4_crs_sla] That history is told in Part Eight and in the Hezbollah volume. [scene:gi8_lebanon|The Lebanon front, 1978–2000] [scene:hz4_zone|The security zone] This section follows the second occupation, which began in the war of 2024 and grew in the war of 2026. The full military and diplomatic story is in the Hezbollah volume; [scene:hz8_war2026|The 2026 war] [scene:hz8_now|Where things stand] here the question is narrower: how much Lebanese ground Israel held, what it said the ground was for, and what Lebanon and the United Nations said.

Five positions, 2024–2026

The cessation of hostilities that took effect on 27 November 2024 provided for a phased Israeli withdrawal over a period that "should not exceed 60 days," with only Lebanese state forces to be armed south of the Litani River. [cite:hz8_toi_text] The deadline was extended to 18 February 2025, and on that date Israel announced it would stay at five hilltop posts inside Lebanon. Its military spokesman said, "We need to remain at those points at the moment to defend Israeli citizens"; President Joseph Aoun insisted on full withdrawal, and Hezbollah's leader, Naim Qassem, said Israel "must fully withdraw" and had "no excuse." [cite:hz8_nbc_jan26] [cite:hz8_national_five] UNIFIL, reporting on the first year of the truce, recorded the Israeli army at five locations plus two buffer areas north of the Blue Line, more than 2,500 Israeli ground activities north of the line and almost 7,800 air violations. [cite:hz8_un_unifil_year] By January 2026 the Lebanese army said it had operational control south of the Litani except at the five Israeli positions. [cite:hz8_cnn_phase1]

The 2026 war and the new line

The second war began on 2 March 2026, when Hezbollah fired on Israel after the joint US–Israeli strikes on Iran of 28 February. [cite:hz8_am_mar2] Israeli ground operations began on 16 March; Defence Minister Israel Katz said the displaced would not return south of the Litani until the safety of northern Israel's residents was guaranteed. [cite:hz8b_afp_ground] A ten-day truce began on the night of 16 April. Al Jazeera described the line Israel then held as up to about ten kilometres deep, with residents barred from 55 villages; Netanyahu said, "we are not leaving." [cite:hz8b_aj_yellow] On 19 April the Israeli military published its first public map of what it called the "Forward Defense Line," running roughly five to ten kilometres from the border and placing dozens of villages south of it. "Five divisions, alongside Israeli Navy forces, are operating simultaneously south of the forward defense line," the military said; their purpose was to dismantle Hezbollah's infrastructure and "prevent direct threats to communities in northern Israel." Katz said border houses that Hezbollah used would be demolished. [cite:gi9_alarabiya_map] Lebanese and international reporting came to call it the Yellow Line, the same name used in Gaza.

The line did not mark the furthest point of the advance. On 31 May Israeli troops crossed the Litani and took Beaufort castle, the deepest Israeli incursion into Lebanon in more than 25 years, and declared the area between the Litani and the Zahrani a combat zone. [cite:hz8b_ap_beaufort] A ceasefire took effect on 19 June under the US–Iran interim deal, and on 26 June Israel and Lebanon signed a framework in Washington for the Lebanese army to take over the south as Hezbollah was disarmed; Qassem called it "a surrender of sovereignty," and Katz ordered the army to prepare for an "extended stay." [cite:hz8b_olj_jun19] [cite:hz8b_almon_framework] [cite:hz8b_aj_reject] In July the two sides agreed on "pilot zones" to be handed to the Lebanese army, with no timetable for a wider withdrawal; Israel left the first village, Zawtar al-Gharbieh, at the end of July. [cite:hz8b_afp_pilot] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines]

By September, NPR reported, the zone behind the Yellow Line covered about 6 per cent of Lebanon and was about five miles (eight kilometres) wide in places, within the five-to-ten-kilometre range of the army's April map, with dozens of villages emptied or destroyed and their residents barred; Lebanon's government counted more than 1.2 million people displaced in the war, and Israel 39 soldiers, a contractor and two civilians killed by Hezbollah. [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] The depth is therefore best given as a range, about five to ten kilometres by the army's map, Al Jazeera's and NPR's accounts. The 6 per cent figure is NPR's; the Israeli military's April map gave a depth but no area, and no Israeli or Lebanese official figure for the zone's area was found.

What each side says the zone is

Israel's stated position is that the zone is a security measure tied to Hezbollah's weapons. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar said on 28 April 2026: "Israel has no territorial ambitions in Lebanon," adding that the presence by the northern border "serves one purpose: protecting our citizens." [cite:gi9_nahar_saar] Netanyahu has tied the duration to disarmament rather than to a date: "as long as it is not disarmed, we will remain in this zone," he said in October, adding that he did not trust Lebanon's government to disarm Hezbollah. [cite:hz8b_national_tampa]

Lebanon's government treats the zone as an occupation of its territory and demands full withdrawal. Going into military talks in Tampa, Florida, on 20 October 2026, the presidency said its delegation would press for an "end to Israeli attacks in Lebanon"; it rejected direct coordination between the two armies and demanded third-party verification, while the pilot-zone process had stalled for months. [cite:gi9_toi_tampa] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said in September that dozens of villages remained under Israeli occupation. [cite:hz8b_olj_salam] Hezbollah's position, as Qassem put it on 6 October, is that it is "ready to cooperate" on the state's monopoly of arms but not under "American diktats," and that "Israel cannot remain in southern Lebanon indefinitely." [cite:hz8b_olj_qassem]

The United Nations position rests on resolution 1701 of 2006, which called for Israel's withdrawal and an area between the Blue Line and the Litani free of armed personnel other than the Lebanese army and UNIFIL. [cite:hz5_un_1701] The force charged with monitoring it is leaving. On 28 August 2025 the Security Council unanimously adopted resolution 2790, extending UNIFIL for a final time to 31 December 2026, followed by a year of "orderly and safe drawdown and withdrawal"; the United States said Lebanese forces should take on the responsibility, and France warned that a premature departure could weaken the Lebanese government's efforts in the south. [cite:gi9_un_2790] Aoun has said he would prefer an extension and otherwise another international force; as of late September none had been agreed. [cite:hz8b_olj_unifil] [cite:hz8b_olj_salam]

What this does and does not show

The documented facts are an occupied zone deeper than at any time since 2000, held without a date of departure, with villages destroyed inside it, and the departure of the UN force that has watched the border since 1978. [cite:hz8b_ap_beaufort] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] [cite:gi9_un_2790] Analysts at the Carnegie Middle East Center described the south as "a wasteland that has functioned as a de facto buffer zone," and noted that hard-line Israeli voices had floated the Awali River, north of the Litani, as the line for a Hezbollah-free zone. [cite:gi9_carnegie_ring] What the record reviewed for this part does not contain is any Israeli government decision, Knesset bill or coalition document claiming sovereignty over Lebanese land, or any official statement that the zone is permanent; the government's stated position is the opposite. The one programme document that drew a line into Lebanon, the Zionist Organization's 1919 proposal, ran its border south of Sidon and asked for the Litani's waters by international arrangement, and the record reviewed here contains no Israeli government claim based on it. [scene:gi2_map1919|The 1919 map] Whether the 2026 zone follows the 1985 pattern, ending in withdrawal, or becomes a standing frontier is an open question as of October 2026; Part Twelve sets out the earlier withdrawals.
