The Fastest Years of Settlement

The numbers. Peace Now and Kerem Navot, which track settlement from Israeli government records, counted 27,941 settlement housing units advanced through planning in 2025, more than double the previous annual record. Since December 2022, the cabinet has approved 102 new settlements, 34 of them in a single decision in March 2026. New outposts, built without authorization, rose from 33 in 2023 to 61 in 2024 and 91 in 2025, most of them farms and hilltop outposts of the kind described in Part Eight. [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis]

Who runs it. The coalition agreement of December 2022 created a Settlements Administration in the Defense Ministry under Bezalel Smotrich, the finance minister and leader of Religious Zionism, who was given a second ministerial post there. In June 2023 the cabinet moved the approval of settlement planning to him, and powers over civilian affairs in Area C that had been held by the army were moved to a civilian deputy reporting to his administration. In March 2023 the Knesset repealed the part of the 2005 disengagement law that barred Israelis from the northern West Bank. In February 2026 the cabinet transferred some demolition powers in Areas A and B, which Oslo gave to the Palestinian Authority, to Israeli authorities, for sites deemed to harm heritage, the environment or water, and changed the rules on Israelis buying West Bank land. [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis]

E1

E1 is the land between Jerusalem and the settlement of Ma'ale Adumim. Building there would link the two, effectively divide the West Bank in two, and, its critics say, make a Palestinian capital in East Jerusalem effectively impossible. Under international pressure, the plan had been frozen for two decades. In August 2025 Israel's planning council gave final approval to 3,401 homes there. Smotrich, announcing it, said the plan would "permanently bury the idea of a Palestinian state," and that "they will talk about a Palestinian dream, and we will continue to build a Jewish reality." He presented it as Israel's answer to the decisions of Britain, France and Canada to recognize a Palestinian state. [cite:wb_itv_e1] [cite:wb_pn_mirabilis]

The Annexation Votes, and Washington's Answer

On 23 July 2025 the Knesset voted 71 to 13 for a non-binding motion declaring the West Bank "an inseparable part of the Land of Israel" and calling on the government to apply Israeli sovereignty to the settlements, Judea, Samaria and the Jordan Valley. [cite:wb_toi_71_13] On 22 October 2025, during a visit by Vice President JD Vance, a bill to apply sovereignty to all the settlements passed a preliminary reading by 25 votes to 24. Netanyahu's office called it a "deliberate political provocation" by the opposition and said that without his party's support it would go nowhere. Vance called the vote "very stupid." President Trump said, "It won't happen," because "I gave my word to the Arab countries," and that Israel "would lose all of its support from the United States" if it annexed. [cite:wb_toi_trump_annex]

Sanctions, Imposed and Lifted

On 1 February 2024 President Biden signed Executive Order 14115, declaring settler violence a national emergency that "undermine[s] the security of Israel," and creating sanctions on those who attacked civilians, forced them from their homes or seized their property. Four settlers were named first; more followed. [cite:wb_willkie_14115] On 20 January 2025, his first day back in office, President Trump revoked the order. Four days later the Treasury removed all 18 individuals and 17 entities it had sanctioned, among them the settlement organization Amana, the Hilltop Youth, and several herding farm outposts; the list also included one Palestinian armed group, the Lions' Den of Nablus. [cite:wb_ofac_20250124]

Where the record stands, as of October 2026: no law of annexation has been passed, and the US President has said none will be; while the administration of the territory, the pace of settlement and the removal of US sanctions have all moved in the direction the annexation motions called for.
