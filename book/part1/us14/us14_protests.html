Cottage Cheese, Tents and the Trajtenberg Committee

In the summer of 2011 the cost of living became, by many accounts, the largest social protest in Israel's history. It began with a dairy product and a Facebook page and grew, within weeks, into tent camps in city centres and marches of several hundred thousand people. The government answered with an expert committee whose recommendations were partly enacted. Because the episode tested what a state does when its citizens say they cannot afford to live, this section follows it from the price rise to the policy, and then sets the outcome beside what the OECD still found fourteen years later. [cite:us14_aje_timeline] [cite:us14_wiki_trajtenberg]

The cottage-cheese boycott

Israel lifted state price supervision of dairy products in 2006. By June 2011 the price of cottage cheese, a staple, had risen nearly 40 per cent in three years, to just over $2 for a standard tub; butter had risen 39.1 per cent and a small carton of cream 69 per cent. Tnuva was the main producer. [cite:us14_jta_cottage] In mid-June Yitzhak (Itzik) Alrov, a 25-year-old cantor, opened a Facebook page calling for a boycott until the price fell; it drew 90,000 members within a week and more than 105,000 in two weeks. Finance Minister Yuval Steinitz said he would "break the monopoly" in the dairy industry, and a Knesset committee called for price controls to be restored. [cite:us14_jta_cottage] [cite:us14_ap_cottage] On 30 June Tnuva cut the price of a 250-gram tub by about 25 per cent, from close to NIS 8 to NIS 5.90, and Strauss and Tara followed. [cite:us14_ap_cottage]

The tent protests

Two weeks later the anger moved to housing. On 14 July 2011 Daphni Leef, a 25-year-old video editor, pitched a tent in Habima Square in Tel Aviv and opened a Facebook page inviting others to join. [cite:us14_wiki_j14] Al Jazeera's timeline dates the encampment of hundreds of young people on Rothschild Boulevard, in protest at high rents, to 17 July. [cite:us14_aje_timeline] The context was rents up 34 per cent nationally and 49 per cent in the Tel Aviv area since 2005. [cite:us14_wiki_j14] Tens of thousands marched on 23 July; on 30 July more than 100,000 gathered in Tel Aviv, with hundreds of thousands protesting nationwide; about 300,000 demonstrated on 6 August. [cite:us14_aje_timeline]

The largest night came on 3 September, the "March of the Million". Israel Hayom counted at least 300,000 in Tel Aviv, 40,000 to 60,000 in Jerusalem and at least 35,000 in Haifa, more than 400,000 in all; Al Jazeera reported an estimated 400,000, and later accounts give about 460,000. The figures are estimates whose authors were not named. [cite:us14_ih_400k] [cite:us14_aje_timeline] [cite:us14_wiki_j14] The demands went beyond rent to the cost of raising children and, from southern dairy farmers, opposition to a government plan to import dairy products: the protest held consumers who wanted cheaper food and producers who feared cheaper imports. [cite:us14_ih_400k]

The government's answer

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu responded first with a housing package on 26 July, promising 50,000 homes and subsidised public transport for students, which the activists rejected as insufficient. [cite:us14_aje_timeline] On 8 August he appointed the Committee for Socioeconomic Change, chaired by Professor Manuel Trajtenberg, then head of the higher-education planning and budgeting committee. [cite:us14_wiki_trajtenberg] It reported on 26 September. Its main recommendations were 196,000 new flats over five years, a fifth of them affordable; free education from age three; corporate and capital-gains taxes raised to 25 per cent; lower prices for supervised products such as milk and eggs; express buses from the periphery; and a 50 per cent public-transport discount for students. Netanyahu called the report an important landmark. Leef said it contained no far-reaching change, and fellow organiser Regev Contes said the numbers "don't even come near what the public needs". [cite:us14_ynet_trajtenberg] The committee also recommended slower growth in defence spending and competition measures in cement, ports, fuel and dairy. [cite:us14_wiki_trajtenberg]

The cabinet chose to enact the package in stages rather than at once. The Knesset approved the tax changes on 5 December 2011; the competition measures passed the cabinet in December 2011 and the housing plan in March 2012; measures on Haredi employment were left out. [cite:us14_wiki_trajtenberg] Free preschool for three- and four-year-olds began in the 2012–13 school year, with NIS 1.3 billion added to the Education Ministry's budget. [cite:us14_jpost_saar] [cite:us14_taub_preschool] The mobile-phone reform, already under way, cut bills sharply over the same period. [cite:us14_globes_kahlon]

What changed and what did not

Measured against the protest's own grievances, the record is mixed. On this book's reading, the parts of the package that depended on a single decision were delivered: the tax rises, free preschool from three and, separately, mobile prices that by the OECD's 2024 data left telecoms 30 per cent below the member average. [cite:us14_wiki_trajtenberg] [cite:us14_taub_preschool] [cite:us14_toi_food52] The parts that depended on breaking market structure or building at scale were not. The cottage-cheese price cut held attention, but in 2014 a new protest compared the price of an Israeli chocolate pudding with a much cheaper German version, and in 2022 there were protests over pasta prices. [cite:us14_ecoscope] By the OECD's 2024 data, dairy and eggs in Israel cost 64 per cent more than the developed-country average, and the 2025 Economic Survey again recommended cutting the tariffs on dairy, fruit and vegetables. [cite:us14_toi_food52] [cite:us14_ecoscope] On housing, the number of average monthly salaries needed to buy the average home rose from 129 in 2010 to 155 in 2022. [cite:us14_wiki_j14] [cite:us14_globes_salaries2023]

The American record in the same years

The United States produced no comparable government commission on the cost of living in the sources used for this part, and its price pressures fell on different items. Its telecoms were among the OECD's dearest, at 51 per cent above the average, while Israel's were 30 per cent below it. [cite:us14_toi_food52] Its housing ratio rose from 4.1 in 2019 to 5.0 in 2024, and a record 22.7 million renter households paid more than 30 per cent of income for housing in 2024. [cite:us14_jchs_pti] [cite:us14_nlihc_jchs2026] Employer family health premiums rose 26 per cent in the five years to 2025. [cite:us14_kff_ehbs2025] The two countries hold national elections within a week of each other, Israel on 27 October and the United States on 3 November 2026; the figures in this part are as of October 2026.
