The Movement and the Government

Gush Emunim. The "Bloc of the Faithful" was founded at Kfar Etzion at the beginning of 1974, in the aftermath of the 1973 war, and its ideology was rooted in the teaching of Rabbi Zvi Yehuda Kook, for whom settling the whole Land of Israel was a religious duty. Its attempts to settle in the hills of Samaria, the first near Oron and then near Sebastia, were removed by the army seven times; after the eighth, at the old Sebastia railway station at Hanukkah 1975, the government compromised and let the settlers stay in an army camp at Kaddum. [cite:wb_ej_gush] The pattern of the Hebron hotel was repeated: an unauthorized settlement, a standoff, and a compromise that left the settlers in place.

1977. When the Likud under Menachem Begin won the election of 1977, Gush Emunim presented the new government with an "emergency" settlement plan, and its blueprint of October 1978 resembled those drawn up by Matityahu Drobles, joint head of the Jewish Agency's settlement department, and by the agriculture minister, Ariel Sharon. [cite:wb_ej_gush] Where the Allon Plan had kept settlement to the Jordan Valley and the edges of Jerusalem, these plans put settlements in the hill country itself, among the Palestinian towns. [cite:wb_wiki_allon]

The numbers. In sixteen years the settler population outside East Jerusalem grew more than twenty-five-fold, from 4,400 in 1977 to 116,300 in 1993, by the count of the Israeli group Peace Now. [cite:wb_peacenow_pop] The Gaza volume's account of [scene:gz2_economy|the same period] shows how the Camp David framework of 1978 and the peace with Egypt in 1979 left the territories' status unresolved.
