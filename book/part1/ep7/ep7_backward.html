Walking It Backward

The usual way to tell this story starts with Epstein and moves outward to the people around him. This section runs the other way. It treats Epstein as one point in a larger structure and asks, for everything that reached him (a job, an introduction, money, protection, a tax status), who sent it, and who stood behind the sender. Each of these is a line that can be followed upward, one step at a time, until the documents run out. Where a line stops is itself a finding: it shows exactly what is not known, and who holds the page that would continue it.

The rule for every step is the one that governs this whole book. A step counts only if a document shows it: a payment, a contract, an appointment, a sworn account, a filing. A rumour, a resemblance or a shared room does not count, however suggestive.

The first job

Epstein began teaching maths and physics at the Dalton School in Manhattan in September 1974, without a degree. [cite:ep1_nyt_dalton2019] One popular account says he was hired by the headmaster, Donald Barr, the father of the later Attorney General William Barr, and Business Insider reported it that way. [cite:ep7_bi_dalton] But Barr had announced in February 1974 that he would leave at the end of that school year, and Epstein started after he had gone; whether Barr played any part is not established, and Dalton has not answered the question. [cite:ep7_snopes_barr] The line stops here: no record shows who recommended him.

Wall Street

The next step is documented. A Dalton parent called Alan "Ace" Greenberg of Bear Stearns, and Greenberg hired Epstein in 1976; Epstein had tutored Greenberg's son. [cite:ep1_bi_birthday2025] [cite:ep1_vf_ward2003] Greenberg is the first powerful man in the record to open a door for him. Epstein left the firm in March 1981, in circumstances never fully explained. [cite:ep1_vf_ward2003]

The missing years and Towers

Between 1981 and 1987 the documents thin out. The next firm step is Steven Hoffenberg's Towers Financial, which paid Epstein as a consultant from 1987 and later collapsed in a fraud of about $460 million. [cite:ep1_vf_ward2003] [cite:ep1_npr_hoffenberg2022] Hoffenberg, speaking from prison, said Epstein had been mentored after Bear Stearns by Douglas Leese, a British defence contractor named in the British parliamentary record in connection with the Al Yamamah arms deal, and that Leese introduced him to the arms dealer Adnan Khashoggi and, he claimed, to Robert Maxwell. Leese's son confirms that his father was a mentor of sorts to Epstein, though not an arms dealer; Leese himself once told a Florida court he was involved in "highly confidential business enterprises". Epstein twice denied knowing Leese when asked in 2002. Leese died in 2011. [cite:ep7_rs_ward2021] The line stops here, at a convicted fraudster's word and a dead man's undisclosed business. The reporter who gathered these accounts, Vicky Ward, wrote that she could not prove all of Hoffenberg's claims. She also established that Epstein secretly cooperated with the prosecutors who convicted Hoffenberg, and was never charged himself. [cite:ep7_rs_ward2021]

The fortune

The largest line runs to Les Wexner. According to the New York Times, it began by chance: an insurance executive and friend of Wexner's, Robert Meister, sat next to Epstein on a flight to Palm Beach. Wexner's own financial adviser, Harold Levin, warned after meeting Epstein, "I smell a rat"; Epstein later had Levin removed, and by 1991 held power of attorney over Wexner's affairs. [cite:ep7_wysu_nyt2025] [cite:ep1_nyt_wexner2019] Wexner told Congress in 2026 that Epstein had been suggested to him as an adviser, at first worked "as a favor" without pay, and was "conniving to gain my trust" from the start. [cite:ep7_yahoo_wexner2026] Here the money line reaches its source: Wexner's own fortune. What the line does not explain is why. The Times put it plainly: "the biggest mystery is what exactly the billionaire got out of his relationship with Epstein." [cite:ep7_wysu_nyt2025] Justice Department documents list Wexner as a potential co-conspirator, which his representatives dispute, and he has never been charged. [cite:ep7_yahoo_wexner2026]

The partner

Ghislaine Maxwell joined Epstein's life in 1991, the year her father died, and was paid by him from 1992. [cite:ep1_cbs_maxwell2025] The line upward from her runs to Robert Maxwell's network. The only account placing Epstein inside it before 1991 is Hoffenberg's, and it is uncorroborated. [cite:ep7_rs_ward2021] The line stops here. Part Seven examines it in full below.

The money after the conviction

After 2008, the largest line runs to Leon Black, the co-founder of Apollo, who says he met Epstein in the mid-1990s and paid him about $158 million between 2012 and 2017 for tax and estate advice that Apollo's own review valued at more than $1 billion. Black paid the Virgin Islands $62 million in 2023 to settle claims related to Epstein. In June 2026 he walked out of a House interview when asked about non-disclosure agreements he had signed with women, then failed to appear for a subpoenaed deposition and sued the committee. [cite:ep7_pbs_black2026] The line is blocked here, not by a lack of documents but by a refusal: the agreements exist, and Congress has asked for them.

The base

The last line runs to the government of the Virgin Islands. According to JPMorgan's court filing, Epstein's companies received about $300 million in territorial tax benefits between 1999 and 2018. Cecile de Jongh, the wife of the territory's governor from 2007 to 2015, managed his local companies for eight years, was paid a salary, bonuses and her children's tuition, and in 2009 certified him as a "bona fide resident" of the territory while he was serving his sentence in Florida. [cite:ep7_rappler_jpm2023] The territory's government, which later sued JPMorgan, says the bank was the party that saw everything and chose profit. [cite:ep7_rappler_jpm2023] The line upward runs to the board that granted the benefits, whose deliberations are not public.

What the walk shows

Followed backward, no line reaches a single hidden figure. Every documented line reaches a named, very rich or very powerful source, and then stops, nearly always at the same kind of barrier: a question nobody with power has been made to answer under oath, or a document that someone holds and has not released. That is the structure above Epstein that the record can actually show. It is not a secret command; it is a set of people and institutions who gave him what he needed and have never been required to explain why.

The walk also shows where an investigation would start if anyone wanted to finish it: the hiring record at Dalton; the business of Douglas Leese; the terms of Wexner's power of attorney; the non-disclosure agreements; the Virgin Islands tax board's files; and the deliberations behind the 2008 agreement. Each is a specific document in a specific place. Part Ten returns to them, because the same lines that built Epstein are still in place for whoever might follow him.
