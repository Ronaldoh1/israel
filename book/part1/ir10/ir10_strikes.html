Talks, a Resolution and Rising Lion

In the spring of 2025 the United States and Iran were negotiating. The talks were mediated by Oman. Iran described them as indirect, with the Omani foreign minister carrying messages between the delegations. The American envoy was Steve Witkoff and the Iranian negotiator was the foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi. A first round was held in Muscat in mid-April, and a second in Rome on 19 April 2025. [cite:ir10_gn_round2] A third round took place in Oman on 26 April and a fourth in Muscat on 11 May. [cite:ir10_ap_round4] On 21 May Oman announced a fifth round, to be held in Rome that Friday, 23 May. [cite:ir10_ap_round5] On 12 June the Omani foreign minister, Badr al-Busaidi, wrote: "I am pleased to confirm the 6th round of Iran US talks will be held in Muscat this Sunday the 15th." [cite:ir10_an_round6]

Enrichment was the point the talks turned on. Witkoff first suggested that Iran might be allowed to enrich to 3.67 per cent, the JCPOA ceiling, and then said that "An enrichment program can never exist in the state of Iran ever again." Araghchi answered that enrichment "should definitely continue and there is no room for compromise on that." He added that Iran might accept limits on its "dimensions, amount and level for trust building." [cite:ir10_ap_round4] In May he said enrichment "will continue — with or without an agreement." The Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei, said: "I don't think nuclear talks with the U.S. will bring results. I don't know." [cite:ir10_ap_round5] At this point Iran held 408.6 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60 per cent, and inspectors had worked without the Additional Protocol since 2021. Part Seven describes how that position was reached. [cite:ir7_isis_may25] [scene:ir7_withdrawal|The withdrawal]

Two other facts form the background. In March 2025 the Director of National Intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, told Congress that the US intelligence community "continues to assess that Iran is not building a nuclear weapon" and that Khamenei "has not authorized the nuclear weapons program he suspended in 2003". She also said that Iran's stockpile of enriched uranium was at its highest level, unprecedented for a state without nuclear weapons. [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard] Then, on 12 June 2025, the IAEA Board of Governors voted 19 to 3, with 11 abstentions, that Iran was not complying with its safeguards obligations. The resolution cited failures since 2019 and uranium traces at undeclared sites. It was the Board's first such finding in nearly two decades. Iran said the resolution had "no technical and legal basis" and announced a new enrichment facility. [cite:ir5_aj_iaea2025]

Thirteen June

In the early hours of 13 June 2025, the day after the Board vote and two days before the scheduled sixth round, Israel attacked Iran. It called the operation Rising Lion. The strikes hit the Natanz enrichment site and military targets, and killed senior commanders and scientists. Among those killed were the chief of the armed forces general staff, Mohammad Bagheri; the commander of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), Hossein Salami; Gholamali Rashid, commander of the Khatam al-Anbiya central headquarters; and Amir Ali Hajizadeh, commander of the IRGC's aerospace force. Several nuclear scientists were also killed. They included Fereydoun Abbasi-Davani, a former head of the Atomic Energy Organization of Iran; Mohammad Mahdi Tehranchi; Abdolhamid Minouchehr, head of nuclear engineering at Shahid Beheshti University; Ahmad Reza Zolfaghari; and Amir Hossein Faghihi, a deputy head of the Atomic Energy Organization. [cite:ir10_na_killed] Counts of the scientists killed differ. Early reports gave six. By the end of the war Israel said it had killed at least 15 senior scientists and about 30 senior commanders. These are Israeli claims. [cite:ir10_toi_numbers] On 14 June Oman said the Muscat round would not take place; its foreign minister added that "diplomacy and dialogue remain the only pathway to lasting peace." [cite:ir10_ii_off]

Netanyahu gave Israel's reason in a video statement that day. He said Iran had "enough highly enriched uranium for nine atom bombs" and had taken "steps to weaponize." He continued: "if not stopped, Iran could produce a nuclear weapon in a very short time. It could be a year. It could be within a few months, less than a year." [cite:ir5_dcnf_jun2025] The warning was conditional, and it was not a claim that a bomb existed. Part Five tests it against his earlier predictions. [scene:ir5_predictions|The predictions] On 14 June the military spokesman said the aim was "eliminating the existential Iranian threat to the State of Israel—the nuclear and missile threat." He said Iran had built infrastructure for "reconverting enriched uranium for clear military purposes." [cite:ir10_idf_jun14] The public record does not include the intelligence behind that last claim. The US intelligence position given to Congress in March had not been publicly revised. When Gabbard's testimony was raised, Trump said: "I don't care what she said," and said Iran was "very close" to a weapon. Iran said its programme was peaceful. [cite:hz11_ap_gabbard] [cite:hz11_pbs_gabbard] On 19 June the IAEA Director General, Rafael Grossi, said: "We did not find in Iran elements to indicate that there is an active, systematic plan to build a nuclear weapon." [cite:ir5_tbs_grossi]

What Washington knew is documented in three parts. The first is the public US statement. On the night of the attack, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said: "Tonight, Israel took unilateral action against Iran. We are not involved in strikes against Iran." He added that Israel had told the United States the action was necessary for its self-defence. [cite:ir10_wh_rubio] The second is what Trump himself said. On 12 June he said of an Israeli strike: "I don't want them going in because, I mean, that would blow it." [cite:ir10_olj_trump] On 13 June he told ABC that the attack had been "excellent" and that "there's more to come. A lot more." [cite:ir10_ih_trump] He also posted that he had given Iran "a 60-day ultimatum" two months earlier: "Today is day 61... Now they have, perhaps, a second chance!" [cite:ir10_olj_trump] The third is reporting. Fox News reported that the administration "knew about the Israeli plans in advance" and that "There were no surprises." The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump had asked Netanyahu on 9 June not to strike. [cite:ir10_med_fox] Together these show that the US government knew the attack was coming and publicly said it was not taking part. Whether Trump privately approved it is not shown by the public record. His own words that week point both ways.

True Promise 3

Iran fired back from the night of 13 June. The IRGC called its attacks "Operation True Promise 3" and described them as "in direct retaliation against Israeli aggression." It said it was hitting military and fuel infrastructure. [cite:ir10_irna_tp3] By Israeli military count, Iran fired about 550 ballistic missiles and about 1,000 drones over twelve days. About 90 per cent of the missiles were intercepted, and at least 31 struck populated areas or critical infrastructure. [cite:ir10_toi_numbers] American THAAD batteries and warships helped intercept them, as set out in [scene:ow7_iran|Defending Israel, fighting Iran]; Part Fourteen counts the cost. [scene:ir14_bill|The American bill]

The worst single strike was in Bat Yam on 15 June, where a missile killed nine people and injured about 200. The same night four women from one family were killed in Tamra. The Weizmann Institute of Science in Rehovot was hit, and on 16 June a strike shut down the Bazan refinery in Haifa. [cite:ir10_aj_map] On 19 June a missile hit Soroka Medical Centre in Beersheba, the largest hospital in southern Israel. Iran's state news agency said the targets were Israeli military intelligence sites at the nearby Gav-Yam technology park. Netanyahu said Iran's "terrorist tyrants" had fired at a hospital and promised to "exact the full price." [cite:ir10_en_soroka] Israel's Health Ministry recorded 3,238 people taken to hospital. Twenty-eight people were killed in Israel, all but one of them civilians, and more than 9,000 were displaced. [cite:ir10_toi_numbers]

Iran's toll was much higher, and the counts differ. By 24 June Iran's Health Ministry gave at least 610 dead. [cite:ir10_toi_numbers] On 7 July the head of Iran's Foundation of Martyrs and Veterans Affairs, Saeed Ohadi, said at least 1,060 had been killed and the figure might reach 1,100. The Washington-based Human Rights Activists News Agency (HRANA) counted 1,190 dead, including 436 civilians and 435 members of the security forces, and 4,475 wounded. [cite:ir10_ap_toll] The two counts use different methods. The government figure is an official total of the dead that does not separate civilians from combatants. HRANA's figure comes from case-by-case reports and includes deaths it could not classify. Neither can be checked independently. This book gives the range: about 1,060 to 1,190 killed in Iran, and 28 killed in Israel.
