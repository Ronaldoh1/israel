Part Four followed Gaza from the withdrawal to the closure. Hamas won the Palestinian elections of January 2006, a soldier, Gilad Shalit, was captured that June, and in June 2007 Hamas took sole control of the strip after fighting Fatah. [cite:gz_wiki_battle2007] [cite:gz_wiki_shalit] In September 2007 Israel's cabinet declared Gaza "hostile territory" and authorized restricting fuel, power, goods and people. [cite:gz_un_hostile2007] The closure that followed limited what entered, almost ended what left, confined fishermen and farmers, and made leaving the strip a matter of permits, while Egypt kept Rafah closed or limited. [cite:gz_wiki_blockade] [cite:gz_un_gaza10] By the UN's measures it left an economy shrinking beside a growing West Bank, an aquifer unfit to drink and 1.2 million people in need of aid. [cite:gz_un_gaza10] [cite:gz_un_gaza2020] The ICRC called it collective punishment; the Palmer panel found the sea blockade lawful; Israel's Supreme Court allowed its fuel and power cuts while holding Israel to the strip's essential humanitarian needs. [cite:gz_icrc_2010] [cite:gz_palmer] [cite:gz_ahmed_hcj] Part Five turns to the wars fought across it.
