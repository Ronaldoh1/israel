Part Eight followed the weapons. Israel's $3.3 billion a year in Foreign Military Financing is paid as a lump sum and spent through the Foreign Military Sales system, under a congressional review shorter than most countries face: 15 days, with higher thresholds, shared with NATO and a few close allies. [cite:ow_crs_aid2025] [cite:ow8_crs_review2021] Since October 2023 that review has been skipped by emergency declaration in December 2023 and again in February and March 2025; by April 2025 there were 751 active cases worth $39.2 billion, and more than 90,000 tons had been delivered. The one hold, on 2,000-pound bombs in May 2024, was lifted in January 2025. [cite:ow8_ap_tank2023] [cite:ow8_at_155] [cite:ow8_toi_rubio2025] [cite:ow8_axios_pause] [cite:ow8_ap_unpause] Missile defence is the most two-way part of the pipeline: co-production with Raytheon and Boeing, US access to Iron Dome's technology, and US-built Tamir interceptors from 2026. [cite:ow8_crs_irondome2014] [cite:ow8_dn_coprod2016] [cite:ow8_rtx_camden] The rules on use are the least: no Israeli unit had been found ineligible under the Leahy laws by mid-2024, the May 2024 report could not verify specific uses, and NSM-20 was revoked in February 2025.
