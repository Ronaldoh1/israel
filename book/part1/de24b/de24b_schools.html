Schools: The Longest File

A school record is often the first file the state keeps on a person and among the longest kept: attendance, grades, discipline, special-education plans, counselling notes, and now the alerts produced by software that reads what students type on school laptops. Colleges add financial-aid forms that carry parents' incomes and Social Security numbers, and for foreign students a federal immigration database tracks enrolment itself. This section sets out who holds these records, which law protects them and where it stops, and what reached enforcement in 2025 and 2026. As elsewhere in this book, a tool's capability is documented as a fact; its use against a given person or group needs its own document.

FERPA and the camera outside its reach

The main federal shield is the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act of 1974 (FERPA), which limits what schools may release from a student's "education records" without consent, subject to listed exceptions such as a health or safety emergency. The definition matters more than the rule. Records that a school's own law-enforcement unit, such as a district police department, creates and maintains for law-enforcement purposes are not education records at all, so FERPA's protections do not apply; their release is governed by school policy and state law, and the Education Department advises schools to keep them separate. [cite:de24b_ed_leu] The same applies to cameras. Surveillance video that a law-enforcement unit creates and keeps for a law-enforcement purpose is not an education record; a copy handed to the principal for a disciplinary case may become one. [cite:de24b_ed_video]

That gap became concrete when school districts bought licence-plate cameras from Flock Safety, the company whose network of automatic plate readers is described in [scene:de24_data_and_fusion|the toolkit part on data]. Flock lets customers share their camera data with other agencies across its network. An investigation by The 74, published with The Guardian on 10 February 2026, examined logs from six Texas districts. On the eight cameras of one district, Alvin, more than 3,100 agencies ran more than 733,000 searches in a single month from December 2025 to January 2026, and 620 of those searches, from 30 agencies, cited immigration reasons. The districts themselves showed no immigration searches, and Flock said customers control their sharing settings. [cite:de28_74_flock] The camera sat at a school, but its data was not a school record.

Software that reads students' writing

Thousands of American schools now use software that scans students' school accounts and devices around the clock for signs of self-harm, violence or bullying and alerts staff. Gaggle is used in about 1,500 districts; GoGuardian and Securly sell similar products. In March 2025 the Associated Press and The Seattle Times reported that a public-records request to Vancouver Public Schools in Washington had returned nearly 3,500 unredacted student documents behind links with no password, that at least six students had potentially been outed to school officials after writing about their sexuality or gender, and that a 2023 RAND study had found "scant evidence" of either benefit or harm. Gaggle said it had since restricted the links to logged-in users after 72 hours. [cite:de24b_ap_gaggle] In Lawrence, Kansas, an AP analysis of public records found more than 1,200 alerts in ten months, nearly two-thirds judged non-issues by school officials and more than 200 set off by homework; nine current and former students sued the district in federal court in August 2025, alleging violations of free speech and unlawful searches. The district declined to comment on the pending case. [cite:de24b_ljw_lawrence] No record links these monitoring products to immigration enforcement.

The files that follow a student

Two larger stores sit above the school. Since November 2005 the federal Institute of Education Sciences has given grants of up to $20 million each to build Statewide Longitudinal Data Systems, which link individual student records from early learning through college and into the workforce, a span the programme calls "P-20W." [cite:de24b_nces_slds] They exist to measure what schooling does for earnings; they are also, by design, a join between education and employment records held by a state.

At the federal level, the Education Department holds the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA), whose accounts hold applicants' and parents' identifying and financial details, and the loan records of more than 42 million borrowers. [cite:us17_ci_defaults] In 2025 financial-aid advisers warned families with an undocumented parent that filing carried some risk; as of May 2025 no sharing of FAFSA data with DHS had been documented, and none is documented since. [cite:de24b_nasfaa_fafsa] What is documented is access from inside: GAO reported on 29 September 2026 that teams from the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) had access to more than 23 systems across four agencies including Education, and that Education gave GAO no record of their background investigations. [cite:de19_gao_sixagency] How defaulted loans reach wages and tax refunds is the subject of [scene:de24b_credit_and_money|the last section of this part].

SEVIS: the one school file built for enforcement

Foreign students are different, because their school record is an immigration record. The Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS), run by ICE, records each student's enrolment, and a terminated record can end a student's lawful status. Freedom-of-information records from 2014 to 2022 show ICE's investigations branch training agents in March 2020 to search SEVIS's 4.9 million records through its Palantir case system. [cite:de24_guardian_icm] In the spring of 2025 more than 1,800 international students had their SEVIS records terminated or their visas revoked, many after checks against the FBI's criminal database; at a court hearing on 25 April 2025 the Justice Department said records would be reactivated while ICE wrote a new policy. The association of international educators, NAFSA, said the reasons given had "ranged from wholly absent, to conflicting, to downright baseless." [cite:de24b_pie_sevis] The use of visa status against student speech, and the court ruling on it, are in [scene:de28_brokers_and_doctrine|the section on brokers and doctrine].

If it were connected

Graded as in [scene:de24b_benefits|the previous section]. School-adjacent cameras into immigration searches: documented, through Flock's sharing network, with the district not the searcher. [cite:de28_74_flock] SEVIS into Palantir's case system: documented for 2014–22 records. [cite:de24_guardian_icm] FAFSA into immigration enforcement: possible. The data would let an officer find a parent's address through a child's aid form; it would need a new disclosure notice or agreement, and none is documented. [cite:de24b_nasfaa_fafsa] Monitoring alerts into police or immigration files: possible, and the FERPA line explains how: an alert passed to a school police unit and kept there would leave FERPA's protection. [cite:de24b_ed_leu] No case of it is documented. State longitudinal systems into enforcement: possible in principle, not supported by any record; they are state-held, and the plainest brake is state law. The strongest protection for a child's file is its dispersal across thousands of districts and fifty states. The weakest point is definitional: a record moved into the hands of a school's police unit, or a camera feed shared across a vendor's network, is no longer an education record.
