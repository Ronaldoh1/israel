On 27 July 1694 a royal charter created the Bank of England, a company through which 1,268 private subscribers had lent the English Crown £1.2 million at 8 percent to fight France. [cite:rs_rd0_boe_museum] Palestine, at that date, was a group of Ottoman districts, part of an empire that had governed the Arab Middle East without internal borders since 1516 and let its recognized religious communities run their own courts and schools. [cite:w_ottoman_millet] Its people were mostly Arabic-speaking Muslims and Christians, with long-established Jewish communities in Jerusalem, Hebron, Safed and Tiberias. No Zionist organization existed, and none would for two centuries. This volume begins its account there, in a City of London where finance already sat inside the legislature, and ends in 1896, the year Theodor Herzl published Der Judenstaat and was refused by the banker who was already paying for Jewish colonies in Palestine. [cite:herzl_judenstaat] [cite:herzl_diaries_vol2_archive]

The stage asks a single question: what actually existed, on the documentary record, before political Zionism did? That means finding out which states, lenders, churches, laws and communities could act on Palestine by 1896, by what instrument, and with what limits. It also means separating the record from the legends that have gathered around several of these institutions, some of them, like the story of Nathan Rothschild and Waterloo, antisemitic in origin. [cite:wiki_dairnvaell_satan_pamphlet]

The actors are many, and they did not act together. The sovereign was the Ottoman sultan: Abdülmecid I, who opened the reform era in 1839, and Abdul Hamid II, who suspended the first parliament in 1878 and whose government barred foreign Jews from settling and buying land in Palestine from 1882. [cite:rs_rd0_gulhane_wiki] [cite:rs_rd0_firstconst_wiki] [cite:rs_rd0_barin_2014] Egypt's Muhammad Ali and his son Ibrahim Pasha held the country from 1831 to 1840, until Britain, Austria, Prussia and Russia restored it to Istanbul. [cite:rs_rd0_oriental1840_wiki] Britain appears as a borrower, an empire guarding its route to India, and the home of an evangelical lobby led by Lord Shaftesbury; the United States, through its ministers at Constantinople and a petition signed by more than four hundred prominent Americans in 1891. [cite:rs_rd0_disraeli_1876] [cite:wiki_christian_zionism] [cite:rs_rd0_frus1888_may] The Rothschild houses and the papal bankers lent to states and to the Pope. On the Palestinian Arab side stand the rebels of 1834, Jerusalem's deputy Yusuf Diya al-Khalidi, the notables who protested against Jewish immigration in 1891 and the mufti Muhammad Tahir al-Husayni. [cite:rs_rd0_rood_1834] [cite:rs_rd0_khalidi_palquest] [cite:rs_rd0_antizionism_wiki] [cite:rs_rd0_beska_2007] On the Jewish side are the Old Yishuv of Jerusalem, the colonists of the First Aliyah and the first arrivals from Yemen, the writers and societies who turned a prayer into a program, the philanthropists Edmond de Rothschild and Maurice de Hirsch, and the Jews who opposed the whole idea, from Reform rabbis in Pittsburgh to the Orthodox community of Jerusalem. [cite:rs_rd0_barin_2014_pop] [cite:rs_rd0_firstaliyah_wiki] [cite:pittsburgh_platform_1885] [cite:rs_rd0_antizionism_wiki_dissent]

The sections follow that board roughly in time. They open with the City of London, the legend of three ruling enclaves, and the loan of 1694. They move to Europe between 1789 and 1815 and the rise of one Frankfurt banking family, then to Palestine between Napoleon's siege of Acre and the bombardment of 1840, through a revolt that historians still argue over. The London house's loans and the papacy's borrowing come next, then the Protestant restorationists of 1839 and 1891. Two Ottoman sections follow: the reform edicts and the parliament of 1877, then the Land Code of 1858 that required land titles to be registered. Then the Jewish story is traced from the religious debate over the Three Oaths, through the first rabbinic and secular programs and the Russian crisis of 1881–82, to the Jews already living in Palestine and the colonies founded from 1878. The Porte's restrictions and the first Arab protests are set beside each other, followed by Edmond de Rothschild's colonies, Hirsch's Argentine alternative, and the Dreyfus Affair that turned a Viennese journalist toward a state.
