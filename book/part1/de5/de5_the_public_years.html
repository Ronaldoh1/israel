The Public Years, 2016–2026

For most of the 2010s Yarvin's political influence, such as it was, ran through a pen name and a small readership. The decade that followed made him a public figure under his own name. This section follows that change, keeping documented contacts, each with its connection type, apart from the influence others attribute to him, as [scene:de1_spine_and_overlay|Part One] requires.

Thiel, Bannon and a leaked email, 2016–2017

In 2017 BuzzFeed News published emails between the far-right writer Milo Yiannopoulos and others, among them messages from Yarvin, written some time after May 2016, in which he said he had been "coaching Thiel," described Thiel as "fully enlightened, just plays it very carefully," and said he had "watched the election at his house." [cite:de5_pein_baffler] Corey Pein, reporting the emails for The Baffler, judged the coaching claim probably boastful. [cite:de5_pein_baffler] It is Yarvin's account of a relationship, not Thiel's, and Yarvin later said that for him "fully enlightened" meant "fully disenchanted." [cite:de1_nyt_interview] Thiel's own recorded view, reported from a 2023 interview with The Atlantic, was that he did not think Yarvin's ideas would "work," though he found him an "interesting and powerful" historian. [cite:de6_cnn_2025] What the record documents between the two men is a venture investment in Yarvin's company and personal acquaintance: Thiel attended a podcast recording Yarvin gave in Los Angeles in February 2020, according to Tablet. [cite:de5_tablet_siegel] [cite:de5_wiki_urbit]

In early 2017, after Donald Trump's first inauguration, reports circulated that Yarvin was advising the President's strategist Steve Bannon. Yarvin denied them, "sometimes playfully and at other times strenuously," as Siegel put it. [cite:de5_tablet_siegel] No document of any contact between the two has been published, and the claim stands as reported and denied.

Gray Mirror and a private loss, 2020–2022

In 2020 Yarvin returned to political writing, this time under his own name, on a paid Substack newsletter called Gray Mirror. [cite:de5_wiki_yarvin] Its own page describes it as "A portal to the next regime," says it has "tens of thousands of subscribers," and presents it as the working draft of a book, Gray Mirror of the Nihilist Prince, offering an "ex nihilo vision of governance." [cite:de5_gm_about] The money behind it is its readers' subscriptions; no institutional backer is documented. His books are published by Passage Publishing, which sells Gray Mirror: Fascicle I, Disturbance (2022) and two volumes of Unqualified Reservations. [cite:de5_passage]

In April 2021 his wife, Jennifer Kollmer, died; he announced her death on his newsletter. [cite:de5_wiki_yarvin] The record of his private life is otherwise left aside here, beyond the published fact that he married again in 2024. [cite:de5_wiki_yarvin]

2021: the year his name entered politics

Three recorded conversations in 2021 carried his ideas into conservative politics. In May he recorded a conversation with the conservative writer Michael Anton for the Claremont Institute's podcast The American Mind, ostensibly about Anton's 2020 book The Stakes. [cite:de5_powerline] The Guardian later reported that Yarvin used it to discuss a president declaring a state of emergency in his inaugural address, while calling the idea of becoming "a Caesar" absurd; Anton's defenders replied that the newspaper's account did not match the recording. [cite:de5_brewminate] [cite:de5_powerline] The two accounts conflict, and both are given.

In September he appeared on Fox Nation's Tucker Carlson Today. [cite:de6_mm_2021] That same month J.D. Vance, then a candidate for the Senate in Ohio, named him on a podcast, Jack Murphy Live. "There's this guy, Curtis Yarvin," Vance said, "who's written about some of these things," and he went on: "I'm sympathetic to that project. But another option is that we should just seize the administrative state for our own purposes." [cite:de5_wbur_vance] [cite:de5_vox_2022] In the same appearance he said a future President Trump should "fire every single mid-level bureaucrat, every civil servant in the administrative state" and "replace them with our people." [cite:de5_nextgov_vance] The remark dates to 2021; it was widely recirculated in 2024, after Vance became Trump's running mate. Kofman reported that the two men had first met in 2015, through Thiel. [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] It is the one documented occasion on which Vance cited Yarvin by name in public, and [scene:de12_the_thiel_years|Part Twelve] places it in Vance's own career.

2025: the guest of honour who said he was outside

Trump's second inauguration brought Yarvin a burst of attention. On 18 January 2025 the New York Times published a long interview with him by David Marchese, in which he argued that presidents such as Washington, Lincoln and Franklin Roosevelt had been "basically national CEOs," and that "it's not even that democracy is bad, it's just that it's very weak." [cite:de6_nyt_transcript] The same week he attended the "Coronation Ball," an inaugural gala hosted by Passage Press, where Politico described him as "an informal guest of honor." [cite:de5_politico_ward] To the same reporter he described a lunch with Michael Anton, by then a senior State Department official, "at a coffee shop across the street from the State Department," and an encounter with the new Vice President at a party, where, he said, Vance greeted him as "Yarvin, you reactionary fascist." "I've interacted with Vance once since the election," he added. [cite:de5_politico_ward] Asked how he measured his influence, he answered: "how I measure my influence is that I try not to." [cite:de5_politico_ward] An adviser to Vance later told CNN the two had met "like once." [cite:de6_cnn_2025]

The rest of the year was a run of public appearances. On 5 May he debated the Harvard political theorist Danielle Allen at Harvard's Faculty Club, before about eighty people, in an event organised by his publisher and an undergraduate society and not sanctioned by the university; he called Harvard "an essentially oligarchic institution," and Allen framed the question as "regime change to unaccountable CEO monarchs." [cite:de5_crimson] In June The New Yorker published Kofman's profile. [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] In September, at the HowTheLightGetsIn festival in London, he told the interviewer Alastair Campbell that the rule of law was a "polite fiction" for the rule of men. [cite:de5_spectator_au] On 7 and 8 October he debated twice at Yale, against the law professor Jed Rubenfeld on a motion to "end the democratic experiment" and, for the Yale Federalist Society, against the economist Garett Jones on "for-profit governance." [cite:de5_ravitch_ydn] [cite:de5_ylsfed] In late November he was a guest of honour at the founding convention of Brazil's newly registered Partido Missão in São Paulo, where in a six-minute speech he argued against racial quotas; he held no role in the party. [cite:de5_icl] The same month the historian Rutger Bregman, in the BBC's Reith Lectures, called him a "neofascist," a critic's label and recorded as such. [cite:de5_wiki_yarvin] Coverage continued into 2026, among it a segment on German-language public television in January. [cite:de5_zdf]

A critic of the government he is said to inspire

The most striking feature of Yarvin's record in 2025 is how often he criticised the administration he is said to have shaped. CNN reported in May his view that it was "barely scratching the surface" of the change needed, and that his influence ran "more through the kids in the administration." [cite:de6_cnn_2025] Kofman reported him complaining that the Department of Government Efficiency had too much libertarian DNA, and dismissing his own influence as worthless until the change he wanted actually came. [cite:de5_kofman_nuso] In an essay earlier in the year he had written that the administration would "wither and eventually dissipate" unless it grew more extreme, and in a Gray Mirror post in early October 2025 he wrote that he had to "start thinking realistically about how to flee the country" if the Democrats returned, later editing the line to a "plan B for 2029." [cite:de5_sfstandard] He told the Berliner Zeitung the same month that he wished Trump were far more energetic. [cite:de5_bz] [scene:de6_claims_vs_record|Part Six] sets these statements against his other claims about his influence.

The worst-case reading, tested

This part's material bears on the hypothesis in one place: the claim, often made, that the theorist of a sovereign executive without a veto is connected to the people who hold executive power. Graded against the record, it divides as follows.

Documented: a Thiel fund and Andreessen Horowitz invested in Yarvin's company; Vance, before he held office, named Yarvin on a podcast and in the same breath called for firing the civil service; and Yarvin had social access to people in the new administration in 2025, a gala, a lunch with Anton and a greeting from the Vice President, on his own account. [cite:de5_wiki_urbit] [cite:de5_wbur_vance] [cite:de5_politico_ward] Probable, but not proven: that some younger staff in the administration had read him. He says so, and an anonymous adviser to the Department of Government Efficiency told the Washington Post that everyone designing policy had read him; one unnamed source and the subject's own claim are not a document. [cite:de5_politico_ward] [cite:de5_kofman_nuso]

Not supported: that Yarvin directs, advises or programs the administration. He holds no office and no contract; no political donations by him have been reported; no meeting with the President is on record; and a Vance adviser says the two met about once. [cite:de6_cnn_2025] On this point the record runs against the worst-case reading rather than merely failing to support it. The theorist's own statements in 2025 are those of an outsider dissatisfied with a government that has not done what he wants, and in October 2025 he was planning for the possibility that elections would remove it. [cite:de5_sfstandard] A destination that its best-known advocate fears is slipping away has not been reached; whether the machinery for it was built anyway, by others and for their own reasons, is the question of Books Three and Five. The next part turns from the man to the program itself. [scene:de6_the_program|Part Six]
