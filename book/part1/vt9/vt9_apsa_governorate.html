APSA, the Governorate and the Holy See's Accounts

If the IOR is the bank, APSA is the owner. The Administration of the Patrimony of the Apostolic See holds the Holy See's own buildings, land, securities and gold, and pays a share of the income to the Roman Curia. The Governorate of Vatican City State runs the territory: museums, post, shops, police and services. Above both sits the Holy See's consolidated financial statement, which the Secretariat for the Economy has published, with gaps, since the Francis reforms. This section takes each in turn and gives only figures the bodies themselves have published, each with its year. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa_mandate] [cite:vt9_hs_fs2024]

From the 1929 settlement to APSA

APSA's oldest component came from the Lateran settlement. In June 1929 Pius XI created a Special Administration to manage the money Italy paid the Holy See under the Financial Convention annexed to the Treaty of 11 February 1929. That money was 750 million lire in cash and one billion lire in state bonds, and [scene:vt3_what_each_got|Part Three] sets out the terms. On 15 August 1967 Paul VI's apostolic constitution Regimini Ecclesiae Universae created APSA to administer the Holy See's property and the goods other Holy See bodies entrusted to it, with the purpose of funding the Curia. The Special Administration became its Extraordinary Section. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa_mandate] Under Francis's constitution Praedicate Evangelium (2022), APSA is "the body entrusted with the administration and management of the movable and immovable patrimony of the Holy See". It also keeps the Curia's accounts and runs its purchasing, and it carries out its financial operations through the IOR. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa_mandate]

The Secretariat of State's money moves to APSA

Until 2020 the Secretariat of State, the pope's chief office, managed a large investment portfolio of its own, including the London building at 60 Sloane Avenue that led to the trial covered in [scene:vt10_london_trial|Part Ten]. Francis ended this by motu proprio (a decree issued on the pope's own initiative), Una migliore organizzazione, of 26 December 2020. From 1 January 2021, ownership of the Secretariat's funds, bank accounts, investments in property and securities, and company shareholdings passed to APSA. Cash balances, whether at the IOR or in foreign banks, had to be transferred by 4 February 2021. Where a change of owner was impossible, APSA's president received a general power of attorney. Peter's Pence became a sub-account of the "Papal Funds" that APSA administers, and the Secretariat for the Economy was given oversight of the whole. [cite:vt9_mp_2020] The decree gave its reason: the Secretariat of State should not perform economic functions already assigned to other offices. [cite:vt9_mp_2020] The change is often dated to 2022. That was the year Praedicate Evangelium wrote APSA's role into the Curia's constitution and the August rescript sent the Holy See's liquid funds to the IOR, as the previous section explains. The transfer of ownership itself took effect on 1 January 2021, and APSA's 2025 accounts still date it from the 2021 financial year. [cite:vt9_apsa_bil2025] [cite:vt9_ewtn_rescript2022]

What APSA owns and earns

APSA published its first budget in July 2021. It listed about 4,051 properties in Italy and about 1,120 abroad, not counting embassies. About 14 per cent of the Italian properties were let at market rents; most of the rest were let at reduced rents, many of them to Church employees. [cite:vt9_globe_apsa2020] The 2023 report put the units let at market rent at 19.2 per cent, at subsidised rent 10.4 per cent and at zero rent 70.4 per cent. Holdings outside Italy, in London, Paris, Geneva and Lausanne, are owned through companies. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2023] The 2024 report named the foreign companies as British Grolux Investments in England, Sopridex in France and Profima in Switzerland. It counted 4,234 units in Italy, 2,866 owned by APSA (1,367 residential and 395 commercial among them) and 1,368 belonging to other Holy See bodies and managed by APSA. It also recorded that APSA paid Italy 6 million euros in municipal property tax (IMU) and 3.19 million in corporate income tax (IRES) that year. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2024]

APSA's results have moved a good deal from year to year. In 2021 it reported a surplus of 8.11 million euros and gave the Curia 30 million. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2021] In 2022 it broke even after contributing 32.27 million euros. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2022] In 2023 it made 45.9 million euros and contributed 37.9 million. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2023] In 2024 it made 62.2 million euros, its president, Archbishop Giordano Piccinotti, called it one of the best results of recent years, and it contributed 46.1 million: a fixed 30 million plus half of the remaining profit. That year the Curia's financial needs were 170.4 million euros. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2024] In 2025 the operating result fell to 22.8 million euros, all of it, 22.7 million, paid to the Holy See. Piccinotti described 2024 as extraordinary and 2025 as "a return to ordinary operating conditions". He explained that about 16 million euros of 2025 investment gains had been booked directly to net worth under a new accounting rule, the Vatican Financial Management Policy, instead of passing through the income statement. [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2025] [cite:vt9_apsa_bil2025]

APSA's 2025 balance sheet is the most complete picture of what the Holy See itself owns. Net equity was 2,686 million euros, up 89 million in the year, mostly because gold, property and securities were revalued. Total assets were 3,337.5 million euros, of which investment property was valued at 2,211 million. APSA managed 4,281 units in Italy, 2,945 of its own and 1,336 belonging to other Holy See bodies, and about 1,200 more abroad through its companies. Physical gold made up 29 per cent of its own portfolio. Of the roughly 1.95 billion euros in securities and cash it managed, 65.5 per cent belonged to third parties and 22.8 per cent to APSA itself. [cite:vt9_apsa_bil2025] [cite:vt9_vn_apsa2025] Two features of the property figures matter for anyone using them. First, they are book values set by APSA's own accounting rules, not market appraisals. Second, most of the units earn little or nothing, because they house Holy See offices and staff.

The Governorate and the museums

The Governorate's main source of income is culture, above all the Vatican Museums. The last separate figure the Secretariat for the Economy released for it was for 2015: a surplus of 59.9 million euros, which it attributed mainly to cultural revenue, above all from the Museums. [cite:vt9_hs_2015] This volume found no published Governorate statement for later years. The Holy See's 2024 statements show the Governorate as a contributor of 20 million euros to the Holy See (15 million in 2023). [cite:vt9_hs_fs2024] The Museums publish their prices. Since 1 January 2024 a full ticket has cost 20 euros and a reduced ticket 8 euros, plus an optional 5 euro booking fee. [cite:vt9_mv_tariffe] Quotidiano Nazionale reported about 6.9 million visitors in 2025, the Jubilee year. [cite:vt9_qn_museums2025] Revenue figures for the Museums in circulation, such as "135 million euros in 2019" or "at least 120 million dollars a year", come from outside estimates, not accounts; Catholic World Report's 2020 figure of "at least" 120 million dollars a year from ordinary tickets was its own reporter's estimate. [cite:vt9_cwr_museums2020] Without published accounts, this volume reports none of them as fact.

The Holy See's consolidated statement and the pension fund

The consolidated statement for 2024, published on 26 November 2025, is the broadest official account. Including the hospitals the Holy See owns, operating income was 1,230.7 million euros and operating expenses 1,275.2 million: an operating deficit of 44.4 million euros, down from 83.5 million. Investment income, one-off asset sales and higher donations turned this into a surplus of 1.6 million, against a deficit of 51.2 million in 2023. Excluding the hospitals, revenue was 546.5 million euros and expenses 527.8 million. Total assets were 6,326.7 million euros and net equity 4,277.0 million. [cite:vt9_hs_fs2024] [cite:vt9_america_hs2024] Of 393.3 million euros spent on the "apostolic mission", 37 per cent went to local churches in difficulty, 14 per cent to worship and evangelisation, 12 per cent to communications and 10 per cent each to the nunciatures and to charity. [cite:vt9_vn_hs2024] The Prefect for the Economy, Maximino Caballero Ledo, said that "an operating deficit of 44.4 million euros remains". [cite:vt9_america_hs2024]

The statement does not include a separate account of the Vatican pension fund. On 21 November 2024 Francis wrote that an analysis showed "a serious prospective imbalance in the fund" and appointed Cardinal Kevin Farrell as its special administrator. The Association of Lay Vatican Employees asked for the fund's balance sheet to be published. [cite:vt9_ap_pension] The 2024 consolidated statements record actuarial losses on employee benefits of 22.5 million euros (34.4 million in 2023) but give no figure for the fund's shortfall. [cite:vt9_hs_fs2024] As of October 2026 this volume found no published balance sheet for the fund.
