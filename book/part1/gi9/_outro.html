Part Nine established that by October 2026 Israeli forces held three belts of land beyond the lines of October 2023. In south Lebanon they held a zone behind the Forward Defense Line, five to ten kilometres deep on the army's own April map and about 6 per cent of the country by NPR's estimate, which Netanyahu said Israel would keep until Hezbollah is disarmed and from which the UN force that has watched the border since 1978 will begin to leave after 31 December 2026. [cite:gi9_alarabiya_map] [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] [cite:hz8b_national_tampa] [cite:gi9_un_2790] In Syria they held the UN buffer zone and the Hermon summit "for an indefinite period," demanded a demilitarised south and resumed the Sufa 53 fortifications, which Syria, the UAE, Qatar, Jordan and Kuwait condemned as breaches of the 1974 agreement. [cite:hz10s_ih_hermon] [cite:gi9_fdd_demil] [cite:gi9_an_sufa] [cite:gi9_uae_sufa] In Gaza the share behind the Yellow Line rose from about 53 per cent at the ceasefire to between 64 and 70 per cent by the sources' various measures, with Netanyahu ordering the army toward 70 and the chief of staff calling the line "a new border line." [cite:gi9_aj_maps] [cite:gi9_reu_orange] [cite:gi9_olj_net70] [cite:gi9_unsco_jun26] [cite:gi9_olj_zamir] Each zone was explained as security against a named armed enemy; none had been claimed as sovereign territory by any government decision or law; and the neighbours and the UN treated all three as occupation. [cite:gi9_nahar_saar] [cite:gi9_pbs_plan] [cite:hz10s_un_guterres] Whether the belt is a security perimeter or the first stage of a frontier is the argument of the moment, and the record will answer it only after the election of 27 October 2026.
