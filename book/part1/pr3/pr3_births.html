Giving Birth in Gaza

Babies kept being born. In October 2025 UNFPA, the UN's reproductive health agency, reported about 130 births a day in Gaza, more than a quarter of them by Caesarean section, with only 15 facilities still able to provide obstetric and newborn care and an estimated 55,000 pregnant women cut off from medical help. About one newborn in five was premature, underweight or born with complications. Nasser hospital's neonatal unit held 200 babies in a space meant for 40, sometimes three to an incubator. A midwife told the agency she had cut a baby's umbilical cord with a knife "heated over a fire." [cite:pr_unfpa_oct2025]

The losses. The health ministry recorded about 3,958 miscarriages in the first half of 2026, a rate more than three times that of the previous six months, and anaemia in 61 percent of pregnant women. [cite:pr_mm_women2026] [cite:pr_memo_starv2026] These are ministry figures, kept by a health system the war had largely destroyed.

The Fertility Clinic

In March 2025 the UN commission of inquiry reported that Israeli forces had "intentionally attacked and destroyed" Al-Basma, Gaza's main in-vitro fertilisation centre, in December 2023, destroying about 4,000 embryos, and that it had found no credible evidence the building was used for military purposes. It called the destruction a measure intended to prevent births, one of the acts that define genocide, and found that forced public stripping and other sexual violence had become part of Israeli forces' "standard operating procedures" toward Palestinians.
