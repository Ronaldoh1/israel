Who Decides for the Territories: The West Bank and Gaza

The previous sections described a parliamentary democracy and its quarrels. This one describes the part of Israel's government that rules people who cannot vote for it. Since 1967 Israel has held the West Bank, and it withdrew its settlers from Gaza in 2005 while keeping control of the Strip's borders. [cite:crs_disengagement_2005] The decisions that shape the lives of the Palestinians there are taken by Israeli bodies in which they have no representation, and the mechanism by which those decisions are taken is as much a part of Israel's architecture as the Knesset.

The map the Oslo agreements drew. The interim agreements of the 1990s divided the West Bank into three kinds of area. In Area A the Palestinian Authority holds civil and security control; in Area B it holds civil control while Israel holds security control; and in Area C, the largest, Israel holds both. [cite:wiki_oslo_areas] [cite:anera_areas_abc] The division was meant to be temporary, pending a final agreement that never came. [cite:wiki_oslo_accords]

How West Bank decisions are made. Settlement construction, expansions and changes of jurisdiction in the West Bank are approved by the cabinet and its ministerial and planning committees, not by votes of the full Knesset and not through negotiation with the Palestinians. [cite:westbank_newrepublic] Since the coalition agreements of December 2022, the minister holding the most authority over civilian matters there has been the Finance Minister and leader of the Religious Zionist Party, Bezalel Smotrich, who received control of the territory's civil administration as a coalition concession, separate from the Defence Ministry that handles security. [cite:smotrich_planning_authority] One minister, from a party with seven of the 120 seats, thereby gained authority over civilian planning in the territory.

On 8 February 2026 the security cabinet, on proposals from Smotrich and Defence Minister Israel Katz, approved the most far-reaching package of West Bank measures in years. It opened the West Bank land registry to make purchases easier; repealed the Jordanian-era law that had barred land sales to non-Arabs, together with the special permit for transactions; moved planning authority over the Jewish settlement in Hebron and the Cave of the Patriarchs, and control of Rachel's Tomb near Bethlehem, from Palestinian municipalities to Israeli bodies; and extended Israeli enforcement against Palestinian building into Area A, citing heritage and archaeological sites. [cite:dv5_ynet_wb_feb2026] [cite:dv5_wt_ap_wb_feb2026] Smotrich's office said, "we will continue to bury the idea of a Palestinian state." [cite:dv5_wt_ap_wb_feb2026] Israel's own security officials opposed the Hebron measures and warned of escalation; President Mahmoud Abbas called the decision "dangerous," Jordan condemned it as an attempt to impose "illegal Israeli sovereignty," and the Israeli group Peace Now said it opened the way to "a massive land grab," while the settler group Regavim praised it. [cite:dv5_ynet_wb_feb2026] [cite:dv5_wt_ap_wb_feb2026]

How Gaza decisions are made. Military operations in Gaza are authorized by the security cabinet: the prime minister sets the political objectives, the chief of staff translates them into plans, and the cabinet approves. [cite:security_cabinet_oct7] As the previous section showed, after June 2024 that cabinet included the coalition's far-right ministers, and in August 2025 it approved a plan to take Gaza City over the army leadership's reported objections. [cite:warcabinet_gantz] [cite:security_cabinet_newsweek] Ben-Gvir resigned in January 2025 over a ceasefire and returned in March when the fighting resumed. [cite:bgv_aljaz_return] Inside the system, a party with six or seven seats could make a ceasefire cost the prime minister his majority.

How the world's court described the result. On 19 July 2024 the International Court of Justice, answering a request from the UN General Assembly, gave its opinion on the occupation as a whole. It found that Israel's continued presence in the occupied Palestinian territory is unlawful; that Israel must end it as rapidly as possible, cease all new settlement activity and evacuate all settlers; that it must make reparation; and that other states must not recognize the situation as lawful or help to maintain it. Several of these findings were adopted by fourteen votes to one. [cite:icj_press_19jul24] [cite:un_icj_ao_record] The court noted that the law of occupation sets no time limit; what made the presence unlawful, in its view, were policies amounting to annexation and the denial of the Palestinian people's right to self-determination, and it found a breach of the prohibition on racial segregation and apartheid. [cite:transcend_icj_ao] [cite:11kbw_icj_ao] An advisory opinion binds no one by itself. The February 2026 measures came nineteen months after it.

What the architecture shows. For the millions of people in the territories, Israel's state is not a parliamentary democracy but an executive: ministers and committees chosen by a coalition they cannot vote in or out, applying decisions through military orders. That is not a secret or a theory; it is the documented decision chain, and its present design dates from the coalition agreements of 2022. Whether the government Gadi Eisenkot is trying to form would change it is one of the questions the 2026 election left open. [cite:dv5_israeled_watch]
