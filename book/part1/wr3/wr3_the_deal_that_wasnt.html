The Deal That Wasn't, 2002–2026

In February 2002 Crown Prince Abdullah proposed, in an interview with the New York Times, full Israeli withdrawal from the occupied territories, including Jerusalem, in return for full normalisation. When the Arab League adopted it at Beirut in March, the terms had hardened: withdrawal to the 1967 lines including the Golan, a Palestinian state with East Jerusalem as its capital, and a "just solution" for refugees under Resolution 194, in exchange for "normal relations" with all 22 members. [cite:arab_peace_initiative] Israel did not accept it.

Through the 2010s, shared fear of Iran's nuclear programme, missiles and allied militias produced quiet security contacts. When the Emirates and Bahrain signed the Abraham Accords in 2020, Saudi Arabia did not, but it opened its airspace to more Israeli flights. [cite:w_abraham] [cite:wr_wiki_saudi_israel]

September and October 2023

On 20 September 2023 Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman told Fox News, "every day, we get closer", while stressing the Palestinian question. [cite:mbs_closer_quote] According to later reporting in the Times of Israel, Washington and Riyadh had drafted that summer a document setting out what Israel would have to concede to the Palestinians. [cite:saudi_us_document_2023] Two weeks after 7 October the talks were paused. On 20 October President Biden told donors that "one of the reasons" Hamas attacked was that it knew he was "about to sit down with the Saudis". [cite:aljazeera_biden_hamas_saudi_thaw] [cite:bloomberg_biden_hamas_saudi_normalization] That was his own reading; Hamas's internal reasons are not on the record.

The same deal carried a trade route. The India–Middle East–Europe corridor, announced at the G20 in New Delhi on 9 September 2023, was to run by sea from India to the Emirates, by rail across Saudi Arabia and Jordan to Haifa, and by sea to Europe, bypassing the Suez Canal. It depended on Saudi–Israeli normalisation and as of 2026 remained a plan rather than a route. [cite:mei_imec_backgrounder] [cite:orf_imec_report]

After the war began

Saudi Arabia then made a credible path to a Palestinian state on the 1967 lines a precondition, not a gesture. [cite:saudi_hardened_position] By 2026 an Israeli security institute judged normalisation "effectively off the table": in an August 2025 survey 99 percent of Saudi respondents saw relations with Israel as negative, the crown prince had accused Israel of genocide, and Riyadh had co-led a Franco-Saudi push to recognise a Palestinian state. [cite:wr_inss_saudi_2026] In July 2026 the United States signed a civil nuclear agreement with Saudi Arabia that does not bar enrichment; President Trump then said it was "totally subject" to Saudi Arabia joining the Abraham Accords. [cite:wr_npr_nuclear_2026] Status as of 26 November 2026: there is no normalisation, the agreement as sent to Congress does not require it, and the nominee for ambassador to Riyadh called it "part of the negotiation process".
