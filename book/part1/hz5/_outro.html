Part Five established that Hezbollah began the 2006 war with a raid that killed eight Israeli soldiers and captured two for a prisoner exchange, and that Israel answered with a campaign against Lebanon's infrastructure as well as Hezbollah, on the stated ground that the Lebanese state was responsible. [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Human Rights Watch identified at least 1,109 Lebanese killed by Israeli attacks, the vast majority civilians, and found Israel's failure to distinguish civilians from fighters the main cause; Hezbollah's roughly 4,000 rockets killed 44 Israeli civilians, and 121 Israeli soldiers died. [cite:hz5_rw_why] [cite:hz5_hrw_why9] [cite:hz5_wiki_war] Israel fired up to four million cluster submunitions by the UN's estimate, and as many as 4.6 million on information from its own soldiers, about 90 percent of the strikes in the final 72 hours; its own commission found that use in breach of international law on protecting civilians. [cite:hz5b_hrw_flooding] [cite:hz5a_irin_winograd] Resolution 1701 required Hezbollah to stay out of the south and Israel to stop offensive operations and withdraw; by 2024 neither the disarmament nor the end of violations had happened. [cite:hz5_un_1701] [cite:hz5_wiki_1701] Israel's Winograd Commission called the war "a serious missed opportunity," and the commander of Israel's northern front promised, in 2008, that what happened to the Dahieh would happen "in every village from which Israel is fired on." [cite:hz5_jvl_winograd] [cite:hz5_jps_khalidi] Part Six turns to Hezbollah itself in the years after 2006: a party in government, an army in Syria and a financial network.
