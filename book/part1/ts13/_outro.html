Part Thirteen established that the name Israel first appears in the record as the name of a people, on Merneptah's stele of about 1208 BCE, and in Genesis as the name given to one man after a night of wrestling; that Hebrew philology reads it as a name about God striving, persisting or ruling, which Genesis explains as Jacob's striving with God. [cite:ts13_wiki_merneptah] [cite:ts13_esv_gen32] [cite:ts13_wiki_israelname] It showed that reading the name as a condition of the soul is old and documented: Philo's "man who sees God", Origen's Israel "according to the Spirit", the kabbalistic Jacob who stands at Tiferet between kindness and restraint, and Jonathan Sacks's Jacob who stops trying to be his brother. [cite:ts13_philo_mut] [cite:ts13_origen_dp] [cite:ts13_chabad_luria] [cite:ts13_sacks_be] The IS-RA-EL reading, by contrast, is modern, of unknown first author, documented no earlier than Heiser's 2008 critique of Jordan Maxwell, and unsupported by the Hebrew; this platform offers it as a labelled symbolic key. [cite:ts13_heiser_maxwell] The ancient mysteries and the ascent literature are documented in dated texts, while the claimed Egyptian lineage of modern lodges was built between the Rosicrucian manifestos of 1614 and AMORC's founding in 1915, much of it from a 1731 novel. [cite:ts13_wiki_rosicruc] [cite:ts13_wiki_terrasson] [cite:ts13_wiki_amorc] The part closed with an argument, labelled as one, that the institutional literalism documented in this volume read one level of the name as the whole of it, with the objections stated beside it.
