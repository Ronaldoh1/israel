Abortion, IVF and Birth Rates

On reproduction the usual picture of the two countries runs against expectation. Israel's law still requires a committee to approve each abortion, yet in practice the committees approve nearly every request and the state pays for most of them. The United States had a constitutional right to abortion from 1973 until the Supreme Court, by six votes to three, overruled Roe v. Wade in Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization on 24 June 2022, and access now depends on the state. [cite:us18a_scotus_dobbs] [cite:us18_kff_abortioncov] Israel's health basket also pays for IVF on terms its own researchers describe as practically unlimited, and its fertility rate, 2.87 children per woman in 2024, was the highest in the OECD; the American rate fell to a record low in 2024. This section sets out each in turn; Part Eight treats gender equality more broadly. [cite:us18_toi_abortion2022] [cite:us18_ijhpr_ivf] [cite:us18a_oecd_sf21] [cite:us18_ap_tfr2024] [scene:us8_gender|Part Eight: gender and disability rights]

Israel: committees that almost always say yes

Israel's abortion law dates from 1977 and sits in the Penal Code. An abortion is lawful only with the approval of a termination committee of two physicians and a social worker, at least one of them a woman; there are 38 such committees in public and private hospitals. A committee may approve on fixed grounds: the woman is under 18 (later widened to under 20) or over 40; the pregnancy resulted from rape, incest or a relationship outside marriage; the fetus has a physical or mental defect; or the pregnancy endangers the woman's life or her physical or mental health. [cite:us18_wiki_abortion_il] There is no ground of simple choice, but the grounds are read widely; the encyclopaedia summary used here puts general approval at 98 per cent. [cite:us18_wiki_abortion_il] In 2020 the Central Bureau of Statistics recorded 16,492 requests to the committees, of which 99.6 per cent were approved; Health Ministry figures reported by Ynet put 2021 requests at 17,548. [cite:us18_toi_abortion2022] About 19,500 legal abortions were performed in 2003, and the rate per 1,000 women of childbearing age had fallen to 9 by 2016, below the American rate of 13.2 in the same comparison. [cite:us18_wiki_abortion_il]

The state pays for most of them. Before 2014 public funding covered approved abortions in certain cases for women aged 20 to 33; a change that year brought elective abortions into the state-funded health basket, though sources disagree on whether an upper age limit remained. Women who become pregnant during military service receive abortions free. [cite:us18_wiki_abortion_il] On 27 June 2022, three days after Dobbs, Health Minister Nitzan Horowitz announced new rules approved by a Knesset committee: applications could be made online without appearing before the committee, questions about contraception were removed from the form, the meeting with a social worker became optional, and drug abortions could be given at health-fund clinics rather than only in hospitals. "The rights to a woman's body are those of the woman alone," he said. Abolishing the committees would need a law, which the Times of Israel judged unlikely to pass. [cite:us18_toi_abortion2022]

The committee system has a documented gap of its own. A 2012 report found that about half of abortions took place in private clinics without committee approval; doctors who perform them risk a fine or up to five years in prison, but no prosecutions are known. [cite:us18_wiki_abortion_il]

The United States after Dobbs

Since 1977, under the Hyde Amendment as KFF summarises it, Congress has barred federal funds for abortion except where the woman's life is endangered or the pregnancy results from rape or incest, so Medicaid pays for abortion only where a state uses its own money; 13 states required such coverage as of mid-2026. [cite:us18_kff_abortioncov] After Dobbs returned the question to the states, 13 states had total bans in force as of 6 January 2026: Alabama, Arkansas, Idaho, Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas and West Virginia. [cite:us18_kff_abortioncov]

The bans changed where and how abortions happen more than how many. The Society of Family Planning's #WeCount study counted about 1.13 million abortions in the formal health system in 2025. Telehealth provision, mostly pills sent by mail, rose from 5 per cent of abortions in April 2022 to 25 per cent in December 2024 and 29 per cent in December 2025; in the total-ban states it made up 97 to 100 per cent of abortions in 2025. The study credits telehealth and providers operating under shield laws with keeping access open. [cite:us18_sfp_wecount]

Fertility treatment

Israel's national health basket pays for practically unlimited in-vitro fertilisation (IVF) for patients under 45 with no more than two children, single mothers included. The authors of a 2016 study of the Maccabi health fund's IVF registry state, citing earlier research, that Israel performs more IVF cycles relative to its population than any other country; they argue that many of these cycles have a low chance of success. [cite:us18_ijhpr_ivf] The same study cites 163,212 IVF cycles in the United States in 2013. [cite:us18_ijhpr_ivf] There is no federal entitlement to fertility treatment in the United States; cover depends on employer insurance and on state mandates. On 18 February 2025 President Trump signed an executive order asking for policy recommendations within 90 days on protecting access to IVF and lowering its cost, and AP reported in July 2025 that his administration was backing the idea of "baby bonuses" to encourage births. [cite:us18a_wh_ivf] [cite:us18_ap_tfr2024] What the order has changed in practice is not measured in any series this book found.

Birth rates over time

The two countries' fertility rates have moved apart. Israel's total fertility rate, the number of children a woman would have at current rates, averaged 2.93 in 1990–99, 2.92 in 2000–04, 3.04 in 2010–14 and 3.08 in 2015–19, and stood at 2.89 in 2022, by Central Bureau of Statistics data. [cite:us18_jvl_tfr] Within that steady total, the rates of different groups have converged: Muslim women's rate fell from 4.67 in the 1990s to 2.75 in 2024, while Jewish women's rose to 3.06. [cite:us18_jvl_tfr] The American rate was about 2.1 in 2007, then fell to 1.621 in 2023 and 1.599 in 2024, the lowest recorded. [cite:us18_ap_tfr2024] Provisional data for 2025 show 3,606,400 births and a general fertility rate of 53.1 per 1,000 women aged 15 to 44, down 23 per cent since 2007. [cite:us18_cdc_births2025]

None of this shows that policy alone sets birth rates. Israel's child allowances were cut in 2003 and 2013 without a lasting fall in the total rate, and its IVF and allowance policies sit alongside religious and family norms that the data do not separate. [cite:us18_nii_children2018] [cite:us18_jvl_tfr] What the record does show is a difference in what each state pays for: Israel funds fertility treatment, most abortions and a universal child allowance; the federal government in the United States funds none of the three as an entitlement, and since 2022 the law on abortion has depended on where a woman lives. [cite:us18_ijhpr_ivf] [cite:us18_kff_abortioncov]
