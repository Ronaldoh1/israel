Part One found the strip's origins in the record. Gaza was a town of about 34,000 and the centre of a district of 137,000 under the British Mandate, overwhelmingly Arab, with a Jewish community that had ended in 1929 and one Jewish outpost founded in 1946. [cite:gz_wiki_subdistrict] [cite:gz_nli_jews] [cite:gz_wiki_kfardarom] The war of 1948 left Israel with most of the district and Egypt with a remnant of about 361 to 365 square kilometres, enclosed by an armistice line that both sides said was not a border. [cite:gz_palquest_refugees] [cite:gz_golan_2024] [cite:gz_wiki_history] Into it came more than 200,000 refugees, joining about 80,000 residents, and the international relief created to keep them from starvation became permanent when the return promised in Resolution 194 did not happen. [cite:gz_palquest_refugees] [cite:gz_un_res302] [cite:rs_rd6_refworld_194]

What is settled is how the strip was made and who filled it. What is contested is the size of the 1948 exodus as a whole, and the meaning of the right of return. What the record leaves open, from 1949 on, is the strip's status: governed by Egypt, annexed by no state, and home to people who were citizens of no state. Part Two follows the nineteen years of Egyptian rule and the occupation that began in 1967.
