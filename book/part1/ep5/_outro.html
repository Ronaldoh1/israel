Read side by side, the entries show the same pattern from different directions. Every one of these people had built their authority long before Epstein entered the record: a fortune, a party, an army, a presidency. The consequences were not the same, and the difference does not follow the strength of the record. The British minister was arrested under a law on misconduct in public office that covers what he is accused of doing. The American presidents, the investor and the former Israeli prime minister have not been charged or named as suspects over Epstein, because nothing in the record so far describes a crime by them; the Clintons were questioned by Congress as witnesses. One of them now heads the government that holds the files. That difference is the subject of Part Eight.
