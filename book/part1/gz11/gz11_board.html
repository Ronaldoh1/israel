From Vision to Structure

Project Sunrise. In December 2025 the Wall Street Journal reported a plan drafted in about 45 days by a team led by Kushner and Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff, called Project Sunrise. It would cost more than $112 billion over a decade and turn the strip into a coastal hub of luxury beach resorts, high-speed rail and AI-powered infrastructure. The United States would anchor it with nearly $60 billion in grants and loan guarantees, and every phase was contingent on Hamas disarming fully and dismantling its tunnels. [cite:gz_tnnd_sunrise]

The Board of Peace. Trump announced the board on 15 January 2026, and its charter was signed at the World Economic Forum in Davos on 22 January. The UN Security Council had welcomed it in November 2025. Trump is its chairman for life, with power to choose his successor and to invite its members; a permanent seat costs a payment of $1 billion. On 19 February the United States pledged $10 billion and nine other members $7 billion. Britain, France, Norway, Sweden and Slovenia declined to join. Critics including the EU's foreign policy chief noted that its charter gave Palestinians no say. [cite:gz_wiki_bop]

The master plan. At Davos, after the signing, Kushner presented a "New Gaza." "In the beginning, we were toying with a free zone, and then a Hamas zone," he said. "And then we said, you know what? Let's just plan for catastrophic success." [cite:gz_almon_newgaza] The plan gives the entire Mediterranean coastline to "coastal tourism," with 180 mixed-use towers. A "New Rafah" would hold more than 100,000 housing units. Industrial zones, the plan says, would create more than half a million jobs, and an airport, rail hub and port would be built in the south. The northern towns of Beit Lahia and Jabalia would become farmland, data centres and advanced manufacturing. People would live in four housing districts, separated by parks and industrial zones and linked by a few roads, which together cover less ground than housing did before the war. Rebuilding would begin only where Hamas had been disarmed, or where areas had been emptied and were under Israeli military control. "We've already started removing the rubble and doing some of the demolition," Kushner said. [cite:gz_gpb_newgaza] On the day he presented it, Israeli fire killed five Palestinians in Gaza. [cite:gz_almon_newgaza]

What the plans leave out. Reporting on the master plan found no provision for the deeds to the land, for how new homes would be allocated to the people of Gaza, for where families in buildings marked for demolition would live, or evidence that Palestinians had been consulted; the slides shown at Davos did not address property rights or compensation. [cite:gz_gpb_newgaza] [cite:gz_almon_newgaza]

Where it stands, October 2026. A year after the ceasefire, Human Rights Watch reported that reconstruction had not begun. The Board of Peace presented a six-month recovery programme on 23 September 2026, to start "the moment funding and access allow." Ninety-four percent of Gaza's people lacked adequate shelter. The area behind the Yellow Line covered about 64 percent of the strip, a line Israeli officials had begun to call a "new border." [cite:gz_hrw_anniv] In its report to the Security Council in May 2026 the board blamed the lack of progress on Hamas's refusal to disarm. [cite:gz_wiki_bop]

The record does not show which of these visions will be carried out, and some have been superseded. What it shows is who has been drawing up Gaza's future, the terms in which they have described it, and that the people who live there, more than two million of them, have had no seat at the table where it is being decided.
