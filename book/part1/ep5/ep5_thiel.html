Peter Thiel

Born 1967, Frankfurt. At the time of contact: co-founder and chairman of Palantir, founder of Founders Fund, co-founder of Valar Ventures. Today: the same, and one of the most influential donors in Republican politics.

Before Epstein

Peter Thiel was born in Frankfurt on 11 October 1967 and moved to the United States as an infant; part of his childhood was spent in southern Africa before his family settled in California. At Stanford he studied philosophy, co-founded the conservative student paper The Stanford Review with David Sacks, and took a law degree in 1992. He clerked for a federal appeals judge, worked briefly as a securities lawyer and a derivatives trader, and wrote speeches for the Education Secretary William Bennett before founding his own investment firm in 1996. [cite:de9_wiki_thiel]

He co-founded the company that became PayPal, sold to eBay in 2002. In 2003 he co-founded Palantir, a data-analysis company whose early backers included the CIA's venture-capital arm, In-Q-Tel; the investment was disclosed in Palantir's 2020 filing to go public. In 2004 he became Facebook's first outside investor, and in 2005 he founded Founders Fund. Palantir's own filings show that its special class of voting shares is held in a trust controlled jointly by Thiel, the chief executive Alex Karp and the co-founder Stephen Cohen. [cite:de9_wiki_thiel] [cite:de27_wiki_pltr]

His politics are on the record in his own words. In a 2009 essay for the Cato Institute, "The Education of a Libertarian", he wrote that he no longer believed that freedom and democracy were compatible. He became a major political donor: a speaker at the 2016 Republican convention and a contributor to Donald Trump's campaign, and in 2022 the largest backer of J. D. Vance's Senate campaign, through a super PAC to which he gave about $15 million. Vance had worked for him. [cite:de10_cato] [cite:de9_fortune_rnc] [cite:de12_cbs_klaidman]

The record with Epstein

The emails released in 2026 show Thiel and Epstein corresponding from early 2014 and arranging to meet several times a year. On 12 June 2014 Epstein offered to help with Thiel's taxes, writing that his "personal tax regime can use some tweaking". Thiel called one of Epstein's proposals "awesome" and asked to meet about it, and wrote: "I *never* question your judgment on tax-related issues." In an exchange about his retirement account, Thiel wrote that it was "somewhere over 2 [billion]". [cite:ep4_stanforddaily_thiel2026] When Epstein asked whether his bad press gave Thiel pause, Thiel replied that if he were intimidated by bad press he "would not have gotten anywhere in life". When Epstein invited him to "the island", Thiel replied: "When are you there?" [cite:ep4_stanforddaily_thiel2026] In August 2015 both men attended a dinner in Palo Alto hosted by Reid Hoffman, with Mark Zuckerberg, Elon Musk and others; Epstein called it "wild". [cite:ep4_stanforddaily_thiel2026] Epstein described Thiel to associates as a "great friend", and the correspondence was still going in early 2019. [cite:ep4_stanforddaily_thiel2026]

The money is the core of the record. Epstein committed about $40 million to Valar Ventures, a fund Thiel co-founded; a confidential estate analysis reviewed by the New York Times found the stake had grown to about $170 million, the largest single asset left in his estate. A Valar spokesman said the firm met Epstein in 2014 because he was then considered "a well-known adviser to world leaders, top universities and philanthropic organizations". [cite:nyt_epstein_thiel_valar_boston] In 2018 Thiel's separate Founders Fund led a $15 million funding round for Carbyne, the Israeli emergency-call company chaired by Ehud Barak that Epstein had pitched years earlier. [cite:bylinetimes_thiel_epstein_valar]

All of it came after Epstein's 2008 conviction. Valar took his money six to eight years after he registered as a sex offender.

What he said, and what the files show

Thiel has given no sworn testimony about Epstein. His spokesman has said Epstein was a limited partner in Valar, not a co-owner, and that Thiel "never went to Epstein's Island". [cite:ep5_bylinetimes_valar2026] [cite:ep4_stanforddaily_thiel2026] The files are consistent with both statements: no ownership document has surfaced, and no flight log or other record places Thiel on the island. The invitation exists, and Thiel answered it with a question rather than a refusal. Palantir has said it "was not aware that Epstein ever invested in or was a shareholder of Palantir", and the files are consistent with that too. [cite:startmag_epstein_palantir_thiel]

No survivor has accused Peter Thiel of anything on the record. No investigation, lawsuit or loss of office is linked to the relationship.

What the news got wrong

Some headlines said Thiel and Epstein "co-owned" a fund. The source was a single 2016 email from Barak describing Valar as "owned by Peter Thiel and JE"; Thiel's spokesman denied it, and no ownership document has appeared. [cite:ep5_bylinetimes_valar2026] The record supports "limited partner". Other claims, that Epstein invested in Palantir or that Palantir served his operation, have no document behind them; every documented tie runs through a person, not the company. [cite:startmag_epstein_palantir_thiel]

What the coverage underplayed runs the other way. All of this happened after the conviction. The stake is the largest asset in the estate. Epstein's money and Thiel's reached the same Barak-chaired company by two routes. And the tax correspondence shows one of the richest men in America welcoming tax advice from a convicted sex offender about the retirement account that later became famous for growing tax-free into billions. Whether Thiel acted on any of that advice is not established.

What is not established

What Thiel personally knew about Epstein's conduct, and when; how often they met after 2014; whether Valar examined Epstein's record before taking his money; and who will ultimately receive the value of the stake.
