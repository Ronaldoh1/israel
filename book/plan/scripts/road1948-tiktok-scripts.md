## Road to 1948 · Part 1 of 5 · "Before the flag: the idea, the bank and the warning (1839–1905)"

**[0:00] HOOK**
ON SCREEN: "1899: 'In the name of God, let Palestine be left alone.'"
In 1899, the former mayor of Jerusalem wrote to the founder of political Zionism and asked him to leave Palestine alone. That was 49 years before the state of Israel. This is the road to 1948, in five parts, and every fact is sourced in the book.

**[0:20] THE IDEA CAME FIRST FROM CHRISTIANS**
ON SCREEN: "1839 · 1840 · 1843".
The first push to "restore" Jews to Palestine didn't come from Jewish leaders. It came from British Christians. In 1839 Lord Shaftesbury pressed the Foreign Secretary, Palmerston, to sponsor Jewish settlement there, and in 1840 Britain's new consulate in Jerusalem was told to protect Jews. The famous line "a land without a people for a people without a land" was first printed in 1843 by a Scottish clergyman. Zionists only started using it in 1901.

**[0:50] THE LAND BECAME SELLABLE**
ON SCREEN: an Ottoman land deed.
Then the law changed. The Ottoman Land Code of 1858 made deeds something you could buy and sell. Rich families in Beirut and Damascus gathered huge tracts, often land farmed by people who held no title at all. That matters later. Meanwhile, from 1882, the Ottoman government barred foreign Jews from settling in Palestine, though they could settle anywhere else in the empire.

**[1:15] MANY JEWS SAID NO**
ON SCREEN: "1885 · Pittsburgh Platform".
And it wasn't one Jewish opinion. In 1885 Reform Judaism's Pittsburgh Platform rejected a return to Palestine outright, and a long religious debate made many Orthodox rabbis oppose it too. Zionism was a movement inside Jewish life, not the same thing as Jewish life.

**[1:35] THE MACHINE**
ON SCREEN: "£1 a share".
In 1897 Theodor Herzl convened the first Zionist Congress in Basel. In 1899 the movement got a bank: the Jewish Colonial Trust, incorporated in London with two million pounds of capital, sold in one-pound shares so ordinary people could own a piece. But the first 100 founders' shares carried as many votes as all the others combined. The leadership kept control. That's the pattern you'll see the whole way: mass support at the bottom, a small, organized centre making the decisions, and money that could be raised, moved and counted.

**[2:00] THE WARNING AND THE REPLY**
ON SCREEN: two letters side by side.
That same March, Yusuf al-Khalidi, Jerusalem's former mayor, wrote that the idea itself was natural and just, but Palestine was inhabited and part of the Ottoman Empire, and he asked that it be left alone. Herzl replied that Jewish immigration would make the people there richer, and asked: who would think of sending them away? Each man's position in that exchange would be repeated for fifty years.

**[2:30] NOT ANYWHERE ELSE**
ON SCREEN: "1903: 295–178 · 1905: rejected".
In 1903 Britain offered land in East Africa. The Congress voted 295 to 178 only to survey it, and in 1905 rejected it. It had to be Palestine.

## Road to 1948 · Part 2 of 5 · "The Bible, the deeds and the promise (1909–1922)"

**[0:00] HOOK**
ON SCREEN: "22% of all land acquired by 1948 came from ONE family in Beirut."
Almost a quarter of all the land Jewish buyers acquired in Palestine before 1948 came from one Christian family in Beirut. Here's how the deeds, a Bible and a world war set up the Balfour Declaration.

**[0:15] THE BIBLE**
ON SCREEN: "1909 · Scofield Reference Bible".
In 1909 Oxford University Press published the Scofield Reference Bible. Its notes taught American Protestants to read a Jewish return to the land as prophecy. The preacher behind that reading, John Nelson Darby, had already toured North America at least five times between 1862 and 1877. That reading helped build a Christian political base for Zionism in America.

**[0:40] THE DEEDS**
ON SCREEN: the Jezreel Valley; "Sursock".
On the ground, land was bought from landlords who didn't live on it. In 1910 the Ottoman governor of Nazareth tried to block one sale at al-Fula; permission came through the buyer's friends in Constantinople. The biggest seller was the Sursock family of Beirut. In one group of their sales, 1,746 farming families, 8,730 people, were evicted. The law said they were owed nothing; they were paid about 17 dollars a person.

**[1:10] THE WAR**
ON SCREEN: "NILI · Zion Mule Corps".
Then came World War One. A Jewish spy ring called NILI worked for British intelligence inside Ottoman Palestine; it was broken in October 1917, one month before Balfour. In 1915 the British Army had already formed the Zion Mule Corps from Jews exiled to Egypt. The promise that came next was paid for, in part, in services.

**[1:35] THE PROMISE**
ON SCREEN: the letter: "148 Piccadilly".
On 2 November 1917, Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour wrote to Walter Rothschild at 148 Piccadilly, a zoologist who had left the family bank in 1908. Britain would favour a "national home" for the Jewish people. The 90 percent of the population who weren't Jewish were named only as "existing non-Jewish communities."

**[2:00] WHAT THE PEOPLE SAID**
ON SCREEN: "72% of 1,875 petitions opposed".
Britain had also made promises to Arab leaders. In January 1919 Emir Faisal's memo said that in Palestine the enormous majority of the people were Arabs. An American inquiry, the King–Crane Commission, collected 1,875 petitions; 72 percent opposed the Zionist plan. It found the population "emphatically against the entire Zionist program." Its report was set aside. And the people on the land weren't silent. In 1911 two Christian cousins in Jaffa founded a newspaper and named it *Filastin*, "Palestine", decades before anyone claims the identity was invented. Journalists like Najib Nassar were already warning landowners not to sell.

**[2:25] AMERICA SIGNS ON EARLY**
ON SCREEN: "21 Sept 1922".
And here's one most people don't know: on 21 September 1922, President Harding signed a resolution of Congress endorsing Balfour, 26 years before Truman recognized Israel.

## Road to 1948 · Part 3 of 5 · "Building under Britain: land, labour, 1933 and the revolt (1920–1941)"

**[0:00] HOOK**
ON SCREEN: "29.4% of village families held no land at all."
In 1930 Britain's own investigator went village to village and found almost a third of the families had no land at all. Britain measured the damage, and kept going. This is the Mandate years.

**[0:15] LAND AND LABOUR**
ON SCREEN: "Hope Simpson, 1930: 6,940 of 23,573 families".
Under the British Mandate, land kept moving from absentee owners to Zionist land funds. Sir John Hope Simpson's 1930 survey found 6,940 of 23,573 families in 104 villages held no land. The Jewish National Fund's draft lease required tenants to farm "only with Jewish labour", so evicted families couldn't even be hired back. At Wadi al-Hawarith, tenants fought eviction in British courts for years; the final order came on 15 June 1933. Britain passed five laws to protect tenants. They failed through loopholes.

**[0:55] BUILDING A STATE INSIDE A COLONY**
ON SCREEN: "1923 · Tel Aviv · first grid".
At the same time, the institutions of a state were being built: Palestine's first electric grid switched on in Tel Aviv in 1923, under a monopoly concession. And not every Jewish voice was welcome. In 1924 Jacob de Haan, an Orthodox anti-Zionist, was shot dead leaving prayers in Jerusalem.

**[1:15] 1933**
ON SCREEN: "25 Aug 1933 · Haavara".
In 1933 the record gets uncomfortable in every direction. On 25 August, Zionist bodies signed the Haavara transfer agreement with the government of Nazi Germany. About 60,000 German Jews reached Palestine under it, with around 105 million Reichsmarks of goods. It saved lives, and it undercut the worldwide boycott of Nazi Germany, and it split the Jewish world. Chaim Arlosoroff, linked to the negotiations, was shot on a Tel Aviv beach that June. The case was never solved.

**[1:50] THE REVOLT AND TRANSFER**
ON SCREEN: "1936–39 · about 5,000 killed".
From 1936 to 1939 Palestinian Arabs revolted: a general strike, then an armed uprising. Britain crushed it: about 5,000 Palestinian Arabs killed, between 108 and 122 hanged; two Jews were hanged. In 1937 the Peel Commission proposed partition and moving about 225,000 Arabs out of a Jewish state, the first official British document to put population transfer in writing. Meanwhile Britain trained the army that would later fight it: the Night Squads in 1938, the Palmach in 1941.

**[2:25] NO ONE ELSE WOULD TAKE THEM**
ON SCREEN: "Évian 1938 · 32 nations".
In 1939 Britain's White Paper capped Jewish immigration at 75,000 over five years and promised an independent state within ten. It gave the Arab side, on paper, much of what it had asked for, just as its revolt was being crushed. In July 1938, 32 nations had met at Évian about Jewish refugees. Only the Dominican Republic offered to take many. And in January 1941, Lehi offered Nazi Germany an alliance; the document was found in German embassy files.

## Road to 1948 · Part 4 of 5 · "The pivot to America, and the war that decided it (1938–1948)"

**[0:00] HOOK**
ON SCREEN: "The first full recognition came from Moscow."
The first country to give Israel full recognition wasn't the United States. It was the Soviet Union. Here's how the movement pivoted to America, and what actually decided 1948.

**[0:15] AMERICA**
ON SCREEN: "1938 · Foreign Agents Registration Act".
With Britain closing the door in 1939, the movement turned to the rising power: the United States. And before 1938 there was no law requiring anyone to register as an agent for a foreign cause. In 1940 Jabotinsky's movement sent Benzion Netanyahu to the United States, and the Bergson group ran rescue campaigns through full-page newspaper ads.

**[0:35] ROOSEVELT**
ON SCREEN: "USS Quincy · 14 Feb 1945".
On 14 February 1945, Roosevelt met the Saudi king Ibn Saud aboard the USS Quincy. On 5 April, a week before he died, he promised no action hostile to the Arab people without consulting both sides. A year earlier a Navy cardiologist had found him in heart failure, and it was kept quiet as the party chose Harry Truman as vice-president.

**[1:00] THE MACHINE IN MOTION**
ON SCREEN: "July 1945 · New York".
In July 1945, a meeting in Rudolf Sonneborn's New York apartment launched an arms-buying network that ran outside public channels for three years. In early 1948 Golda Meyerson, later Golda Meir, raised about 50 million dollars in America, and it paid for Czechoslovak arms.

**[1:25] WASHINGTON WAVERS**
ON SCREEN: "ORE-55 · trusteeship".
Washington wasn't united. The day before the UN vote, the new CIA predicted war and danger to American interests. In March 1948 the US proposed a UN trusteeship instead of the partition it had voted for. The UN partition vote on 29 November 1947 passed 33 to 13 with 10 abstentions, and it needed two-thirds; the Philippines, Haiti and Liberia were reported as pressured to vote yes. Then, on 14 May, Truman gave de facto recognition eleven minutes after the state was declared. Full recognition came only in January 1949. The USSR gave full recognition on 17 May 1948.

**[1:55] THE WAR**
ON SCREEN: "7% → 56% → 78%".
And then the war decided what the money and the diplomacy hadn't. On 15 May about 35,000 Israeli troops faced 20,000 to 25,000 Arab troops, by the historian Avi Shlaim's count. At Deir Yassin in April, about 110 people were killed; that's current scholarship's figure, not the 254 cited for decades. In July, 50,000 to 70,000 people were expelled from Lydda and Ramla under written orders. In June, Israel's cabinet agreed not to let refugees return. Land in Jewish ownership: about 7 percent. The UN plan: about 56. The armistice line: about 78.

## Road to 1948 · Part 5 of 5 · "Seen from the land, the accounts left open, and the tests"

**[0:00] HOOK**
ON SCREEN: "Two-thirds of the population had no vote on the plan for their country."
When the UN voted to partition Palestine, two-thirds of the people who lived there had no seat in the room. This last part tells the story from their side, then shows what the record says about the whole road.

**[0:15] THEY WEREN'T "OCCUPIED" UNTIL 1917**
ON SCREEN: "1911 · Ottoman parliament".
Under the Ottomans, Palestine's Arabs were subjects with elected deputies. In 1911 Jerusalem's deputies raised Zionism in the Ottoman parliament. Occupation began with the British army in 1917. From 1919 to 1928, seven Palestinian congresses demanded an elected government; Britain never recognized their executive. In 1923 they boycotted Britain's appointed council; their leader Musa Kazim al-Husseini wrote that their sole object was independence. In 1929 about 300 women met as the first Palestine Arab Women's Congress.

**[0:45] SOLD FROM UNDER THEM**
ON SCREEN: "absentee landlords · no title".
Most of the land wasn't seized; it was sold, by absentee landlords, many of them Arab families in Beirut and other cities. The families farming it had no title, so the sale and the eviction were the same legal act. At the UN, Jamal Husseini warned the partition line would be "a line of fire and blood." By 1949, 700,000 to 750,000 people had been displaced and about 500 villages emptied or destroyed. They had warned about it for fifty years.

**[1:15] THE ACCOUNTS LEFT OPEN**
ON SCREEN: "1 Oct 1948 · constitution promised".
The Declaration of Independence promised a constitution by 1 October 1948. In 1950 the Knesset deferred it; there still isn't one. A Custodian of Abandoned Property was appointed in July 1948, and laws through 1953 turned flight into state title. In 1949 Syria's new ruler made a peace offer; he was overthrown and executed that August. In 2021 Israel's cabinet approved about 50 million dollars in compensation to families in the Yemenite children affair, after three inquiries. And in 1959 the American lobby founded in 1953–54 took the name AIPAC.

And we scored eight other homelands that were proposed: Argentina, Sinai, East Africa, Birobidzhan, Madagascar, Alaska, Sosúa, Suriname. Not one comes out above 2 percent. Every one was someone else's land, someone else's territory, or offered without self-rule.
