Three Layers

The Readiness Audit in [scene:de34_what_is_built|Part Thirty-Six] counted what has been built. This part asks a narrower and harder question: if the end state the texts describe were actually enforced on the people living under it, what would happen to them, and how much of that already exists in American law. The answer has three layers, and they have to be kept apart, because the worst-case reading depends on running them together. The first is the end state itself, in the texts' own words. The second is the machinery that exists in the United States as of October 2026, almost all of it built by governments of both parties, and almost all of it for some other purpose. The third is the gap between the two: what a government would still need, in law, in capacity and in the consent of courts, to turn the second layer into the first. The sections that follow take the consequences rung by rung, then domain by domain, then right by right, and end with the question the texts treat most harshly, what becomes of people judged unproductive.

The first layer: the end state, in the texts' words

The clearest compact statement of the end state is not Curtis Yarvin's but Nick Land's summary of him, written in 2012. "To a neocameralist, a state is a business which owns a country," Land quotes Yarvin, writing as Mencius Moldbug; ownership is divided into shares, "each share has one vote, and the shareholders elect a board, which hires and fires managers," and "this business's customers are its residents." [cite:de35_land_ebook] Neocameralism, the name for this design, is defined in [scene:de6_the_program|Part Six]. Land then draws the consequence for the residents in his own voice. Once power is turned into shares, the owners can begin "rational corporate governance, beginning with the appointment of a CEO," and "there is no longer any need for residents (clients) to take any interest in politics whatsoever. In fact, to do so would be to exhibit semi-criminal proclivities." Taxes become "sovereign rent." A resident who is badly served can "notify its customer service function" or leave. The slogan follows: "No voice, free exit." [cite:de35_land_ebook]

The passage Land quotes next sets out what the residents would keep and what they would lose. The model states, Yarvin wrote, have "minimal crime and high levels of personal and economic freedom"; they are "weak only in political freedom, and political freedom is unimportant by definition when government is stable and effective." [cite:de35_land_ebook] Yarvin has kept the substance since. In 2012 he told an audience of technology workers that Americans would have to "get over their dictator phobia" and accept "a national CEO [or] what's called a dictator." [cite:de6_pein_2014] In January 2025 he told the New York Times that "monarch is good, it's a neutral term." [cite:de6_audit_nyt] Asked in the same interview about rulers like Nero, he called it "the most important question of all" and gave no mechanism for removing one. [cite:de35_nyt_interview]

Two things about this layer matter for everything that follows. The first is what the texts say about the people who do not fit. Yarvin's answer for residents who cost a sovereign corporation more than they pay, in the 2008 Patchwork posts, was emigration if any other realm would take them and, failing that, "permanent solitary confinement" in virtual reality, which he called "a humane alternative to genocide." [cite:de6_audit_le] [cite:de6_tnr_duran] The second is what the texts do not contain. They specify no penal code, no list of punishments for dissent, no system of fines, blacklists or purchase controls, and no programme of compelled labour. Land's own economic program points the other way, toward "hard money" and the abolition of central banking. [cite:de35_land_ebook] The end state is a structure of authority, not an enforcement manual, and the enforcement manual has to be read from somewhere else.

The second layer: the machinery that exists

That somewhere else is the American state as it stood in October 2026. Its pieces were set out across Books Five and Six, and are only listed here. The state already holds records on most residents' benefits, schooling, employment and credit, and since 2025 several of those walls have been lowered: Medicaid data reached ICE and was shared with Palantir before a court paused it, and a federal judge in September 2026 let a notice allowing welfare data to go to DHS take effect ([scene:de24b_benefits|Part Twenty-Five]). [cite:de24b_npr_medicaid] [cite:de24b_bl_tanf] It knows who travels, and it can stop some of them: passports are denied for tax debt over $66,000 and child-support arrears over $2,500, and a Minnesota woman's trusted-traveller status was revoked three days after a Border Patrol agent identified her by face while she watched an immigration operation ([scene:de25b_who_can_stop_you|Part Twenty-Seven]). [cite:de25b_irs_passport] [cite:de25b_crs_childsupport] [cite:de25b_bu_cleland] Its immigration agency runs an integration layer that joins brokers, government records and field data, graded link by link in [scene:de28_the_ecosystem_map|Part Thirty]. Its money rails can freeze a person out of banking without a court, as the sanctions on judges of the International Criminal Court showed ([scene:de26_money_rails|Part Twenty-Eight]). [cite:de35_ap_icc] Its legal levers were loosened in the same years: the president gained at-will removal of independent commissioners, about 8,000 senior civil servants were moved into a schedule exempt from adverse-action procedures, and a national security memorandum told investigators to pursue the funders of groups it described ([scene:de30_nspm7_and_detachment|Part Thirty-Two]). [cite:de0_slaughter] [cite:de30_wh_eo14410] [cite:de30_npr_8000] [cite:de30_wh_nspm7] And its enforcement arm doubled: ICE says it grew from about 10,000 to about 22,000 officers and agents, funded with $45 billion for detention to 2029 ([scene:de32_ice_as_template|Part Thirty-Four]). [cite:de32_kwtx_hiring] [cite:de32_aic_hr1]

None of these pieces was built from the texts. Most predate the 2025 administration; several were built or kept by its predecessors. The child-support passport rule dates from 1996, the terrorist watchlist from the years after 2001, and the private detention estate from administrations of both parties. [cite:de25b_crs_childsupport] [cite:de25b_cbs_watchlist] [cite:de32_brennan_14006] What changed in 2025 and 2026 was scale, speed and the lowering of walls between systems that were built apart.

The third layer: the gap

The gap between the two layers is not a matter of tools. It is a matter of triggers, joins and consent. Almost every consequence in the machinery fires on something specific and contestable: a debt of a set size, a conviction, a lapsed visa, a designation by name. For non-citizens, immigration law supplies a trigger of wide reach, and the record shows it used on speech: a federal judge found in September 2025 that the government had pursued an ideological deportation policy against non-citizen academics, and vacated it in January 2026. [cite:de28_ch_aaup] [cite:de30_audit_aaup] For citizens, no trigger of comparable reach exists. No statute lets the government designate a domestic group as terrorist, and the law firm DLA Piper's reading of the 2025 memorandum is that it creates no new legal authority. [cite:de30_dla_nspm7] [scene:de34_missing_pieces_and_triggers|Part Thirty-Six] lists the dated events that would change that.

The joins are the second part of the gap. Each system in the second layer keeps its own list. A tax debt reaches the passport office; a sanctions designation reaches every bank. No document shows one standing list, carried from system to system, that marks a person as an enemy and denies them everything at once; China's court blacklist of judgment debtors, which airlines and railways enforce, is the nearest working model, and its trigger is unpaid debt, not speech ([scene:de36_the_reference_model|Part Thirty-Nine]). [cite:de35_tr_china] The third part of the gap is consent. Courts struck down the law-firm orders, the Harvard funding freeze and the speech-based deportation policy, vacated food-restriction waivers in five states, and briefly lifted the sanctions on a United Nations rapporteur before an appeals court restored them. [cite:de24b_gma_lawfirms] [cite:de35_hm_harvard] [cite:de35_bd_aragon] [cite:de35_jc_stay] Those rulings were not final in every case, and others went the government's way.

Read together, the layers give the part its method. The worst case is not a new apparatus; it is the existing one with a common trigger and fewer brakes. The question for each rung of consequence is therefore the same: what the theorists prescribe, in their own words; what exists now, and against whom; and what extension would be needed to reach the end state. No document shows that anyone is building that extension, and the texts' authors did not ask for most of it. The next section climbs the ladder from being watched to compelled labour.
