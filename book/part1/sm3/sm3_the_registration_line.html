The Registration Line

The Foreign Agents Registration Act requires people who act for a foreign government to register and disclose it. In 1952 Abraham Feinberg, Truman's donor, was ordered to register under the Act for publishing a bimonthly, Israel Speaks, on behalf of Israeli interests. He considered selling it and then shut it down rather than register. [cite:feinberg_fbi_file] [cite:israellobby_feinberg_fbi]

The larger case came ten years later. On 21 November 1962 the Justice Department ordered the American Zionist Council to register as an agent of a foreign principal, alleging that it had received more than $5 million from the Jewish Agency, a body already registered under the Act. [cite:israellobby_org_fara_doj_order] The Senate Foreign Relations Committee, under J. William Fulbright, put a figure of $5,100,001.02 for 1955 to 1962 into its record; a later historian puts it at $1.5 million, and the two figures are unreconciled. [cite:jta_fulbright_jewish_agency_hearing] [cite:rs_cw5_barda_2026] By nearly every account the Council never registered, and the order was never enforced to completion. [cite:rs_cw5_doj_fara_1964] [cite:rs_cw5_sres26] The founder of the committee that became AIPAC, Isaiah Kenen, had himself been a registered foreign-agent lobbyist and an employee of Israel's information office at the United Nations, a status that ended when he moved to the American committee. [cite:wrmea_aipac] [cite:rs_cw5_barda_2026]

Today AIPAC has never registered under the Act. Its position is that it is an American organisation, registered as a lobby and funded by private donations, that receives no financial help from Israel or any other foreign group. [cite:pm_wiki_aipac] Its lobbying disclosures are modest: about $3.3 million in 2024. [cite:pm_pp_aipac990] The Israeli government's own spending runs separately and is larger: its public-diplomacy budget rose to about $730 million for 2026, and more than $100 million has moved through its registered agents in the United States since 2023. [cite:pm_jta_730m] [cite:pm_quincy_eighth]

What changed is little. In 1962 a registered foreign body paid an unregistered American one; today the state and the American lobby keep separate books, and the government's spending is disclosed through its agents. The question Fulbright asked, where the line should run, is still argued in the same terms.
