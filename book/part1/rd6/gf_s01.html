Who Paid for It All? The Funding Ledger

For decades two explanations of how the movement was paid for before 1948 have circulated. One says it ran on a few wealthy patrons. The other says it ran on secret government money. The familiar versions stop there. The documents do not. They show organized private giving on a mass scale, increasingly American, sustained over decades. Throughout this section, amounts are given as they stood at the time and, where the source allows, in today's dollars.

The movement's permanent fund was Keren Hayesod, its central fundraising arm. From April 1921 to December 1929 it raised $20.1 million worldwide, about $370 million today. More than half, $11.0 million, came from the United States. The next largest sources were South Africa ($1.5 million), Germany ($0.98 million), Romania ($0.88 million), Britain ($0.88 million), Poland ($0.82 million) and Canada ($0.74 million). [cite:jta_kh_decade] Over its first twelve years, the United States gave about half of the £4.82 million raised. [cite:jta_kh_12yr] Before 1948 the fund paid for immigration, settlement, defense, water and public works. [cite:jvl_kh]

The scale changed with the United Jewish Appeal, the American umbrella campaign formed in 1939. It raised more than $124 million between 1939 and 1945, and $100 million in 1946. [cite:raphael_uja] In 1947 it raised about $110 million. [cite:cambridge_solidarity] In 1948 it raised a record $150 million from more than a million contributors in 4,950 communities. [cite:jta_uja_1948] That campaign was described as the largest non-government humanitarian campaign in American history. [cite:jta_uja_1948] [cite:cambridge_solidarity] Across 1946 to 1948 the three campaigns came to about $360 million, or about $5.2 billion today.

Where the money went needs care. Of the 1948 total, $74.7 million went to the United Palestine Appeal, $64.9 million to the Joint Distribution Committee for relief in Europe, and $10.4 million to resettling refugees in the United States. [cite:jta_uja_1948] Roughly half went to Palestine, about 43 percent to relief in Europe and about 7 percent to the United States. To count the whole sum as funding for Israel would overstate it; much of it fed Holocaust survivors in Europe. In today's dollars, measured by the consumer price index of the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the 1948 campaign comes to about $2.0 billion and its Palestine share to about $1.0 billion. [cite:bls_cpi]

The conversions follow a stated method. Each amount is multiplied by the ratio of the 2025 consumer price index, 321.943, to the index for the year in question. That is the same method the government's own inflation calculator uses. [cite:bls_cpi] Sums in pounds are first converted at the exchange rates of the period, and values before 1913 are approximate.

Arms were bought through separate channels, and they are harder to total. The purchases ran through front companies. Estimates of the cost of Czech arms run from about $14.5 million to about six times that. Golda Meyerson's fundraising tour brought in about $50 million, which went largely to weapons. The government of Somoza's Nicaragua supplied $3 million in letters of credit. [cite:israelaffairs_italy] These are ranges, not a total.

The ledger has an honest limit. No audited grand total exists, and some streams overlap, so they are shown separately rather than added together. Even so, the shape is clear. The bank, the land, the immigration and the arms were built over half a century on giving that came from around the world, grew increasingly American, and by 1948 was drawn from more than a million individual donors. That does not fit the picture of a handful of patrons, a point taken up again in [scene:pn_s01|seven founding narratives, checked].
