The Tools That Were Never Used

The usual defence of the outcome is that the law was not up to it. The record says otherwise. Over half a century Congress built tools aimed at exactly this kind of case, and the Justice Department has used them, elsewhere, with success.

The comparison with TD Bank is the sharpest. In October 2024 the bank's American arm pleaded guilty to Bank Secrecy Act violations and became the first American bank in history to plead guilty to conspiracy to commit money laundering, paying more than $3 billion. [cite:ep8_icij_tdbank2024] JPMorgan, which kept Epstein as a client for five years after his conviction despite internal warnings, settled with his victims and the Virgin Islands for $290 million and $75 million without admitting wrongdoing, and no criminal case was brought against it or any of its executives. [cite:aj_jpm] [cite:npr_jpm] [cite:ep3_aj_usvi_jpm2023] The tools existed for both banks. They were used against one.

The prosecutors who took the case in New York in 2019 did weigh going further. An 86-page prosecution memo of December 2019 found that Epstein "worked with others, including employees and associates who facilitated his exploitation of minors", and weighed the liability of several of them; only Maxwell was charged, and the department says many allegations against prominent men were uncorroborated or not credible. [cite:ep8_abc_cocon_memo] In July 2025 the Justice Department and FBI closed their review, saying they had found no evidence of a "client list". [cite:ep5_npr_memo2025] Each of these is a decision not to prosecute, and under the rules in the previous section no court can review it.

The missing referee

The hardest conflict for any justice system is when the people who might be investigated sit at the top of the system itself. In this case the president is among the people named in the files, as Part Five sets out, and the department's second-ranking official, later its acting head, Todd Blanche, was his former personal defence lawyer. [cite:cnn_maxwell] The law once had an answer to that, the court-appointed independent counsel, and it has been gone since 1999. Under the rules that replaced it, appointing a special counsel is the Attorney General's decision. [cite:everycrsreport_iclaw] House Democrats asked for one on the president's Epstein ties in October 2026; none has been appointed. [cite:ep8_politicalwire_sc2026] The same gap would shield any administration's friends. It is the keystone of the whole structure.

Why survivors keep their own list private

One more rule shapes what the public sees. An accusation made in a court filing is protected against a defamation suit; the same accusation made in public is not. That is why nearly every named allegation in this case lives in lawsuits and depositions rather than at press conferences. The rule protects people from false accusations, which matters, as the dispute between Virginia Giuffre and Alan Dershowitz shows. [cite:ep4_cbs_dershowitz2022] Its cost falls on the people with the least money.
