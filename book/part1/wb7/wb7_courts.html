Two Systems of Law

One territory, two laws. An Israeli settler and a Palestinian neighbour in the West Bank do not live under the same law. The Israeli settler remains under Israeli law and Israeli courts, and Israel's Supreme Court has held that its Basic Laws protect them there. The Palestinian is governed by military orders issued by army commanders since 1967, layered on the Jordanian law in force before, and is tried in military courts for offences ranging from traffic violations to petty theft. The Association for Civil Rights in Israel, documenting the two systems in 2014, called the result "institutionalized discrimination." Israel's Justice Ministry, responding, did not dispute the facts it described, but defended them on security grounds. [cite:wb_acri_twosys]

The military courts. In 2010, according to an internal army document reported by Haaretz, 99.74 percent of the trials of Palestinians in the military courts ended in conviction: 25 acquittals in 9,542 cases. [cite:wb_972_conviction]

Detention without trial. The army can also hold Palestinians without charge or trial, by administrative detention orders, on intelligence that neither the detainee nor their lawyer is shown. In August 2023, before the war, HaMoked, an Israeli rights group working from Israel Prison Service data, counted 1,201 Palestinians held in this way, the most since it began monthly counts in 2001. [cite:wb_tna_hamoked2023] By September 2025 it counted 3,577 administrative detainees, among 11,040 Palestinian prisoners, and 2,662 more held as "unlawful combatants." [cite:wb_mem_hamoked2025]

Children. In 2013 UNICEF reported that about 700 Palestinian children aged 12 to 17 were arrested, interrogated and detained by the Israeli military system each year, and found their ill-treatment "widespread, systematic and institutionalized": arrests at night by heavily armed soldiers, abuse during transfer, interrogation involving physical violence and threats, shackling in court and detention inside Israel, away from their families. It also noted that Israel had raised the age of adulthood in its military courts from 16 to 18. [cite:wb_un_unicef2013]
