Europe, Canada, Australia and the Caucus Network

Outside Britain and the United States, the record of pro-Israel advocacy is patchier, because few countries require lobbyists to disclose what they spend. What can be documented falls into two patterns. In wealthy democracies allied with the United States, national organizations modeled more or less closely on AIPAC lobby parliaments and take their members to Israel. In much of Latin America, Africa and Asia, the main vehicle is different: a network of parliamentary caucuses organized around Christian support for Israel. This section takes the two patterns in turn and gives the amounts where they are known.

Europe: ELNET. The European Leadership Network, known as ELNET, was founded in October 2007 and is registered in Belgium, France, Germany, Poland, Britain, Italy and Israel; it describes itself as "the most influential pro-Israel advocacy organisation in Europe." One of its co-founders, Raanan Eliaz, had worked as a consultant for AIPAC. [cite:pm_wiki_elnet] [cite:pm_intercept_elnet] Much of its money comes from the United States. According to The Intercept, its American fundraising arm, Friends of ELNET, began in 2012 with nearly $1 million from the advocacy group StandWithUs, raised more than $9.1 million in 2023, and has received at least $11 million from more than 100 American foundations and charities since 2022. ELNET says it has taken about 300 European policymakers on trips to Israel, and it has credited its own work for Germany's approval of a $3.5 billion purchase of Israel's Arrow 3 missile-defense system. [cite:pm_intercept_elnet] [cite:pm_wiki_elnet] That claim is ELNET's own. The decision was the German government's, and the record does not allow the lobby's share in it to be measured.

Canada: CIJA. Canada's lobbying registry makes public the contacts of the Centre for Israel and Jewish Affairs, a Canadian advocacy body. Analyzing the registry, the Canadian outlet The Maple found that CIJA had lobbied, or sponsored trips to Israel for, 156 of the 269 sitting MPs running in the 2025 election, 58 percent; it had lobbied them 579 times since 2008 and spent C$678,277 on their trips since 2007. In May 2023 Canada's Commissioner of Lobbying wrote that sponsored travel "could reasonably be seen to create a sense of obligation," and the Lobbyists' Code was changed that month to bar lobby groups from sponsoring travel for officials they lobby. [cite:pm_maple_cija] Canada is the one country in this volume that closed the channel described in Part Seven.

Australia: AIJAC. The Australia/Israel and Jewish Affairs Council runs the Rambam program, which takes federal politicians, journalists and others to Israel; a delegation in 2012, for example, included six Coalition MPs and senators. [cite:pm_aijac_rambam] The sources reached for this volume give no total for the program's cost or the number of Australian politicians who have taken part.

The caucus network. The Israel Allies Foundation, the body that drafted the model anti-boycott law described in Part Eight, also runs the widest international network in this volume. It grew from the Knesset Christian Allies Caucus, formed in 2004, and the Congressional Israel Allies Caucus, formed in 2006, and in December 2020, when Honduras formed a caucus, it counted 50 countries and about 1,200 parliamentarians. [cite:pm_wiki_iaf] [cite:pm_ih_iaf50] Its affiliates have included caucuses in the Philippines, South Korea and Japan in Asia, South Africa, Malawi and Cameroon in Africa, Brazil and Honduras in Latin America, and the European Parliament. [cite:pm_wiki_iaf] [cite:pm_ih_iaf50] It calls its method "faith-based diplomacy," and its president, Josh Reinstein, has called the network "the greatest tool that Israel has in its diplomatic arsenal." [cite:pm_ih_iaf50] Its own budget is small: about $1.4 million of revenue in 2018, from American donors including the Moskowitz family foundations, and from Concert, an Israeli nonprofit that receives matching funds from Israel's government, an arrangement the Forward said raised a question under the Foreign Agents Registration Act. [cite:pm_wiki_iaf] Its stated goals include anti-boycott laws and the moving of embassies to Jerusalem, and it linked the new caucuses in Honduras and Guatemala to those countries' embassy decisions. [cite:pm_wiki_iaf] [cite:pm_ih_iaf50]

Two things stand out from the table. The first is the scale of American money abroad: ELNET's European work is substantially funded from the United States, and the Israel Allies network was built from an American base. The second is how little is known. Among the sources used here, only those for Britain, Canada and the United States count lawmakers' or lobbyists' dealings from public registers; for most of the countries in the caucus network, the record consists of the network's own announcements.
