Part Five turns from money to speech, and to an asymmetry most civics lessons leave out. The First Amendment restrains government, not companies, so a platform may spend without limit on political advocacy and may remove whatever its users post, while those users have no constitutional claim against it. The part sets out the three Supreme Court decisions that produce this result, then lays the documented record of platform moderation side by side, from Palestine-related content to the Hunter Biden laptop story and the suspension of President Trump, each case beside the company's own account. It ends where users do hold rights, against government pressure on the companies, examining the Biden and Trump administrations under one test, and with what it would take to undo Citizens United.
