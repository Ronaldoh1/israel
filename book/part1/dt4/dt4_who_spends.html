Who Spends

The rules described in the previous section opened a channel. This section measures what flowed through it. It puts every large spender on the same footing: the party leaders' super PACs on both sides, the presidential super PACs, the billionaires on both sides, industry groups such as the crypto sector's Fairshake, issue lobbies such as AIPAC's United Democracy Project, and organised labour. The figures come from disclosure reports as compiled by OpenSecrets and others. They show who gave and who spent, not what any donor received in return; that question is the subject of the next section.

The 2024 record

Outside spending, meaning spending by groups other than the candidates' own campaigns and the parties, reached a record $4.5 billion in the 2024 federal elections, according to OpenSecrets, with more than half coming from groups that do not fully disclose their funding. Outside spending on the presidential race alone passed $2 billion, against about $1.14 billion in 2020. Super PACs reported more than $1 billion received from dark-money groups and shell companies, whose own donors are not public. Money from such anonymous sources had been under $71.7 million in 2016, $653 million in 2020 and $617 million in the 2022 midterms. [cite:dt4_os_2024record] The OpenSecrets figures for individual groups run to mid-October 2024 and are snapshots rather than final totals.

The largest spenders, both sides

The biggest single outside spender of 2024 was on the Democratic side. Future Forward USA PAC, which backed Kamala Harris, spent about $517.1 million on the presidential race; $136.4 million of its money came from its own nonprofit arm, whose donors are not disclosed, and its named donors included Dustin Moskovitz with $38 million, Michael Bloomberg with $19 million, and Reid Hoffman with $10 million. On the Republican side, MAGA Inc. spent about $377 million for Donald Trump, with $151.5 million from Timothy Mellon; America PAC, funded by Elon Musk, more than $169 million; Preserve America more than $112.2 million, about $100 million of it from Miriam Adelson; and Right for America about $69.6 million, mainly from Isaac and Laura Perlmutter. [cite:dt4_os_2024record]

The party leaders in Congress run their own pairs of super PACs and nonprofits. In 2024 the Republican House group, the Congressional Leadership Fund, spent nearly $216.7 million and the Republican Senate group, the Senate Leadership Fund, more than $211 million, with about $40 million and $54 million respectively from their nonprofit affiliates. The Democratic House group, House Majority PAC, spent more than $196.1 million, and the Democratic Senate nonprofit, Majority Forward, took in more than $113.2 million, passing about $70.8 million to Senate Majority PAC. The four leadership nonprofits together moved about $250 million from anonymous donors into their allied super PACs. [cite:dt4_os_2024record]

Concentration differed by side. The Brennan Center found that about 44 per cent ($481 million) of the money raised to support Trump came from ten donors, against about 8 per cent ($126 million) of Harris's support from her top ten. [cite:ad15_brennan_cu15] By individual giving, OpenSecrets recorded Musk at more than $291 million; Mellon, Adelson, Richard and Elizabeth Uihlein, Kenneth Griffin and Jeffrey and Janine Yass at $100 million or more each, all on the Republican side; and Bloomberg at $64 million and Moskovitz at $51 million on the Democratic side. [cite:de7_os_donors2024]

Industry and issue spenders

Some of the heaviest spenders are organised around a single industry or cause rather than a party. Fairshake, a super PAC formed on 23 March 2023 and funded by the cryptocurrency industry, works through two affiliates, one for Democratic primaries and one for Republican. Its largest donors through 2024 were Coinbase with $93.5 million, Ripple Labs with $45 million, Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz with $33.5 million each, and Jump Trading with $25 million. With its affiliates it spent more than $133 million in 2024, nearly twice as much on Republicans as on Democrats, including more than $10 million against Katie Porter in California's Democratic Senate primary, and it had raised more than $193 million for 2026 by January 2026. [cite:dt4_wiki_fairshake]

AIPAC, the American Israel Public Affairs Committee, is a lobby that advocates for US support of Israel. It formed its own PAC on 15 December 2021 and a super PAC, the United Democracy Project, on 3 January 2022. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2022] Sludge, working from Federal Election Commission data, counted nearly $126.9 million in combined spending by the two in the 2023–24 cycle, and $104.3 million in the 2026 cycle by early August 2026. [cite:pm_sludge_final2024] [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] The lobby's spending is reported, and is measured here exactly as Fairshake's or any party committee's is; its record in the 2026 primaries, where its candidates won some races and lost others, is taken up in the next section and in detail in [scene:pm5_2026|Follow the Money].

Organised labour is harder to measure on the same scale, because unions spend through PACs, super PACs and their general treasuries, and the available totals use different methods. From OpenSecrets data, the American Federation of Teachers' PAC raised about $12 million and spent about $13 million in the 2024 cycle, including $3 million to Senate Majority PAC and $1.6 million to House Majority PAC. [cite:dt4_74_aft] The Commonwealth Foundation, a free-market advocacy group, puts the political and ideological spending of the four largest public-sector unions, the NEA, AFT, SEIU and AFSCME, at more than $915 million in 2023–24, about 85 per cent from member dues. That figure covers state as well as federal activity, its method is not set out on the page that reports it, and it cannot be compared directly with the OpenSecrets totals above. [cite:dt4_cf_unions] No neutral like-for-like total for union election spending in 2024 was found.

The 2026 cycle so far

The 2026 midterms are on course to cost more. From January 2025 to June 2026 the four party-leadership super PACs raised $714.6 million, according to Issue One, a campaign-finance reform group, and $197.7 million of it came from their affiliated nonprofits, whose donors are not disclosed. The split was 58 per cent to the Republican-aligned pair and 42 per cent to the Democratic-aligned pair. [cite:dt4_issueone_2026] Elon Musk gave more than $276 million to Republican-aligned groups from July 2026 to mid-October, including $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund, and his known cycle total passed $440 million according to America PAC's pre-general filing. [cite:de11_ap_2026] [cite:de11_ibt_fec] Through 5 October 2026, OpenSecrets reported, the top six outside spenders had each spent more than $60 million and together accounted for over a third of all outside spending; Democratic candidates had tended to outraise Republican candidates, while outside groups leaned to the right. [cite:dt4_marketplace_2026] These are figures as of October 2026; final totals will not be known until after the 3 November election.

The pattern across both cycles is consistent. Both parties' leadership organisations rely on nonprofits that hide their donors. Both sides have billionaire donors giving nine-figure sums, though in 2024 the largest individual sums and the greatest concentration were on the Republican side. Industry and issue groups spend across party lines to shape primaries. The super PAC, the channel through which most of this money moved, did not exist before 2010.
