The Attack

The assault began on the morning of Saturday 7 October 2023. Gunmen from the Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades, Hamas's armed wing, and from at least four other Palestinian armed groups crossed the fence at many points at once. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7] The Israeli army's later inquiry found that Hamas used more than sixty routes of attack, where Israel's plans had expected attackers at no more than eight points along the border. [cite:gz_pbs_idfprobe]

They attacked at least nineteen kibbutzim and five moshavim, the towns of Sderot and Ofakim, army bases and posts along the border, two music festivals and a beach party. Human Rights Watch, which investigated the day, counted 1,195 people killed; by the news agency AFP's cross-checking, 815 of them were civilians. The armed groups took 251 people, civilians and members of the security forces, back into Gaza as hostages. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7]

Nova. The largest single loss of life was at the Nova music festival near Re'im. Rockets were seen at about 6:30 a.m., and at 6:35 a police officer, Deputy Superintendent Nivi Ohana, decided to shut the event down, a decision credited with saving hundreds of lives. Gunmen reached the site soon after. The Israeli army's investigation, released in April 2025, counted 378 people killed there, 344 civilians and 34 members of the security forces, revising an earlier count of 364; 44 were taken hostage. [cite:gz_wiki_nova]

Be'eri. In Kibbutz Be'eri, a community of about a thousand people, 101 civilians and 31 members of the security forces were killed, and 30 residents and two other civilians were taken to Gaza. Large Israeli forces did not enter the kibbutz until about six in the evening. In one house, that of Pessi Cohen, gunmen held fourteen Israelis; a brigadier general, Barak Hiram, ordered a tank to fire on the house, a gun battle followed, and thirteen of the fourteen were found dead. [cite:gz_toi_beeri] How they died is one of the questions the next sections return to.

What the investigators found. Human Rights Watch concluded that killing civilians and taking hostages were "central aims of the planned attack", not the acts of fighters who had gone beyond their orders, and that "the planned murder of civilians and the hostage-taking were crimes against humanity." It documented war crimes including wilful killing, cruel treatment, the mutilation of bodies and crimes involving sexual and gender-based violence. Hamas's authorities told it that their fighters had been instructed not to target civilians; its investigation found evidence to the contrary in many cases. [cite:gz_hrw_oct7]
