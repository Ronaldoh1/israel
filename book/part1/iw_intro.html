This volume follows the contest over what Americans see and hear about Israel and Gaza: who owns the outlets, how the coverage of the war was weighed, what the platforms removed and at whose request, what Israel's government paid to influence the conversation, how a definition of antisemitism moved into platform rules and university discipline, what a think tank proposed and what the government then did, and the surveillance tools that now sit behind all of it.

It is a volume about many institutions at once, and it makes no claim that one hand directs them. Some of what follows is a foreign government's documented campaign, registered under American law. Some is the ordinary behaviour of media businesses. Some is American policy. Each is named for what it is. Where the record shows pressure from one side, it also shows the critics on the other: the same newspaper is accused of bias by both. Other volumes carry parts of the story: The One-Way Mirror and The Pressure Machine for the lobby and the law, Washington for the officials, and the Dark Enlightenment books for the technology stack.
