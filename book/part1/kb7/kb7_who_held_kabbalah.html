Who Held the Kabbalah

Every book on this shelf has asked who held sacred knowledge and what that holding did. Kabbalah gives an answer with more keepers than any before it, and with a gate that was opened from inside.

Three findings stand out. The first is that the gate here was a teacher's gate, not an empire's. The rabbis who restricted Kabbalah held no army; their tools were age limits, bans and excommunication, and the reason they gave was the student's own safety. [cite:kb_ou_age40] [cite:kb_mjl_danger] The second is that the danger they named was not imaginary. The movement of 1665 and 1666 did carry a people to the edge of redemption and then to the sultan's doorway. [cite:kb_brit_sabbatai] The third is that the gate did not hold. Within two generations a popular movement, Hasidism, opened the tradition's central experience to everyone, and survived the attempt to excommunicate it. [cite:kb_brit_devequt] [cite:kb_tau_hasidism]

The record also shows the continuity. Like the Zohar, the books on this shelf gained authority by an older name: Enoch, the Kebra Nagast and the Zohar all claimed a greater age than they had. [cite:kb_brit_deleon] [cite:ds_bas_enoch] [cite:et_wiki_kebra] Like the Canon's guardians, the guardians of Kabbalah closed the gate tightest after a mass movement claimed direct access to God. [cite:kb_ou_age40] And like every tradition on this shelf, Kabbalah was borrowed and sold by outsiders who took its prestige and not its discipline. [cite:kb_wiki_xkab] [cite:kb_herald_kc] The difference is that in this case the tradition's own people opened the door.
