From AZEC to AIPAC

A common belief holds that AIPAC appeared suddenly in the 1950s as a new and independent lobbying group with no earlier history. The institutional record shows a continuous line of descent from the wartime American Zionist Emergency Council. It also shows a federal foreign-agent case along the way, and that case needs to be stated precisely, because it concerned a successor body rather than the original one.

The American Zionist Emergency Council, built under Abba Hillel Silver in 1943, did not dissolve after statehood. In 1949 it was renamed the American Zionist Council and registered as a tax-exempt nonprofit. [cite:azec_powerbase] This was an ordinary institutional continuation, not the founding of a new organization.

The foreign-agent question arose more than a decade later. On 21 November 1962 the Internal Security Section of the Justice Department, under Attorney General Robert Kennedy, ordered the American Zionist Council to register as an agent of a foreign principal under the Foreign Agents Registration Act. The department alleged that the Council had received more than $5 million from the Jewish Agency over the preceding eight years. [cite:azc_fara_doj] The order was directed at the 1949 successor, not at the 1943 body. It was an allegation against one organization at one time, later withdrawn, and it should not be read back across the whole lineage as a single continuous violation.

The Justice Department later withdrew its registration demand. In 1963 the American Zionist Council's lobbying functions were separated into a newly restructured organization, the American Israel Public Affairs Committee. AIPAC was set up to operate solely on private American donations, and on that basis it asserted that it was not a foreign agent. [cite:aipac_wiki]

The sequence is short and each step is documented: a wartime council, a rename in 1949, a foreign-agent order in 1962, its withdrawal, and a restructuring in 1963. Taken together, it answers the question of where AIPAC came from. It was not a new creation without antecedents. It was the lobbying arm of an organization whose roots ran back to the campaign of the 1940s, reorganized after a federal challenge so that its funding came from American donors alone.
