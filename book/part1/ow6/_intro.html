Part Six turns from the intelligence agencies to the agencies that police the American homeland. It follows four kinds of tie between the Department of Homeland Security and Israel: a 2007 memorandum between the two governments' security ministries; research programmes written into US law in 2014 and widened in 2016 and 2021; joint grants to companies; and the purchase by Immigration and Customs Enforcement of Israeli-made tools for opening phones. It then turns to the police: the private exchange programmes that have taken American police chiefs to Israel since 1992, who pays for them, what their sponsors say they teach, what their critics say, and what the evidence shows; and the New York Police Department's office inside an Israeli police headquarters. Each tie is set beside the reason its sponsors gave for it, and beside what the United States receives in return.
