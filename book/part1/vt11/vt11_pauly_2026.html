François Pauly, the IOR and the Paris Inquiry, 2026

In 2026 two pieces of news put the word "Rothschild" back beside the Vatican. In March the Institute for the Works of Religion (IOR), the Holy See's financial institution, announced that a former chief executive of the Edmond de Rothschild group would chair its board. In the same month French prosecutors searched the Paris offices of a company of that group in an unrelated inquiry. Earlier material on this platform joined the two and read them as the latest link in the "unbroken chain" tested in [scene:vt11_chain_tested|the previous section]. This section sets out what each record says, as of October 2026, and what it does not say. No wrongdoing by François Pauly is documented in any source used for this volume.

The appointment

The IOR is governed under a structure set by John Paul II's chirograph of 1 March 1990: a commission of five cardinals oversees it, and a lay Board of Superintendence is responsible for its administration and management. [cite:vt11_va_ior1990] The chair of that board is commonly called the IOR's president. On 25 March 2026 the IOR announced that François Pauly would be the next president of the Board of Superintendence, succeeding Jean-Baptiste Douville de Franssu, who had held the post since 2014. It described the change as the result of a carefully planned succession process run over the previous twelve months by the Board and the Commission of Cardinals. [cite:vt11_teleborsa_pauly] According to the Luxembourg business paper Paperjam, the Board had proposed Pauly in December 2025 and the Commission of Cardinals approved him on 28 January 2026. [cite:vt11_paperjam_pauly] De Franssu stayed in office until the Board's meeting of 28 April 2026, which approved the IOR's accounts for 2025. [cite:vt11_teleborsa_pauly] When the IOR published its 2025 report on 11 May 2026, recording net profit of €51 million, a dividend of €24.3 million to the pope and client assets of €5.9 billion, Vatican News reported that de Franssu had concluded his term and that Pauly "has taken office". [cite:vt11_vn_ior2025] No later change in the post was found in this volume's check as of October 2026.

The Holy See's stated reasons are on record. Cardinal Giuseppe Petrocchi, president of the Commission of Cardinals, praised de Franssu's "essential contribution" and called Pauly's experience a factor of great importance for consolidating what had been achieved and strengthening relations with the financial sector; de Franssu described the years since 2014 as a "profound structural transformation". [cite:vt11_paperjam_pauly]

Earlier platform material got the dates wrong. It described Pauly as "CEO of Edmond de Rothschild group 2019–2023" and placed the appointment under a heading dated 28 April 2026. The record is a proposal in December 2025, approval on 28 January, announcement on 25 March and handover after 28 April 2026. [cite:vt11_paperjam_pauly] [cite:vt11_teleborsa_pauly]

The career, as the record gives it

Pauly is a Luxembourg citizen whose career in finance spans some 35 years, mostly in private banking. [cite:vt11_ubp_pauly] [cite:vt11_teleborsa_pauly] The IOR's own announcement listed: banking from the late 1980s; deputy chief executive of Dexia Crediop in Italy (2002–2003); chief executive and chairman of Banque Internationale à Luxembourg (2011–2016); a seat on the board of the Vatican pension fund (2017–2021); chairman of the insurer La Luxembourgeoise; and membership of the Economic Affairs Commission of the Archdiocese of Luxembourg. [cite:vt11_teleborsa_pauly] That list, as reported, does not mention Edmond de Rothschild.

Other records fill the gap, and they describe the job in slightly different words. On 7 June 2021 the French financial weekly Option Finance reported his appointment as "CEO du Groupe" Edmond de Rothschild, noting that he had been a director of Edmond de Rothschild (Suisse) and of its Luxembourg subsidiary since 2016; the trade journal Private Banker International gave the same title, "group CEO", after the group board's meeting of 4 June 2021. [cite:vt11_of_pauly2021] [cite:vt11_audit_pbi_pauly] The Spanish Catholic site InfoVaticana describes him as chief executive and chair of the executive committee of Edmond de Rothschild (Suisse) from June 2021, after the death of Benjamin de Rothschild, with his departure formalized in March 2023. [cite:vt11_iv_pauly] Union Bancaire Privée, on whose board he has sat since 2024, says that "most recently" he was chief executive of Banque Edmond de Rothschild, and lists him at 31 December 2024 as a member of the IOR's Board of Superintendence. [cite:vt11_ubp_pauly] He was therefore already on the IOR board before he was chosen to chair it.

Two points about the employer matter. First, InfoVaticana, which framed the appointment critically under the headline "an ex-CEO of the Rothschild group", states in its own text that Edmond de Rothschild is distinct from Rothschild & Co, the other main firm bearing the family name; it does not claim that any Rothschild interest influences Vatican decisions. [cite:vt11_iv_pauly] Second, Pauly was a salaried executive of that group for under two years; no source describes him as a member of the Rothschild family or an owner of its bank. [cite:vt11_of_pauly2021] [cite:vt11_iv_pauly] The finding is one banker's CV. It is not evidence that a family or a firm controls the IOR.

His predecessor's career is a useful comparison. De Franssu came from the French public financial group Caisse des Dépôts and from Invesco, whose European business he ran from 1997 to 2011, and he presided over the European Fund and Asset Management Association from 2009 to 2011; Pope Francis named him to the commission reviewing the Vatican's economic structures in March 2014, and he became IOR president on 9 July 2014. [cite:vt11_wiki_franssu] [cite:vt11_agi_franssu2014] Its last two chairs both came from European banking and asset management; the Rothschild name appears in one of their CVs, not in the IOR's governance.

The Paris inquiry: what the release says

On 24 March 2026 the French national financial prosecutor, Pascal Prache, issued a press release. [cite:vt11_pnf_aidan] It states that searches took place on Friday 20 March 2026 at several places, including the Paris head office of Edmond de Rothschild (France) in the 8th arrondissement. It states that the national financial prosecutor's office had opened a preliminary inquiry on 17 February 2026 into "corruption passive d'agent public étranger et complicité de ce délit" (passive bribery of a foreign public official and complicity), entrusted to the central anti-corruption police office. It states that the inquiry followed a report sent on 16 February 2026 by the French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs, made after the revelations of the "Epstein Files" and the French press coverage that followed. And it states that the inquiry is directed "notamment" (in particular) at Fabrice Aidan. [cite:vt11_pnf_aidan]

The release does not describe Aidan's role or position. It does not say that he or anyone else has been charged, placed under formal investigation or taken into custody. Edmond de Rothschild appears in it only as a place searched. It does not mention the Vatican, the IOR or François Pauly. [cite:vt11_pnf_aidan] A preliminary inquiry in French law is an investigation by the prosecutor's office, not a charge. Fabrice Aidan is the target of a preliminary inquiry and has not been charged, according to the prosecutor's release, and he is presumed innocent. Reuters reported on the same day that he has denied wrongdoing, and that a source close to the bank said it was co-operating fully with the investigation. [cite:vt11_reuters_aidan] No later public step in the case was found for this volume as of October 2026.

Earlier platform material called Aidan a "former Edmond de Rothschild employee … charged with bribery" and credited him with "200+ Epstein DOJ file appearances". The prosecutor's release supports neither phrase: it states no charge and no count of file appearances. [cite:vt11_pnf_aidan] Both are dropped. Pauly had left Edmond de Rothschild in March 2023, almost three years before the inquiry opened, and no source connects him, the IOR or the Holy See to it. [cite:vt11_iv_pauly] [cite:vt11_pnf_aidan]
