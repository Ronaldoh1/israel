Natanz, Wye River and the Hostage Channel, 1998–2026

After the Cold War the CIA–Israel relationship moved from the Soviet target to three others: the Israeli–Palestinian security arrangements of the 1990s, Iran's nuclear programme, and, after 7 October 2023, the hostages held in Gaza. Each is documented in public sources, and each shows the liaison working at the level of agency directors. This section sets out what is on the record, how the liaison exchange works as far as the sources describe it, and the one thing the record does not show: any American mechanism for overseeing what Israel's services do with what they receive.

Wye River, 1998. The agency's most public role came in the peace process. The Wye River Memorandum, signed on 23 October 1998, created American–Palestinian and American–Palestinian–Israeli security committees, the three-party one meeting at least every two weeks. [cite:ow3_crs_wye] The Congressional Research Service reported that its many references to the United States were "understood to create an unusually visible role for the CIA," that CIA Director George Tenet had been among the negotiators, and that some Members of Congress were concerned enough for the Senate Intelligence Committee to plan hearings. [cite:ow3_crs_wye] Melman and Raviv record that Tenet, as deputy director, had been tasked in 1996 with building Yasser Arafat's security forces into a professional body, and took charge of the mission again in 2001 after the second intifada began. [cite:ow3_tablet] The CIA's on-the-ground role in this period was therefore directed chiefly at the Palestinian side's security commitments; CRS describes a committee that reviewed Palestinian steps against armed groups. [cite:ow3_crs_wye]

Olympic Games, 2006–2010. The deepest operational partnership on record is the cyberattack on Iran's enrichment plant at Natanz. According to David Sanger of the New York Times, the programme, code-named Olympic Games, began under President George W. Bush around 2006, and Israel's Unit 8200 supplied technical expertise and intelligence on Natanz that was critical to its success. [cite:ow3_sw_sanger] Sanger said a "beacon" mapped the plant's systems and "phoned home, back to the National Security Agency and the Unit 8200." [cite:ow3_npr_sanger] He also gave the American reason for bringing Israel in: Bush "didn't want to see the Israelis bomb the Iran facilities," and "the Israelis needed to be convinced that there was an alternative to a military attack." [cite:ow3_npr_sanger] The worm escaped through a programming error and was discovered in July 2010; President Obama, briefed with Vice President Biden and CIA Director Leon Panetta, decided to continue, and the last attack took out nearly 1,000 of the 5,000 centrifuges then spinning. [cite:ow3_sw_sanger] The official internal estimate was a delay of 18 months to two years. [cite:ow3_npr_sanger] Neither government has officially acknowledged the operation; Sanger's account rests on unnamed participants. The partnership on Iran continued: in July 2026 Roman Gofman, who became head of the Mossad on 2 June 2026, made his first Washington visit to meet CIA Director John Ratcliffe and White House officials about Iran; the CIA and the Mossad declined to comment. [cite:ow3_fmt_gofman] [cite:ow3_axios_gofman]

The hostage channel, 2023–2024. After the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023, the CIA director became one of the principal negotiators. On 9 November 2023 William Burns met the Mossad chief, David Barnea, and Qatar's prime minister in Doha; during the releases that followed, Burns and his staff were in constant contact with a joint Israeli–Qatari "situation room" at Mossad headquarters, and the first deal exchanged 50 hostages for a four-day pause. [cite:ow3_axios2023] The three met again on 28 November, with Egypt's intelligence chief, Abbas Kamel, also present. [cite:ow3_axios2023] On 28 January 2024 Burns, Barnea, the Qatari prime minister, Kamel and Israel's Shin Bet chief, Ronen Bar, met in Paris to discuss a framework for a two-month pause and phased releases. [cite:ow3_bs_paris] Neither Israel nor the United States negotiated directly with Hamas; Qatar mediated. [cite:ow3_axios2023] The last twenty living hostages were released on 13 October 2025. [cite:gz_kt_hostages]

What flowed the other way. The war also changed the American side of the exchange. The Wall Street Journal reported in April 2024 that a secret White House memorandum, issued shortly after 7 October and amended days later, expanded intelligence sharing with Israel to include raw material such as live drone video over Gaza. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] The same report said the United States had expanded its own collection on Gaza, having previously relied largely on Israel for it. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] The stated purpose was limited: an administration official said sharing "is focused on hostage-recovery efforts and preventing future incursions into Israel," and, according to people familiar with it, the United States did not share intelligence intended specifically for ground or air strikes. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] Israel's military spokesman, Rear Admiral Daniel Hagari, said: "We are experiencing unprecedented levels of intelligence coordination." [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing]

What the CIA does not do. The public record contains no mechanism by which the CIA, or any other American body, inspects how Israel's services use the intelligence they receive, or audits their operations in Gaza and the West Bank. The Journal reported that Israel certifies its own compliance with American conditions, "sometimes orally," and that officials found it hard to know how American intelligence was used once it was combined with Israel's own data. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] Representative Jason Crow wrote to the Director of National Intelligence in December 2023 asking for details, citing "some pretty big inconsistencies" between Israeli and American accounts of civilian harm. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing]

The levers that do exist are blunt. Reuters reported in December 2025 that, in the second half of 2024, the United States cut off a live drone feed over Gaza for at least a few days and restricted how Israel could use certain intelligence on high-value targets, because of concern over civilian deaths and over the Shin Bet's treatment of prisoners; sharing resumed after Israel gave assurances, and President Biden declined a more formal cutoff in his last weeks in office. [cite:ow3_reu_withheld] The Israeli military's response was: "The strategic intelligence cooperation continued throughout the war." [cite:ow3_reu_withheld] Reuters also reported that American intelligence gathered in 2024 indicated Israeli military lawyers had warned of evidence that could support war-crimes charges, and that government lawyers concluded it remained legal to keep supplying weapons and intelligence because the United States had not gathered its own evidence of violations. [cite:ow3_reu_lawyers] Israel strongly denies war crimes, and the State Department said: "We do not comment on intelligence matters." [cite:ow3_reu_lawyers]

The mechanism is therefore the reverse of the one Part Four documents for signals intelligence. There, American raw data goes to Israel under rules the United States wrote and Israel applies. Here, American visibility into what Israel does with the product depends on Israeli assurances, and the legal threshold for stopping the flow depends on evidence the United States has not, on the record, set out to collect itself. Both governments describe the relationship as one of mutual benefit, and the documented returns, from the Khrushchev speech to Natanz to the hostage negotiations, are real.

What the record does not settle. The text of the post-7 October memorandum has not been published. Whether the restrictions reported by Reuters remain in place, as of October 2026, is not publicly known. Sanger's account of Olympic Games has not been officially confirmed. And the public record does not show any congressional hearing devoted to the CIA–Israel liaison as such.
