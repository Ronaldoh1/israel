The Repurposing Test

Book Five showed that most of the inventory was built or bought for immigration enforcement. The worst-case reading needs it to be turned to another use: against critics. This section tests that step. A critic here is defined by what a person said or did. That includes critics of the US government and critics of any foreign government, since several of the cases on record concern speech about Israel's war in Gaza. The section does not ask whether the tools could be aimed at people because of their religion or ethnicity. That is a different question under different law, and none of the cases below was argued or decided on that footing.

The test separates two things the record keeps separate. One is a documented case, a named person or group against whom a tool was used, with a dated outcome. The other is programme-level use, a standing practice of turning the tools on critics as a class. The first exists. The second, as of October 2026, does not appear in any document, ruling or admission.

Non-citizens: speech as a ground

For people who are not citizens, speech became a formal factor in 2025. The test case was AAUP v. Rubio. At trial in July 2025, Peter Hatch of DHS's intelligence office testified that a "Tiger Team" of analysts had written more than a hundred reports on foreign students and academics. The names came from a list of about 5,000 compiled by Canary Mission, a privately run website that profiles campus activists. [cite:de28_crimson_hatch] On 30 September 2025 Judge William Young found the policy unconstitutional. On 22 January 2026 he vacated it and presumed retaliation in any adverse immigration action against the plaintiff groups' members. The government's appeal to the First Circuit had not been decided as of October 2026. [cite:de28_ch_aaup] [cite:de30_audit_aaup]

Two individual cases show the route in practice. Rumeysa Öztürk, a Turkish doctoral student at Tufts, was arrested on a street in Somerville, Massachusetts, in March 2025 after the State Department revoked her visa. The only basis that emerged was an opinion piece she had co-written in the student newspaper about her university's response to the war in Gaza. She spent 45 days in detention in Louisiana before a federal judge ordered her release, finding a substantial claim that she was being punished for her speech. On 29 January 2026 an immigration judge ended the removal case, finding that DHS had not proved her deportable. [cite:de34_an_ozturk] In April 2026 the government settled: it dropped the deportation effort and affirmed her lawful status, and she finished her doctorate and returned to Turkey. [cite:de34_wt_ozturk]

Mahmoud Khalil, a permanent resident and recent Columbia graduate who had negotiated for campus protesters, was arrested on 8 March 2025. On 11 April an immigration judge found him removable solely on the Secretary of State's determination that his presence "would have potentially serious adverse foreign policy consequences." A federal judge found that policy likely unconstitutional and released him in June 2025. In September an immigration judge ordered him deported on a separate ground, alleged omissions in his green-card application. [cite:de34_ccr_khalil] [cite:de34_tribune_khalil] On 15 January 2026 the Third Circuit, by two votes to one, held that the district court had lacked jurisdiction. Rehearing by the full court was denied on 22 May, and on 26 May the court stayed its mandate while Khalil asked the Supreme Court to hear the case. DHS said he should "self-deport now." [cite:de34_ccr_khalil] [cite:de34_tribune_khalil] [cite:de34_nfts_khalil]

Visa revocation is the quieter tool. By 18 August 2025 the State Department said it had revoked more than 6,000 student visas since January. About 4,000 were for violations of law such as assault or drunk driving, and an official put about 200 to 300 at "support for terrorism," without saying what conduct that covered beyond fundraising for Hamas. [cite:de34_ewn_6000] [cite:de34_fox_6000] On 14 October 2025 it revoked the visas of six foreigners over social-media comments on the assassination of Charlie Kirk, saying the country "has no obligation to host foreigners who wish death on Americans." [cite:de34_gn_kirkvisas] A consular cable of 18 June 2025 told officers to look for "hostile attitudes toward our citizens, culture, government, institutions, or founding principles" in student applicants' public posts. [cite:de28_reuters_visa]

Critics of a foreign government: sanctions

The sanctions tool reached furthest. Under the February 2025 order against the International Criminal Court, the government designated the court's prosecutor, judges and three Palestinian human-rights groups. On 9 July 2025 it designated Francesca Albanese, the UN's special rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territory, citing her calls for ICC warrants against Israel's prime minister and former defence minister. [cite:ow11_wiki_eo14203] [cite:de34_jurist_albanese] A designation freezes the person's assets in the United States and bars Americans from dealing with them. That bar reached Albanese's American-citizen daughter. A district judge blocked the sanctions on 15 May 2026 as likely to violate free speech; the D.C. Circuit stayed that order on 22 May, OFAC relisted her on 27 May, and an appeals order in June kept the sanctions in force while the appeal continues. [cite:ow11_jurist_albanese2026] [cite:ow11_jns_albanese_dc] [cite:de34_jurist_albanese]

Citizens: specific cases, each with an outcome

For citizens the record holds cases, not a programme, and most were checked. DHS used administrative subpoenas, demands for records issued without a judge, to try to unmask the people behind six Instagram accounts that had named a Border Patrol agent. A magistrate judge ordered Meta not to comply, and DHS withdrew. [cite:de28_int_meta] A Philadelphia-area man who emailed a DHS lawyer urging "common sense and decency" in an asylum case was the subject of a subpoena issued four hours later. Agents came to his home, and DHS withdrew the subpoena when the ACLU moved to quash it in February 2026. [cite:de28_aclu_doe] Nicole Cleland, a Minnesota citizen who had been observing agents, lost her Global Entry and PreCheck three days after a Border Patrol agent addressed her by name and said he had used face recognition. Her suit, filed in July 2026, is pending, and CBP denies retaliation. [cite:de25b_bu_cleland]

The Director of National Intelligence revoked the security clearances of at least 37 current and former officials in August 2025, citing "politicization or weaponization of intelligence"; the memo reportedly cited no specific evidence. [cite:de24b_kvia_gabbard] Executive orders against four law firms restricted their clearances and building access. Four district judges found them unconstitutional, and the government's appeal was argued in the D.C. Circuit on 14 May 2026. [cite:de24b_gma_lawfirms] After six members of Congress told service members in a video that they could refuse illegal orders, the Pentagon moved to discipline one of them, Senator Mark Kelly. Judge Richard Leon enjoined it in February 2026, finding that the Defense Secretary had "trampled on Senator Kelly's First Amendment freedoms." [cite:de32_npr_kelly] Officials pressed Apple and Facebook to remove tools that tracked immigration agents, pressure a federal judge found likely coercive. [cite:de26_reason_rosado]

What repurposing would legally need

The cases show where the law draws its lines. For non-citizens, immigration law itself supplies a route. Visas can be revoked, and a removal ground can be invoked on the Secretary of State's word, so the tools built for immigration can reach a critic without any new authority, subject to the courts. For citizens there is no removal and no visa, and the passport cannot be denied for belief: the Supreme Court closed that route in Kent v. Dulles in 1958. [cite:de25b_kent] To turn the stack on a citizen as a critic, the state would need one of four things.

The first is a criminal charge, which brings a grand jury, a trial jury and a judge. The terrorism convictions on record under the new directive followed a shooting at an ICE centre in July 2025; a grand jury reportedly declined to indict in the Kelly matter. [cite:de30_kera_prairieland] [cite:de32_npr_kelly] The second is a "terrorism" label. NSPM-7 lets the Attorney General recommend domestic groups for designation and names "anti-capitalism," "anti-Christianity" and opposition to immigration enforcement among its indicators. No statute provides for designating a domestic group, so the label carries investigative and reputational consequences, not the asset freezes and material-support liability that attach to foreign designations. [cite:de30_wh_nspm7] [cite:de30_bondi_memo] [cite:de30_dla_nspm7] The third is a new statute, and Congress has passed none of this kind. The fourth, and the cheapest, is a rule change: an internal procedure, a data-sharing notice or a vetting cable. Several of these were issued in 2025 and 2026, but for non-citizens or for eligibility checks, not for citizens' speech. [cite:de24b_bl_tanf] [cite:de28_reuters_visa]

If it were connected

The join this test points to is between the "terrorism" route and the integration layer. An entry on the Attorney General's list, which is not public, could be matched against the same people-search, travel and financial records ICE already reaches. Treasury and IRS investigators are already directed by NSPM-7 to trace funders. [cite:de34_int_list] [cite:de30_wh_nspm7] Grade: possible. The tools and the directive both exist, but no document shows list entries loaded into a case system, and an amicus brief's claims that DHS used the directive to monitor nonprofits are allegations no court has ruled on. [cite:de30_pd_amicus] Each documented case above went through a named official and a human decision, and most were reviewed by a judge.

The test therefore returns a split result. Turning immigration and foreign-affairs tools on non-citizen critics is documented: the policy existed, a court found it unconstitutional, and the visas and sanctions tools remain in use. Turning the stack on citizen critics is documented only case by case, and most of those cases were blocked, withdrawn or are still being litigated. A programme against critics as a class is not documented. [scene:de34_the_end_to_end_map|The next section] lays out the whole pipeline the worst case would need, link by link.
