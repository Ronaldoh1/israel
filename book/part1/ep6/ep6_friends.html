Friends Who Advised

Some of the work was done by friends rather than hired professionals, and in the files their help reads as affection.

Kathryn Ruemmler was White House counsel to President Obama until 2014. After she returned to private practice, Epstein gave her designer handbags and a fur coat; in a 2018 email she thanked "Uncle Jeffrey" for a gift, and elsewhere she described him as like an "older brother". She became general counsel of Goldman Sachs in 2020. In February 2026, after the emails were released, she announced her resignation, effective at the end of June; the bank said she "regrets ever knowing him", and she has since called him a "monster". [cite:ep6_ap_ruemmler2026]

The most ambitious rescue was planned in the last months before his arrest. Steve Bannon, the former White House chief strategist, recorded hours of interviews with Epstein for a documentary meant to rebuild his image; two hours of the footage are in the files, and Epstein's brother has said there were about fifteen. In April 2019 Bannon texted that he would "rebuild your image", and set out three steps: push back on the "lies", counter the "pedo/trafficking narrative", and recast Epstein as a philanthropist. He arranged "totally confidential" media training at Epstein's house, and Epstein told him his representatives had used a "script" from Bannon in dealing with The New Yorker. In June Epstein wrote that Trump would be anxious to know the two men were friends; Bannon replied, "Dangerous." He had planned to visit the house the day after the arrest in July. That night a text came from Epstein's number: "All canceled." [cite:ep6_nbc_bannon2026] Bannon has since called on his own programme for a special prosecutor to investigate an Epstein cover-up. He did not respond to requests for comment on the texts. [cite:ep6_nbc_bannon2026]

Larry Summers, the former Treasury Secretary and president of Harvard, stepped back from public commitments and took leave from teaching in November 2025 after his correspondence with Epstein was released. [cite:ep6_wbur_chomsky2025]

Who benefited

Set out together, the record answers the question this part began with. Epstein got back the access he had lost. The people who did the work were paid in fees, grants, gifts and introductions. The institutions got money, which some later gave away. The cost was carried by the women whose accounts were cut from a magazine, kept off television and buried in search results, at the moment when publication might have protected others.

The rebuild worked for ten years. It ended not because any of these institutions changed its mind, but because one newspaper went back to the records.
