Why It Is Rarely Reported, and the Whole Mirror

Each of the cases in this part was reported, and most by major American outlets: Pollard's case widely, the NSA documents by The Guardian and The Intercept, the Iran-talks operation by The Wall Street Journal, the Washington devices by Politico, the Pegasus hacks by Reuters. [cite:ow4_alternet_guardian] [cite:ow4_statewatch2015] [cite:ow5_wiki_esp] [cite:ow10_reu_state] The common complaint that the news "doesn't report it" is, on the record, inexact. What is rare is the second story: official confirmation, a public consequence, or a follow-up that settles what happened. The reasons are documented, and they are mostly structural rather than editorial.

The documents are classified, and released late or never. The CIA's damage assessment in the Pollard case was written in 1987 and released only in 2012, after an appeal to the Interagency Security Classification Appeals Panel. [cite:ow3_nsa_pollard] The CIA has never released its internal operational documents on NUMEC, more than half a century after the uranium went missing. [cite:ow3_nsa_numec] The terms of American intelligence-sharing agreements are not published at all; what the public knows of the 2009 NSA memorandum comes from a leak. [cite:ow4_aclu] [cite:ow4_alternet_guardian] A reporter can publish what a leaked document says; without the government's confirmation, the story stops there.

The sources are anonymous, and the answers are denials. The reports of Israeli operations after Pollard rest on unnamed officials: three former officials in Politico's 2019 report, and unnamed officials again in the 2026 reports on the Defense Intelligence Agency. [cite:ow5_wiki_esp] The Israeli government's response has been categorical denial, "a blatant lie" in 2019, "does not conduct espionage against the United States" in 2015, and the American government's response has usually been silence: in 2019, according to Politico's sources, there was no public rebuke. [cite:ow5_wiki_esp] [cite:ow_td_spying2015] When the government that was targeted will not confirm, the reporting stays at the level of allegation, and the record in this book has to mark it so.

The courts open some doors and close others. One of the few public records of an Israeli spyware company's conduct was made in an American courtroom, in WhatsApp's suit against NSO, where a jury found against the company and awarded damages. [cite:ow10_axios_jury] The same litigation shows the limit: Apple abandoned its parallel suit in 2024, saying it might never obtain NSO's most important files, and citing a report that Israeli officials had taken files from NSO's offices and sought secrecy for the step, which Israeli officials denied was interference. [cite:ow10_stripes_apple]

The ground is closed. In Gaza the obstacle is physical. Since October 2023 foreign reporters have entered Gaza only on visits escorted by the Israeli army. [cite:pr_wlrn_ban_full] These conditions, and the debate over how American newsrooms covered the war, are documented in [scene:pr8_gate|The Gate], [scene:gz12_access|Who Could Report], [scene:gz12_coverage|Inside American Newsrooms] and [scene:pr11_america|How the Dead Were Reported]. Studies of that coverage reached opposite conclusions, and both belong in the record.

An interpretation, labelled as such. This volume's reading of the evidence is that the scarcity of follow-up reflects the four conditions above more than any decision by editors: a subject made of classified documents, anonymous sources, denials, and territory closed to independent reporters will produce first reports and few second ones. The record used here does not document an instruction to any American newsroom to avoid the subject, and this book does not assert one.

The finding. Read across the table, the one-way claim survives in a precise form. The relationship is not one-way in what moves: intelligence, technology and money flow in both directions, and the United States watches its partner as its partner watches it. [cite:ow4_aj_wsj2015] [cite:ow_td_spying2015] It is one-way in verification. For every major category of what the United States sends, the record shows American officials unable to see, or not permitted to check, how it is used once it arrives, and relying instead on Israeli assurances. [cite:ow3_wsj_sharing] [cite:ow4_axios_nsm20] [cite:ow11_js_blaha] That is the mirror the title names: the United States can see clearly what it gives, and the glass darkens at the point of use.
