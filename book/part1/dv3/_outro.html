Part Three set out to find the Vatican in the making of Israel, and found that the word, like London, names several things that must be kept apart.

Vatican City is a real sovereign state of an unusual kind: made by treaty with Mussolini in 1929, ruled by one man who holds every power, staffed by citizens who belong to it only while they hold office, and dependent on Italy for its water, its prisons, the police in its main square and the minting of its coins. [cite:dv3_lateran_text] [cite:dv3_vn_fundlaw_2023] [cite:dv3_wiki_vatican_city] It differs from an ordinary city in both directions at once: no city has its sovereignty, and almost no city of its size depends so completely on its neighbour. The Holy See is the government that matters, a church administration of sixteen departments with relations with 184 states and an operating budget of about €1.2 billion. [cite:dv3_cwr_pe] [cite:dv3_aleteia_dipl_2026] [cite:dv3_america_budget_2024] Its money history runs from Rothschild loans in the 1830s, through the Lateran settlement and the Ambrosiano scandal, to a bank whose new president, appointed in 2026, once led the Edmond de Rothschild group; each link is documented, and none carries money from the Vatican to the Zionist movement. On Israel itself, the Holy See's record is one of refusal from 1904, recognition in 1993, and two parallel relationships since 2015. The claim that the refusal was a front does not survive the private documents, which say what the public ones said. [cite:dv3_jvl_vatican] [cite:dv3_dawn_irish_files] The theology that made Christians in Britain and America into Israel's supporters was Protestant in its immediate form and lived in London and Washington, but its root was the Vatican's: the church it grew from, the authority whose rejection set it loose, and the voice that never publicly opposed it. Under the wider definition this volume adopts, that makes the Vatican the root parent of the idea, while it remained an opponent of the state in every material act. Part Four turns to the second of those cities: a federal district governed by a legislature its residents cannot vote for, which became the military and diplomatic guarantor of the state the Vatican refused to bless.
