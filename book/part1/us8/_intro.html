Part Eight asks how each state treats its own citizens when they differ by origin, religion, sex, sexuality or disability. On the Israeli side it measures what the record shows for Arab citizens, Haredi Jews, Mizrahi Jews and Ethiopian Israelis: the law that frames their status, from the 1948 declaration to the 2018 Nation-State Law, the government plans meant to close the gaps, and poverty, employment, education and health over time. On the American side it follows the Black–white gaps in poverty, income, wealth and mobility from the 1960s civil-rights laws to the Supreme Court and executive decisions of 2013–2026 that narrowed them. A third section sets the two countries side by side on women's pay, LGBTQ rights and disability law. Palestinians under Israeli control who are not citizens are the subject of Part Nine.
