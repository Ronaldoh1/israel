# Writing "The Lines You've Heard" entries

Each entry answers one line people repeat online. The reader sees: the line, a verdict, your brief, and a "Read more" link to the volume that holds the full record (added automatically). Write like a calm, authoritative historian talking to someone who just saw the line in a comment section.

For each claim in your job file's themes, write `/home/claude/israel/book/lines/entries/<claim id>.html`:
- 1 or 2 short paragraphs (`<p>`), **90–170 words total**.
- First sentence: what the record actually shows, stated plainly. Then the one or two facts that matter most (a date, a number, a document, a named source). If the record shows a real kernel of truth in the line, say so — the book holds every claim to the same standard.
- Match the claim's verdict; if your source shows the verdict is wrong, write what the source supports and note it in your reply.
- Keep `[cite:key]` markers from the source on the sentences they support (only keys from your job file).
- **No new facts**: use only the scenes in your job file. Keep numbers, names and dates exactly as the source has them. Keep hedges for disputed points.
- No internal words (tier, confirmed, entity, scene, simulation, module). Never generalize about a people or religious group.
- Allowed tags: `<p>`, `<em>`, `<strong>`.

Reply with one line: files written, plus any verdict you think should change and why.
