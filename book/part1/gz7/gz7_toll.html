The Dead, the Ruins and the Famine

The count. As of 12 October 2026, the Gaza health ministry counted 74,382 Palestinians killed since October 2023. [cite:gz_aj_year2026] Its figures do not distinguish civilians from fighters, and they leave out people missing under the rubble and those who died of starvation or disease. For more than two years Israel cast doubt on them; Haaretz later reported that the Israeli army had accepted the ministry's estimate of about 71,000 dead. Leaked Israeli intelligence figures, published by the Guardian and +972 Magazine in August 2025, indicated that at least 83 percent of those killed were civilians. [cite:gz_yahoo_idf71k]

The ministry's count is also likely to be low. A study in The Lancet by researchers at the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine and Yale, using three independent sources of names, estimated 64,260 deaths from traumatic injury between October 2023 and the end of June 2024, when the ministry had counted 37,877: an undercount of about 41 percent. It found that 59 percent of the dead were women, children or people over sixty-five, and it did not estimate how many were fighters. [cite:gz_aj_lancet]

The ruins. The UN's satellite centre, examining images taken on 11 October 2025, the day after the ceasefire began, identified 123,464 structures destroyed, 17,116 severely damaged and 33,857 moderately damaged, and estimated that about 81 percent of all structures in the Gaza Strip had been damaged. [cite:gz_unosat2025]

Aid. From March 2025 Israel blocked aid, saying that Hamas was stealing what the UN delivered. In late May it was replaced in part by the Gaza Humanitarian Foundation, backed by Israel and the United States, which handed out food at its own distribution sites. The UN human rights office recorded more than 2,146 people killed near those sites and along aid convoy routes by early September. The foundation said it was the only operation that "reliably and safely" fed Gazans, and that it distributed more than three million boxes of food; it closed its sites in November 2025, after the ceasefire. [cite:gz_aj_ghf]

Famine. On 22 August 2025 the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, the UN-backed system that defines famine, found that "famine is now occurring in Gaza City and its surrounding area," with more than half a million people in catastrophic conditions, and projected that it would spread south by the end of September. It named its causes as "conflict, displacement, restricted access, and food system collapse." Israel's foreign ministry said that "there was no famine in Gaza." [cite:gz_aj_ipc]
