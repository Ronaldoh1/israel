For a generation after 1948, the American promise that more productive work brings higher pay held closely for the typical worker. This part dates the turn. It measures the split between productivity and the typical worker's pay that opened in the 1970s, sets the Economic Policy Institute's chart beside the economists who argue much of the gap is a matter of measurement, and shows what survives that dispute. It then follows two changes that can be dated to the years between 1973 and 1986: the fall of private-sector union power, from the air traffic controllers' strike of 1981 to the labour board of 2026, and the shareholder turn, from Milton Friedman's 1970 essay and the SEC's 1982 buyback rule to record buybacks, chief executives' pay and the tax and interest-rate decisions of the Reagan and Volcker years.
