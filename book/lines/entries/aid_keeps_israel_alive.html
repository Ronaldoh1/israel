Israel's own spending dwarfs the US aid it receives. Under the current agreement the US provides $3.8B a year from 2019 to 2028 [cite:washington_inst_mou], while Israel spent an estimated $52 billion on its own defense in 2025 and budgeted roughly $49 billion for 2026, about 13 to 14 times the aid [cite:israelpolicyforum_aid]. Israel's own INSS puts the aid at about 0.6% of GDP and says it could be absorbed, and Israel earned $14.8B from arms exports in 2024, roughly three times the aid [cite:inss_us_aid] [cite:sipri_arms].

Supporters of the aid say much the same. Israel Policy Forum, which backs continued cooperation, says Israel was "clearly capable" of covering regular peacetime defense costs without it before October 2023, and Prime Minister Netanyahu has proposed phasing the aid out [cite:israelpolicyforum_aid] [cite:fdd_netanyahu_aid]. One caveat: wartime spending has run higher, and Israel's 2026 defense increase came alongside cuts to education, health and welfare.
