The turn is documented, and its size depends on how it is measured. On the Economic Policy Institute's September 2026 edition, net productivity rose 93.7 per cent from late 1979 to mid-2026 while the typical worker's hourly compensation rose 32.7 per cent [cite:ad4_epi_gap2026]; critics who match price indexes and count all workers find average compensation kept far closer pace [cite:ad4_nber_feldstein] [cite:ad4_heritage_sherk], and the Bureau of Labor Statistics found that in the median industry about half the gap reflects price indexes and half a real fall in labour's share [cite:ad4_bls_btn2017]. Private-sector union membership fell from 16.8 per cent in 1983 to 5.9 per cent in 2025 [cite:ad4_bls_spotlight2016] [cite:us13_bls_union2025], large strikes became rare after 1981 [cite:ad4_bls_stoppages], and a labour law without fines was enforced by a board whose independence was in doubt by 2026 [cite:ad4_epi_unlawful] [cite:ad4_proskauer_slaughter]. Meanwhile the SEC's 1982 safe harbor eased buybacks that passed $1 trillion a year in 2025 [cite:ad4_sec_10b18_2003] [cite:ad4_spdji_q3_2025], and chief executives' pay rose from about 31 to about 325 times a typical worker's [cite:ad4_epi_ceo2025].
