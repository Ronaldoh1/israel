From Lobby to Electoral Spender, 2021–2026

For most of its history AIPAC gave no money to candidates. Pro-Israel campaign money existed, but it flowed through separate political action committees and individual donors, and AIPAC itself lobbied. That changed in December 2021, and the change is the single largest shift in how the votes described in this part are contested. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2022] This section sets out the documented spending by cycle, with the date and source of each figure, and then the strongest evidence on both sides of the question of what the money achieves. The detailed accounts are in the Follow the Money volume: [scene:pm3_after_2021|the money after 2021] and the race-by-race record in [scene:pm5_2022|2022], [scene:pm5_2024|2024] and [scene:pm5_2026|2026].

The two committees. AIPAC announced in December 2021 that it would form a political action committee and a super PAC. AIPAC PAC was created on 15 December 2021 and the United Democracy Project, the super PAC, on 3 January 2022. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2022] The two do different legal work. The PAC collects contributions from AIPAC's members and passes them, almost all earmarked, to endorsed candidates within federal contribution limits. The super PAC may raise unlimited sums and spend them on advertising for or against candidates, provided it does not coordinate with campaigns. [cite:pm_sludge_pac2026] [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2022] UDP's spokesman said its aim was "to build the broadest bipartisan pro-Israel coalition in Congress possible." [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2022]

The figures differ because they count different things: whether transfers to other committees and running costs are included, and when the filings were read. [cite:pm_sludge_final2024] [cite:pm_israelite_2024] None of the 2026 numbers is final; the general election of November 2026 and the reports that follow it will change them.

Where the money went. In the 2022 cycle every dollar of UDP's independent spending went into Democratic primaries; its largest donors were AIPAC itself ($8.5 million), Jan Koum ($2 million) and four donors at $1 million each. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2022] In 2024 the super PAC spent about $14.5 million against Representative Jamaal Bowman of New York and, by later counts including support for his opponent, about $8.6 million in the race that unseated Representative Cori Bush of Missouri. [cite:factcheck_udp_2024] [cite:rollcall_bush_primary_2024] AIPAC's own account of 2024 says its PAC endorsed 361 candidates, of whom 326 won their general elections, 120 Democrats and 206 Republicans, and that its endorsements covered 75 per cent of House members and 84 per cent of senators. [cite:pm_aipac_memo_2024] In 2026 the super PAC began routing more than 40 per cent of its primary spending through committees with names unconnected to Israel, according to Politico as reported by FactCheck.org, spent more than $30.6 million in Michigan's Democratic Senate primary, and lost it. [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026] It spent about $4.1 million against Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, one of six members who voted in July 2025 to strip $500 million in missile-defence funding for Israel from the defence bill; [cite:pm_ji_mtg_amendment] Massie lost his primary on 19 May 2026, in a race in which a super PAC aligned with President Trump also spent heavily against him. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] That race is told in [scene:pm5_massie|the Massie primary].

Travel. A further channel bears directly on how members of Congress learn about Israel. AIPAC's affiliated charity, the American Israel Education Foundation, sponsored about 75 per cent of the privately funded trips by House members and staff to Israel since 2012, according to the Howard Center for Investigative Journalism's analysis of House disclosures; more than a quarter of all privately sponsored foreign trips by House members and staff went to Israel. [cite:cnsmd_trips_study] The trips are permitted under the congressional rules on privately sponsored travel, [cite:pm_citizen_travel] and are examined in [scene:pm7_congress|the trips].

What the money achieves: the evidence on both sides. That pro-Israel committees spend heavily, and that most members of Congress who vote for aid to Israel have received pro-Israel money, is documented. Whether the money causes the votes is a different claim, and the strongest evidence cuts both ways. A Guardian analysis found that pro-Israel donors had given more than $58 million to sitting members in their most recent elections, and that members who received more were more likely to back military support for Israel; members supportive of Israel after 7 October 2023 had received about $125,000 on average, against about $18,000 for members who spoke for Palestinians. The same analysis found that party, not money, best predicted members' statements on civilian casualties, and that the data could not show that money had changed anyone's position. [cite:pm_guardian_2024] An analysis by Dany Bahar, published as an opinion piece in The Forward in June 2026, examined 788 candidates backed by AIPAC's PAC in 2022 and 2024 and found that about 86 per cent were incumbents, who win about 95 per cent of the time whoever funds them; in tight primaries AIPAC's candidates won about 79 per cent, against 84 per cent for the NRA, 83 per cent for Planned Parenthood and 88 per cent for the Sierra Club. He concluded that AIPAC's results "do not outperform those of any other major single-issue lobby." [cite:ow2_bahar_forward] Against that, the defeats of Bowman, Bush and Massie are documented cases in which the super PAC was the largest outside spender against a sitting member who had opposed Israel policy, and each lost. [cite:rollcall_bush_primary_2024] [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding]

The record therefore supports two findings and not a third. It shows that the money is large and aimed, and that in a small number of primaries it was the largest single outside force. It shows that most of the money goes to incumbents who already vote with it. [cite:ow2_bahar_forward] [cite:pm_aipac_memo_2024] It does not show, for any named member, that a donation changed a vote; no member's own statement reached for this volume says so, and this volume does not attribute motives that the record does not contain.
