The 2026 War

The ceasefire did not collapse over Lebanon. It collapsed over Iran. On 28 February 2026 the United States and Israel opened a joint air campaign against Iran; Israel described its strikes as pre-emptive, and President Donald Trump announced American combat operations. Within hours Trump wrote that Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, Iran's supreme leader, was dead, and Iranian state television confirmed it early the next day. [cite:hz8_am_khamenei] Khamenei was killed in those opening strikes of 28 February, which were joint American and Israeli operations; his son Mojtaba was named his successor on 8 March. [cite:hz8_wiki_iranwar] The wider war, its causes and its costs belong to [scene:ow7_iran|the account of American forces in that war] and to [scene:hz12_wars|Part Twelve]; this section follows the Lebanese front.

How it started

On 2 March Hezbollah launched missiles and drones at Israel, its first fire since the strikes on Iran began and its first acknowledged attack on Israel since December 2024. Its statement tied the attack to Khamenei's killing and to fifteen months of Israeli strikes in Lebanon, saying Israel's continued attacks and assassinations gave it "the right to defend ourselves and respond at the appropriate time and place." The Israeli army began striking targets across Lebanon, including Beirut's southern suburbs, and said it "will not enable the organization to constitute a threat to the State of Israel." On 28 February Lebanon's presidency had said the American ambassador assured it Israel would not escalate against Lebanon so long as there were no hostile acts from the Lebanese side. [cite:hz8_am_mar2]

The Lebanese government's response was without precedent. On the afternoon of 2 March the cabinet banned all "military and security actions" by Hezbollah and ordered the army to "immediately and firmly begin implementing" its disarmament plan north of the Litani, using "all necessary means." Salam announced the "absolute rejection, without ambiguity or possible interpretation, of any military or security action" from Lebanese territory outside state institutions. Hezbollah's two ministers objected to the text but did not resign; "We expressed our opposition to the text, and that's all," said one of them, Mohammad Haidar. [cite:hz8_olj_ban] Hezbollah thus went to war against Israel in defiance of a decision of the government it sat in, and Israel went to war against Hezbollah in a country whose government had just outlawed Hezbollah's military activity.

Israel's campaign

On 16 March troops of the Israeli army's 91st Division began what it called "limited and targeted ground operations against key Hezbollah strongholds in southern Lebanon." An army spokesman said Hezbollah was "firing hundreds of rockets a day" at Israel. Defence Minister Israel Katz said displaced Lebanese would not be allowed back "south of the Litani area until the safety of residents in the north (of Israel) is guaranteed." Lebanese authorities then counted about 850 dead and more than 830,000 people registered as displaced. [cite:hz8b_afp_ground] By 24 March, AFP reported, Israeli strikes had killed at least 1,039 people in Lebanon and displaced more than a million. [cite:hz8_am_mar24] On 8 April, the day a ceasefire between the United States and Iran took effect, Israel struck what it said were more than 100 Hezbollah command centres and military sites in Beirut, the Bekaa and the south, its heaviest strikes of the war. "The battle in Lebanon continues, and the ceasefire does not include Lebanon," the army said. Three Lebanese sources told Reuters that Hezbollah had stopped attacking Israeli targets that morning. [cite:hz8b_reu_apr8] Reuters reported dozens killed that day and hundreds wounded. [cite:hz8b_reu_apr8]

Israel's stated aim throughout was to remove Hezbollah's ability to threaten northern Israel. Hezbollah's stated aim was to answer the killing of Khamenei and the strikes of the truce years. By 31 May Netanyahu claimed that 3,000 Hezbollah fighters had been killed; Hezbollah did not publish figures of its own, and the claim is Israel's alone. [cite:hz8b_ap_beaufort] On Israel's side, the army reported 32 soldiers and four civilians killed by 19 June. [cite:hz8b_olj_jun19] By September Israel put its losses to Hezbollah attacks at 39 soldiers, one military contractor and two civilians, and UNIFIL said seven of its peacekeepers had been killed during the year. [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] The two Israeli counts do not match on civilians; each is given as reported.

The toll, by date

Lebanon's health ministry counts all deaths from Israeli attacks, without separating civilians from fighters, and calls its figures provisional until rubble is cleared and bodies identified. For 2 March to midnight on 16 April it reported 2,294 killed and 7,544 injured, including 100 health and rescue workers killed. [cite:hz8_olj_toll] The government's disaster-management unit raised the toll to 2,454 dead and 7,658 wounded on 21 April. [cite:hz8_aawsat_2454] By 2 May the ministry's count was 2,659 killed and 8,183 injured, with 41 dead in the previous 24 hours and more than one million people registered as displaced. [cite:hz8_aj_may2] It reached 3,350 by 31 May, 3,912 by 19 June when the fighting largely stopped, and 4,246 by 27 June. [cite:hz8b_ap_beaufort] [cite:hz8b_olj_jun19] [cite:hz8b_aj_reject] On 11 October 2026 the ministry's emergency operations centre gave 4,387 killed and 12,424 injured since 2 March. The ministry's larger headline figure, 8,954 killed and 30,660 injured, counts from 8 October 2023 and so includes the 2023–24 war and the truce months; it should not be quoted as the toll of the 2026 war. [cite:hz8b_qna_toll] Totals above 4,000 that are sometimes given for the period before the April truce do not match the ministry's own dated counts. The Lebanese government put the number displaced at more than 1.2 million. [cite:hz8b_npr_lines]

A truce with shooting

A ten-day truce, on terms released by the US State Department, took effect on the night of 16 April, after 46 days of bombardment and ground fighting. Israel kept its forces in place behind what its officers called a "Yellow Line," modelled on the one in Gaza, about 10 kilometres deep, and barred residents from returning to 55 Lebanese towns and villages inside it. Netanyahu said the army was staying in a "reinforced security buffer zone": "we are not leaving." Lebanon and Hezbollah called the zone an occupation; Hezbollah called the truce "an insult to our country" and the government's parallel talks with Israel a "humiliation." [cite:hz8b_aj_yellow] It did not stop the strikes. Lebanon's government counted 3,491 Israeli air strikes, 407 controlled demolitions and six operations that razed villages between 17 April and 7 June, while Hezbollah kept firing at northern Israel and Israel answered with strikes on Beirut's suburbs. Trump said that in the Middle East ceasefires involve "shooting in a more moderate manner." [cite:hz8_an_jun8]

Talks went on alongside the fighting: on 29 May Israeli and Lebanese military officers met at the Pentagon for the first time in decades. [cite:hz8b_nn_pentagon] On 31 May Israeli troops crossed the Litani and captured Beaufort castle near Nabatieh, which Israel had held from 1982 to 2000, the army's deepest push into Lebanon in more than 25 years. Netanyahu called it "a dramatic shift in the policies we are leading"; Katz said Israel would hold the castle while troops destroyed homes and other sites he said Hezbollah used. Israel declared the land between the Litani and the Zahrani a combat zone. [cite:hz8b_ap_beaufort]

The war on this front largely stopped on 19 June. A US official announced an Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire from 4 p.m. that day, brokered by American and Qatari negotiators with Iran's help, under an interim US–Iran deal that called for military operations to end on all fronts, Lebanon included. Israeli forces were to stay in the south, and Israel kept the right to act against emerging threats. An Israeli official said: "If Hezbollah does not attack us, then for us it is not a time of war." [cite:hz8b_olj_jun19] Hezbollah has not answered Israeli strikes since mid-June, though the strikes have continued. [cite:hz8b_olj_qassem]

On 26 June the Israeli and Lebanese ambassadors in Washington signed a US-brokered framework agreement at the State Department. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said it "establishes a clear process" to disarm Hezbollah and dismantle its infrastructure, and that it would let Israel return to its borders once the threat to its citizens was removed; it also created a trilateral military coordination group run by the United States. [cite:hz8b_almon_framework] It set out a path toward ending the state of war and a full peace agreement, but, Al Jazeera's correspondent noted, "the word withdrawal is not in [the] text." Qassem called it "humiliating, shameful and a surrender of sovereignty" and rejected tying Israel's withdrawal to Hezbollah's disarmament; a Hezbollah MP, Hassan Fadlallah, warned that any attempt by the army to enforce it would lead to "civil war." Katz said he and Netanyahu had ordered forces to prepare for an "extended stay." [cite:hz8b_aj_reject]

The agreement's mechanism was a set of "pilot zones": small areas from which Israel would withdraw and which the Lebanese army would take over, with US Central Command coordinating. No timetable for wider withdrawal was set, and Israeli officials said their forces would stay in a 10-kilometre security zone for as long as Hezbollah stayed armed. [cite:hz8b_afp_pilot] Israel left the first village, Zawtar al-Gharbieh, at the end of July. [cite:hz8b_npr_lines] Aoun met Trump in Washington on 21 July; Rubio told him Lebanon would "never be fully at peace" until the Hezbollah question was settled. [cite:hz8b_ap_aoun] A seventh round of talks, in Rome on 4 and 5 August, ended split over disarmament: Hezbollah, which is not a party to the talks, said it would pull back south of the Litani but would not disarm north of it. [cite:hz8b_aj_rome]
