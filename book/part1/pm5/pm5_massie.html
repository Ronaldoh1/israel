Thomas Massie: The Republican Primary

Until 2026 the super PAC's largest campaigns had all been fought inside the Democratic Party. In May of that year it took part in the most expensive House primary in American history, and the target was a Republican. Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky's 4th District had taken no money from pro-Israel PACs, had voted against foreign-aid packages including aid to Israel, and had criticized the lobby's influence openly. [cite:trackaipac_congress] [cite:mee_massie_aipac] He lost his primary on 19 May 2026 to Ed Gallrein, by 54.9 percent to 45.1. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] This section sets out the money, the other force in the race, and the words of both sides, because the race is cited as proof of two different things, and the record supports parts of each.

The money. Louisville Public Media's tally of all filings put the race's total cost at about $37 million, with about $32.6 million of that in advertising according to the tracker AdImpact, a record for any House primary. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] [cite:mee_massie_aipac] Pro-Israel committees provided a large share. By Louisville Public Media's count AIPAC's super PAC spent $4.1 million against Massie and the Republican Jewish Coalition's super PAC, the RJC Victory Fund, $3.9 million, about $8 million in all; FactCheck.org, counting at a different date, put the AIPAC super PAC's spending at over $3 million against Massie and nearly $500,000 for Gallrein, and Politico counted more than $9 million from pro-Israel groups. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] By AdImpact's measure pro-Israel groups bought about half of the advertising that benefited Gallrein. [cite:mee_massie_aipac]

The other force. The pro-Israel committees were not alone, and on most accounts they were not the decisive force. President Trump recruited Gallrein and endorsed him in October 2025 against Massie, who had crossed him on several fronts: on the release of the Justice Department's files on Jeffrey Epstein, on the American strikes on Iran, and on the 2025 tax-and-spending law. [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways] [cite:wapo_massie_loses] A super PAC aligned with the president, MAGA KY, spent about $7.4 million. [cite:lpm_massie_gallrein_funding] The Associated Press judged Trump's opposition the key factor in the result, and PBS described the outcome as leaving "no doubt" about the president's power over his party. [cite:pbs_massie_takeaways] On these accounts, Massie faced a serious challenge with or without the pro-Israel spending.

What each side said. Both sides described the race in terms of Israel. Before the vote Massie said that a "foreign lobby has fully funded" the effort against him "to the extent that they've never done in any Republican race ever before." [cite:mee_massie_aipac] The super PAC's spokesman called him "the most anti-Israel Republican in the House," and after the result AIPAC congratulated Gallrein on "defeating anti-Israel incumbent Thomas Massie." [cite:pm_factcheck_udp_2026] [cite:mee_massie_aipac] The pro-Israel committees, in other words, claimed the result as theirs, and the president's allies claimed it as his. Both claims rest on real spending; neither can be measured against the other from the filings.

What the race shows. Three things can be said from the record. First, the pro-Israel committees spent more against Massie than in any previous Republican race, and their spending was a large share of the record total. Second, their spending converged with the opposition of the most powerful figure in Republican politics, and the sources used here attribute the result chiefly to him. Third, the convergence was itself the pattern: a member who opposed both the president and the lobby's priorities faced both at once. Comparing Massie with a colleague makes the point concrete. Senator Ted Cruz of Texas, also a Republican, has described himself as Israel's "strongest defender" in the Senate, has received about $1.4 million from pro-Israel PACs and donors over his career, and has faced no comparable campaign. [cite:cruz_senate_ami] [cite:trackaipac_congress] The records differ chiefly in position. [scene:cm_s09|The simulation's comparison of Cruz, Omar and Massie] sets the three records side by side.

The race is cited in two directions: as proof that opposing the lobby is fatal even for a Republican, and as proof that the lobby had become, for the first time, an open issue in a Republican primary. That argument, and the members of both parties who have chosen to refuse the money, are the subject of Part Six.
