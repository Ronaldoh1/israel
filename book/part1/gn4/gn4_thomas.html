The Gospel of Thomas and the Kingdom Within

The Gospel of Thomas has no story. It has no birth, no miracles, no trial and no resurrection. It is a list of 114 sayings, most beginning "Jesus said", introduced by a single line: "These are the secret sayings which the living Jesus spoke". [cite:vt25_thomas_lambdin] The first saying explains what the reader is meant to do with them: whoever finds their interpretation "will not experience death". [cite:vt24_gthom] Salvation, in this gospel, is the work of understanding.

Its most famous saying is the third. Jesus warns against leaders who say the kingdom is in the sky, for then the birds would get there first, or in the sea, for then the fish would. Instead, "the Kingdom is inside of you, and it is outside of you". [cite:vt25_thomas_lambdin] [cite:vt24_gthom] The thought returns at the end of the book, when the disciples ask when the kingdom will come and Jesus answers that it "is spread out upon the earth, and men do not see it". [cite:vt25_thomas_lambdin] The kingdom is not a future event to be announced by authorities. It is present, and the task is to see it.

That idea is not confined to Thomas, and the way it reached modern readers is itself a story about who controls a text. The Gospel of Luke, in the canon, has Jesus say that the kingdom of God is entos hymōn. Many modern English Bibles translate the phrase as "among you" or "in your midst", which makes the kingdom a social presence. The classicist Ilaria Ramelli argued in 2009 that "among" is practically unattested for entos, that the Latin Vulgate rendered it intra vos, "within you", that the Syriac Peshitta has "inside", and that Origen, Athanasius, Basil and Gregory of Nyssa all read it as "inside you". She cites Thomas's third and 113th sayings as parallels. [cite:vt24_ramelli] The kingdom within is in the canonical gospels too, if the translation lets it through.

Thomas also sets aside the outward practices that the bishops' churches were organising. "If you fast, you will give rise to sin for yourselves", says the fourteenth saying, and prayer and almsgiving are treated the same way; true circumcision, says the fifty-third, is in spirit. [cite:vt24_gthom] [cite:vt25_thomas_lambdin] The last saying is the most troubling to modern readers. Peter says, "Let Mary leave us, for women are not worthy of life", and Jesus answers that he will lead her to make her male, so that she too may become a living spirit. [cite:vt25_thomas_lambdin] The saying shows that the gospel was not a manifesto for equality in the modern sense, and also that the question of women's place among the disciples was already being argued.

When Thomas was written is one of the most disputed questions in the field. Three Greek fragments from Oxyrhynchus in Egypt date from shortly after 200 to the middle of the third century, and the complete Coptic copy from shortly before 350. [cite:vt25_ecw_thomas] Stephen Patterson has argued for a date around 70 to 80, as early as the canonical gospels; Mark Goodacre argues that Thomas knew the canonical Synoptic gospels and dates its final form to around 135, an argument one reviewer found methodologically subjective. [cite:vt25_ecw_thomas] [cite:vt25_goodacre_thomas] The question matters because an early Thomas would be an independent witness to the sayings of Jesus.

The church writers who mention Thomas show how it was handled. Hippolytus, writing between 222 and 235, says that a group he calls the Naassenes cited "the Gospel according to Thomas", and calls them impious. [cite:vt25_hipp_ref5] Cyril of Jerusalem, a century later, told candidates for baptism that "the Manichaeans also wrote a Gospel according to Thomas", and added the rule that books not read in the churches should not be read privately either. [cite:vt25_cyril_cat4] That rule is the whole mechanism of the canon in a sentence. The bishops' churches decided what was read aloud, and the faithful were told not to read anything else at home. A gospel whose message was that the kingdom is within and must be found by each reader's own interpretation was exactly the kind of book that rule was written to exclude.
