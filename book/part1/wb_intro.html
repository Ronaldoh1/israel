This volume follows the West Bank, the hill country west of the Jordan River, about 5,655 square kilometres, from the partition plan of 1947 to October 2026. It asks what happened to the land and to the people on it: who governed them, how the land passed from Palestinian to Israeli control acre by acre, how movement, law and surveillance were organized around that change, and what the law says about it and why it has not been enforced.

It begins with the plan that assigned this hill country to an Arab state that was never created, and with the war of 1948 that left it in Jordan's hands. It follows nineteen years of Jordanian rule; the occupation of 1967 and the first settlements; the settlement enterprise that grew from them; the map drawn at Oslo, which divided the territory into Areas A, B and C; the barrier, the roads and the checkpoints that divided the Palestinian towns from one another; the two legal systems and the surveillance that govern daily life; the demolitions, the evictions and the settler violence through which communities have been displaced; and the years since October 2023. Four parts then step back: to the law and the reasons it has not been enforced; to the split between Fatah's West Bank and Hamas's Gaza; to the money; and to what is settled, contested and open.

Israel's stated reasons, security above all, are given beside each policy, and where the record divides, both sides are given. It describes the acts of governments, armies, settler organizations and armed groups, not the character of peoples. Its companion is Gaza, Documented, and the two volumes are written to be read together.
