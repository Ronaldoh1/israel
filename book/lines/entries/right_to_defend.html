Both parts of the line have a real basis, and the law attaches conditions to the second. The October 7 attack, in which Hamas killed 1,195 people, 828 of them civilians, and took 251 hostages, was a crime. [cite:wiki_october7_attacks] Article 51 of the UN Charter recognizes every member state's "inherent right" of self-defense. In 2004, however, the International Court of Justice advised that Article 51 did not justify Israel's separation wall, because the threat came from territory Israel itself occupies. That opinion was non-binding and not unanimous. [cite:icj_wall_op] Israel's defenders argue that Gaza has not been occupied since 2005 and that the right applies against non-state groups like Hamas. [cite:justia_selfdef]

Both sides agree on one thing: the laws of war, including distinction, proportionality and precaution, bind every party, Hamas included. Whether specific Israeli operations met that standard is disputed. Israeli military lawyers say they did; B'Tselem and international law scholars have documented operations they assess as violations.
