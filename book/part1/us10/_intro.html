Part Ten compares how each state watches the people under its authority and what limits its law sets. On the Israeli side it follows the Shin Bet's standing access to telephone data, the use of that access to track the population during the pandemic and the court rulings that narrowed it, the police Pegasus affair of 2022, the biometric database and the wartime powers to hack private cameras. On the American side it follows the line from the Church Committee and the 1978 surveillance act through the Snowden disclosures to Section 702, whose statute lapsed in June 2026 while collection continued, and then the newer questions: data the government buys rather than obtains by warrant, and tax and health records opened to immigration enforcement through software firms such as Palantir. Israel publishes no surveillance statistics comparable to America's, so the Israeli account rests on laws, rulings and official inquiries rather than a series.
