What Readers Can Do

The record in this book was built by people outside the institutions that failed. That is the most practical lesson it offers, and it suggests what ordinary readers can do.

Follow the law that would give the first one teeth. The Epstein Files Transparency Act II would let survivors, state officials and members of Congress go to court to make the Justice Department comply with the first Act, and would let state attorneys general prosecute Epstein's co-conspirators. As of 9 October 2026 its discharge petition had 216 of the 218 signatures needed to force a vote, with the vote on hold until after the November election. [cite:ep14_ny1_efta2] Whether a representative has signed is public, and asking is legitimate.

Watch the states. New Mexico's attorney general and legislature did what no federal agency did: reopened the case, searched the ranch and took testimony. [cite:ep10_kob_interim2026] State attorneys general elsewhere have the same powers within their borders, and a state inquiry does not depend on federal permission.

Support reporting and the courts. A newspaper series and a journalist's lawsuit did more to open this record than any agency. [cite:ep9_npr_brown2021] [cite:ep9_clearinghouse_phang]

Believe early reports. In this case, in the FBI's handling of Larry Nassar, at the BBC and in Rotherham, the first reports were made years before anyone acted. [cite:factually_farmer] [cite:ep12_opb_nassar2021] [cite:ep12_itv_savile2016] [cite:ep12_c4_rotherham2014] The single most protective act available to anyone is to take the first report seriously.

Protect the survivors' privacy, and nobody else's. The law already draws that line: victims' names withheld, the powerful's names not. [cite:ep8_govinfo_act] Readers who share material from the files can draw it too, and should never circulate a survivor's name or image.

If you or someone you know needs help: in the United States, the National Human Trafficking Hotline is 1-888-373-7888 (text 233733), the RAINN National Sexual Assault Hotline is 800-656-4673, and the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is 988. [cite:ep14_polaris_hotline] [cite:ep14_survivors_hotlines] Elsewhere, contact your local services.
