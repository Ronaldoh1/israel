A Bank in Jaffa, an Offer in East Africa

In the eighteen months from early 1902 to the summer of 1903, the Zionist movement ran on two tracks at once. It built banking infrastructure inside Palestine, and it seriously considered building its future somewhere else entirely. The two tracks sit uneasily together in later memory, which tends to hold that Palestine was always the only place Zionists were seriously willing to build a state. The record of these months shows a more complicated picture.

The first track was financial. On February 27, 1902, the Jewish Colonial Trust founded the Anglo-Palestine Company as its Palestine-facing subsidiary, with initial capital of about £50,000. David Wolffsohn, Herzl's close associate and later his successor, was its chairman. Its manager was Zalman David Levontin, one of the founders of Rishon LeZion in 1882. The company opened its first branch in Jaffa in 1903, and by 1914 it had expanded to Jerusalem, Beirut, Hebron, Haifa, Safed, Tiberias and Gaza.[cite:apc_wiki] [cite:jewishencyclopedia_colonial_trust] It financed land purchase, agriculture and credit cooperatives. Its later names trace an unbroken institutional line: it became the Anglo-Palestine Bank around 1930 and Bank Leumi in 1950–51. The modest subsidiary of 1902 was not yet the bank it would become, but the line from one to the other is direct.

What settlement financing meant in practice can be seen in a single loan. The Anglo-Palestine Company lent money to the Ahuzat Bayit association, which used it to build, in 1909, the first neighborhood of what became Tel Aviv.[cite:apc_wiki] Six years after the Jaffa branch opened, a bank, an association and a loan had produced the beginnings of a city. The full history of Tel Aviv's founding is larger than this one connection, but the connection itself is documented.

The second track was territorial, and it began with violence. In April 1903 came the Kishinev pogrom. In its aftermath the British Colonial Secretary, Joseph Chamberlain, offered Herzl the Guas Ngishu plateau in British East Africa for Jewish settlement.[cite:uganda_scheme_wiki] [cite:wiki_kishinev_pogrom] The offer is remembered as "the Uganda Scheme," but the name is inaccurate: the territory lay in present-day Kenya. Its timing was not incidental. It came on the heels of an internationally reported massacre, and the documents establish the offer and its trigger. They do not, by themselves, establish what longer-term calculations Britain may have had.

The offer went to the Sixth Zionist Congress in Basel in August 1903. The delegates voted 295 to 178 to send an investigatory commission to East Africa.[cite:uganda_scheme_wiki] What they approved was limited: an examination of the territory, not its adoption. Herzl framed the proposal explicitly as a Nachtasyl, a "night shelter," a temporary refuge from immediate danger rather than a replacement for the Land of Israel. Even so, the margin was far from a landslide, and the question was serious enough to come close to breaking the organization apart.

The clearest sign of how serious it was came from the Russian Zionist delegation, which walked out of the Congress in protest at the vote.[cite:uganda_scheme_wiki] A walkout is a stronger form of objection than a dissenting vote. It would be wrong to read the walkout as the view of every Russian Zionist, or to assume the rift was never repaired; the movement continued to function as a single body afterward. But it shows that the East Africa question was a genuine internal crisis, not a footnote.

Six years after Basel, then, the movement's own Congress came within a contested vote of formally exploring a territory other than Palestine. It did so not as a stunt but as a response to escalating pogrom violence. This was the closest the movement came, after 1897, to diverging from Palestine, and it happened in the same months that its Palestine bank was opening its doors in Jaffa. How the movement resolved the contradiction, and at what cost to its own unity, is the subject of [scene:s04f|the rejection of the East Africa offer].
