Isis, Queen of Heaven

The fullest account of an initiation into the mysteries of Isis is in a Latin novel of the second century, the Metamorphoses of Apuleius. Its narrator, Lucius, restored by the goddess from the shape of a donkey, tells only what may be told: that he "reached the very gates of death", passed through all the elements and returned, and saw "the sun at midnight shining brightly". [cite:ts13_cam_apuleius] In the same book Isis names herself "Queen of heaven" and "the natural mother of all things". Her spring festival carried a model ship to the sea for the safety of sailors; it was still celebrated in Italy in 416, decades after Theodosius's bans. [cite:vt36_fordham_apuleius] [cite:vt36_wiki_navigium]

Her last great temple stood on the island of Philae, at Egypt's southern frontier. The last known inscription in Egyptian hieroglyphs was carved there in 394. [cite:vt35_wiki_philae] According to Procopius, Justinian's commander Narses closed it around 535 to 537, put the priests under guard and "sent the statues to Byzantium". The temple became a church of St Stephen. [cite:vt33_procopius_philae] [cite:vt33_lemans_philae_church] The historian Jitse Dijkstra argues that its worship was already fading, so that the order closed a temple whose priests were few. [cite:vt33_gozzoli_dijkstra]

A widely repeated claim holds that Isis simply became Mary. The record tests it piece by piece.

The resemblances are real and the documented routes are mostly different: scribes, hymns and councils, not a captured temple. What is documented is a stated policy of continuity. In 601 Pope Gregory I wrote to a missionary bound for England that the temples of the gods should not be destroyed, only the idols in them; they were to be purified and given altars, so that the people would "come to places familiar and dear to them". [cite:vt36_fordham_mellitus] The Church took places and customs openly, as a method of mission. The full account is in [scene:vt36_isis_and_mary|The Vatican Architecture: Isis and Mary].

The counter-record belongs beside it. The Church that is said to have hidden the goddess raised a woman higher than most Christian bodies ever have: Mother of God in 431, the Immaculate Conception defined in 1854, the Assumption in 1950. It also fixed a limit. As recently as November 2025 a doctrinal note judged it "always inappropriate" to call her Co-redemptrix, because "Christ is the only Mediator". [cite:vt36_va_munif1950] [cite:vt36_vn_mpf2025] The Mother was honoured at the highest rank the institution allows a creature, and refused, explicitly, the rank of a goddess.
