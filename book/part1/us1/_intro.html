Part One sets out the question this volume answers and the terms on which it answers it. It first states precisely what American aid to Israel is: a fixed ten-year grant of military money, paid early and spent mostly with American contractors, whose weight in Israel's economy has fallen from about an eighth of GDP in 1985 to just over half a per cent today, and what that money does and does not free up in Israel's budget. It then asks why Americans compare the two countries at all, sets the aid against the size of the American budget, tests the common claim that American taxpayers pay for Israeli health care, and lays down the rules by which the rest of the book compares the two systems: systems rather than peoples, the same sources for both countries, figures over time rather than snapshots, and a plain statement of whom each figure covers.
