Iran's backing of all three groups is well documented, but each has its own origin, and calling them "just proxies" erases it. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Qods Force has funded, armed and trained Hamas for decades, with reported figures ranging from $22–70 million a year historically to $350 million by 2023, per Israeli security sources. [cite:congress_iran_hamas_testimony] Yet Hamas, a Sunni movement, was historically wary of taking too much money from the region's dominant Shia power. [cite:jstribune_hamas_iran_relationship] It was founded in 1987 during the first intifada, after twenty years of occupation.

Hezbollah formed after Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon, with about 1,500 Revolutionary Guard instructors, and its first aim was ending the occupation of the south. Former Israeli prime minister Ehud Barak said in 2006 that "it was our presence there that created Hezbollah"; an Israeli research center argues Iran was the founding cause. [cite:wiki_hezbollah_mirror] [cite:terrorism_info_hezbollah_birth] The Houthis grew from a 1990s Zaydi revival and fought Yemen's government and a Saudi-led coalition long before attacking Israel. The frame is right about alignment and selective about causes.
