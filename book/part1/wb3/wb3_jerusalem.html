East Jerusalem

The laws of June 1967. Within three weeks of the war, Israel annexed East Jerusalem in all but name. On 27 June 1967 the Knesset amended the law to let the government apply Israeli law to any area it designated, and the next day the government applied it to an enlarged city: about 70 square kilometres were added to Jerusalem's boundaries, only about 6 of them the Jordanian municipality of East Jerusalem and about 64 taken from 28 surrounding villages and from parts of Bethlehem and Beit Jala. [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex]

The Mughrabi Quarter. In the Old City, the Mughrabi Quarter in front of the Western Wall was bulldozed to make room for the plaza that stands there now. [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex] After nineteen years in which Jews could not reach the Wall, Israel opened it; the families of the quarter in front of it lost their homes.

The world's response. On 21 May 1968 the Security Council declared Israel's legal and administrative measures in Jerusalem invalid and called for them to be rescinded. When the Knesset passed a Basic Law on Jerusalem in 1980, the Council declared it "null and void" and asked states with embassies in the city to withdraw them. [cite:wb_wiki_ejannex] The enlarged boundary drawn in June 1967 remains the line inside which, as later parts show, Israel builds without calling it settlement.
