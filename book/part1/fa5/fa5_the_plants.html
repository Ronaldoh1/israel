The Plants

Plants appear at the centre of several rites on this shelf. At Delphi the Pythia chewed laurel leaves before she spoke. [cite:fa_brit_delphi] At Eleusis the initiates broke their fast with the kykeon, a drink of barley, water and pennyroyal; whether it was psychoactive has been argued since 1978 and is not settled. [cite:og_wiki_kykeon] [cite:og_prh_road] In northern Mexico and the American Southwest, the peyote cactus was a sacrament long before the Spanish arrived.

The Inquisition of New Spain made peyote a matter of faith. Its General Edict of Faith of 1571 already condemned the use of peyote for sacraments and divination. On 9 June 1620 it issued an edict specifically against peyote, framed as preventing "heresy, idolatry, and superstition", and posted it on church and convent doors across New Spain, Guatemala, Yucatán, Honduras and as far as the Philippines, where peyote does not grow. A report the Inquisition requested in 1619 preceded it. [cite:fa_harvard_peyote] Practitioners responded by giving the plant Catholic names and images, such as Santa Rosa, as a protective cover. [cite:fa_harvard_peyote]

Four centuries later the ban came from a secular state, and so did the relief. Peyote is a controlled drug in the United States, and members of the Native American Church may use it in their all-night ceremonies. [cite:og_unl_nac] In 1990 the Supreme Court held that a state need not exempt sacramental peyote from its general drug laws; in 1994 Congress made "the use, possession, or transportation of peyote by an Indian for bona fide traditional ceremonial purposes" lawful. [cite:og_mtsu_airfa]

The laboratory has returned to the plants as well. In 2000 Johns Hopkins researchers became the first in the United States to obtain regulatory approval to resume research with psychedelics. A study of a single dose of psilocybin, the active compound of certain mushrooms, published in 2006, reported "mystical-type experiences having substantial and sustained personal meaning" and is widely credited with renewing the field. In 2019 private donors funded the university's Center for Psychedelic and Consciousness Research. Small studies there have reported relief from cancer-related distress and rapid reductions in depression. [cite:fa_jhu_psych]

This book reports the record and does not recommend the use of any substance. The Hopkins studies were conducted under medical supervision with screened volunteers; their findings are small and continuing. [cite:fa_jhu_psych] What the record shows is a plant forbidden first as idolatry and later as a drug, then protected as a sacrament, and a compound once studied in secret now studied openly as a door to what its researchers call mystical experience.
