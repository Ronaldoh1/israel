December 1987: The Uprising, and Hamas

The fifteen years of quiet that Ariel Sharon claimed for his campaign of 1971 ended in the Jabaliya refugee camp, the largest in the strip. [cite:gz_afp_camps] What began there in December 1987 became the first intifada, an uprising across the West Bank and Gaza that lasted until the Oslo agreements. The same week, Gaza's branch of the Muslim Brotherhood gave itself a new name and a new purpose. This section sets out how the uprising started, what it cost by the competing counts, and how Hamas came into being.

Jabaliya, 8–9 December 1987. On 8 December an Israeli truck crashed into cars carrying Palestinian workers home from Israel, killing four people, three of them from Jabaliya. Rumours spread that the crash was deliberate, in revenge for the stabbing death of an Israeli businessman; Israel denied it. The next day young people in the camp threw stones and petrol bombs at army vehicles, and soldiers opened fire, killing 17-year-old Hatem al-Sisi. [cite:gz_wiki_intifada1] Demonstrations spread from the camp to the rest of the strip and to the West Bank.

The cost. In the uprising's first year 142 Palestinians were killed in the Gaza Strip, 77 of them shot, 37 from the effects of tear gas and 17 from beatings; no Israelis were killed there that year. Tear gas was used heavily in the camps, among them al-Shati, Jabaliya and Bureij, and one calculation counts 60,706 people injured in Gaza by gunfire, beatings or tear gas between 1988 and 1993. [cite:gz_wiki_intifada1] Across both territories, the Israeli human rights organization B'Tselem counts between 1,087 and 1,204 Palestinians killed by Israeli forces over the six years, about 241 of them children, and 179 Israelis killed by Palestinians; official Israeli figures give 200 Israelis. Palestinians also killed Palestinians: an estimated 771 by the count of the Associated Press, or 942 by the army's, were executed as alleged collaborators. [cite:gz_wiki_intifada1] Israel arrested tens of thousands, with estimates ranging from 57,000 to 120,000, deported 481 people and demolished between 1,882 and 2,532 homes. Faced with the first months' death toll, defence minister Yitzhak Rabin adopted a policy he described as "might, power and beatings." [cite:gz_wiki_intifada1]

The birth of Hamas. Gaza's Islamist movement predated the uprising. In 1973 the cleric Ahmed Yassin founded the Mujama al-Islamiya, a charitable association linked to the Muslim Brotherhood. Hamas, the Islamic Resistance Movement, dates its founding to 10 December 1987, the day after the uprising began, when the Brotherhood in Gaza took up an activist and nationalist line under that name. [cite:gz_wiki_hamas] Its charter, signed on 18 August 1988, defined it as a wing of the Muslim Brotherhood, called for an Islamic state in all of Palestine and rejected any negotiated settlement, saying the conflict could be resolved only through jihad. [cite:gz_wiki_hamas] In 2017 Hamas published a "Document of General Principles and Policies" that accepted a Palestinian state on the 1967 lines without recognizing Israel; it did not formally repudiate the charter. [cite:gz_wiki_hamas]

A disputed beginning. Whether Israel helped the movement grow is a question the record raises but does not close. The historian Rashid Khalidi and reporting in the Times of Israel are cited for the view that Israel initially and discreetly supported the Islamists as a counterweight to the secular PLO. [cite:gz_wiki_hamas] The later chapters of this volume, and the library's [scene:s20|account of October 7], return to the record of Israeli policy toward Hamas, which is better documented for the years after 2007 than for the 1980s.
