Azerbaijan: Oil for Arms

Relations date from Azerbaijan's independence in the early 1990s, when Israel sought a partner on Iran's northern border. Azerbaijan is Shia-majority but governed as a secular state; Iran is home to an estimated 20 million ethnic Azerbaijanis. [cite:rs_az_iran] [cite:rs_az_normalize] Baku long avoided opening an embassy in Israel so as not to offend Muslim states, and did so in Tel Aviv in 2023. [cite:w_az_isr]

The exchange

Azerbaijani crude has supplied between 40 and 60 percent of Israel's oil in recent years, carried by the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline to Turkey's Mediterranean coast; Baku did not stop shipments during the Gaza war. [cite:jamestown_az_2026] [cite:w_btc] [cite:rs_az_normalize] Its state oil company also took a 10 percent stake in Israel's Tamar gas field. [cite:jamestown_trilateral] In the other direction, Stockholm International Peace Research Institute data show Israel supplied about 69 percent of Azerbaijan's major arms imports in 2016–2021. American arms sales to Azerbaijan have been restricted since 1992 under Section 907 of the Freedom Support Act, which has made Israel's role larger. [cite:newarab_az_accords]

Karabakh

Israeli weapons played a key role in Azerbaijan's victories over Armenia in 2020 and 2023. [cite:rs_az_normalize] AP reported that Israel supplied weapons in the weeks before the offensive of 19 September 2023; Armenia's ambassador said Israeli weapons were "firing at our people", and Israel's ministries declined to comment. [cite:csm_israeli_arms_karabakh] [cite:jpost_armenian_envoy] After a nine-month blockade of the enclave's only road to Armenia, the offensive forced its authorities to surrender in two days, and more than 100,000 ethnic Armenians, most of the population, fled within a week; Armenia called it ethnic cleansing. Nagorno-Karabakh is internationally recognised as Azerbaijani territory, and Baku pledged to protect Armenians' rights. [cite:abc_karabakh_100k] [cite:pbs_karabakh_80]

Between Israel and Iran

In December 2024 AIPAC's leaders visited Baku for the first time and told President Aliyev that his support for Israel "will be recognised"; analysts describe Azerbaijan as a candidate for the Abraham Accords, though it already has full relations. [cite:newarab_az_accords] [cite:jamestown_trilateral] On 5 March 2026, during the war with Iran, drones struck the Azerbaijani exclave of Nakhchivan; Aliyev called it a terrorist act by Iran, which denied it. [cite:w_nakhchivan_2026] Azerbaijan said it would not join operations against Iran, sent humanitarian aid there, and denied a CNN report that Israel had used its territory. [cite:inss_az_2026] An Israeli security institute describes Baku's conduct as "alignment tempered by restraint". [cite:inss_az_2026]
