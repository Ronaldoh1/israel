The AI Layer: Models Inside the State

A large language model is a program trained on vast amounts of text to predict and produce language; it can summarise documents, translate, write code and sort messages by topic or tone at a speed no team of analysts can match. "Agentic" systems go a step further and let a model take actions, such as searching databases or filling in forms, in a chain of steps. For a government, the attraction is scale: a model can read every document in a case file, every post in an applicant's history, or every intercepted message in a language its analysts do not speak. The risk is the same scale applied to error, since models produce confident wrong answers on sarcasm, quotation and slang. Government use is graded by security level: the Pentagon's "Impact Level" ratings run from public data to IL5, for sensitive unclassified information, and IL6, for data classified up to Secret. [cite:de26_ds_genaimil] [cite:de26_reg_claudepal] This section follows the policy that governs these models, the contracts that put them inside the government, and the one dispute in which a vendor's limits on use were tested.

The policy turn

On 30 October 2023 President Biden signed Executive Order 14110 on the "safe, secure, and trustworthy" development and use of AI, the federal government's main rulebook for the technology at the time. On 20 January 2025, his first day back in office, President Trump revoked it in Executive Order 14148, a list of rescissions that also revoked the 2021 order on private prisons. [cite:de26_ucsb_14148] On 23 July 2025 he signed three further orders alongside an "AI Action Plan": one to remove regulations the administration called onerous, one to promote exports of American AI, and one titled "Preventing Woke AI in the Federal Government," which directs agencies to buy only models that are "truth-seeking" and ideologically neutral and that do not skew answers toward positions such as diversity programmes. [cite:de26_nbc_woke] The orders loosen rules on how models are built and add a content condition on what the government buys.

The contracts

The models entered the state under both administrations. In November 2024, under Biden, Anthropic, Palantir and Amazon Web Services announced that Anthropic's Claude models would run inside Palantir's AI platform on Amazon's cloud at IL6; Anthropic said it granted exceptions to its usage rules that were "carefully calibrated" for "carefully selected government agencies," including foreign intelligence analysis, while keeping bans on uses such as disinformation and weapons. [cite:de26_reg_claudepal] On 14 July 2025 the Pentagon's Chief Digital and AI Office announced awards of up to $200 million each to Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and xAI to build "agentic AI workflows across a variety of mission areas." [cite:de26_cdao] The same day xAI launched "Grok for Government," days after apologising for its chatbot's antisemitic posts on X. [cite:de26_eng_grokgov] [cite:de11_reuters_grok] Through GSA's "OneGov" programme, OpenAI and Anthropic offered their government models for $1 per agency per year, and on 25 September 2025 xAI offered Grok 4 for 42 cents; an earlier xAI deal had reportedly fallen through after the antisemitic output, more than thirty advocacy groups objected, and GSA said Grok 4 met its guidelines. [cite:de26_fs_onegov] The Pentagon's internal chat platform, GenAI.mil, opened with Google's Gemini for Government and was planned for more than three million personnel; ChatGPT and Grok were added at IL5 on 31 August 2026. [cite:de26_ds_genaimil] Inside DOGE, Reuters reported in May 2025 that staff were expanding use of a customised Grok to analyse federal data, which [scene:de19_the_access|Part Nineteen] examines. [cite:de19_reuters_grok]

The supply-chain-risk case

The one documented test of whether a vendor could limit how the government uses its model began in February 2026. The Pentagon sought to replace two prohibitions in its contract with Anthropic, on mass domestic surveillance and on fully autonomous weapons, with permission for "all lawful use," and on 24 February set a deadline of 27 February. [cite:de26_d1_anthropic] On 26 February Anthropic's chief executive, Dario Amodei, said that in a "narrow set of cases" AI "can undermine, rather than defend, democratic values," named those two uses, and said the company "cannot in good conscience accede." The Pentagon's spokesman, Sean Parnell, said the department had no interest in mass surveillance of Americans, which he called illegal, or in autonomous weapons without human involvement, and asked only to use the model "for all lawful purposes," adding: "We will not let ANY company dictate the terms regarding how we make operational decisions." [cite:de26_wt_anthropic]

On 27 February Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced that Anthropic would be designated a supply-chain risk, a status normally used to keep products from untrusted suppliers out of military systems, and the President ordered all agencies to stop using Anthropic's services; the designation was formalised on 3 March. Under Secretary Emil Michael wrote that no private company would "insert their opinions in the chain of command." [cite:de26_d1_anthropic] [cite:de26_eng_openai] As the designation was announced, on 27–28 February, OpenAI also announced an agreement to deploy its models on a classified Pentagon network, cloud-only; its chief executive, Sam Altman, said the agreement included "prohibitions on domestic mass surveillance" and "human responsibility for the use of force," and that OpenAI had asked for the same terms to be offered to every AI company. [cite:de26_eng_openai] The contract text has not been published, so how its terms compare with those Anthropic refused cannot be checked from the record.

The two court rulings rest on different statutes and, by the reading of commercial lawyers, do not conflict. The California court found that the narrower statute, aimed at adversaries, did not reach a company's good-faith negotiation of usage limits; reporting described the judge as calling the Pentagon's actions "troubling and retaliatory." The appeals court held that the broader procurement statute reaches "any person," and that the department could reasonably conclude a supplier able to change its model's behaviour through training posed a risk to its use. [cite:de26_taft_dccir] [cite:de26_ds_enbanc] Anthropic said it "respectfully disagree[s]" and noted that another court had held the parallel designation unlawful; the Pentagon did not comment on the rehearing petition. [cite:de26_d1_anthropic] [cite:de26_ds_enbanc]

What the record shows, and does not

Documented: the federal rules on how AI is built were withdrawn and replaced with a procurement test on content; frontier models from four companies sit inside defence and civilian agencies at prices of a dollar or less; and the one vendor that held out for written limits on domestic surveillance was excluded, an exclusion one court has set aside and another has upheld. Reported, from officials speaking to Axios in March 2025 and not confirmed by a published document: a State Department effort called "Catch and Revoke" that would use AI-assisted reviews of tens of thousands of student-visa holders' social-media accounts; the department did not comment directly. [cite:de26_audit_axios_catchrevoke] [cite:de26_audit_catchrevoke] The published social-media vetting rules are in [scene:de28_brokers_and_doctrine|Part Thirty], and ICE's address-scoring tool in [scene:de28_ice_contracts|its first section]. Not documented: any of these models being used for mass surveillance of Americans. The Pentagon has said it does not seek that and that it is illegal; the record cannot show a negative, and the contract terms that would settle the question are not public. The capability a frontier model adds, reading everything at once, is real; its use against domestic critics would need its own document, and none has surfaced. [cite:de26_wt_anthropic] [cite:de26_eng_openai]

If it were connected

A model placed inside an integration platform is the clearest join in this layer. Documented: since November 2024 Anthropic's models have been offered inside Palantir's AI platform at the Secret level, so a language model can sit on top of whatever data a Palantir customer has loaded; and frontier models from four companies are installed across defence and civilian agencies. [cite:de26_reg_claudepal] [cite:de26_ds_genaimil] Reported: AI-assisted reading of visa holders' posts. [cite:de26_audit_axios_catchrevoke] Possible, with no document: a model running over ICE's joined records, the people-search profiles, Medicaid addresses and phone extractions that [scene:de28_the_ecosystem_map|the ecosystem map] shows reaching its platforms, to rank people for arrest or to read everything a flagged person has posted. That would need only a deployment inside the agency's own system, not a new law or a new contract type; what stands in the way is contract terms that are not public and, for the one vendor that wrote limits down, an exclusion still in court. Not supported: any model in use for mass surveillance of Americans; the Pentagon says it seeks no such use, and no record shows one. [cite:de26_wt_anthropic]
