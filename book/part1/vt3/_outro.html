Part Three has read the 1929 bargain and followed it to 2026. In 1929 the Holy See gained a sovereign territory, protected properties, 750 million lire in cash and a billion lire in bonds, a state religion, civil effect for its marriages and religious teaching in state schools; Mussolini's government gained recognition of Rome as Italy's capital, a Holy See pledged to stay out of disputes between states, a bishops' oath of loyalty and a clergy barred from parties. [cite:vt3_lateran_text] The 1931 clash over Catholic Action showed that the two sides bargained rather than merged. [cite:vt3_nab_1931] [cite:vt3_isacem_1931] The Villa Madama Agreement of 18 February 1984 ended the state religion and the oath, and Law 222 of 1985 replaced state pay for priests with the otto per mille, which in 2026 gave the Italian Bishops' Conference about 1.06 billion euros, allocated by the choices of about 27 per cent of taxpayers. [cite:vt3_l121_1985] [cite:vt3_l222_text] [cite:vt3_teleborsa_8x2026] The property-tax exemption that the European Commission found unlawful for 2006 to 2011 was ordered recovered after the Court of Justice's judgment of 6 November 2018, and as of October 2026 Italy had yet to report collecting any of it. [cite:vt3_curia_pr2018] [cite:vt3_ff_sept2026] The Holy See, Vatican City and the Church in Italy have appeared here as three different beneficiaries of one settlement. Part Four separates them properly and shows how the smallest of the three, the City, actually runs, beginning with [scene:vt4_holy_see_vs_city|the Holy See and the City State].
