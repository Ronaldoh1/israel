Part Two set out the organization at the center of the network. AIPAC grew from Isaiah Kenen's office, opened in 1951 and given its present name in 1959, into a membership organization that reports more than five million members; it is built as four bodies: a 501(c)(4) lobby, a 501(c)(3) foundation that pays for congressional travel, a PAC and a super PAC. [cite:pm_wiki_aipac] [cite:pm_pp_aipac990] [cite:pm_pp_aief990] Its lobbying spending is small, about $3.5 million in 2018 and $3.8 million in 2025; its campaign spending, since December 2021, is large: $44 million in 2022, $95.1 million in 2024, and more than $100 million in the 2026 cycle by August. [cite:pm_tablet_lobbying] [cite:pm_cjn_lobby2025] [cite:pm_israelite_2024] [cite:pm_sludge_100m_2026]

What is settled is the structure and the size. What is contested is the old question of what the structure is: an American advocacy organization exercising ordinary rights, or a body that should be treated in law as acting for a foreign state; and the newer question of what its campaign money does. What the record leaves open is the money behind the foundation and the lobby, whose donors are not public. Part Three counts the campaign money cycle by cycle.
