Who Reported Gaza

The journalists of Gaza were, for the most part, Gazans. With foreign reporters kept out, the international agencies relied on local freelancers and staff, and Arab networks such as Al Jazeera on their own Gaza bureaus. [cite:gz_wlrn_ban] [cite:pr_aj_afp2025] They reported from the same streets, shelters and hospitals as the people in their stories, and lived under the same bombardment and the same hunger.

Reporting hungry. In July 2025, as the IPC moved toward its finding of famine, Agence France-Presse asked Israel to let it evacuate its freelance journalists from Gaza, saying their situation had become "untenable." AFP's journalists' society wrote: "We fear learning of their deaths at any moment." One of its photographers, Bashar, wrote that his "body is thin" and that he no longer had the strength to work. [cite:pr_aj_afp2025]

Reporting bereaved. In October 2023 Wael Dahdouh, Al Jazeera's Gaza bureau chief, learned on air that an Israeli strike on Nuseirat camp had killed his wife, a son, a daughter and a grandson. In December he was wounded in a drone strike that killed his cameraman. On 7 January 2024 his eldest son, Hamza, also an Al Jazeera journalist, was killed with a colleague when their car was struck; the army said it had targeted a "terrorist" operating a drone that threatened troops, and Al Jazeera said they were not flying a drone. "We are going to proceed as long as we are alive and breathing," Dahdouh said. [cite:pr_nbc_dahdouh] [cite:pr_wiki_killing] Part Six follows Hamza Dahdouh's case and others, one by one.
