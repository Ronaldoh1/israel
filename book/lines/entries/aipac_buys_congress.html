The record shows heavy, legal spending to reward allies and defeat opponents; it does not show votes being bought. Federal law bars foreign governments and foreign nationals from giving in any US election, so the money in campaign filings comes from American donors and American PACs [cite:fec_foreign_nationals]. AIPAC itself gave nothing to candidates until it created a PAC and the United Democracy Project super PAC in late 2021 [cite:opensecrets_aipac_2019]. Since then the scale has been large: pro-Israel PAC spending reached over $100M in 2024, including about $14.5M against Rep. Jamaal Bowman and about $8.5M against Rep. Cori Bush, both of whom lost their primaries [cite:opensecrets_udp] [cite:factcheck_udp_2024].

Whether money changes votes is harder to show. A Guardian analysis found that members who took more pro-Israel money more often backed military support for Israel, but party, not money, best predicted their statements on civilian casualties [cite:guardian_gaza_donors]. Before 1971, candidates' donors were largely undisclosed, so claims of systematic payments to members of Congress before 1948 cannot be proved or disproved [cite:wiki_fcpa_1925].
