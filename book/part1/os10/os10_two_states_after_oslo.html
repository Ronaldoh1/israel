Two States, After Oslo

On 23 December 2016 the Security Council adopted Resolution 2334, declaring that Israel's settlements in territory occupied since 1967, including East Jerusalem, have "no legal validity" and demanding that settlement stop; it passed 14 to 0, with the Obama administration abstaining instead of vetoing. It had no enforcement mechanism, Israel denounced it, and the next administration moved American policy the other way. [cite:deccan_unsc_2334]

Internationally, recognition of a Palestinian state grew. Spain, Ireland and Norway recognised Palestine in May 2024; on 21 and 22 September 2025 Britain, Canada, Australia, France, Portugal, Belgium and others followed, bringing the total to 157 of 193 UN members, including four of the five permanent members of the Security Council. The United States is the exception. [cite:wiki_recognition_palestine] [cite:aljazeera_157_list]

Israel moved the other way. On 21 February 2024 the Knesset voted 99 to 9 to oppose any "unilateral" recognition of a Palestinian state, and in September 2025 Netanyahu said at a settlement that "there will be no Palestinian state". [cite:toi_knesset_99] [cite:globalsecurity_two_state] Support among the peoples fell too: by December 2022, 33 percent of Palestinians and 34 percent of Israeli Jews supported two states, down from majorities a decade earlier. [cite:globalsecurity_two_state] The West Bank's settler population, with East Jerusalem, is now around 737,000. [cite:globalsecurity_two_state]
