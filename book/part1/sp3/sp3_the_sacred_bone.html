The Sacred Bone and the Secretion

The bone at the base of the spine carries a religious name. "Sacrum" comes from the Latin os sacrum, "holy bone", a translation of the Greek hieron osteon. Three explanations are given: that it was the part of an animal offered in sacrifice, that the soul was believed to reside there, or that the Latin is a mistranslation, since the Greek hieron can mean "great" as well as "holy", and the bone is large. [cite:sp_wikt_sacrum] Which is right is disputed. The name, either way, is ancient.

Rabbinic tradition has its own bone of the spine. The luz, "almond" in Hebrew, is said not to decay, and the body will be rebuilt from it at the resurrection. In a midrash, the Emperor Hadrian asks Rabbi Joshua ben Hananiah how the dead will be raised, and the rabbi answers, from the luz of the spine; he shows that it survives water, fire and the mill, and that it breaks the hammer and anvil that strike it. [cite:sp_wiki_luz] Where the luz is has never been settled: some identify it with the top vertebra under the skull, others with the coccyx or the sacrum. Several hadiths in Islam carry the same idea of an indestructible bone from which the body is remade. [cite:sp_wiki_luz]

The sacred secretion: the oldest source

The Canon recorded the popular modern teaching of a sacred secretion: that an oil is formed in the body each month, descends and, if conserved, rises to the head, and that this oil is what "Christ", the anointed, originally meant. It found that the widely shared version dates from social-media posts of 2017 and that the ancient texts on this shelf contain no such teaching. [cite:cn_sub_secretion] The teaching is older than those posts. In The Anti-Christ, reprinted from the journal Azoth in June 1918, the American homeopath and occultist George W. Carey (1845–1924) wrote that "there is a seed or Psycho-Physical germ born in the or out of, the Solar Plexus". [cite:sp_carey_antichrist] [cite:sp_wiki_carey]

Carey's timing and route are specific. The seed is born "every twenty-eight and one-half days", when the moon is in the sign the sun occupied at the person's birth; it "is carried up Pneumo (or vagus) gastric nerve", crosses into the cerebellum, which he calls the tomb, and if it is saved it rises to the pineal gland. [cite:sp_carey_antichrist] Two years later he and the astrologer Inez Eudora Perry published God-Man: The Word Made Flesh. [cite:sp_wiki_carey] The original teaching, in other words, is an American occult text of 1918, not an ancient one, and in it the seed travels by a nerve of the gut and the chest, not up the spinal column.

What physiology says

The fluid most often named in the modern version is cerebrospinal fluid. An adult has about 150 millilitres of it, most of it made by the choroid plexus inside the brain's ventricles at a rate of 400 to 600 millilitres a day, so that it is renewed four or five times every twenty-four hours. It cushions the brain, makes it buoyant, reducing its effective weight from about 1,500 grams to about 50, and helps clear waste, especially in deep sleep. [cite:sp_ncbi_csf] It is made continuously, not once a month. The reading can use it as an image; the physiology does not support it as a mechanism.
