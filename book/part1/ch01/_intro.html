Before there was a flag, there was ground: a land whose Roman province was renamed Syria Palaestina after crushing the Bar Kokhba revolt, that passed through Byzantine, Muslim, Crusader, Mamluk and, from 1516, Ottoman hands, and that held both a continuous if smaller Jewish presence and a majority population of Arabic-speaking Muslims and Christians. The modern conflict did not begin with a blank map. It began with two documented histories laid on the same territory.

This opening stage clears that ground before the narrative of the state begins. It starts with the present-day map, because most confusion begins with geography: Israel within its 1949 armistice lines, the West Bank and its Oslo-era divisions, Gaza, and East Jerusalem. It explains who is meant by "the Palestinians," and what the legal words "occupation," "apartheid" and "genocide" mean, who has applied them and who disputes them. It then turns to the older record: how the story is usually taught, what the name "Israel" first meant in Genesis, a thousand years of Jewish life across the Muslim-majority world, and the departures of 1948 to 1972.

The question running through every section is simple to state and harder to answer: what does the record actually show, as distinct from the version most readers were handed?
